# Instructional Design and Creativity: A Response to the Critics

## Summary
Walter Dick's 1995 *Educational Technology* article responds to critics who argue that systematic instructional design stifles creativity. Dick contends that the systematic ID process does not constrain but rather channels creative work: the Dick and Carey model provides a structure within which creative decisions about content, strategies, media, and assessment are made at every stage. Dick distinguishes between the process of design (which should be systematic) and the products of design (which can and should be creative and varied).

## Key Concepts
- **Systematic ID**: A sequenced, iterative process for designing instruction (analyze → design → develop → implement → evaluate)
- **Creativity within constraints**: Creative decisions occur at every step of the ID process, not despite it
- **Design products vs. design process**: The model specifies process, not predetermined outcomes
- **Objectivist vs. constructivist critique**: Critics conflate systematic process with behaviorist outcomes; Dick argues the process is compatible with constructivist goals

## Theoretical Framework
Dick defends a systems-based approach, arguing that systematic process and creative outcomes are not contradictory. He positions the Dick and Carey model as an open framework that accommodates diverse learning theories and innovative solutions within its stages.

## Methods
Conceptual response and defense; draws on experience with the Dick and Carey model and examples of creative ID practice within systematic frameworks.

## Main Arguments
1. Critics misunderstand the Dick and Carey model as prescribing content or strategies when it only prescribes a design process
2. Every step of systematic ID requires creative judgment: choosing objectives, selecting media, devising assessment, etc.
3. Systematic ID is not inherently behaviorist; constructivist outcomes can be pursued within the model
4. The real danger is abandoning systematic process for unguided creativity, which risks ineffective instruction

## Limitations
Dick's defense may understate the degree to which the model's linear logic can constrain iterative, emergent, and participatory design approaches valued by constructivists. The exchange with Rowland reveals genuine paradigmatic disagreement.
