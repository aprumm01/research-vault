# From Big Data to Rich Theory: Integrating Critical Discourse Analysis with Structural Topic Modeling

## Metadata
- **Authors:** Ana M. Aranda, Kathrin Sele, Helen Etchanchu, Jonne Y. Guyt, Eero Vaara
- **Year:** 2021
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1111/emre.12452
- **Source PDF:** [[European Management Review - 2021 - Aranda - From Big Data to Rich Theory  Integrating Critical Discourse Analysis with.pdf]]

---

## Overview
This paper proposes a mixed-methods framework that integrates [[Critical Discourse Analysis]] (CDA) with [[Structural Topic Modeling]] (STM) to address the challenge of analyzing large textual corpora in management research. Using the tobacco industry's discursive legitimation struggles as an illustrative example, the authors develop a stepwise model that leverages STM's ability to handle scale while preserving CDA's critical depth and contextual sensitivity. The paper is methodologically focused, offering both practical guidance and theoretical justification for combining these two paradigmatically distinct approaches.

## Research Overview
- **Research question:** How can management researchers integrate quantitative topic modeling with qualitative critical discourse analysis to analyze large-scale textual data without sacrificing theoretical richness?
- **Methodology:** Explanatory sequential [[mixed methods]] design; stepwise model: (1) STM as exploratory/sampling device, (2) CDA for in-depth critical interpretation; applied to tobacco industry discourse data
- **Context:** Management studies / organizational theory; *European Management Review*, 2021; multi-institutional European authorship

## Theories of Knowledge
- **Epistemological stance:** Critical realist with explicit mixed-paradigm positioning; CDA is post-positivist and critical; STM is computational/positivist — the paper argues for principled bridging
- **Key frameworks:**
  - [[Critical Discourse Analysis]] (CDA) — Fairclough, Wodak; texts as "sites of struggle"; discourse as social action embedded in power relations
  - [[Structural Topic Modeling]] (STM) — Roberts et al. (2014); extension of LDA that incorporates document-level metadata as topic covariates
  - [[Discursive Legitimation]] — Vaara et al.; how actors use language to justify and contest legitimacy
  - [[Mediatized Society]] — Wodak's "discursive swarming" — meaning made through overlapping, interconnected discourses at scale
- **Knowledge construction:** Knowledge is constructed through discourse; large-scale discursive data requires computational assistance to map the terrain before critical interpretation can proceed

## Central Arguments
1. CDA alone is powerful but cannot scale to the volume of textual data now available; premature sampling risks missing systemic discursive patterns.
2. STM alone produces statistically meaningful patterns but lacks the critical orientation and contextual depth to explain *why* those patterns matter.
3. The integration of STM (breadth) and CDA (depth) is not merely methodological convenience — it requires explicit epistemological negotiation and transparent paradigmatic assumptions.
4. A stepwise model (STM first, then CDA) allows researchers to use computational output to guide purposive sampling for critical qualitative analysis.

## Evidence
- Illustrative example: tobacco industry discursive legitimation struggles; corpus of media texts and industry documents
- STM identifies topic clusters across the corpus; topic prevalence linked to document metadata (time, source, actor type)
- CDA then applied to high-prevalence topics and representative documents: reveals how industry actors legitimize tobacco through health-risk minimization, economic framing, and individual freedom rhetoric
- The integrated approach captures both breadth (which actors say what, when) and depth (how legitimation strategies are constructed linguistically)

> "Combining both approaches overcomes their limitations and provides great potential for exploring phenomena that matter in our mediatized society." (Aranda et al., 2021, p. 197)

> "Texts as 'sites of struggles' that are inextricably entwined with material social elements." (Wodak, 2001, as cited in Aranda et al., 2021, p. 198)

## Conclusion
The paper provides a replicable, theoretically grounded framework for integrating computational and critical methods in management research. It extends the conversation about [[mixed methods]] into the domain of large-scale discourse analysis and offers practical steps for researchers facing massive textual datasets. Implications for fields beyond management — including organizational informatics, HCI, and design research — are significant.

## Bias Check
- The tobacco industry example, while illustrative, is a particularly clear-cut case of discursive manipulation — may make the method appear more powerful than it would be with messier or more ambiguous discourse
- The paper is written from within management studies; application to other domains requires adaptation of the stepwise model
- STM involves researcher choices (number of topics, covariates) that introduce subjectivity not fully acknowledged

## Discussion Questions
1. How does this framework differ from and complement the approach in [[Baumer_2017_GroundedTheoryTopicModeling]]? Could the two be combined?
2. What are the ethical implications of applying [[Structural Topic Modeling]] to social media data — who controls the corpus, and what is excluded?
3. How does [[discursive legitimation]] operate in the context of AI — who are the actors, and what are the contested legitimacy claims?
4. What would CDA reveal about AI discourse that STM alone would miss?
5. How does the concept of "discursive swarming" apply to contemporary AI media coverage?
