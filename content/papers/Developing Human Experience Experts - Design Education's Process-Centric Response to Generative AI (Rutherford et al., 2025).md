# Developing Human Experience Experts: Design Education's Process-Centric Response to Generative AI

## Summary

This paper addresses how design education must evolve in response to generative AI tools that increasingly handle technical production tasks. As AI becomes capable of generating layouts, writing code, and producing design assets, the author argues that designers must shift from being technical producers to becoming human experience experts who understand complex social contexts, anticipate intended and unintended consequences, and create meaningful interventions aligned with human needs and values.

The paper presents a process-centric framework for design education built on four theoretical foundations: systems thinking, phenomenology, activity theory, and futures studies. Drawing from implementations in undergraduate and graduate courses at Miami University from 2023-2025, Cheatham demonstrates how these theoretical foundations translate into practical teaching methods that emphasize process over product. The framework recognizes that AI tools excel at pattern recognition and asset generation but cannot comprehend how design decisions impact human experiences across different contexts, cultures, abilities, and emotional states.

Through specific classroom interventions including ripple effect diagrams, Wicked Problems assignments with Causal Layered Analysis, and Specifications Grading, the paper shows how design education can develop contextual understanding and "Practical Imagination" that AI tools cannot replicate. The findings suggest that process-centric education better prepares designers to create meaningful human experiences in an AI-enabled future while maintaining ethical awareness of design's broader societal impacts.

## Key Concepts

- **Human Experience Expert**: A designer who understands complex human needs, contexts, and consequences rather than focusing primarily on technical production skills. This role emerges as AI handles increasingly sophisticated technical tasks.

- **Process-Centric Education**: An educational approach that values the journey of research, iteration, and critical thinking over final artifacts. Recognizes that final products (apps, prototypes, papers) are less valuable than the thinking skills required to create them.

- **Practical Imagination**: The ability to envision intended and unintended consequences caused by design creations, developed through futures studies methods and rooted in research evidence, critical thinking, and visionary dreaming.

- **Ripple Effect Diagrams**: Teaching tools based on the futures cone that help students anticipate personal, regional, and global consequences of AI use across different time horizons (now, soon, future).

- **Specifications Grading**: An alternative grading model where assignments have no partial points but can be revised and resubmitted. Uses three levels (Success, Not Done Yet, Incomplete) to emphasize iteration and continuous improvement over one-time submission.

- **Causal Layered Analysis (CLA)**: A futures studies method with four layers (Litany, Social/Structural, Worldview, Myth/Metaphor) that helps students identify surface-level phenomena and buried root causes of problems.

## Theoretical Framework

The paper proposes four interconnected theoretical foundations for developing human experience experts:

1. **Systems Thinking**: Examines how systems pertaining to human experiences involve interrelated elements influenced by human concerns. Uses frameworks like STEEP (Sociocultural, Technological, Economic, Ecological, Political/legal) and BATWOVE (Beneficiaries, Actors, Weltanschauung, Owners, Victims, Environmental constraints) from Soft Systems Methodology.

2. **Phenomenology**: Addresses how experiences are co-created in people's minds based on their perceptions. Past experiences shape how people perceive and make meaning of moments, creating subjective realities that vary across individuals and contexts.

3. **Activity Theory**: Focuses on how products, services, and systems are used to complete activities rife with human concerns. A design's format mediates interactions between person and experience, shaped by social factors, motivation, perception, and values.

4. **Futures Studies**: Develops foresight for consequences using tools like the futures cone (possible, plausible, probable, preferable futures) and Causal Layered Analysis to anticipate effects of design interventions.

## Methods

The paper employs a practitioner-research approach, documenting implementations across eight courses at Miami University from Fall 2023 to Spring 2025, including:

- Creating With Generative AI: Shaping the Future (undergraduate, 49-54 students per term)
- Communication Design Studio 1: Interaction (undergraduate, 22-23 students)
- Design Systems (undergraduate, 19 students)
- Design Research Methods (undergraduate and graduate levels)
- Experience Design Studio: Meaning-Making and Motivation (graduate)
- Intermediate Interaction Design (undergraduate)

Three key interventions are analyzed:
1. **Ripple Effect Diagrams**: Evolved from simple text boxes to structured diagrams guiding students through personal/regional/global and now/soon/future consequence analysis
2. **Wicked Problems + CLA Assignment**: Students identify wicked problems in domains like Communication, Energy, Health, Education, then analyze through CLA layers
3. **Specifications Grading**: Implemented across all courses to shift focus from grades to iterative learning

## Main Arguments

- As generative AI tools handle technical production tasks, design education must transition to developing human experience experts who understand when AI use is warranted and how it will impact human experiences in social, economic, ecological, and political contexts.

- AI cannot comprehend how design decisions impact human experiences across different contexts, cultures, abilities, and emotional states. It lacks the lived experience, consciousness, and empathy needed to understand the nuanced emotional journey of complex service experiences.

- Traditional education focused on production skills and final portfolio pieces is insufficient. Process-centric education develops critical capabilities that AI cannot replicate: understanding of social systems, psychology, behavior, and foresight.

- The measure of successful design education will not be technical sophistication of final portfolios, but the demonstrated ability to understand and shape human experiences.

- The future of design lies not in competing with AI's capabilities, but in developing uniquely human abilities to understand, empathize, and create experiences that resonate with the complexity of human life.

## Limitations & Critiques

- The paper draws primarily from a single institution (Miami University), which may limit generalizability across different educational contexts, cultures, or design disciplines.

- Evidence of effectiveness is largely observational and anecdotal (e.g., noting that 28 out of 43 students initially focused only on personal impacts). More rigorous assessment of learning outcomes would strengthen claims.

- The four theoretical foundations are synthesized from existing literature but the integration framework is novel and not yet validated through longitudinal studies of graduate outcomes.

- The paper acknowledges that students "may lack extensive life experience" which is crucial for developing Practical Imagination, but does not fully address how education can compensate for this limitation.

- Specifications Grading implementation details are limited; the paper acknowledges it is "outside the scope" to fully explain the technical operation of this grading model.

- The framework focuses on design education but may not fully account for rapid AI advancement that could eventually address some of the current limitations described (contextual understanding, ethical sensitivity).
