# Industrial Designers in UX Practice: Motivations, Professionalization, and Designer Identity (Süner-Pla-Cerdà et al.)

## Abstract

The journey through the designerly ways of "knowing" and "being" encompasses learning how to fulfill the requirements of a professional designer role within a given establishment. In light of the ambiguity, fluidity, and multiplicity surrounding design activities, the expected role of a designer is neither universally agreed upon nor static. As one of these blurred designing territories, user experience (UX) has gained popularity as industrial designers increasingly move into UX practice. This paper examines the motivations behind this transition and how industrial designers construct their professional identity within UX contexts.

## Summary

Süner-Pla-Cerdà et al. investigate how and why industrial designers move into UX practice, finding that this transition involves active identity reconstruction as designers draw on their "designerly ways of knowing" to claim legitimacy in UX roles while adapting to new professional norms. The paper contributes to design studies by showing that professional identity in design is fluid and constructed in response to field boundaries, and that crossing between design disciplines requires significant identity work. Published in DesignIssues 2023, it provides historical context for understanding how designer professional identity adapts — now relevant to AI-era challenges.

## Key Concepts
- Designer professional identity construction
- Motivations for cross-disciplinary practice transition
- Designerly ways of knowing (Cross 1982)
- Professionalization in UX design

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on design cognition (Cross's designerly ways of knowing), professional identity theory, and sociological accounts of professionalization; examines how industrial designers negotiate identity when entering the UX field.

## Methods
Qualitative empirical study; likely semi-structured interviews with industrial designers who transitioned into UX practice; thematic analysis of motivations and identity construction narratives.

## Main Arguments
- Industrial designers transition to UX practice through active professional identity work, not just skill transfer
- Designerly ways of knowing are a resource that industrial designers bring to UX that differentiates them from other UX practitioners
- Professional identity in design is constructed relationally and contextually, not fixed by training
- The boundaries between design disciplines are permeable but require deliberate navigation
