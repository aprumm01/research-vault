# Competences for the Modern Designer: Systematic Literature Review (Silver and Ruokamo)

## Abstract

Higher education institutions offering design education need to review their teaching content in relation to a changing society and digitalization. In an emerging post-digital era, higher education is in a state of change. Metacognitive skills and a focus on digitalized world competencies are becoming integral components of curricula in modern design education. This systematic literature review identifies and synthesizes the competences needed for designers in contemporary and future professional contexts.

## Summary

Silver and Ruokamo synthesize the literature on what competences modern designers need, finding that metacognitive, digital, and collaborative skills are increasingly emphasized alongside traditional craft and visual design skills. The review maps how design education scholarship has responded to digitalization and changing industry demands, providing a framework for curriculum renewal. The paper is directly relevant to questions about how design programs should adapt to AI-integrated professional practice.

## Key Concepts
- Designer competences (taxonomy/framework)
- Metacognitive skills in design education
- Post-digital era design practice
- Curriculum reform in higher design education

## Theoretical Framework
Competence-based education framework applied to design; systematic literature review methodology drawing on educational psychology and design studies to synthesize empirical and theoretical claims about designer preparation.

## Methods
Systematic literature review following established protocols; database search of design education and higher education literature; thematic synthesis of competence frameworks across studies.

## Main Arguments
- The competences required of professional designers have expanded significantly with digitalization
- Metacognitive and adaptive skills are now as important as technical design skills
- Design higher education curricula lag behind industry expectations for competency development
- A synthesized competence framework is needed to guide curriculum reform
