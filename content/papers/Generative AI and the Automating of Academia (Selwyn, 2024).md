# Generative AI and the Automating of Academia

## Summary
Through a national survey of n=284 UK academics (June 2023, 7 months post-ChatGPT launch), this study examines how generative AI tools intersect with neoliberal academic labor conditions. Despite GAI offering potential relief from intensive work demands, findings reveal digital automation extends rather than alleviates dysfunctions of audit culture and academic precarity. While 51.5% of academics use GAI for work, 71% report it’s changing how they work, and 83% anticipate increased future use. The study argues GAI amplifies work intensification through automated efficiencies that create new exploitation opportunities, yet may also enable positive disruption through reclaiming scholarly craftsmanship and subverting managerial authority by pursuing interests beyond contractual remit.

## Key Concepts
- **Neoliberal Audit Culture**: Quantified value systems, performance metrics, and competitive accountability driving academic overwork, precarity, and productivity mania (rankings, citations, research income as markers of worth)
- **Work Intensification & Accelerated Labour**: Pre-emptive compensation for peer-review rejection, accommodation of invisible tasks, and endless marshalling by datafication/surveillance technologies
- **Academic Precarity**: Fixed-term contracts (18.7%), part-time roles (19.5%), chronic fatigue, declining health, "quiet quitting," and exodus from universities post-COVID
- **GAI as Labour Accelerator**: Tools rationalized for productive efficiency and gain, softening precarity terms but risking further discombobulation of collective identity
- **Scholarly Craftsmanship & Slow Scholarship**: Potential for GAI to catalyze re-engagement with deep intellectual work by disrupting zero-sum quantification games through praxis of refusal

## Theoretical Framework
**Neoliberalism in Higher Education** (Maisuria & Cole 2017; Shore & Wright 2000)
- New Public Management technologies penetrating universities since 1980s
- Corporate audit culture emphasizing cost-effectiveness, economic function, quantitative mesmerization
- Performance-related anxieties driving one-upmanship treadmill and personal brand management

**Slow Scholarship** (Berg & Seeber 2016; Wahab et al. 2022)
- Resistance to productivity mania and acceleration logic
- Reclaiming academic autonomy through reorganizing labor
- Praxis of refusal (Simpson 2007) against managerial opprobrium

**Academic Capital** (Bourdieu 1988, Homo Academicus)
- Status games as indivisible feature determining professional longevity
- Rigid stratification and power asymmetries between elite minority and subaltern majority
- Capital acquisition through quantitative markers (publications, citations, income, followers)

## Methods
**Anonymous Online Survey** (Qualtrics platform, June-August 2023)
- **Sample**: n=428 total respondents, n=284 academics (removed professional services staff)
- **Distribution**: Convenience sampling via professional mailing lists, social media, online platforms
- **Target**: UK university staff (not representative sample, but illuminating general patterns/trends)
- **Timing**: 7 months post-ChatGPT launch (November 2022)
- **Analysis**: Descriptive statistics for trends + thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke 2006) of open-ended responses using Dovetail

**Participant Demographics**:
- Location: England (81.9%), Wales (8.8%), Scotland (6.4%), N. Ireland (2.9%)
- Institution: Pre-1992 universities (61.7%), Post-1992 (28.4%)
- Positions: Senior Lecturer/Reader (31.5%), Lecturer (27.0%), Professor (15.4%)
- Employment: Full-time (80.5%), Part-time (19.5%); Open-ended (77.2%), Fixed-term (18.7%)
- Top disciplines: Education (11.9%), Social Studies (10.9%), Business (8.09%)

## Main Arguments
1. **GAI Extends Rather Than Alleviates Academic Malaise**: Digitalization through GAI tools deepens neoliberal dysfunctions instead of offsetting intensive demands—automated efficiencies create new exploitation opportunities rather than work-life equilibrium

2. **Contradictory GAI Adoption Pattern**: Despite only 51.5% currently using GAI, 71% report it’s changing their work and 83% anticipate increased use, revealing rapid normalization regardless of direct adoption

3. **Dual Potential of GAI Tools**: Academics use GAI both opportunistically (accommodating managerial performance demands) and subversively (pursuing professional interests outside contractual remit, reclaiming autonomy)

4. **COVID-19 Amplification**: Pandemic emergency intensified unsustainable work burden through digital transition, yet "snap-back" to physical presence rescinded accommodations, creating jarring subjective experience with ongoing trauma layered on pre-existing neoliberal harms

5. **Collective Identity Crisis**: Academics’ application of digital tools for productive efficiency risks further discombobulation of collective identity/purpose and waning of critical agency trapped as homo economicus individuals neglecting collective intelligence

6. **Potential for Positive Disruption**: GAI may catalyze (re)engagement with scholarly craftsmanship by disrupting industrialization of academic labor if harnessed deliberately rather than as mere productivity accelerator

## Limitations & Critiques
**Research Limitations Acknowledged**:
- Convenience sampling not designed for representative sample, only illuminating general patterns
- Snapshot of trends 7 months post-ChatGPT, no longitudinal tracking of evolution
- Removed professional services staff testimony (n=144) deserving separate analysis
- No significant statistical variation across disciplinary groups limits comparative analysis
- Focus only on UK context may not generalize to other higher education systems

**Contextual Challenges Identified**:
- Academic authorship and scientific integrity profoundly unsettled by LLMs (Thorp 2023)
- Student (mis)use receiving attention while academic use "largely scant" in literature
- Transformational potential simultaneously asserted widely yet declaimed as hype (Jandrić 2023)
- Trust in university leadership at all-time low with normalized industrial action
- Compassion fatigue and institutional economic priorities driving return to pre-pandemic inequities
- Accommodations helping manage workload being rescinded despite personnel losses (deaths, quits, eliminations)

**Critical Concerns Raised**:
- Risk of academics becoming further trapped in productivity metrics despite GAI tools
- Potential for GAI to accelerate flooding of academic output and superfluity of prudence
- Threat to resilience in milieu of pronounced work intensification
- Further dilution of academia as intellectual project through production line mentality
- Zero-hours contracts (1.9%) and precarity continuing despite technological "solutions"
