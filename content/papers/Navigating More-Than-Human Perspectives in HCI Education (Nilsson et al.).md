## Summary
Nilsson et al. report on challenges encountered when teaching more-than-human perspectives in HCI and technology design education, drawing on experience with nearly 800 students across multiple institutions. They describe teaching activities developed to help students engage with non-anthropocentric design thinking—considering animals, ecosystems, and posthuman entities as stakeholders—and reflect on the persistent difficulties of shifting students' default human-centered assumptions.

## Key Concepts
- More-than-human perspectives
- HCI education
- Posthumanism
- Non-anthropocentric design
- Design pedagogy

## Theoretical Framework
Posthumanist theory and more-than-human design theory frame the pedagogical challenge of moving design students beyond human-centric frameworks to consider the needs of non-human beings and systems.

## Methods
Reflective pedagogical study; analysis of teaching activities piloted with approximately 800 students across multiple HCI and design courses at Malmö University and partner institutions.

## Main Arguments
- Teaching more-than-human perspectives requires overcoming deep-seated anthropocentric defaults in design students
- Hands-on activities and design challenges are more effective than lectures for shifting perspective
- Students struggle with the abstraction of designing for non-human entities without concrete methods
- HCI education must actively cultivate posthuman sensibilities to prepare designers for complex ecological and AI challenges
