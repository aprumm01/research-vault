# The Intertwined Histories of Artificial Intelligence

## Summary
In this paper, I argue that the fields of artificial intelligence (AI) and education have been deeply intertwined since the early days of AI Specifically, I show that many of the early pioneers of AI were cognitive scientists who also made pioneering and impactful contributions to the field of education These researchers saw AI as a tool for thinking about human learning and used their understanding of how people learn to further AI.

## Key Concepts
- Co-evolution of AI and education fields
- Cognitive science as bridge between AI and learning
- Information-processing psychology (Newell & Simon approach)
- Constructivism (Piaget/Papert approach)
- Expert performance vs. developmental learning focus
- Intelligent tutoring systems (ITS)
- Microworlds and learning environments
- Society of Mind theory

## Theoretical Framework
Historical analysis tracing two distinct approaches to cognition and learning: (1) information-processing psychology emphasizing expert performance and problem-solving, and (2) constructivism emphasizing developmental learning and children's thinking. Demonstrates bidirectional influence between AI and education research.

## Methods
Historical analysis using archival documents, published works, and intellectual biographies of key figures (Herbert Simon, Allen Newell, Marvin Minsky, Seymour Papert) to trace interconnections between AI and education from 1950s-1990s.

## Main Arguments
- AI and education have been deeply intertwined since AI's inception, not separate fields
- Early AI pioneers (Simon, Newell, Minsky, Papert) were cognitive scientists who made major contributions to both AI and education
- These researchers viewed AI as tool for understanding human learning and vice versa
- Two contrasting approaches emerged: Newell/Simon focused on expert performance and problem-solving; Minsky/Papert focused on children's development and constructivist learning
- Newell and Simon believed studying performance must precede studying learning; Minsky argued developmental theory essential to understanding final performance
- This unified perspective of simultaneously advancing human and machine cognition is largely lost in contemporary AI and learning sciences
- Intelligent tutoring systems and educational AI tools emerged from these early interconnections

## Limitations & Critiques
- Focuses primarily on Western (particularly US) researchers and institutions
- Limited attention to other important AI research traditions (e.g., connectionism's later resurgence)
- Historical narrative may oversimplify complex intellectual relationships and debates
- Does not fully address why the unified AI-education perspective declined
- Limited discussion of contemporary implications beyond brief conclusion
- May romanticize "golden age" of interdisciplinary collaboration without acknowledging field specialization benefits
