## Abstract

Purpose The explosive growth in the number of digital tools utilized in everyday learning activities generates data at an unprecedented scale, providing exciting challenges that cross scholarly communities. This paper aims to provide an overview of learning analytics (LA) with the aim of helping members of the information and learning sciences communities understand how educational Big Data is relevant to their research agendas and how they can contribute to this growing new field. Design/methodology/approach Highlighting shared values and issues illustrates why LA is the perfect meeting ground for information and the learning sciences, and suggests how by working together effective LA tools can be designed to innovate education. Findings Analytics-driven performance dashboards are offered as a specific example of one research area where information and learning scientists can make a significant contribution to LA research. Recent reviews of existing dashboard studies point to a dearth of evaluation with regard to either theory or outcomes. Here, the relevant expertise from researchers in both the learning sciences and information science is offered as an important opportunity to improve the design and evaluation of student-facing dashboards. Originality/value This paper outlines important ties between three scholarly communities to illustrate how their combined research expertise is crucial to advancing how we understand learning and for developing LA-based interventions that meet the values that we all share.

## Summary
This introductory chapter to a learning sciences handbook section examines the points of contact and productive tensions between the learning sciences and educational technology, two fields that share subject matter but have developed distinct disciplinary identities, methods, and values. The author argues that greater integration would benefit both fields — learning sciences needs implementation infrastructure that educational technology provides, while educational technology needs the deeper theoretical commitments of learning sciences. The chapter maps where collaboration is already productive and where disciplinary friction remains.

## Key Concepts
- Learning sciences (LS) as a field
- Educational technology (ET) as a field
- Disciplinary boundary work
- Theory-practice integration
- Design-based research as a bridge methodology

## Theoretical Framework
Interdisciplinary meta-analysis; draws on sociology of knowledge and disciplinary history to understand why two closely related fields have developed separately and what conditions enable productive integration.

## Methods
Conceptual review and comparative disciplinary analysis; identifies convergent and divergent trends in the two fields' research agendas, methodological preferences, and publication venues.

## Main Arguments
- Learning sciences emphasizes theory-building and deep understanding of learning; educational technology emphasizes practical application and scalable implementation
- Design-based research is a productive meeting ground because it requires both theoretical rigor and practical relevance
- Greater collaboration requires each field to understand the other's values and epistemological commitments
- The separation has costs: LS produces insights that don't reach practice; ET develops tools without sufficient theoretical grounding

## Limitations & Critiques
The chapter reflects a particular institutional moment in the early 2000s; the relationship between the fields has evolved since and the characterization may be outdated in some respects.
