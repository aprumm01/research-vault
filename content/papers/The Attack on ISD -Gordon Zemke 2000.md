# The Attack on ISD -Gordon Zemke 2000

## Summary
Critical analysis of Instructional Systems Design (ISD/ADDIE model) by training industry experts who helped create and promote it. Four major charges argue ISD is outdated for contemporary training challenges.

## Key Concepts
- ADDIE model (Assess, Design, Develop, Instruct, Evaluate) as dominant ISD framework
- Prefigured work vs configured work distinction
- Adult self-directed learning (Allen Tough's research: 8 projects/year, 100+ hours each)
- "Instructional technology" as wishful thinking rather than actual science
- Performance gap as deficit in well-defined jobs vs problem-solving in configured work
- WAT team (experienced designers with repertoire of solutions) vs bureaucratic process
- Master performer assumption and best practice cloning

## Theoretical Framework
[[frameworks/Instructional Systems Design]] - ADDIE/ISD model under critique

## Methods
journalistic critique, expert interviews

## Main Arguments
- ISD too slow and clumsy for speed-driven New Economy (3-month needs analysis obsolete before completion)
- ISD is not true "technology" - people learn even when ISD principles ignored
- Lock-step ISD process mistakes suggestions for prescriptions, focuses on process over outcomes
- ISD produces boring cookie-cutter programs geared to slowest learners
- Model assumes stupid learners and superior experts - arrogant and false
- Behavioral psychology roots limit addressing emotional/attitudinal/experiential elements
- ISD assumes known jobs with master performers - breaks down with configured work requiring problem-solving
- 90% of time spent managing bureaucratic approval process vs actual training design

## Limitations & Critiques
- Journalistic piece lacks empirical evidence - relies on expert opinions and anecdotes
- Critique focuses on misuse/over-bureaucratization rather than fundamental ISD principles
- Alternative approaches mentioned (coaching, open classrooms, web forums) not as well-developed
- Does not acknowledge contexts where systematic design still valuable (compliance training, safety)
- May conflate ISD as learning theory with ISD as project management system
- Limited discussion of how to maintain quality without systematic approach
- Critics mostly from performance consulting rather than diverse training contexts
