## Summary
Naik et al. at Purdue analyze reflections from 33 student teams in an HCI design course to examine how students exercise judgment when using AI tools in design work. They identify both established forms of design judgment (instrumental, appreciative, quality) and two emergent types: agency-distribution judgment (deciding how much to delegate to AI) and reliability judgment (assessing when AI outputs can be trusted). The study maps how AI tool use introduces new cognitive demands on design students.

## Theoretical Framework
Design cognition theory and emerging human-AI interaction theory frame the taxonomy of judgment types, extending existing design judgment frameworks to account for AI as a design collaborator.

## Methods
Qualitative analysis; examination of written reflections from 33 student teams; thematic coding of judgment types observed in AI-assisted design work.

## Main Arguments
- AI tool integration in design education introduces novel judgment demands beyond traditional design judgment
- Agency-distribution judgment—deciding what to delegate to AI—is a critical and underexplored design competency
- Reliability judgment—evaluating when to trust AI outputs—requires development through explicit pedagogical attention
- Design education must prepare students for the cognitive demands of AI-augmented design work
