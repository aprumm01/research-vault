# User experince design professionals perceptions of genAI

## Summary
1 Among creative professionals, Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) has sparked excitement over its capabilities and fear over unanticipated consequences How does GenAI impact User Experience Design (UXD) practice, and are fears warranted We interviewed 20 UX Designers, with diverse experience and across companies (.

## Key Concepts
- Experienced UX designers confident in originality, creativity, and empathic skills viewing GenAI as assistive tool
- Human factors of enjoyment and agency with humans as arbiters of AI alignment
- Skill degradation and creativity exhaustion risks especially for junior designers
- Copyright and ownership concerns in GenAI-generated design outputs
- AI literacy and equal access requirements for professional relevance
- Distinction between GenAI for repetitive tasks versus core creative design work
- Homogenization risks from widespread GenAI adoption in design practice
- Productivity gains balanced against job displacement concerns for junior practitioners

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on responsible AI and participatory AI frameworks, emphasizing creative professionals' knowledge as essential for AI development, combined with human-AI collaboration principles.

## Methods
Qualitative interview study with 20 UX designers across diverse experience levels and company sizes (startups to enterprises 10,000+) in Europe and United States, using thematic analysis.

## Main Arguments
- Experienced designers view GenAI as productivity tool for repetitive tasks while maintaining confidence in unique human capabilities
- GenAI serves assistive role for skilled designers but creates serious concerns for junior designer development
- Enjoyment and agency remain distinctly human contributions that GenAI cannot replicate in design practice
- Risk of "creativity exhaustion" from GenAI output speed and homogenization threatening design originality
- Junior designers may be trained as prompters rather than developing systematic design skills
- Copyright and intellectual property issues require urgent attention as GenAI becomes embedded in workflows
- AI literacy education essential for designers to remain relevant and critically evaluate GenAI capabilities
- Unequal access to advanced GenAI tools may create new professional hierarchies within UX field

## Limitations & Critiques
- Sample primarily from Europe and US limiting global perspectives on GenAI impact
- Interview methodology captures perceptions at single point in time during rapid GenAI evolution
- Self-selected participants may have pre-existing interest or concern about GenAI
- Does not include perspectives of design educators shaping next generation of practitioners
- Limited examination of actual GenAI tool usage versus hypothetical integration scenarios
- Does not address how different design specializations (interaction, visual, research) experience GenAI differently
- Minimal discussion of collective action or professional organization responses to GenAI threats
- Focus on individual designer agency may underemphasize structural and organizational factors
