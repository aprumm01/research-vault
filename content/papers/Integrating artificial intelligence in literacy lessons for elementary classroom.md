# Integrating artificial intelligence in literacy lessons for elementary classrooms a co-design approach

## Summary
Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools are becoming increasingly popular in education, providing teachers with new opportunities to enhance student learning experience and build upon existing teaching practices This study employs a co-design approach to develop AI-integrated learning materials and explore their implementation in elementary classrooms In collaboration with researchers, twenty-five in-service teachers co-designed engaging, ageappropriate lesson plans aligned with the national elementary curriculum and adaptable to diverse classroom needs.

## Key Concepts
- **Co-design approach** for developing AI-integrated learning materials for elementary literacy
- 25 in-service teachers collaborating with researchers to design lesson plans
- AI tools providing new opportunities to enhance student learning and build on teaching practices
- Engaging, age-appropriate lessons aligned with national elementary curriculum
- Adaptable materials for diverse classroom needs
- Teacher-researcher collaboration model for educational AI integration

## Theoretical Framework
- **Co-design methodology**: Participatory approach involving teachers as design partners
- Educational technology integration frameworks
- Literacy instruction theory and pedagogy
- Human-centered AI design in educational contexts

survey

## Main Arguments
- Co-design approach effectively engages teachers in developing AI-integrated literacy materials
- AI tools offer opportunities to enhance learning while building on existing teaching practices
- Teacher involvement essential for creating age-appropriate, curriculum-aligned AI lessons
- 25 in-service teachers successfully co-designed engaging lesson plans with researchers
- Materials designed for adaptability across diverse classroom contexts and needs
- Collaborative development ensures educational AI integration respects pedagogical expertise
- Elementary literacy instruction can benefit from thoughtful AI integration

## Limitations & Critiques
- Study focuses on development phase; implementation outcomes need examination
- Sample of 25 teachers may not represent full diversity of elementary educators
- National curriculum alignment may limit transferability to other contexts
- Long-term effectiveness of co-designed materials requires evaluation
- Need to assess actual classroom implementation and student outcomes
- Balance between teacher autonomy and structured AI integration materials
