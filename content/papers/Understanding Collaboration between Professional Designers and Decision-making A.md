# Understanding Collaboration between Professional Designers and Decision-making AI A Case Study in the Workplace

## Summary
Published: 16 October 2025 Latest updates: hps://dl.

## Key Concepts
- Decision-making AI vs. generative AI in creative workflows
- Convergent thinking (evaluation/selection) vs. divergent thinking (exploration) in design
- Appropriate trust and reliance on AI systems
- AdScoreAI - performance prediction model for advertising design effectiveness
- Algorithm-in-the-loop systems (AI recommends, humans decide)
- Research-practice gap in human-AI decision-making studies

## Theoretical Framework
Appropriate trust and reliance theory, creativity support tools (CSTs) literature, human-AI co-creation, situated work practices (CSCW)

## Methods
Case study with 12 professional graphic designers and 3 managers at online advertising design company; semi-structured interviews exploring long-term collaboration with decision-making AI over 4+ years

## Main Arguments
- Decision-making AI supports convergent thinking (evaluation/selection) while generative AI supports divergent thinking (ideation) in creative workflows
- Most human-AI decision-making research uses novice participants in lab settings, creating research-practice gap with professional contexts
- Effective human-AI collaboration requires appropriate trust (accurate recognition of AI capabilities) and appropriate reliance (knowing when to follow/ignore AI advice)
- Decision-making AI in creative work introduces tensions between objective data-driven evaluation and subjective designer judgment
- Professional designers must adapt workflows and develop strategies for incorporating AI evaluation into iterative design processes
- Three key factors influence trust/reliance: system factors (transparency, XAI), human factors (expertise, self-confidence), interaction factors (cognitive load, risk perception)
- Designers experience both benefits (efficiency, objective quality assurance) and challenges (workflow changes, conflicts with professional norms)

## Limitations & Critiques
- Single company case study limits generalizability across creative industries
- Online advertising design has unique quantifiable performance metrics (CTR, CVR) not available in other creative fields
- Study focused on mandatory AI use context, may differ from voluntary adoption scenarios
- Long-term perspective valuable but limits ability to capture initial adaptation phase
