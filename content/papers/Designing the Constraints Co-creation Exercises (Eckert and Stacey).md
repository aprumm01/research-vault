## Summary
Eckert and Stacey examine how design teams gather contextual constraints by framing co-creation exercises as analogous to constraint-gathering phases in artistic design. Through analysis of a user experience co-creation study with Chinese consumers conducted by a European car company, they show how narrative co-creation produces rich value data that can inform design constraint spaces. The paper bridges participatory design methods with constraint-based design theory.

## Key Concepts
- Design constraints
- Co-creation exercises
- Design context framing
- User values elicitation
- Cross-cultural design research

## Theoretical Framework
The authors draw on constraint-based models of design from fashion/textile research and adapt them to UX/industrial design contexts, treating consumer co-creation as a form of constraint discovery.

## Methods
Case study analysis of a co-creation exercise conducted with Chinese consumers; comparative analysis with constraint-gathering methods in artistic design practice.

## Main Arguments
- Co-creation exercises generate narrative accounts of consumer values that can be translated into design constraints
- Framing constraint-gathering as creative participation yields richer, more actionable design data than surveys
- Cultural context (e.g., Chinese consumers) requires adapted facilitation strategies
- Design constraints emerging from co-creation are often implicit and require interpretation
