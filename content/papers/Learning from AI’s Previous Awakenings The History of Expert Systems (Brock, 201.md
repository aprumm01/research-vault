# Learning from AI’s Previous Awakenings: The History of Expert Systems (Brock, 2018)

**Year:** 2018
**Venue:** IEEE Annals of the History of Computing

## Summary
David Brock's 2018 IEEE Annals article examines the history of expert systems as a case study in AI's cyclical pattern of hype and disappointment. The paper argues that understanding the rise and fall of expert systems in the 1980s-90s offers critical lessons for navigating contemporary AI enthusiasms, particularly around the gap between laboratory promise and real-world deployment.

## Key Concepts
- Expert systems hype cycle
- Knowledge engineering bottleneck
- AI winter and revival
- Technology commercialization
- Historical epistemology of AI

## Theoretical Framework
Brock uses a historical-analytical lens grounded in the sociology of technology and Science and Technology Studies (STS), tracing how expert systems were constructed as revolutionary, then reassessed as limited, through institutional and commercial dynamics.

## Methods
Historical analysis drawing on archival sources, industry reports, technical literature, and retrospective accounts from AI researchers and practitioners active during the expert systems era.

## Main Arguments
- Expert systems were oversold as general-purpose AI but succeeded only in narrow, well-defined domains
- The knowledge acquisition bottleneck — the difficulty of encoding human expertise — was the central failure point
- Commercial pressures accelerated unrealistic claims, contributing to the eventual AI winter
- Contemporary AI developers risk repeating these dynamics without historical perspective

## Limitations & Critiques
The paper focuses on the U.S./Western expert systems industry and may underweight parallel developments elsewhere; its prescriptive lessons for current AI are suggestive rather than empirically grounded.
