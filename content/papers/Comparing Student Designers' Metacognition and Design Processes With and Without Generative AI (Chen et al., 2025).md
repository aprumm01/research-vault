# Comparing Student Designers' Metacognition and Design Processes With and Without Generative AI (Chen et al., 2025)


## Summary
Chen, Chen, Lin, and Lin investigate the role of Generative AI in student design processes and metacognitive operations, publishing in the International Journal of Technology and Design Education (2025). Using Retrospective Think-Aloud protocols and semi-structured interviews, they compared five students under a normal design condition (without GenAI) with four students under a GenAI-assisted condition. Results show GenAI use was concentrated in the early, divergent phases of design; GenAI-assisted students showed frequent phase transitions early on and stabilization in later phases, consistently concluding in the Design phase. Importantly, GenAI played a dual role in metacognition -- propelling design actions forward while also creating opportunities for reflection.

## Key Concepts
- GenAI as concentrated in early, divergent phases of the design process
- Retrospective Think-Aloud (RTA) protocol as a metacognitive research method
- Dual role of GenAI in metacognition: propulsion and reflection
- Phase transition patterns: frequent transitions early, stabilization late
- Problem-solution space iterations facilitated by GenAI
- GenAI as a catalyst for metacognitive quality and frequency

## Theoretical Framework
Qualitative design education research; draws on design process research (problem-solution space models, phase transition literature) and metacognition theory; Retrospective Think-Aloud methodology adapted from verbal protocol analysis; published in International Journal of Technology and Design Education (Springer, 2025); situated within broader research on AI-assisted studio design pedagogy.

## Methods
Qualitative study with two conditions: Normal (n=5, without GenAI) and GenAI-assisted (n=4); Retrospective Think-Aloud protocols administered after design sessions; semi-structured interviews conducted afterward; design process phases coded and transitions tracked; preliminary study framing acknowledges small sample size; Taiwan-based participants.

## Main Arguments
- GenAI use by student designers was predominantly concentrated in early phases (divergent orientation), paralleling human designers' tendencies to use tools for ideation and exploration
- Under GenAI-assisted conditions, students showed frequent phase transitions in early stages followed by stabilization; all consistently concluded in the Design (execution) phase
- GenAI facilitated an additional pathway for iteration between problem and solution spaces, enabling different design trajectories than the normal condition
- GenAI plays a dual role in metacognition: it propels design actions (enabling faster progression) and creates opportunities for reflection (pausing to evaluate GenAI output)
- GenAI functions as a metacognitive catalyst, enhancing both the quality and frequency of meaningful metacognitive operations

## Limitations & Critiques
The sample is very small (n=9 across two conditions), limiting generalizability and statistical conclusions; the paper is explicitly framed as preliminary. The RTA protocol may introduce retrospective distortion. The study is conducted in a single Taiwanese educational context with undergraduate industrial design students, which may not generalize to other disciplines, cultures, or more advanced learners. The interaction effects between individual designer skill and GenAI use are not disentangled.
