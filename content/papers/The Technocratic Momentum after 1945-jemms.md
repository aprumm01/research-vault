# The Technocratic Momentum after 1945-jemms

## Summary
in the context of the technocratic ideology of the Cold War These ideologies did not simply vanish after 1989 The catchwords were “programmed instruction” and “teaching machines”, accompanied by the promise that all students would make efficient learning progress.

## Key Concepts
- Educationalization of the Cold War (education as national defense issue)
- Technocratic ideology in education policy
- Behaviorist learning theory (B.F. Skinner's operant conditioning)
- Teaching machines and programmed instruction
- Expert-driven democracy replacing local school board control
- Human capital theory as education rationale

## Theoretical Framework
Behaviorism (Skinner), operant conditioning, cybernetics, technocratic governance theory

## Methods
Historical analysis examining education policy documents, conference proceedings, and published literature from 1945-1960s

## Main Arguments
- The Cold War led to fundamental shift in education governance from local democratic control to centralized expert-driven systems
- Both East and West converged on technocratic teaching ideologies despite opposing political systems
- Sputnik (1957) triggered educationalization in the US, making education a national defense issue for the first time
- Behaviorist psychology (Skinner) offered "scientific" solutions through teaching machines and programmed instruction promising efficiency and individualization
- Progressive education (Dewey) was blamed for weakening national competitiveness
- Transnational organizations (OECD, UNESCO) spread technocratic education ideology globally
- The apparent failure of teaching machines did not lead to modification of underlying ideology, only to modification of devices

## Limitations & Critiques
- Focus primarily on Western developments with limited analysis of Eastern bloc
- Historical scope ends before full assessment of long-term impacts
- Limited examination of teacher and student perspectives on these reforms
