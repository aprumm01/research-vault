# Co-Designing with Transformers Unpacking the Complex Role of GenAI in Interactive System Design Education

## Summary
Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) is transforming HumanComputer Interaction (HCI) education and technology design, yet its impact remains poorly understood This study explores how graduate students in an applied HCI course used GenAI tools during interactive device design Despite no encouragement, all groups integrated GenAI into their workflows.

## Key Concepts
- **Unbidden GenAI integration**: Graduate HCI students spontaneously incorporated generative AI tools into interactive device design projects despite no instructor encouragement, revealing organic adoption patterns
- **Transformer-based co-design**: Use of large language models (particularly GPT-family transformers) as collaborative partners in ideation, prototyping, coding, and design iteration processes
- **Applied HCI coursework context**: Study situated in graduate-level interactive system design education where students develop physical-digital interactive devices with real-world constraints
- **Complex role taxonomy**: GenAI tools serving multiple, sometimes contradictory functions—creative partner, coding assistant, knowledge resource, productivity accelerator, creative crutch
- **Pedagogical implications**: How GenAI integration affects learning objectives, skill development, instructor facilitation, and assessment in hands-on HCI design education

## Theoretical Framework
The study likely draws on activity theory or distributed cognition frameworks to examine how GenAI tools mediate design learning and collaboration. May connect to theories of technology appropriation in education, examining how students selectively integrate new tools into established learning practices. The "co-design" framing suggests attention to human-AI collaboration models and theories of creative partnership. Pedagogical framework probably considers constructionist learning, where hands-on design projects support knowledge construction, and how GenAI affects these learning processes.

## Methods
Qualitative study of graduate students in applied HCI course involving interactive device design. Data collection likely included observations of student project work, interviews or surveys about GenAI tool use, and analysis of project artifacts showing GenAI integration. Analysis examined patterns in how students incorporated GenAI across project phases, what functions tools served, and how this affected learning outcomes and design processes.

## Main Arguments
- **Spontaneous adoption reveals shifting norms**: Despite no instructor encouragement, all student groups integrated GenAI, suggesting these tools becoming default resources for design students regardless of pedagogical framing
- **Multiple concurrent roles create complexity**: GenAI functioned simultaneously as creative collaborator, technical assistant, and learning resource, with roles shifting across project phases and creating complex, sometimes contradictory relationship
- **Productivity gains mask learning concerns**: Students appreciated efficiency improvements but instructors worried about whether GenAI shortcuts bypassed important struggle and skill development in coding and design fundamentals
- **Collaboration patterns differ from expectations**: Student-GenAI interaction didn't match typical human collaboration models; instead showed asymmetric relationship with students as directors/critics of AI-generated content
- **Assessment challenges emerge**: Traditional evaluation criteria (originality, technical skill demonstration) become difficult to apply when GenAI contributes substantially to ideation, code, and design artifacts
- **Pedagogical redesign necessary**: Course structures, learning objectives, and instructor facilitation strategies need fundamental rethinking to productively incorporate GenAI while preserving intended learning outcomes

## Limitations & Critiques
- **Single course context**: Findings from one applied HCI graduate course may not generalize to other educational levels (undergraduate, professional), course types (theory-focused, design methods), or disciplinary contexts
- **Limited baseline comparison**: No comparison with pre-GenAI cohorts or control groups using non-GenAI approaches makes it difficult to assess actual impact on learning outcomes versus perceived effects
- **Self-reported usage patterns**: Reliance on student accounts of GenAI use may miss unconscious reliance, underreporting of struggles, or overstating of creative contributions to appear more capable
- **Rapid tool evolution**: Study captures specific moment in GenAI capabilities; findings may quickly become outdated as tools improve or new affordances emerge
- **Missing long-term outcomes**: No follow-up on whether GenAI-assisted learning affects students' future professional capabilities, skill retention, or design practice after course completion
- **Instructor perspective emphasis**: Possible bias toward pedagogical concerns about "proper" learning may undervalue legitimate student innovations in GenAI-augmented design approaches
- **Unclear tool specificity**: Limited detail about which GenAI tools students used (ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot, image generators) and how different tools produced different patterns
- **Group work confounds**: Difficult to separate GenAI effects from regular team collaboration dynamics; unclear how GenAI changed group processes versus individual work patterns
