# Closing the Sociotechnical Gap in AI: Measuring Practitioners' Attitudes and Perceptions

## Summary
Sadek, Calvo, and Mougenot (Dyson School of Design Engineering, Imperial College London, IEEE Technology and Society Magazine, 2024) argue that a "socio-technical gap" exists in AI development: technical practitioners are trained to focus on system performance but lack frameworks for assessing how AI systems affect the humans who use and are affected by them. The authors call for systematic measurement of AI practitioners' attitudes and perceptions as a way to close this gap — arguing that practitioner mindset is a root cause of value alignment failures, not just a symptom. The paper situates this argument within broader responsible AI and value-sensitive design discourse.

## Key Concepts
- Socio-technical gap in AI development
- Practitioner attitudes and perceptions as research object
- Human-centered approach vs. technical-mindset culture
- Value alignment through practitioner culture change
- Measurement frameworks for AI practitioner attitudes

## Theoretical Framework
Responsible AI and value-sensitive design; identifies practitioner culture as a structural problem that precedes technical ethics failures.

## Methods
Position paper / perspective piece (4 pages, IEEE Technology and Society Magazine, May 2024, DOI: 10.1109/MTS.2024.3392280). Authors: Imperial College London, Dyson School of Design Engineering.

## Main Arguments
- Technical AI practitioners systematically underweight socio-technical implications because their training rewards narrow technical optimization
- Closing the socio-technical gap requires measuring and changing practitioner culture, not just adding ethics checklists
- The field lacks instruments for assessing practitioners' human-centered attitudes at scale — this is presented as a research priority
- Unhelpful practitioner cultures actively resist values-oriented practices, making structural intervention necessary

## Limitations & Critiques
Short position paper — makes the case for measurement without providing validated instruments. Does not address how to change practitioner culture once measured. Single-institution perspective from design engineering (not computer science).
