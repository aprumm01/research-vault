# Vibe Coding for UX Design-Understanding UX Professionals Perceptions of AI-Assisted Design and Development

## Summary
HC] 12 Sep 2025 Vibe Coding for UX Design: Understanding UX Professionals’ Perceptions of AI-Assisted Design and Development JIE LI, Cake Researcher, Netherlands YOUYANG HOU, Notion Labs, United States LAURA LIN, Collov AI, United States RUIHAO ZHU, Cornell University, United States HANCHENG CAO, Goizueta Business School, Emory University, United States ABDALLAH EL ALI, Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica Utrecht University, The Netherlands Generative AI is reshaping UX design practices through “vibe coding”, where UX professionals express intent in natural language and AI translates it into functional prototypes and code Despite rapid adoption, little research has examined how vibe coding reconfigures UX workflows and collaboration Drawing on interviews with 20 UX professionals across enterprises, startups, and academia, we show how vibe coding follows a four-stage workflow of ideation, AI generation, debugging, and review.

## Key Concepts
- **Vibe coding**: Intent-driven workflow where natural language prompts are translated into functional UX prototypes and code through generative AI
- **Four-stage iterative workflow**: (1) Context setup & ideation, (2) AI generation, (3) Manual debugging, (4) Testing & review
- **Intending the right design vs designing the right intention**: Tension between speed and reflective practice
- **Best practices**: Clear/precise communication, rich situational context, iterative refinement with human-led verification
- **Differences from Figma**: Vibe coding faster for functional prototypes but Figma remains superior for visual refinement
- **UX professionals**: Includes UX/UI designers, software engineers, product managers, UX researchers, and hybrid designer-developer roles

## Theoretical Framework
Builds on human-AI collaboration theory, responsible AI frameworks, and workflow transformation research. Frames vibe coding as shift from deterministic to probabilistic intent mediation where authorship moves from code execution to intention-setting. Draws on concepts of epistemic labor redistribution between humans and machines.

## Methods
- Semi-structured interviews with 20 UX professionals
- Participants from enterprises, startups, and academia
- Geographic distribution: North America (n=12), Europe (n=6), Asia (n=2)
- Experience levels: >5 years (n=9), 3-5 years (n=6), <3 years (n=5)
- Organization sizes: Large enterprises >10k (n=8), large orgs 1k-10k (n=4), medium 500-1k (n=2), small startups <50 (n=3), academic (n=3)
- Most common tools: Cursor (n=16), Replit (n=11), Lovable (n=9), Bolt (n=8), V0 (n=8)
- 60-minute interviews conducted May-June 2025
- Hybrid deductive/inductive thematic analysis using Miro, resulting in 565 statement cards and 6 themes

## Main Arguments
- **Four-stage workflow is highly iterative**: Despite sequential structure, process involves frequent back-and-forth between stages
- **Six key themes**: (1) Workflow stages, (2) Benefits (productivity, cognitive offloading, learning, creativity), (3) Challenges (unreliability, fine-tuning difficulties, pseudo-productivity, over-reliance), (4) Trust and accountability shifts, (5) Collaboration reconfigurations, (6) Future developments
- **Dual tension**: Efficiency gains vs erosion of critical thinking; acceleration vs reflection
- **Role blurring**: Traditional boundaries between UX designers, engineers, and product managers dissolving
- **Asymmetries in outcomes**: Creates gap between "AI-literate" (can debug/verify) and "AI-dependent" practitioners
- **Ownership redefinition**: Authorship tied to intention-setting rather than execution, raising disclosure questions
- **Educational potential conditional**: Learning opportunity only for those already skilled in coding
- **Best practices critical**: Success depends on precise communication, rich context, and iterative refinement with human verification

## Limitations & Critiques
- Sample may over-represent early adopters with positive views
- Interview-based perceptions rather than observed behaviors
- Cross-sectional snapshot during rapid tool evolution
- Thematic saturation achieved but sample size modest (n=20)
- Geographic concentration in North America and Europe
- May not capture experiences of those who abandoned vibe coding tools
- Limited longitudinal data on practice evolution
- GDPR compliance required careful data handling
