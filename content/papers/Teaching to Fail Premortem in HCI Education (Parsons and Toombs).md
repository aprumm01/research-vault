# Teaching to Fail: Premortem in HCI Education (Parsons and Toombs)

## Summary
Parsons and Toombs present the premortem as a pedagogical technique for HCI and design education — a structured anticipatory failure analysis in which students imagine a future in which their design project has failed and work backwards to identify what caused it. The technique addresses a gap in design education: while iteration and learning-from-failure are valorized in design discourse, curricula rarely provide structured opportunities to develop failure imagination before projects are implemented. Classroom implementations show that premortems build systems thinking, ethical foresight, and design resilience.

## Key Concepts
- Premortem methodology (Klein 2007)
- Failure imagination in design education
- Anticipatory failure analysis
- Systems thinking and ethical foresight
- HCI pedagogy and design resilience

## Theoretical Framework
Adapts Klein's premortem technique from organizational decision-making to design education contexts; draws on design cognition research on problem framing and on critical design/speculative design traditions that cultivate anticipatory thinking.

## Methods
Classroom implementation study; qualitative analysis of student premortem artifacts and reflections across multiple design studio courses; iterative refinement of the technique based on pedagogical experience.

## Main Arguments
- Designers rarely develop systematic failure imagination — they focus on successful use scenarios, leaving them vulnerable to foreseeable but unanticipated failure modes
- Premortems democratize systems thinking: by imaginatively inhabiting a failed future, students surface assumptions about context, users, and system behavior that would otherwise remain implicit
- Ethical foresight is developed by premortems in a way that abstract ethics modules do not achieve — imagining harm concretely is more motivationally and cognitively powerful than reasoning about harm abstractly
- Regular premortem practice builds design resilience: the capacity to maintain forward momentum while honestly accounting for uncertainty and risk

## Limitations
- Effectiveness depends heavily on facilitation quality and student willingness to engage seriously with imagined failure
- Premortem outputs can become superficial if students approach them as a compliance exercise rather than genuine anticipatory work
