## Summary
Parsons and Toombs introduce the premortem technique—imagining a project has already failed and working backward to identify likely causes—as a pedagogical strategy in HCI education. Adapted from decision research and organizational planning, the premortem invites students to engage in prospective failure analysis during ongoing projects, surfacing hidden risks, challenging assumptions, and counteracting groupthink. The paper argues the technique develops critical design judgment and realistic project assessment.

## Key Concepts

## Theoretical Framework
Decision research (Klein's naturalistic decision-making), design critique theory, and reflective practice (Schön) inform the adaptation of premortem exercises to design education contexts.

## Methods
Pedagogical design case; description of premortem exercise implementation in HCI design courses at Purdue University and Indiana University; anecdotal observations of student learning.

## Main Arguments
- The premortem flips "will this work?" into "what went wrong?"—activating more honest critical assessment
- Prospective failure analysis surfaces risks that students typically suppress due to optimism bias and groupthink
- The premortem is well-suited to mid-project reflection, prompting course corrections before failure is irreversible
- Design educators can use premortems to develop students' professional judgment about risk, uncertainty, and design decisions
