# Cognitive Co-Evolution Model for Design (Cash et al.)

## Summary
Cash, Stanković, and Štorga propose the Cognitive Co-Evolution Model of design — a framework that characterizes design as a simultaneous, iterative evolution of the designer's understanding of the problem space and solution space. Drawing on empirical studies of design cognition, the model extends Dorst and Cross's (2001) co-evolution account by specifying how cognitive representations at multiple levels (goal, concept, detail) co-evolve through external working and reflection, and how different design strategies produce different co-evolution trajectories with different quality outcomes.

## Key Concepts
- Co-evolution of problem and solution spaces
- Cognitive representation levels in design
- External working (sketching, prototyping as thinking)
- Design fixation and exploration strategies
- Design quality and process relationships

## Theoretical Framework
Builds on Dorst and Cross's co-evolution thesis; integrates cognitive psychology of representation change, dual-process theory, and design protocol analysis; frames design cognition as active construction and transformation of mental models rather than search through pre-formed problem spaces.

## Methods
Empirical design protocol studies; coding and analysis of design session transcripts and artifacts; comparative analysis across designers with different expertise levels and strategy preferences.

## Main Arguments
- Problem and solution co-evolve — neither is fixed while the other is explored; understanding this co-evolution is essential for explaining how design quality develops
- External representations (sketches, models) are cognitive tools that enable and structure co-evolution, not merely documentation of internally completed thinking
- Different co-evolution trajectories (breadth-first exploration vs. depth-first commitment) produce systematically different design outcomes — better solutions emerge from richer early exploration
- Design fixation (premature commitment to an early solution) can be understood as a co-evolution pathology: one space evolves while the other stagnates

## Limitations
- Protocol studies capture verbalization and drawing but may miss internal cognitive processes that aren't externalized
- Model is descriptive/analytical; translating it into prescriptive design education interventions requires additional work

## Connections
Links to [[Designerly Ways of Knowing (Cross 1982)]] on design cognition; relates to [[Anatomy of Prototypes (Lim Stolterman Tenenberg)]] on external representations; connects to [[Metacognitive Support Agents for Human-AI Co-Creation (Gmeiner et al. CMU)]] on supporting design cognition with AI.
