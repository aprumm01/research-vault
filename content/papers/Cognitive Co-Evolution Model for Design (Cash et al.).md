## Summary
Cash et al. develop and elaborate the cognitive co-evolution model of design thinking, which explains how designers simultaneously develop both the problem and solution spaces throughout the design process. The model accounts for the dynamic, iterative nature of design cognition — designers do not first fully specify a problem and then solve it, but rather problem and solution co-evolve through cycles of reflection, exploration, and refinement. The paper provides empirical and theoretical elaboration of this foundational design cognition model.

## Key Concepts
- Cognitive co-evolution (simultaneous problem-solution space development)
- Design cognition and thinking
- Problem space and solution space in design
- Iterative design process modeling

## Theoretical Framework
Design cognition theory; extends Dorst and Cross's (2001) co-evolution model with empirical evidence from protocol studies; draws on cognitive science and design research to explain how expert designers navigate the problem-solution interface.

## Methods
Empirical and theoretical elaboration; likely protocol study analysis; 32-page paper in design research journal examining the cognitive co-evolution model through multiple studies.

## Main Arguments
- Design problems and solutions co-evolve simultaneously — sequential problem-then-solution models are inadequate
- Expert designers actively manage the interaction between problem and solution spaces
- Co-evolution is a distinctive feature of design cognition that distinguishes design from other problem-solving
- Understanding co-evolution has implications for design education, tools, and assessment
