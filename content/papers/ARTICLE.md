# Using Generative AI Tools in Collaborative UX Design Courses


## Abstract
Artificial Intelligence and their derived Generative technologies are playing a cru - cial role in many applications that involve an active collaboration among machine assistants and human users. That is the case for User Experience courses that al - lowed students and instructors work together with Generative Artificial Intelligence tools to produce a collaborative design. The main purpose of this research consisted in reviewing several stages in design tasks that could take advantage of Artificial Intelligence tools by boosting a prompt-based conversation among instructors and students. Results obtained through the use of these tools demonstrated their huge potential in facilitating co-creative tasks. In this context, a first cohort of students was able to collaborate on the ideation of User Experience projects by exchanging prompts to define persona profiles and checking their suitability. ## Summary
This paper examines how design educators integrated generative AI tools into collaborative UX design courses, documenting the pedagogical strategies, student experiences, and learning outcomes that emerged. The study finds that AI tools can enhance collaborative ideation and rapid prototyping while creating new tensions around individual contribution, creative ownership, and skill development. The authors develop recommendations for educators on how to structure AI-assisted collaborative design projects to maximize learning without undermining the collaborative skills that UX education seeks to develop.

## Key Concepts
- Generative AI in UX design education

## Theoretical Framework
Collaborative learning theory; design pedagogy; CSCW perspectives on AI-mediated teamwork; draws on project-based learning frameworks.

## Methods
Qualitative study of multiple UX design courses integrating AI tools; student surveys, focus groups, and design artifact analysis; instructor reflective practice documentation.

## Main Arguments
- AI tools change the division of labor in collaborative design projects in ways that require new pedagogical scaffolding
- Without explicit instruction, students tend to use AI to bypass collaborative negotiation rather than to enhance it
- Effective AI-assisted collaboration requires structured activities that preserve human-to-human creative dialogue
- Assessment must evolve to evaluate the quality of AI direction and curation, not just final design outputs

## Limitations & Critiques
Study conducted during rapid AI tool evolution; findings may not transfer to different course structures or student populations; long-term effects on collaborative design skill development are not tracked.
