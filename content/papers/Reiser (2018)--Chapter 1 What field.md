## Summary
Robert Reiser's chapter examines the question of what field instructional design and technology (IDT) belongs to, tracing its historical development from audiovisual communications through programmed instruction to contemporary technology-enhanced learning. Reiser argues that IDT is a coherent field defined by its focus on systematic approaches to designing effective instruction, with a distinct history of theory and practice that justifies its status as an independent discipline.

## Key Concepts
- Instructional design and technology (IDT)
- Field identity
- History of educational technology
- Systematic instruction design
- Audiovisual communications

## Theoretical Framework
Historical analysis and field theory inform Reiser's argument about IDT's disciplinary identity, tracing the conceptual lineage from early media studies to contemporary performance improvement.

## Methods
Historical and conceptual analysis; review of key developments, figures, and defining moments in IDT's disciplinary history.

## Main Arguments
- IDT is a distinct field defined by systematic approaches to designing instruction and improving performance
- The field's history spans audiovisual communications, programmed instruction, and computer-based learning
- IDT's identity has been shaped by both theoretical frameworks and practical applications in training and education
- Understanding the field's history is essential for practitioners to situate their work and contribute to its future
