# Designer experiences and perspectives on the role of generative AI in industrial design

## Summary
This paper investigates human-AI co-creation in industrial design through five workshops and focus group discussions with 32 industrial designers and students, examining experiences and perspectives on GenAI integration into workflows. Participants engaged hands-on with AI-based image generators (Midjourney, Stable Diffusion). Thematic analysis triangulated with observation notes and AI-generated works reveals perceived professional opportunities, barriers, and risks of image generation. Findings map factors influencing GenAI adoption, framing interplay of communication, automation, and designerly autonomy. Addresses gap in attitudinal research examining industrial designers' practical/professional concerns and motivations. Scoping review of 53 papers shows existing research focuses on case-specific applications in concept development phases, with limited holistic examination of profession-wide impact.

## Key Concepts
- **Human-AI co-creation**: AI shifting from automation tool to design material and co-creative partner in creative process
- **Design with AI**: Incorporating AI into creative design process beyond product and production
- **Wicked problems**: Unique, ill-defined design problems requiring abductive sensemaking, not right/wrong but better/worse solutions (Rittel & Weber)
- **Abductive reasoning**: Design thinking forming relationships across unrelated information pieces (Kolko)
- **GenAI in design**: Supporting discovery, visualization, creation, testing; designers train and regulate AI (Shi et al.)
- **Design-specific challenges**: Feasibility constraints (form, function, materials, manufacturability, usability, ergonomics) vs. art/design freedom
- **Front-end phases**: Concept development tasks (inspiration, ideation, form development, styling) where most GenAI research concentrates

## Theoretical Framework
Builds on **design thinking literature evolution**: from rational/objective process → scientizing design with computational methods → rejecting scientific notion, establishing design as unique discipline (Cross, Archer). Grounded in **wicked problems theory** (Rittel & Weber) and **abductive reasoning** (Kolko) distinguishing design from mathematical/scientific models. References **computational design history** and artificial design intelligence research (Cross, Gero). Draws on **human-AI co-creation frameworks** (Hwang, Grabe et al.) and **human-centered AI** principles (Shneiderman, Ozmen Garibay et al.). Positions within **design cognition research** examining natural vs. artificial design intelligence.

## Methods
**Scoping review**: Web of Science and Scopus databases (May 2025), 149 records → 53 papers (21 journal articles, 32 conference/book chapters) after exclusions. Content analysis coded applied research (n=37) for design phase/task and product/sector; attitudinal (n=6), theoretical/review (n=8), mixed (n=2). **Five workshops and focus group discussions** with 32 industrial designers and design students. **Thematic analysis** of discussion data **triangulated** with observation notes and AI-generated works collected during workshops. Hands-on experiences with selected AI-based image generators (Midjourney, Stable Diffusion). Mixed-methods providing comprehensive view of tool performance, pragmatic uses, impacts on design performance/creativity, anticipated professional effects.

## Main Arguments
- **GenAI disrupting design professions** but case-specific applied studies dominate; holistic examination of professional impact lacking
- AI co-creative role significantly enhanced by recent GenAI advancements, warranting investigation of designer adaptation
- Industrial design involves unique feasibility concerns (human factors, use flow, context, manufacturability) unlike art/design fields with fewer constraints
- **Trade-offs require investigation**: Predictability vs. inconsistency, inspiration vs. limited versatility, efficiency gains vs. biased/inaccurate results
- GenAI models lack understanding of human factors, produce low-feasibility content, inaccurately convey functionality
- Product designers face challenges converging inspiring AI content into user-friendly, functioning physical products
- Domain-specific challenges necessitate examining industrial designers' attitudes beyond general creative fields
- Existing research concentrates on concept development/front-end phases; embodiment, detailing, presentation underexplored
- Factors influencing adoption involve interplay of **communication** (prompt engineering, conveying intent), **automation** (efficiency, handling laborious tasks), **autonomy** (creative control, professional identity)
- Need for designer-friendly GenAI systems addressing industrial design specifics

## Limitations & Critiques
Study acknowledges scoping review limitations: 53 papers may not capture all relevant work; no quality assessment applied given novelty and conference prevalence. Workshop sample (32 participants) limited in size and potentially geographic/cultural scope (context not specified). Hands-on experience limited to image generators (Midjourney, Stable Diffusion); doesn't examine 3D modeling AI or other GenAI forms relevant to industrial design. Focuses on designers' perceptions and anticipated effects rather than longitudinal observation of actual practice changes. Doesn't empirically test whether perceived barriers/opportunities manifest in real-world adoption patterns. Limited discussion of how experience level (students vs. professionals) affects perspectives. Scoping review reveals gap in embodiment, detailing, presentation phases but current study also focuses on concept development through image generation. Doesn't address how organizational contexts (freelance vs. corporate) shape adoption.
