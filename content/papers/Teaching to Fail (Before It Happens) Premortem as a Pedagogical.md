# Teaching to Fail (Before It Happens): Premortem as a Pedagogical

## Summary
This paper introduces the premortem — originally a project risk management technique — as a pedagogical tool in design education. By asking students to imagine a future design failure and then work backward to identify causes, the premortem develops students' capacity for critical reflection, failure analysis, and ethical foresight before committing to a design direction. The author presents case studies of premortem use in design studio courses, arguing that anticipatory failure analysis produces more robust and ethically grounded design outcomes than traditional critique-only approaches.

## Key Concepts
- Premortem as pedagogical technique
- Prospective retrospection (imagining future failure)
- Design failure analysis and foresight
- Risk literacy in design education
- Studio critique augmentation

## Theoretical Framework
Reflective practice tradition (Schön); organizational learning theory; design pedagogy; applies premortem from behavioral economics (Kahneman) and project management to educational contexts.

## Methods
Design pedagogy case study; qualitative analysis of student work and reflections from multiple design studio courses using premortem exercises; instructor reflective practice documentation.

## Main Arguments
- Traditional design critique is retrospective; premortems shift students toward prospective critical thinking
- Anticipating failure activates different cognitive processes than evaluating existing work, producing more honest risk identification
- Premortems build ethical design reasoning by forcing students to consider who might be harmed by their design decisions
- The technique is low-cost, transferable across studio contexts, and scales well in both individual and team projects

## Limitations & Critiques
Pedagogical effectiveness is documented qualitatively; longitudinal evidence for transfer of premortem reasoning into professional practice is not yet established; effectiveness may vary by studio culture and instructor facilitation skill.
