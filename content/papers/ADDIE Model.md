# ADDIE Model


## Summary
The ADDIE Model (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation) is the foundational instructional systems design process model used across instructional design, training, and human performance technology. Emerging from military training research in the 1970s, ADDIE provides a systematic, iterative framework: the Analysis phase identifies performance gaps and learner needs; Design translates goals into learning objectives and instructional strategies; Development produces instructional materials; Implementation delivers instruction; Evaluation measures outcomes and informs revision. In this research vault, ADDIE serves as the backbone of instructional design theory and is the primary reference framework for understanding systematic approaches to training design and their relationship to HPT and performance-based outcomes.

## Key Concepts
- Five phases: Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation
- Systematic design: each phase informs subsequent phases iteratively
- Performance gap analysis as the foundation of the Analysis phase
- Formative and summative evaluation as distinct evaluation types
- ADDIE vs. agile ID: critiques of linearity and calls for faster iteration
- Dick and Carey model as an elaborated ADDIE variant

## Theoretical Framework
Instructional Systems Design (ISD) tradition; behaviorist and cognitive psychological foundations; AECT and ISPI professional communities; Kirkpatrick's four-level evaluation model as a common companion framework; connected in this vault to HPT, instructional design theory, and performance improvement practice.
