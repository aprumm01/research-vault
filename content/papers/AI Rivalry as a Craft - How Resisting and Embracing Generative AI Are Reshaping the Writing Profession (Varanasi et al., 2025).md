# AI Rivalry as a Craft: How Resisting and Embracing Generative AI Are Reshaping the Writing Profession

## Summary
This CHI 2025 paper investigates how writing professionals are adapting their practices in response to generative AI (GAI) technologies like ChatGPT. Through 25 semi-structured interviews with experienced writers across diverse fields (journalists, screenwriters, copywriters, technical writers, etc.), the researchers identify four distinct strategies that professionals employ using the theoretical lens of Job Crafting.

The study distinguishes between "resisting" and "embracing" orientations toward GAI, and between "approach crafting" (expanding roles) and "avoidance crafting" (streamlining roles). This creates a 2x2 framework of strategies: Human-driven expansion strategies (resisting GAI while expanding human identity and skills), Human-driven localization strategies (resisting GAI while creating niche identities), GAI-driven expansion strategies (embracing GAI to enhance creative workflows), and GAI-driven delegation strategies (embracing GAI to offload tedious tasks).

A key finding is the emergence of "AI rivalry" as a phenomenon where professionals who resist GAI proactively engage in competitive behaviors against AI, carving out professional niches and emphasizing uniquely human capabilities. The paper contributes to understanding how creative workers navigate technological disruption through bottom-up, worker-led practices rather than top-down organizational mandates.

## Key Concepts
- **Job Crafting**: Theoretical framework describing how workers proactively modify their tasks, relationships, and cognitive boundaries to enhance work outcomes
- **AI Rivalry**: New phenomenon where professionals develop competitive strategies against GAI while shaping their professional identities
- **Human-driven Expansion Strategies**: Approaches emphasizing human-centric skills, identity visibility, and differentiation from GAI
- **GAI-driven Delegation Strategies**: Approaches using GAI to offload repetitive tasks while reducing dependencies on others
- **AI-Managerial Labor**: New form of work involving prompt engineering, output verification, and workflow reorganization around GAI

## Theoretical Framework
The study applies Job Crafting theory to understand how writing professionals reshape their work in response to GAI. Job Crafting focuses on bottom-up changes workers make to their roles, tasks, and relationships, attributing agency to workers rather than managers or organizations.

## Methods
Qualitative interview study with 25 writing professionals (avg. 17 years experience) who had been exposed to GAI for at least 12 months. Semi-structured interviews (65-77 minutes) explored daily work practices, GAI tool usage, changes in work relationships, and perspectives on the future. Data analyzed using inductive thematic analysis with 61 codes organized into themes using Job Crafting theory.

## Main Arguments
- Writing professionals employ four distinct strategies combining GAI orientation (resist/embrace) with crafting approach (expansion/avoidance)
- Professionals who resist GAI craft both their identities and practices, while those who embrace GAI primarily craft only their practices
- Human-driven strategies emphasize visibility of human labor, professional identity, and competitive differentiation
- GAI-driven strategies focus on workflow efficiency, creative enhancement, and reducing emotional/managerial labor
- Common productivity metrics are insufficient for assessing the value of different strategies

## Limitations & Critiques
- Sample limited to U.S.-based writing professionals
- Cross-sectional design captures practices at one point in time as GAI technologies continue evolving
- Study focused on individual worker strategies rather than organizational or industry-level responses
- Findings may not generalize to other creative professions or non-creative work contexts
