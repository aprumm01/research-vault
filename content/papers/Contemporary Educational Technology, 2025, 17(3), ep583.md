# Contemporary Educational Technology, 2025, 17(3), ep583

## Summary
Bora and Kölemen (Bingol University and Beykoz University, Turkey) conduct a case study examining AI-supported education in a digital photography course in Turkish higher education. Using a mixed-methods design, they investigate how AI integration affects student learning outcomes, creative development, and perceptions of AI tools. The study finds that AI-supported instruction improves technical skill acquisition and enhances creative exploration, but raises concerns about over-reliance on AI suggestions and reduced development of independent aesthetic judgment.

## Key Concepts
- **AI-supported education**: Instructional environments where AI tools assist with feedback, skill development, or content generation
- **Over-reliance**: Risk that students accept AI outputs uncritically rather than developing independent judgment
- **Aesthetic autonomy**: Capacity to develop personal visual sensibility independent of algorithmic suggestion
- **Aesthetic homogenization**: Risk that AI tools trained on dominant visual styles narrow creative exploration
- **Instructional scaffolding for AI**: Course structure mediating whether AI expands or constrains creative development

## Theoretical Framework
Educational technology and instructional design frameworks, integrating cognitive theory of learning with AI-enhanced pedagogy. Self-determination theory is drawn on to assess motivational effects of AI integration.

## Methods
Mixed-methods case study in a Turkish higher education digital photography course. Quantitative: pre/post assessments of technical skill and creative output quality. Qualitative: student interviews and reflective journals on AI tool use and learning experience.

## Main Arguments
1. AI integration in creative education can accelerate technical skill acquisition by providing immediate, personalized feedback
2. The risk of aesthetic homogenization is real: AI tools may narrow the range of creative exploration
3. Effective AI integration in creative courses requires explicit scaffolding to ensure students critique AI outputs rather than passively accepting them
4. Students' perceptions of AI are largely positive, but instructors must design against the tendency toward dependency

## Limitations
Single course, single institution, Turkish context—limited generalizability. Creative quality assessment is inherently subjective. Short study duration may not capture longer-term effects on aesthetic development.
