# Kirkpatrick Evaluation Model


## Summary
The Kirkpatrick Model is the dominant framework for evaluating training and instructional interventions in organizational and HPT contexts, structured around four levels: Reaction (participant satisfaction), Learning (knowledge and skill acquisition), Behavior (transfer of learning to the job), and Results (impact on organizational outcomes). Developed by Donald Kirkpatrick in the 1950s-60s and widely adopted by ISPI, ASTD/ATD, and training practitioners worldwide, the model provides a practical evaluation hierarchy that moves from easy-to-measure (Reaction) to difficult-but-important (Results). In this research vault, Kirkpatrick frames the evaluation dimension of HPT and instructional design, and appears in discussions of how to assess the effectiveness of AI-assisted training and performance interventions.

## Key Concepts
- Four levels: Reaction, Learning, Behavior, Results
- Transfer of learning: the critical and often neglected Level 3
- ROI (Return on Investment) as Phillips' extension of Kirkpatrick Level 4
- Formative vs. summative evaluation in the ADDIE context
- Challenges of Level 4 evaluation: attribution, causality, time lag
- Kirkpatrick as a communication tool for business stakeholders

## Theoretical Framework
Applied training evaluation; behavioral objectives tradition; situates training outcomes within organizational performance frameworks; developed by Donald Kirkpatrick (1959-1996); Jack Phillips' ROI Methodology as a fifth-level extension; connected in this vault to HPT, ADDIE, and Instructional Design Theory nodes.
