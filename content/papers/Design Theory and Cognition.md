# Design Theory and Cognition


## Summary
Design Theory and Cognition is a thematic node encompassing the scholarly study of how designers think, reason, and solve problems -- the cognitive processes, strategies, and structures that constitute designerly ways of knowing and doing. In this research vault, the node grounds inquiry into how AI tools interact with, support, or disrupt design cognition: papers examine how GenAI changes ideation patterns, metacognitive processes, problem-solution space navigation, and the nature of design expertise. The community spans foundational design research (Cross, Schon, Lawson) and more recent HCI and design education studies examining AI-mediated design practice.

## Key Concepts
- Design as a reflective conversation with the situation (Schon)
- Problem-solution co-evolution and the wicked problems framing
- Designerly ways of knowing: abductive reasoning, pattern recognition, judgment under uncertainty
- Protocol studies and think-aloud methods for studying design cognition
- GenAI's role in design process phases: ideation, iteration, reflection
- Metacognition in design: designers' awareness and regulation of their own design thinking

## Theoretical Framework
Design research tradition (Cross, Lawson, Dorst, Schon); cognitive science applied to design (Simon's design as problem-solving); situated and distributed cognition frameworks; HCI research on AI-assisted design; connected in this vault to AI in Design Education, GenAI in UX and Design Practice, and Design Judgment nodes.
