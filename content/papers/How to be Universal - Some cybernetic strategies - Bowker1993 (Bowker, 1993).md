# How to be Universal - Some cybernetic strategies - Bowker1993

## Summary
The new discipline of cybernetics expanded exponentially in the period 194370 Unlike traditional scientific disciplines, its practitioners claimed (to varying degrees) that they were producing a new universal science This paper anatomizes the claim to universality, details some rhetorical strategies used to support that claim, and examines some practical consequences for the general economy of the sciences argued by cyberneticians.

## Key Concepts
- **Cybernetics as universal science**: Claims to universality in cybernetics discipline (1943-70)
- Exponential expansion of cybernetics in mid-20th century
- **Rhetorical strategies** supporting claims to universality
- Distinction between cybernetics and traditional scientific disciplines
- Varying degrees of universality claims among practitioners
- Practical consequences for general economy of sciences
- Historical analysis of disciplinary boundary-making and universalist discourse

## Theoretical Framework
- **Science and Technology Studies (STS)**: Analysis of scientific knowledge production and disciplinary claims
- **Rhetoric of science**: Strategies used to establish legitimacy and scope
- History of cybernetics as interdisciplinary movement
- Epistemology of universalist vs specialist knowledge claims

## Methods
- Historical analysis of cybernetics literature and discourse (1943-1970)
- Rhetorical analysis of universality claims
- Examination of practical consequences for scientific organization

## Main Arguments
- Cybernetics practitioners claimed to produce new universal science, unlike traditional disciplines
- Multiple rhetorical strategies deployed to support and justify universality claims
- Varying degrees of universalist ambitions among different cybernetics practitioners
- Claims to universality had practical consequences for organization and economy of sciences
- Cybernetics' universalist rhetoric shaped its relationship to other disciplines
- Historical analysis reveals strategies for establishing new interdisciplinary fields
- Universality claims reflected and shaped scientific authority and legitimacy

## Limitations & Critiques
- Historical analysis focused on specific period (1943-1970)
- Retrospective analysis may not capture all contemporary debates and contestations
- Focus on rhetorical strategies may not fully address material/institutional dimensions
- Need to examine longer-term consequences beyond immediate period
- Relationship between rhetoric and actual practice requires further examination
