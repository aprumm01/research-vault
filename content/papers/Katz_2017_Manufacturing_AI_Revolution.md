# Manufacturing an Artificial Intelligence Revolution

## Summary

This critical paper argues that the contemporary AI "revolution" has been largely manufactured by major technology corporations through strategic rebranding of existing technologies. Katz contends that the AI label has been applied to what is essentially statistical analysis of big data, and that this rebranding serves corporate and political interests. The paper examines how AI's revival has been shaped by Silicon Valley companies hiring academics and promoting a behaviorist view of intelligence.

## Key Concepts

- **AI Rebranding**: Relabeling of big data and machine learning as "artificial intelligence"
- **Behaviorist AI**: Intelligence defined through input-output relationships, ignoring cognition
- **View from Nowhere**: AI's claim to objective, context-free intelligence
- **Governance by Numbers**: Using metrics and algorithms to manage society
- **Algorithmic Accountability**: (Critiqued) framework for addressing AI bias

## Theoretical Framework

The paper draws on:

1. **Science and Technology Studies**: Critical analysis of technology claims
2. **Political Economy**: Corporate interests in technology promotion
3. **Philosophy of Mind**: Critiques of behaviorist theories of intelligence
4. **Critical Theory**: Analysis of technology's role in power structures

## Methods

- Historical analysis of AI terminology usage (media and academic)
- Analysis of corporate hiring patterns and research funding
- Critical examination of AI system capabilities versus claims
- Case studies of AI failures in image recognition
- Analysis of algorithmic sentencing as policy example

## Main Arguments

1. **Manufactured Revolution**: AI's current prominence is corporate-driven, not technologically driven
2. **Behaviorist Foundations**: Current AI systems rely on behaviorist assumptions incompatible with human intelligence
3. **View from Nowhere**: AI claims objectivity while embodying particular perspectives (white, male, Western)
4. **Masking Institutional Power**: Focus on algorithmic bias obscures structural inequalities
5. **Governance by Numbers**: AI enables neoliberal policies of measurement and control
6. **Thoughtlessness**: AI promotes a society that delegates thinking to machines

## Evidence Presented

- Analysis of "AI" mentions in media and academic publications over time
- Table of academics hired by tech companies to head AI labs
- Examples of deep learning failures on socially significant images
- Critique of algorithmic sentencing in criminal justice

## Limitations and Critiques

- Strong polemical tone may undermine some analytical claims
- Limited engagement with technical AI advances
- Post-2017 developments (especially LLMs) not addressed
- Some arguments rely heavily on selected examples
- Alternative visions for ethical AI development not offered
