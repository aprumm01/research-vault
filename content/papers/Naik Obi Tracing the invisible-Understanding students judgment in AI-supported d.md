## Key Concepts
- Student judgment in AI-supported design work
- Generative AI as design collaborator
- Design workflow integration of AI tools
- Established vs novel forms of design judgment
- HCI design course context
- Student team reflections and practices
- Invisible aspects of design judgment
- AI tools reshaping design cognition
- Collaborative dynamics with AI systems

## Theoretical Framework
- Design cognition theory
- Reflective practice (Schön)
- Human-AI collaboration frameworks
- Situated learning in design education
- Distributed cognition with AI tools

## Methods
- Qualitative analysis of student reflections
- Case study of 33 student teams
- HCI design course as research context
- Thematic analysis of judgment types
- Grounded theory approach

## Main Arguments
- Students make both established and novel types of judgments when using AI in design
- AI tools function as collaborators rather than mere aids in student design work
- Generative AI integration reveals previously invisible aspects of design judgment
- Traditional design judgment forms persist but are transformed by AI collaboration
- New forms of judgment emerge specific to AI-mediated design processes
- Students must develop meta-level judgment about when and how to use AI tools
- Design education needs to explicitly address AI-specific judgment capabilities
- Tracing judgment processes makes tacit design knowledge visible
- Student reflections reveal tensions between AI capabilities and design intentionality

## Limitations & Critiques
- Limited to single HCI course context, generalizability uncertain
- Student teams may not represent professional design practice
- Self-reported reflections may not capture all judgment processes
- 33 teams provides breadth but limited depth per team
- Rapidly evolving AI tools may date findings quickly
- Does not track long-term development of AI-mediated design judgment
- May not capture full diversity of student experiences and backgrounds
- Assessment criteria and course structure may shape reported judgments
- Limited comparison with non-AI-supported design processes
