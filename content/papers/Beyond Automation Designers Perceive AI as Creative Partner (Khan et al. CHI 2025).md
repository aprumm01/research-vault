# Beyond Automation: Designers Perceive AI as Creative Partner (Khan et al. CHI 2025)

## Summary
Khan et al.'s CHI 2025 study challenges the dominant "automation vs. augmentation" binary by showing that professional designers increasingly perceive AI as a creative partner rather than a tool for automating routine tasks. Through interviews with 20 professional designers across disciplines, the authors document how designers co-create with AI — using it for inspiration, unexpected juxtapositions, and exploratory ideation — in ways that reframe the designer's role as creative director and curator rather than sole originator. The paper calls for HCI to move beyond efficiency-focused framing of AI toward collaboration-centered models.

## Key Concepts
- AI as creative partner (vs. automation tool)
- Creative co-authorship and designer identity
- Inspiration-seeking and serendipitous ideation with AI
- Designer-as-curator and director
- Beyond automation/augmentation binary

## Theoretical Framework
Human-AI collaboration framework; draws on creativity research, professional identity theory, and HCI literature on AI tools in creative work; challenges prior framing from automation literature.

## Methods
Semi-structured interviews with 20 professional designers (product, graphic, UX, industrial); thematic analysis of how designers conceptualize and experience AI in creative practice.

## Main Arguments
- Designers are developing a third relationship with AI beyond automation and augmentation: genuine creative partnership
- The partnership model involves iterative co-creation where the designer directs, curates, and responds to AI outputs
- This relationship challenges traditional notions of creative authorship and requires new professional identity frameworks
- HCI design of AI tools should support co-creative workflows, not just efficiency gains

## Limitations & Critiques
Sample of 20 designers drawn primarily from tech-forward contexts may not represent the full diversity of design practice; the "creative partner" framing may romanticize AI agency; findings may shift rapidly as tools evolve.
