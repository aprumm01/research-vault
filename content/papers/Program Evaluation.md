## Summary
Fitzpatrick, Sanders, and Worthen's foundational textbook chapter establishes the basic purpose, uses, and conceptual distinctions of program evaluation, framing it as a systematic process of determining the value or merit of educational and social programs. They distinguish formal from informal evaluation, formative from summative purposes, and internal from external evaluators, providing students and practitioners with a conceptual foundation for navigating the landscape of evaluation approaches.

## Key Concepts
- Program evaluation
- Formative vs. summative evaluation
- Internal vs. external evaluators
- Evaluation purposes
- Needs assessment

## Theoretical Framework
Evaluation theory draws on social science methodology, policy analysis, and judgment theory to establish standards for systematic, credible assessment of program value.

## Methods
Textbook chapter; conceptual framework development; orienting questions and case-based illustrations of evaluation concepts and distinctions.

## Main Arguments
- Evaluation serves multiple purposes: accountability, improvement, knowledge generation, and political/organizational legitimacy
- Formative evaluation informs program improvement; summative evaluation judges worth for continuation or replication decisions
- External evaluators offer independence but lack contextual knowledge; internal evaluators have the reverse tradeoff
- Good evaluation requires explicit articulation of criteria and standards for judging program merit
