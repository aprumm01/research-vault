## Summary
This preprint study examines whether AI development contributes to labor market polarization—the hollowing out of middle-skill jobs—using panel data from 2000 to 2022 across multiple countries. Employing Generalized Method of Moments (GMM) analysis, the authors find evidence that AI adoption increases displacement in routine cognitive and manual jobs while creating demand at the high-skill end, contributing to wage inequality and job market bifurcation.

## Theoretical Framework
Labor market segmentation theory and task-based models of technological change (Autor et al.) frame the analysis of how AI affects employment distributions across skill levels.

## Methods
Quantitative; dynamic panel analysis using GMM; macroeconomic data from 2000–2022; cross-national comparative analysis.

## Main Arguments
- AI development is associated with polarization of the labor market, displacing middle-skill routine jobs
- High-skill non-routine work gains value under AI adoption, widening income inequality
- The relationship between AI and employment is heterogeneous across sectors
- Policy interventions are needed to address structural unemployment caused by AI-driven polarization
