## Summary
First systematic literature review focused specifically on the integration of LLMs (GPT-4, Gemini, PaLM) in UI/UX design workflows, synthesizing 38 peer-reviewed studies from 2022–2025. Maps LLM applications across the design lifecycle from ideation through evaluation, and identifies persistent challenges including hallucination, prompt instability, and limited explainability.

## Key Concepts
- Large language models (LLMs) in design
- Prompt engineering
- Human-in-the-loop workflows
- Hallucination and prompt instability
- UI/UX design lifecycle

## Theoretical Framework
Systematic literature review (SLR) methodology; design lifecycle framework spanning ideation, prototyping, evaluation, and refinement; human-AI collaboration lens.

## Methods
SLR: keyword search across databases; 38 peer-reviewed studies selected (2022–2025); data extraction covering LLM applications, best practices, and challenges; PRISMA-aligned process.

## Main Arguments
- LLMs are integrated across ideation, wireframing, prototyping, and evaluation stages of the design lifecycle
- Prompt engineering and human-in-the-loop workflows are the most common integration patterns
- Hallucination, prompt instability, and limited explainability are persistent barriers to reliable LLM use in design
- Ethical and inclusive integration requires ongoing human oversight throughout the design process

## Limitations & Critiques
The rapidly evolving field means findings may be outdated quickly; publication bias likely favors positive applications; primarily English-language sources; the narrow 2022–2025 window may miss longer-term adoption patterns.
