## Summary
Tsao et al. conduct a PRISMA-compliant scoping review of 57 empirical studies (2022–2025) examining how creative professionals perceive and integrate generative AI across visual art/design, writing/literature, performing arts, and environmental/spatial design. They identify universal trends including a shift from tool use toward AI as creative collaborator, concerns about authenticity and intellectual property, and emerging professional identity crises, synthesizing these findings into a conceptual model of GenAI integration in creative practice.

## Theoretical Framework
Creative cognition theory and professional identity frameworks inform the conceptual model of how practitioners across creative domains perceive and respond to generative AI integration.

## Methods
Scoping review following PRISMA-ScR guidelines; analysis of 57 empirical papers from multiple databases focusing on professional creative practice with GenAI tools (2022–2025).

## Main Arguments
- Creative professionals across domains show common patterns: initial excitement, followed by concerns about authenticity and ownership
- GenAI is increasingly positioned as a creative collaborator rather than a production tool
- Intellectual property and attribution remain the most significant unresolved issues for creative practitioners
- Professional identity is being renegotiated as AI encroaches on previously human-exclusive creative domains
