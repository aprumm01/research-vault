# How Knowledge Workers Think Generative AI Will (Not) Transform Their Industries

## Summary
Woodruff et al. (Google LLC and California State University Long Beach, CHI 2024) examine how knowledge workers across professional domains form beliefs about generative AI's likely — and unlikely — transformative effects on their industries. Through professional interviews and thematic analysis, they find that workers' assessments of AI transformation are not random: they systematically invoke core professional values, specialized expertise, and identity as evidence that their field will resist or transcend AI disruption. The paper, with 54 citations and 25,862 downloads as of early 2026, is among the most widely read CHI 2024 studies on worker AI perceptions.

## Key Concepts
- Knowledge worker beliefs about AI transformation
- Professional identity as a buffer against AI disruption narratives
- Core professional values in technology sensemaking
- Industry-specific AI transformation assessments

## Theoretical Framework
Professional identity theory and sensemaking; workers use professional frameworks to interpret technological change, constructing narratives that protect their sense of occupational value.

## Methods
Professional interviews across multiple knowledge work domains; thematic analysis by a research team spanning Google LLC and California State University. CHI 2024 (27 pages, DOI: 10.1145/3613904.3642700).

## Main Arguments
- Knowledge workers assess AI transformation through the lens of what makes their work distinctively human — specialized judgment, relational work, creative interpretation
- Workers perceive AI as likely to transform routine tasks but unlikely to replace the "core" of their professional identity
- These beliefs are partly protective sensemaking — workers emphasize AI's limitations in ways that affirm professional value
- The same features workers cite as AI-resistant (ambiguity, context, judgment) are precisely where AI is rapidly improving, suggesting a gap between perception and trajectory

## Limitations & Critiques
Interview-based; participants' stated beliefs may not predict actual behavior change. Google-affiliated research team may introduce framing bias. Does not study actual AI-mediated work, only beliefs about future states.
