# Exploring the Impact of AI Language Model ChatGPT on User Experience (IJTIM)

## Summary
This paper examines the impact of ChatGPT on user experience design work, investigating how UX practitioners are incorporating the large language model into research, writing, and prototyping tasks. Through a survey and interview study with UX practitioners, the authors document how ChatGPT is being used to generate interview questions, synthesize research notes, draft UX writing, and explain design rationale — while identifying the accuracy, hallucination, and generic output problems practitioners encounter. The paper provides a practical assessment of ChatGPT's current utility and limitations for UX work.

## Key Concepts
- ChatGPT in UX research and design workflows
- AI hallucination and accuracy concerns in UX
- UX writing and AI text generation
- AI for design documentation and rationale
- Practitioner AI adoption in UX

## Theoretical Framework
Technology assessment; human-AI collaboration in professional practice; UX practice research; draws on technology adoption and practitioner-AI collaboration literature.

## Methods
Mixed-methods study; online survey with UX practitioners; follow-up qualitative interviews; analysis of ChatGPT use patterns, perceived utility, and experienced limitations.

## Main Arguments
- UX practitioners are adopting ChatGPT rapidly for text-heavy tasks (documentation, question generation, synthesizing notes) where it provides genuine time savings
- Hallucination and generic output are the most significant practical concerns, requiring practitioners to develop verification habits
- ChatGPT is least useful for tasks requiring deep contextual knowledge of specific users, organizations, or projects
- Effective ChatGPT integration requires practitioners to develop prompt literacy and output verification skills as new professional competencies

## Limitations & Critiques
Survey at a single point in time as ChatGPT was newly available; findings may date quickly; the sample of UX practitioners willing to respond to an AI survey may skew toward early adopters.
