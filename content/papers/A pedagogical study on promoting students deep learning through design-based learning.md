# A Pedagogical Study on Promoting Students' Deep Learning Through Design-Based Learning

## Summary
This paper investigates the effectiveness of design-based learning (DBL) in promoting deep learning among postgraduate students in engineering design education at Wuhan University of Science and Technology in China. The researchers conducted a quasi-experimental study comparing a control class using traditional teaching methods (49 students in 2019) with an experimental class using DBL methods (56 students in 2021). The study addressed three research questions: whether DBL promotes deep learning, whether it improves academic performance, and what students' views are on the approach.

The authors developed a three-stage DBL teaching model consisting of: (1) creating a situation stage, (2) design scheme stage, and (3) evaluation and reflection stage. The practical teaching project focused on "Library Service Design for Wuhan University of Science and Technology" over an 8-week course (32 class hours). Results showed statistically significant improvements in students' deep learning status (learning motivation, learning input, and learning strategy) and deep learning abilities (critical thinking, problem solving, teamwork, learning to learn, and learning perseverance). The experimental class also significantly outperformed the control class academically, with more students achieving A and B grades and none receiving D grades.

Student evaluations were highly positive, with 85.7% expressing satisfaction with the approach, appreciating its emphasis on active thinking and deep learning. The study concludes that DBL is effective for promoting students' deep learning and improving teaching quality in engineering design education.

## Key Concepts
- **Design-Based Learning (DBL)**: A learning model that emphasizes learning scientific knowledge and professional skills through involvement in designing projects in real-life situations, similar to problem-based learning but with design as the central activity
- **Deep Learning**: High-level or active cognitive processing involving critical thinking, making connections between old and new knowledge, reflecting in authentic situations, and solving complex problems through deep information processing
- **Backward Thinking Model**: Nelson's DBL learning model with six steps: clarify theme, determine problem, establish evaluation standards, establish models, teaching guidance, and evaluation/modification
- **Deep Learning Process**: Consists of three dimensions - learning motivation, learning input, and learning strategy
- **Deep Learning Ability**: Comprises three domains (cognitive, interpersonal, personal) with six competencies: critical thinking, problem solving, team cooperation, effective communication, learning to learn, and learning perseverance

## Theoretical Framework
The study draws on multiple theoretical foundations: (1) Kolodner's "learning by design" model and the "design-based scientific inquiry learning cycle"; (2) Nelson's "Backward Thinking" model derived from Bloom's taxonomy; (3) Fortus et al.'s "Design-Based Science learning cycle" with five steps; (4) The National Research Council (NRC) classification of deep learning competencies into cognitive, interpersonal, and personal dimensions; and (5) The Hewlett Foundation's six 21st-century competencies for deep learning. The authors synthesize these frameworks to construct a DBL teaching model with five characteristics: situational, design-centered, integrative, iterative, and reflective.

## Methods
The study employed a quasi-experimental design with pre-test and post-test measures. Participants included 49 postgraduate students in the control class (2019, traditional teaching) and 56 in the experimental class (2021, DBL teaching) from the "Artistic Design and Methodology" course. Data collection involved: (1) Two validated questionnaires - the Deep Learning Process Questionnaire (16 items across 3 dimensions, Cronbach's alpha = 0.869) and the Deep Learning Ability Questionnaire (17 items across 6 dimensions, Cronbach's alpha = 0.888), both using 5-point Likert scales; (2) Student grade comparisons based on four criteria (integrity, rationality, innovativeness, aesthetic) totaling 100 points; (3) Depth interviews with three students representing different achievement levels. Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS 25.0, including t-tests for comparing pre-test and post-test results.

## Main Arguments
- DBL effectively promotes students' deep learning by increasing learning motivation, enhancing learning input, and improving learning strategies
- The DBL approach significantly improves students' higher-order thinking capabilities, including critical thinking, problem solving, and learning to learn abilities
- DBL leads to better academic performance, with the experimental class showing significantly higher grades (mean = 86.571) compared to the control class (mean = 82.674)
- Students are highly satisfied with DBL, viewing it as innovative, interactive, and effective for promoting active thinking and deep learning
- The integration of design thinking throughout the learning process enables students to apply knowledge to real-world problems while developing interpersonal skills through collaboration

## Limitations & Critiques
- Small sample size limited to a specific teaching context at one university, limiting generalizability to other areas of engineering education
- The research does not go far enough into the investigation of deep learning, lacking more authoritative and comprehensive examination
- The research lacks an authoritative survey of student learning satisfaction
- Effective communication ability showed relatively low improvement (mean values of 3.22 pre-test and 3.60 post-test), indicating need for further improvement in teaching strategies for this dimension
- Communication and interaction within different groups was identified as needing further improvement
- The 2020 cohort could not be included due to COVID-19 disrupting situational teaching sessions
