# The Future of Work is Blended Not Hybrid (Constantinides et al.)

## Summary
Constantinides, Verma, Sadeghian, and El Ali argue that "hybrid work" — the flexible physical/remote model that emerged post-pandemic — is already obsolete as a framing because it misses the more fundamental transformation underway: AI systems are now co-workers, co-authors, and co-decision-makers, making human and AI contributions inseparable. They coin "blended work" to describe this state: where work happens through AI, with AI outputs embedded in knowledge production, creative work, and service delivery. The provocation calls for urgent research on AI-mediated work practices, boundary work, and accountability structures.

## Key Concepts
- Blended work (vs. hybrid work)
- Human-AI co-authorship and co-creation
- Algorithmic decision-making in knowledge work
- Work-life boundary erosion
- AI transparency and accountability in work contexts

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on CSCW, HCI, and organizational sociology of work; the "blended" framing builds on but rejects hybrid work discourse as insufficiently radical — argues for a qualitative shift in the human-work-technology relation, not merely a spatial reconfiguration.

## Methods
Provocation/position paper; conceptual argumentation with illustrative examples from knowledge work, creative professions, and service sectors; survey of related literature on AI in work contexts.

## Main Arguments
- The hybrid work frame (where to work) is a distraction from the deeper question of how human agency and AI agency are blended within the work itself
- AI systems now co-author reports, generate drafts, allocate tasks, and make recommendations that shape human decisions — the boundary between human and AI work is dissolving
- "Blended work" demands new design considerations: transparency about AI contributions, mechanisms for human override, legibility of algorithmic logic to workers
- The key ethical question is not whether AI will change work, but whether workers and societies will actively shape those changes or have them imposed

## Limitations
- Provocation paper format prioritizes rhetorical force over empirical demonstration; the "blended work" concept needs empirical validation
- Risks conflating distinct phenomena: task automation, AI collaboration, and algorithmic management have different implications for worker agency
