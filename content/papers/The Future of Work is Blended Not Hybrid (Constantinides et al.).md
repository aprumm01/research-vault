## Summary
Constantinides et al. argue that work is no longer simply "hybrid" (split between home and office) but "blended"—deeply integrated with AI co-workers, algorithmic decisions, and AI-mediated outputs that reshape human roles, agency, and expertise. They contend that AI has fundamentally infiltrated knowledge, creative, and service work, redistributing agency and creativity in ways that require new conceptual frameworks beyond simple human-machine binary thinking.

## Key Concepts
- Blended work
- AI co-workers
- Human agency in AI work
- Knowledge work transformation
- Post-hybrid work

## Theoretical Framework
Organizational theory and human-computer interaction frame the reconceptualization of work from "hybrid" to "blended," drawing on sociotechnical systems theory to analyze AI's integration into work practices.

## Methods
Provocation paper; conceptual argument drawing on literature review and theoretical analysis; presented at an HCI venue.

## Main Arguments
- Hybrid work as a category is obsolete; work is now blended with AI at every level
- AI infiltration into creative and knowledge work redistributes agency and authorship in unprecedented ways
- Human workers must develop new forms of agency that operate alongside and through AI systems
- Research and design frameworks must account for AI as a co-worker, not just a tool
