# AI-Driven User Experience Design: Exploring Innovations and Challenges in Del...

## Summary
Padmasiri, Kalutharage, Jayawardhane, and Wickramarathne (SLIIT, Sri Lanka, 2024) present a short survey-style paper on AI-driven UX design, examining innovations and challenges in delivering tailored user experiences in contemporary digital products. The paper addresses how AI technologies — particularly those enabling personalization and adaptive interfaces — are reshaping UX practice. It explores the tension between AI's capacity to generate personalized experiences at scale and the practical challenges of implementation, including data requirements, ethical considerations, and design quality concerns.

## Key Concepts
- AI-driven UX personalization and tailoring
- Adaptive interfaces and intelligent UX systems
- Innovations vs. challenges in AI-augmented UX
- Digital experience design in the AI era

## Theoretical Framework
Primarily practitioner-oriented; surveys AI applications in UX design from an applied and innovation lens rather than a theoretical framework. Positions AI as a design tool for scaling personalized experiences.

## Methods
Short paper (6 pages) from Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology; likely a literature review or conceptual/survey paper rather than an empirical study. Published 2024.

## Main Arguments
- AI enables UX designers to deliver personalized, adaptive experiences that would be infeasible to produce manually at scale
- Key innovations include recommendation systems, predictive UI, and AI-generated content tailored to individual users
- Challenges include data privacy, algorithmic bias, over-personalization (filter bubbles), and loss of designer control over experience quality
- Effective AI-driven UX requires collaboration between designers and data/AI specialists

## Limitations & Critiques
Short conference/workshop paper — limited empirical depth. Perspective from a developing-economy academic context (Sri Lanka) offers geographic diversity but may not reflect the tooling and resource realities of major UX design organizations. Primarily descriptive rather than advancing novel theory or findings.
