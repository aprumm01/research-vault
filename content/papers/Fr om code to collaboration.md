# Fr om code to collaboration:

## Summary
Rastogi and Pandita (Symbiosis Institute of Business Management, Pune) examine IT professionals' perceptions of AI's impact on workforce dynamics through a qualitative study of 27 specialists at multinational corporations. Using thematic analysis of semi-structured interviews, the study finds that IT workers experience AI as simultaneously enabling and threatening: AI streamlines repetitive coding and documentation tasks but creates anxiety about skill obsolescence and role ambiguity. The paper argues that organizations must invest in continuous reskilling, build psychological safety, and redesign work to leverage human-AI complementarity.

## Key Concepts
- **AI-driven workflow reconfiguration**: AI tools change how knowledge work is structured and distributed within teams
- **Skill obsolescence anxiety**: IT professionals' fear that AI will render their existing expertise less valuable
- **Role ambiguity**: Uncertainty about how AI adoption will redefine job responsibilities and performance expectations
- **Human-AI complementarity**: Humans and AI are most productive when their respective strengths are combined
- **Psychological safety**: Organizational climate where workers feel safe to experiment with and adapt to AI tools

## Theoretical Framework
Qualitative organizational behavior research drawing on technology acceptance, organizational change, and human-AI interaction frameworks. Thematic analysis grounds findings in participants' lived experiences.

## Methods
Purposive sampling of 27 IT specialists from multinational corporations; in-depth semi-structured interviews; thematic analysis following systematic coding procedures.

## Main Arguments
1. IT professionals see AI as a productivity tool but fear it will commoditize their specialized skills over time
2. Successful AI integration requires organizational interventions beyond tool deployment: leadership communication, reskilling infrastructure, and participatory role redesign
3. Effective human-AI collaboration requires new meta-skills (AI literacy, prompt engineering, output validation) that current training programs do not provide
4. AI's impacts on workforce dynamics are socially shaped by organizational context, not technologically determined

## Limitations
Single-industry study (IT sector); findings may not transfer to less digitally mature sectors. Purposive sampling limits representativeness. Qualitative findings cannot be generalized to statistical populations.
