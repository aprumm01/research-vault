# Cogent Social Sciences

**DOI:** [10.1080/23311886.2025.2476737](https://doi.org/10.1080/23311886.2025.2476737)

## Summary
Agarwal (2025, *Cogent Social Sciences*) develops a multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) framework for optimizing employee roles in the era of generative AI. Drawing on co-creation dynamics between humans and AI systems, the paper analyzes how organizations should redesign roles to maximize value from human-AI collaboration. Using analytical hierarchy process (AHP) and fuzzy MCDM methods, Agarwal identifies criteria for role allocation decisions (task complexity, AI substitutability, human judgment requirement, relational content) and proposes a framework for systematically assigning tasks to humans, AI, or collaborative configurations.

## Key Concepts
- **Co-creation dynamics**: The ways humans and AI systems jointly produce value; neither fully substituting for nor simply augmenting the other
- **MCDM (Multi-Criteria Decision Making)**: Analytical framework for evaluating alternatives against multiple competing criteria
- **AHP (Analytical Hierarchy Process)**: Method for weighting and comparing decision criteria through pairwise comparisons
- **Role optimization**: Systematic redesign of job roles to align human capabilities and AI capabilities with task requirements
- **AI substitutability**: The degree to which a given task can be effectively performed by AI without human involvement

## Theoretical Framework
Human-AI teaming and organizational design theory, combined with operations research methods (AHP, fuzzy MCDM). The framework is normative—prescribing how organizations should allocate tasks—rather than descriptive of how they currently do.

## Methods
Conceptual framework development using multi-criteria decision-making methods; illustrative application to hypothetical role allocation scenarios. Published in *Cogent Social Sciences* (open access, Taylor & Francis).

## Main Arguments
1. Generative AI forces systematic reconsideration of every organizational role; piecemeal adaptation is insufficient
2. Role optimization requires explicit criteria for what tasks should be allocated to humans, AI, or collaborative configurations
3. High-judgment, relational, and ethically consequential tasks should remain human-led; routine, data-intensive tasks are candidates for AI automation
4. MCDM methods provide organizations with a structured, defensible basis for role redesign decisions

## Limitations
MCDM criteria weights are context-dependent and require expert elicitation that may introduce bias. The framework is prescriptive without empirical validation that organizations applying it achieve better outcomes. Rapidly changing AI capabilities mean substitutability assessments may become outdated quickly.
