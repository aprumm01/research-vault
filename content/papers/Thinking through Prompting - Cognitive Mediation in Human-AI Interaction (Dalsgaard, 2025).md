# Thinking through Prompting: Cognitive Mediation in Human-AI Interaction

## Summary
This theoretical paper proposes a conceptual reframing of prompting as a cognitive interface rather than a simple input mechanism. Drawing on theories of external cognition, interface semiotics, and pragmatist theories of inquiry, the author argues that prompts function as cognitive interfaces that mediate between user intent and algorithmic generation through three interrelated dimensions: semantic projection, compositional structuring, and interpretive negotiation.

The paper challenges classical models of interface design that frame interfaces as structured environments guiding action through visible or tangible cues. Instead, prompting involves a dense, evolving semiotic and interpretive space created through prompt-response cycles. Users construct, revise, and interpret speculative representations, testing hypotheses and adjusting framing in response to opaque system behavior.

The author develops the argument across several theoretical layers: prompts as emergent interfaces (not pre-given but enacted through use), prompting as epistemic externalization (supporting expression and exploration), and prompting as instrumental inquiry (discovering intent through use). A vignette about a marketing team developing fitness app promotional material illustrates how the three dimensions operate in practice.

## Key Concepts
- **Semantic Projection**: How users project intent into representational form through language that resonates with the model's learned patterns; requires anticipatory framing
- **Compositional Structuring**: How prompts are composed from conceptual components (task, tone, subject, style, constraints) forming modular, reusable templates
- **Interpretive Negotiation**: How users read, interpret, and revise system responses through iterative dialogue; reveals biases and misalignments
- **Prompt Literacy**: Tacit knowledge and craft practice developed through experimentation, discernment, and iterative refinement

## Theoretical Framework
The paper integrates external cognition theory (Scaife and Rogers, Zhang and Norman), interface semiotics and cultural theory (Bertelsen and Pold), and pragmatist theories of inquiry (Dewey, Schon's reflection-in-action). It positions prompting within design research traditions emphasizing reflective tool use and externalization.

## Methods
Conceptual and theoretical analysis developing a framework for understanding prompting as cognitive interface work. The paper uses a research vignette (marketing team developing fitness app content) to illustrate and ground the theoretical dimensions in practice.

## Main Arguments
- Prompts are best understood as cognitive interfaces that mediate between intention and generation, supporting users in articulating, refining, and transforming their goals
- The prompt should be viewed as a site of cognitive labor rather than a static interface element
- Prompting is epistemic activity; users develop "prompt literacy" through trial-and-error, reflection, and informal knowledge sharing
- Interface characteristics are enacted through use; prompt-response cycles shape what users see as possible, relevant, or needing revision
- Prompting is less about "getting the AI to do something" and more about exploring what can and should be asked, said, or represented
- Future research should examine how prompting evolves in everyday workflows through ethnographic, autoethnographic, or interaction-analytic methods

## Limitations & Critiques
- The paper is conceptual rather than empirical; vignettes are illustrative rather than drawn from formal studies
- The approach privileges conceptual breadth over empirical specificity; future studies should examine recurring dynamics
- The work does not engage with internal mechanics of generative models or how prompts are tokenized/parsed
- Not all prompting is reflective or strategic; many sessions are instrumental, functional, repetitive, or incidental
- The paper does not present design interventions; future work should explore interfaces supporting prompt inquiry
