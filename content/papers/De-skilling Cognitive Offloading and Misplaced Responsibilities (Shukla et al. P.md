# De-skilling Cognitive Offloading and Misplaced Responsibilities (Shukla et al. Purdue)

**DOI:** [10.1145/3706599.3719931](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706599.3719931)

**Year:** 2025
**Venue:** CHI 2025
**Institution:** Purdue University

## Key Finding
GenAI in design risks de-skilling, cognitive offloading, misplaced responsibility paradoxes

## Connections

## Summary
Shukla et al. argue that AI-enabled cognitive offloading — delegating cognitive tasks to AI systems — poses risks of de-skilling and misplaced responsibility in professional work contexts. The paper examines cases where AI assistance reduces practitioners' opportunities to develop and maintain competencies, and where responsibility for outcomes becomes diffuse or attributed to the AI rather than to human professionals. The analysis calls for careful attention to the long-term skill and responsibility implications of AI adoption in professional practice.

## Key Concepts
- Cognitive offloading to AI systems
- De-skilling under AI assistance
- Misplaced responsibility in human-AI systems
- Professional competency maintenance with AI

## Theoretical Framework
Cognitive science and professional ethics frameworks; draws on extended mind theory and professional responsibility literature to examine the skill and accountability consequences of routine cognitive offloading to AI.

## Methods
Analytical/conceptual paper with case examples from professional contexts; Purdue University; argues from cognitive science and ethics perspectives about AI offloading risks.

## Main Arguments
- Cognitive offloading to AI can produce de-skilling — reduced ability to perform tasks without AI assistance
- Responsibility for AI-assisted decisions becomes misplaced when practitioners over-attribute agency to AI
- De-skilling and misplaced responsibility are systemic risks, not just individual failures
- Professions and organizations must actively manage cognitive offloading to preserve essential competencies
