# Changing Design Education for the 21st Century (Meyer & Norman, 2020)

## Summary
Argues that design education fails to prepare designers for the complex, societal-scale challenges of the 21st century, with programs still organized around tacit knowledge transfer and aesthetic skill development rather than systems thinking and collaborative problem-solving. Calls for a DesignX approach—design applied to major, wicked challenges—that requires interdisciplinary integration, longer educational timelines, and explicit cultivation of the analytical and advocacy functions of design practice.

## Key Concepts
- **DesignX**: Design applied to complex sociotechnical challenges requiring interdisciplinary teams and systemic approaches
- **Tacit knowledge transfer**: The informal, apprenticeship-based learning that dominates studio education
- **Design-driven transformation**: Using design thinking as a lever for organizational and societal change
- **Complex sociotechnical systems**: The class of challenges that current design education leaves graduates unprepared to address

## Main Arguments
1. Current design education is optimized for aesthetic skill and tacit craft, not for complex systems challenges
2. The most valuable designer capacities—systems thinking, cross-disciplinary communication, advocacy—are rarely taught explicitly
3. DesignX requires a fundamentally restructured curriculum with longer timelines and explicit teaching of complexity navigation
