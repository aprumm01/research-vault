## Summary
Li, Hou, Lin, Zhu, Cao, and El Ali (arXiv:2509.10652, 2025) present a qualitative study of how UX professionals experience and perceive vibe coding -- AI-assisted design and development via natural language prompting. Twenty UX professionals from enterprise, startup, and academic contexts were interviewed (May-June 2025). The study identifies a four-stage vibe coding workflow, surfaces a central tension between efficiency and reflective design intent, and examines the professional implications of AI-assisted design for UX practitioners.

## Key Concepts
- Vibe coding as AI-assisted design and development via natural language prompting
- Four-stage workflow: context setup and ideation, AI generation and refinement, manual debugging, testing and review
- Core tension: "intending the right design" (efficiency-focused) vs. "designing the right intention" (reflection-focused)
- Deskilling risks and ownership questions for junior UX designers
- Social stigma and professional identity threats from AI reliance
- Cognitive offloading and creativity support as perceived benefits

## Theoretical Framework
Qualitative interview study grounded in HCI and UX practice research; draws on theories of cognitive offloading, design cognition, and professional identity; situates vibe coding within the broader shift toward AI-assisted creative workflows; published on arXiv (2509.10652, 2025) and framed within CHI-adjacent HCI scholarship.

## Methods
Semi-structured interviews with 20 UX professionals (enterprise, startup, and academic contexts); conducted May-June 2025; thematic analysis of interview transcripts; participants included designers, researchers, and developers with varying AI tool experience; inductive coding approach to surfacing themes.

## Main Arguments
- Vibe coding follows a four-stage workflow: (1) context setup and ideation, (2) AI-assisted generation and refinement, (3) manual debugging and error correction, (4) testing and design review
- The central tension is between "intending the right design" (using AI to accelerate toward known outcomes) and "designing the right intention" (using the process to discover and refine design goals)
- Benefits include accelerated iteration cycles, cognitive offloading of routine tasks, creativity support, and lowered barriers to prototyping
- Challenges include code unreliability, integration difficulties, over-reliance risk, deskilling of junior practitioners, and social stigma around AI-assisted work
- Ownership in vibe coding is tied to the quality of intention-setting and prompting, not execution, shifting professional value toward framing and judgment

## Limitations & Critiques
The sample (n=20) is relatively small and limited to three context types, which may not capture the full range of UX professional experience. The study was conducted in mid-2025, reflecting a rapidly evolving tooling landscape; findings may shift as capabilities and norms change. Self-reported interview data may not capture actual design behaviors. The arXiv preprint status means the paper has not yet undergone full peer review.
