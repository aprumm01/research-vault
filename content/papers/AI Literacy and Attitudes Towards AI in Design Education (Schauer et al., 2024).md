# AI Literacy and Attitudes Towards AI in Design Education (Schauer et al., 2024)

## Summary
Reports a two-part quantitative and qualitative survey of communication and architectural design students at HTW Berlin examining AI literacy and attitudes toward generative AI. Finds that design students rate their AI literacy as moderate, express both optimistic (productivity, creative augmentation) and critical (job loss, copyright, creativity reduction) views, and identify significant competency gaps in ethical reasoning and practical use of specific AI technologies.

## Key Concepts
- **AI literacy**: Students' self-assessed ability to understand, critically evaluate, and use AI tools
- **Competency gaps**: Identified shortfalls between student AI skills and those required for AI-integrated design practice
- **Attitudes toward AI**: The mixed affective and evaluative orientations design students hold toward GenAI

## Main Arguments
1. Design students are aware of AI's potential but lack structured competencies to use it critically and ethically
2. Curricular integration of AI must address both technical skills and ethical reasoning
3. Attitudes vary by discipline—communication vs. architecture—suggesting domain-specific imaginary formation
