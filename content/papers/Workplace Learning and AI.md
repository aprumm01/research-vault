# Workplace Learning and AI


## Summary
Workplace Learning and AI is a thematic node examining how workers learn and develop expertise in organizations that are adopting and integrating AI tools -- including both formal training interventions and the informal, situated learning that occurs as practitioners encounter AI in their daily work. In this research vault, the node bridges the HPT and instructional design traditions (which focus on systematic performance improvement) with more recent HCI and organizational research documenting how workers are actually learning to use, adapt to, and resist GenAI tools. Activity Theory frameworks appear in this community as a way to analyze how AI mediates workplace learning activity systems and generates new contradictions and learning opportunities.

## Key Concepts
- Situated learning: workplace learning as embedded in authentic practice
- Informal and incidental learning: most workplace AI learning is self-directed and unplanned
- AI as a mediating artifact in workplace activity systems (Activity Theory)
- Communities of practice: peer learning and knowledge sharing around AI tools
- Performance support vs. training: just-in-time AI assistance vs. pre-event instruction
- Organizational learning and AI: how organizations build collective AI competence

## Theoretical Framework
Workplace learning research; situated cognition (Lave and Wenger); Activity Theory (Engestrom); organizational learning (Argyris, Senge); HPT and ADDIE traditions; informal learning frameworks (Marsick and Watkins); connected in this vault to Human Performance Technology, Sensemaking and AI Adoption, and AI and Future of Work communities.
