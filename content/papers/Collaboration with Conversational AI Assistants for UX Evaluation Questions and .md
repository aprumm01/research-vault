## Summary
Explores whether conversational AI assistants can support UX evaluators analyzing usability test recordings by enabling a question-and-answer dynamic, using a Wizard-of-Oz design probe study with 20 participants interacting via text or voice. Finds five categories of evaluator questions and that text interaction generates more questions and is perceived as more efficient, while both modalities earn similar satisfaction and trust ratings.

## Key Concepts
- UX evaluation and usability testing
- Conversational AI assistants
- Wizard-of-Oz study design
- Human-AI collaboration in analysis
- Question taxonomy (user actions, mental model, AI help, product info, demographics)

## Theoretical Framework
Human-AI collaboration framework applied to UX analysis workflows; addresses the gap between AI-generated non-interactive visualizations and the interactive Q&A dynamic evaluators actually need.

## Methods
Wizard-of-Oz design probe; 20 participants interacted with simulated AI assistants via text or voice while analyzing usability recordings; qualitative coding of 5 question categories; satisfaction and trust surveys.

## Main Arguments
- UX evaluators ask questions across five categories: user actions, user mental model, AI help requests, product/task information, and user demographics
- Text-based interaction produces more questions than voice, though question length is similar across modalities
- Text assistants are perceived as significantly more efficient; both modalities are rated equally in satisfaction and trust
- Conversational AI could reduce the burden of solo analysis and improve completeness of usability evaluation

## Limitations & Critiques
Wizard-of-Oz methodology means findings reflect simulated rather than actual AI performance; 20 participants limits statistical power; lab setting may not reflect the time pressures and workflows of industry UX evaluation.
