## Summary
This study examines technological anxiety among art practitioners and students triggered by AI-generated content, analyzing anti-AI movements on platforms such as ArtStation and GitHub. It argues that anxiety centers on intellectual property disputes and fears of occupational substitution, and proposes strategies—including emphasizing core artistic values and improving educator AI proficiency—to alleviate that anxiety.

## Key Concepts
- Technological anxiety
- Intellectual property in generative AI
- Occupational substitution
- Compliance anxiety
- Core artistic values

## Theoretical Framework
Dialectical analysis from four perspectives (technology, legal principles, user perspectives, and historical context) is used to interrogate the rationality of anti-AI resistance and to surface underlying structural causes of anxiety.

## Methods
Qualitative; content analysis and case studies tracking social media movements (ArtStation, GitHub), prominent industry figures' remarks, and art school graduation exhibition reviews. No direct artist interviews.

## Main Arguments
- Technological anxiety in the art world is primarily driven by IP and job replacement fears that are partly irrational when examined dialectically
- Anti-AI movements contain legitimate concerns but also overcorrect against tools that could be integrated thoughtfully
- Core artistic values (emotional expression, craftsmanship) are not replaceable by AI and should anchor art education
- Teacher AI proficiency is a key leverage point for reducing student compliance anxiety

## Limitations & Critiques
The study draws on Chinese institutional contexts and publicly visible social media cases rather than direct artist interviews, which limits generalizability; proposed solutions are prescriptive rather than empirically tested.
