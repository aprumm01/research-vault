# Perceptions and Integration of Generative Artificial Intelligence in Creative Practices and Industries: A Scoping Review and Conceptual Model

## Summary
This scoping review synthesizes empirical research on how creative professionals across four domains—visual art and design, writing and literature, performing arts, and environmental and spatial design—perceive and integrate generative AI into their workflows. Following PRISMA-ScR guidelines, the authors analyzed 57 papers published between 2022–2025, identifying universal trends including the shift from creation to curation, emergence of new literacies (prompt engineering, AI evaluation), and career-stage differences in AI adoption. The paper contributes a conceptual framework positioning creative professionals' attitudes along two dimensions: knowledge codifiability and output materiality.

## Key Concepts
- **Creation-to-curation shift**: Across creative domains, professionals increasingly position themselves as curators and meta-creators who direct and refine AI outputs rather than producing work from scratch
- **Prompt engineering as new literacy**: The capacity to craft effective prompts that elicit desired AI outputs has emerged as a professional competency distinct from traditional creative skills
- **Knowledge codifiability dimension**: The degree to which creative knowledge can be made explicit versus remaining tacit and embodied; fields with more explicit/codifiable knowledge show higher AI integration
- **Output materiality dimension**: Whether creative outputs are physical/permanent (lower AI adoption) or digital/ephemeral (higher AI adoption), shaping the conditions under which AI integration is viable
- **Career-stage differentiation**: Entry-level professionals embrace AI as natural extension of digital tools while senior practitioners resist, fearing expertise devaluation
- **Erasure by obscurity**: Visual artists face a specific threat from AI's prolific output volume drowning out human-made work in search results and platforms
- **Conceptual vs. final production**: Across all domains, GenAI is predominantly positioned in early-stage conceptualization rather than final production phases, preserving human judgment for convergent creative work

## Theoretical Framework
The authors develop a conceptual framework built around two axes: knowledge codifiability (from tacit/embodied to explicit/codifiable) and output materiality (from physical/permanent to digital/ephemeral). This framework explains differential AI integration rates across creative fields: fine arts and literary fiction occupy the tacit/physical quadrant with highest resistance, while commercial graphic design and digital content occupy the explicit/digital quadrant with highest adoption.

The review also engages with sociological theories of creative professions, including debates about expertise, authorship, and cultural value. The concept of "co-creativity" is examined as a proposed framework for human-AI collaborative making, though the review notes that definitions of creativity remain contested across disciplines. The paper draws on Godin et al.'s sociological understanding of creativity as intentional configurations that deliver surprise for an audience, and Beaty's neuroscientific emphasis on idea generation and evaluation.

## Methods
Scoping review following PRISMA-ScR (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews) guidelines. 57 papers from 2022–2025 were analyzed from multiple databases, focusing primarily on empirically based studies of professional creative practice across four identified domains. The review was conducted by researchers from the University of Hong Kong and Chinese University of Hong Kong. The methodology was chosen over systematic review because the research aims involved mapping professional discussions and integration characteristics rather than evaluating intervention effectiveness.

## Main Arguments
- GenAI integration in creative industries follows an inverse relationship with traditional notions of "pure" creativity: commercially oriented domains embrace AI for efficiency while fields prioritizing embodied practice and cultural authenticity demonstrate significant resistance
- Career stage is a critical variable that institutional and policy responses to AI must account for: entry-level professionals see AI as natural progression while senior practitioners experience it as a threat to expertise hierarchies and professional identity
- All creative fields consistently confine GenAI to early-stage conceptualization rather than final production, suggesting a shared human judgment for convergent creative phases that may represent a stable human-AI division of labor
- The disconnect between educational emphasis on critique and industry's pragmatic adoption of AI creates a gap between what creative programmes teach students to do and what employers actually use AI for
- The conceptual framework's two-axis model explains observed variation better than single-variable explanations (cost, skill requirements, etc.) and provides a basis for predicting AI adoption trajectories in emerging creative domains
- Key tensions—productivity vs. meaningful engagement, volume vs. distinction, precision vs. serendipity, individual vs. collective intelligence—are not resolvable through technology alone and require deliberate professional and institutional navigation

## Limitations & Critiques
The 57-paper corpus, while systematically selected, may underrepresent Global South perspectives, non-English language scholarship, and creative practices outside the Western tradition. The rapid pace of AI development means that findings from 2022–2025 may be quickly outdated as tools improve. The focus on professional creative practice means that findings may not directly apply to student creative practice in educational settings.

The scoping review methodology, while appropriate for the research aims, precludes causal conclusions about the relationship between AI adoption and creative outcomes. The conceptual framework, though analytically useful, has not yet been empirically tested, and the operationalization of "knowledge codifiability" and "output materiality" as dimensions requires further refinement.
