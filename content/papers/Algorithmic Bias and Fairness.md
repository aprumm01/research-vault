# Algorithmic Bias and Fairness


## Summary
Algorithmic Bias and Fairness is a thematic node capturing research on how AI systems encode, amplify, or perpetuate social biases -- and what fairness means in practice for algorithmic decision-making. In this research vault, the node connects primarily to the responsible AI ethics community and the AI and Future of Work community, reflecting concerns about how design decisions in AI tools reproduce inequities in hiring, creative work valuation, and professional opportunity. The corpus includes papers examining how AI-generated content and recommendations reflect training data biases and how fairness interventions may themselves embed contested value judgments.

## Key Concepts
- Algorithmic bias: systematic errors producing unfair outcomes for certain groups
- Fairness definitions: statistical parity, equalized odds, individual vs. group fairness
- Training data bias and historical inequality reproduction
- Bias in generative AI outputs: image generation, text completion, code suggestions
- Accountability and transparency as prerequisites for fairness
- Fairness-accuracy tradeoffs in machine learning systems

## Theoretical Framework
Critical AI studies; fairness, accountability, and transparency (FAccT) research tradition; feminist and critical race theory applied to algorithmic systems; design ethics; connected in this vault to Responsible AI and Ethics, Responsible AI Governance, and AI and Future of Work nodes.
