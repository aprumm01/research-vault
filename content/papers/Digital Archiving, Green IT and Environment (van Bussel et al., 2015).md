# Summary

This paper proposes the **Green Archiving Model**, which integrates Green IT practices with Digital Archiving (DA) theories to address the environmental impact of exponential data growth. The authors argue that the "data deluge" (data storage doubling every 40 months) threatens both DA objectives and Green IT goals by consuming ever-increasing amounts of electricity for storage.

The model combines two archival science theories:
1. **Information Value Chain (IVC)** - manages data through generation, capture, storage, processing, distribution, and preservation
2. **Archival Retention Levels (ARLs)** - assigns retention periods based on economic, social, cultural, financial, administrative, fiscal, and legal value

The research demonstrates through case studies that organizations can reduce stored data by approximately 45% and electricity costs by 35% through systematic application of the Green Archiving Model.

# Key Concepts

## Data Deluge
- Data storage capacity doubles every 40 months
- Annual growth rate of approximately 40%
- Creates unprecedented pressure on ICT infrastructure and energy consumption

## Green IT Components
The paper identifies six components of Green IT:
1. **Product longevity** - extending hardware lifespan
2. **Software and deployment optimization** - virtualization, resource allocation
3. **Power management** - reducing idle consumption
4. **Materials recycling** - proper disposal and reuse of e-waste
5. **Telecommuting** - reducing travel-related emissions
6. **Low power IT** - energy-efficient hardware design

## Digital Archiving Dimensions
Four dimensions for managing organizational information:
1. **Quality** - ensuring "immutable mobiles" for consultation and reconstruction
2. **Context** - capturing metadata about organizational, technological, and societal environment
3. **Relevance** - fitting organizational objectives of performance and accountability
4. **Survival** - overcoming security and durability challenges for preservation

## Green Archiving Model
Combines Green IT components with archival retention theories:
- Uses ARL checklists to identify data that can be permanently deleted
- Applies retention schedules to systematically dispose of valueless data
- Integrates IVC to manage data throughout its lifecycle
- Aims to reduce storage capacity, power consumption, and environmental impact

# Key Findings

## Case Study Results
Three exploratory case studies in a multinational trade corporation revealed:

1. **IT Infrastructure Scan**: 35% of 18 TB headquarters storage was duplicate files; ARL checklists would significantly reduce storage capacity

2. **ARL Schedules Pilot**: Global storage of 45 TB could be reduced by 30% using ARL checklists; 37% was duplicate data; projected 25% reduction in electricity costs

3. **Chief Information Officer Analysis**: 10% (3.5 TB) of stored data could be disposed of immediately as retention periods had passed; only 5% of data needed retention beyond 20 years

## Quantified Benefits
- **45% reduction** in amount of stored data achievable using ARLs and Retention Schedules
- **35% reduction** in electricity costs for data storage
- Data deduplication alone can reduce storage needs by 20-30%

## Environmental Impact Statistics
- From 2000-2005, electricity consumption in data centers doubled
- 2005-2010: electricity consumption jumped 56%
- Each PC/laptop generates ~4 tons of CO2 annually; servers generate ~8 tons
- In 2006, global E-waste production was 20-50 million tonnes/year
- E-waste represents 8% of municipal waste in rich countries and is fastest growing waste stream

# Relevance to Sustainable Computing

This paper is highly relevant to sustainable computing research for several reasons:

1. **Intersection of disciplines**: Bridges archival science, information management, and environmental sustainability - demonstrating that sustainability solutions require interdisciplinary approaches

2. **Practical framework**: Provides an actionable model (Green Archiving) that organizations can implement to reduce both storage costs and environmental impact

3. **Quantified impact**: Offers concrete metrics (45% data reduction, 35% cost savings) that can inform sustainable ICT policy and practice

4. **Systemic perspective**: Addresses the often-overlooked environmental impact of data storage, which continues to grow exponentially

5. **Value-based deletion**: Proposes that sustainable computing isn't just about efficient storage but about critically evaluating what data needs to be stored at all

6. **Future research direction**: The authors envision "low power ICT that will automatically appraise, select, preserve or permanently delete data and records" - pointing toward automated sustainable data management

## Limitations
- Case studies provide provisional results requiring validation
- Fourth objective (reducing CO2 and E-waste) deferred to future research
- Model tested in limited organizational contexts
