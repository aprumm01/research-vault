# A Method to Evaluate the UX of Systems Integrating AI (Springer, 2024)

## Summary
Spallazzo, Sciannamè, and Ceconello (Politecnico di Milano, Springer 2024) present a framework for evaluating the user experience of AI-infused systems — products and services where AI is a functional component rather than the core purpose. Published as a SpringerBriefs volume in the PoliMI series, the book addresses a methodological gap: standard UX evaluation methods were not designed for systems whose behavior is probabilistic, adaptive, or opaque. The authors develop a systematic approach for assessing the distinctive qualities of AI-infused systems, including dimensions like explainability, perceived agency, and trust calibration that conventional usability metrics miss.

## Key Concepts
- UX evaluation frameworks for AI-infused systems
- Probabilistic and adaptive system behavior in UX
- Explainability and transparency as UX qualities
- Trust calibration in human-AI interaction
- AI-infused vs. AI-native systems distinction

## Theoretical Framework
User experience theory extended to account for AI-specific qualities; draws on HCI evaluation methodology and responsible AI design principles to define new assessment dimensions.

## Methods
Framework development / methodology paper (SpringerBriefs, 115 pages); likely combines literature review, case analysis, and proposed evaluation instrument. Published 2024 by Politecnico di Milano researchers.

## Main Arguments
- Conventional UX evaluation (usability testing, heuristic evaluation) is insufficient for systems with adaptive AI components
- AI-infused systems require evaluation of qualities like explainability, appropriate reliance, and agency perception — not just task efficiency
- A systematic UX evaluation method must address the gap between what AI systems do and what users understand them to be doing
- Practitioners need tools that surface sociotechnical mismatches before deployment, not just post-hoc usability problems

## Limitations & Critiques
Framework paper — empirical validation of the proposed method is not documented in the available excerpt. SpringerBriefs format (short monograph) limits the depth of case study evidence. Primarily European academic context.
