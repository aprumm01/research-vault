# A pedagogical study on promoting students deep learning through design-based learning

## Summary
This paper illustrates the design-based learning (DBL) approach to promoting the deep learning of students and improving the quality of teaching in engineering design education We performed three aspects of research with students in a typical educational activity The first study investigated students’ deep learning before and after the DBL approach, both in terms of deep learning status and deep learning ability.

## Key Concepts
- **Design-Based Learning (DBL)** - Learning model emphasizing scientific knowledge and professional skills acquisition through designing projects in real-life situations
- **Deep Learning** - Active cognitive processing involving critical thinking, making connections between old and new knowledge, and solving complex problems through deep information processing
- **Higher-Order Thinking** - Cognitive processes including critical thinking ability and problem-solving ability, developed through DBL approach
- **Backward Thinking Model** - DBL model derived from Bloom's taxonomy that begins with highest level reasoning rather than basic facts
- **3P Model (Presage-Process-Product)** - Framework measuring deep learning through motivation and strategy dimensions
- **21st Century Competencies** - Skills framework including critical thinking, problem solving, team cooperation, effective communication, learning to learn, and learning perseverance
- **Cognitive Dimensions** - Three main domains of deep learning competencies: cognitive, interpersonal, and personal
- **Iterative Design Process** - Cyclical learning approach where design solutions are revised and optimized multiple times

## Theoretical Framework
- **Bloom's Taxonomy** - Classification of cognitive learning from low to high: knowledge, comprehension, application, analysis, synthesis, and evaluation
- **Hewlett Foundation Deep Learning Framework** - Six competencies framework covering cognitive, interpersonal, and personal dimensions
- **National Research Council (NRC) Deep Learning Framework** - Three-dimensional competency framework: cognitive, interpersonal, and personal domains

## Methods
experimental

## Main Arguments
- DBL approach distinctively stimulates students' motivation to learn, making them more actively engaged in study
- Students' higher-order thinking capabilities are enhanced through DBL, particularly critical thinking and problem-solving abilities
- DBL incorporates five key characteristics: situational learning, design-centricity, integrative knowledge application, iterative processes, and reflective practice
- Three-stage DBL model (creating situation, designing scheme, evaluation and reflection) effectively promotes deep learning across cognitive, interpersonal, and personal dimensions
- Experimental results show significant improvements in learning motivation (3.46 to 4.03), learning input (3.67 to 3.97), and learning strategy (3.41 to 3.74) after DBL implementation
- DBL teaching model improves student academic performance, with experimental class achieving 25.0% A-level grades versus 16.3% in control class using traditional methods
- Students demonstrate high satisfaction with DBL approach, with 85.7% agreeing it promotes active and deep learning, and 82.1% finding it innovative and interesting

## Limitations & Critiques
- Study conducted over only two years (2019 and 2021) with limited sample size (49 control, 56 experimental students)
- Context-specific research limited to postgraduate students in one engineering design course at single Chinese university
- Effective communication ability showed relatively low improvement (3.22 to 3.60), indicating need for improved teaching strategies in this dimension
- Interaction between students from different majors presented challenges, with only 69.7% positive responses for "rich and efficient interaction"
- Low-performing students remained confined to their expertise without taking initiative to learn different professional content
- Study acknowledges barriers to collaboration between students from different design disciplines with different perspectives
- Research focused on specific library service design project, which may limit generalizability to other design contexts
- Did not specifically investigate emotional experiences during DBL activities or long-term retention of deep learning abilities
- No follow-up study to assess whether deep learning improvements persist beyond the course duration
- Limited discussion of implementation challenges and resource requirements for scaling DBL approach to larger class sizes
