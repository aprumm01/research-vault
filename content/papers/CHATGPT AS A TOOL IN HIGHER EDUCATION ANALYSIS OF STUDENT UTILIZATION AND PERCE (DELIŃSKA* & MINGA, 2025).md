# CHATGPT AS A TOOL IN HIGHER EDUCATION – ANALYSIS OF STUDENT UTILIZATION AND PERCEPTION

## Key Concepts
- **Generative AI in education**: Computational techniques creating new meaningful content (text, images, audio) based on training data, with ChatGPT as conversational agent using large language models (LLMs) trained on extensive internet datasets
- **Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)**: Framework positing that perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use determine technology acceptance, with factors like feedback quality, evaluation practices, and social norms affecting ChatGPT adoption intentions
- **Dual-edged nature of AI tools**: Benefits include quick information retrieval, writing assistance, efficiency, and personalized learning vs. risks including reliability concerns, negative impacts on creativity/critical thinking, academic integrity issues, and over-reliance
- **Academic integrity challenges**: Plagiarism and cheating facilitated by AI-generated text indistinguishable from human writing, raising contract cheating concerns and necessitating institutional policy development
- **Cultural and contextual moderators**: Personal morality, religion-based ethics, gender, age, and disciplinary contexts influence ChatGPT adoption and usage patterns across different educational settings
- **Turing Test achievement**: ChatGPT-4 (2024) confirmed to operate consistently with human behavior, exhibiting significantly more altruistic and cooperative behavior than humans in Stanford studies

## Theoretical Framework
The paper integrates multiple theoretical perspectives on technology adoption and AI in education:

1. **Technology Acceptance Model (TAM, Davis 1989)**: Primary framework examining how perceived usefulness and ease of use shape attitudes and behavioral intentions toward ChatGPT. Extended to include feedback quality, evaluation practices, and social norms as additional adoption factors

2. **AI transformation theory**: Positions AI as socio-economic change driver and frontier of computational advancements referencing human intelligence for complex decision-making (Berente et al., 2021). Emphasizes AI as process rather than phenomenon

3. **Educational integration framework**: Examines ChatGPT's role across academic tasks (information retrieval, writing, translation, summarization, research support) for both students and lecturers, considering administrative and research applications

4. **Ethical and pedagogical considerations**: Addresses dual nature of generative AI—enhancing learning processes through personalization and interaction while introducing challenges to critical thinking development, academic integrity, and emotional intelligence limitations

The theoretical contribution synthesizes technological capability perspectives with educational outcome concerns, emphasizing need for balanced understanding and structured implementation.

## Methods
Mixed-method approach combining literature review with empirical survey. CAWI (Computer-Assisted Web Interview) technique surveying N=260 full-time students (undergraduate and graduate) at Faculty of Management, University of Gdańsk, Poland. Data collection April-May 2024. Selective quota sampling by gender (66.9% female) and age (5% age 18, detailed age distribution provided). Survey examined: activities using ChatGPT, motivations for use, attitudes toward technology (trust, security, reliability), impacts on creativity/critical thinking, and educational needs. Analysis included demographic characteristics and usage pattern identification.

## Main Arguments
- **ChatGPT widely used for diverse academic tasks with mixed impacts**: Students primarily use ChatGPT for quick information retrieval, writing assistance, and idea refinement, valuing efficiency and potential quality improvements. However, concerns emerge regarding content reliability and possible negative effects on creativity and critical thinking skills

- **Technology Acceptance Model factors drive adoption**: Perceived usefulness, ease of use, feedback quality, evaluation practices, and social norms significantly influence positive attitudes and behavioral intentions. Cultural factors (morality, religion) and personal characteristics (gender, age) moderate usage patterns across different contexts

- **Academic integrity represents urgent challenge**: AI-generated text indistinguishable from human writing facilitates plagiarism and contract cheating, posing serious assessment validity concerns. Institutions urgently need to develop clear policies and guidelines for ethical AI use in academic work

- **Benefits span productivity, engagement, and learning enhancement**: ChatGPT improves productivity, communication skills, programming comprehension, and academic performance. Provides personalized tutoring, interactive learning opportunities, and support for understanding complex concepts, fostering engagement and satisfaction

- **Trust and limitations create adoption barriers**: Despite benefits, trust concerns arise from response biases, limited knowledge, lack of emotional intelligence, and incomplete understanding of complex tasks. These limitations affect student engagement and require transparent communication about tool capabilities

- **Structured educational programs necessary**: Results highlight need for institutions to develop structured programs supporting responsible and ethical AI use. Guidelines should balance innovation with integrity, provide clear usage boundaries, and integrate AI thoughtfully into curricula matching disciplinary needs

## Limitations & Critiques
- **Single institution and faculty limitation**: Study limited to Faculty of Management at University of Gdańsk, affecting generalizability across academic disciplines, institutional types, and cultural contexts. Different fields may exhibit distinct usage patterns and concerns

- **Temporal snapshot during rapid evolution**: April-May 2024 data collection captures moment in quickly evolving landscape; findings may become outdated as ChatGPT capabilities improve and user behaviors adapt. Topic "relatively new and rapidly evolving" per authors' acknowledgment

- **Lack of longitudinal perspective**: Cross-sectional design cannot track how acceptance and perception evolve over time, how initial concerns are addressed (or persist), or how usage patterns change with experience and skill development

- **Self-reported usage and perception**: Survey methodology relies on students' self-reports about their behaviors, motivations, and attitudes, potentially subject to social desirability bias, recall errors, or misrepresentation of actual usage

- **Limited demographic diversity**: Sample 66.9% female with specific age distribution; gender and age effects on ChatGPT adoption may not generalize. Missing information about socioeconomic status, prior technology experience, or academic performance levels

- **Missing comparative analysis**: Study doesn't compare ChatGPT users vs. non-users on actual academic performance metrics, critical thinking assessments, or creativity measures, limiting ability to empirically validate concerns about negative impacts

- **Institutional policy context unclear**: Results don't specify whether University of Gdańsk had formal ChatGPT policies during study period, making it difficult to interpret findings about ethical concerns and usage patterns in context of institutional guidance

- **Methodological details sparse**: Selective quota sampling procedure not fully described (quotas based on what characteristics?); survey instrument validity and reliability not reported; analysis techniques beyond descriptive statistics not specified

- **Literature review selectivity**: While synthesizing multiple studies (Table 1), review may exhibit selection bias toward English-language publications and particular geographic contexts (UAE, Peru, Saudi Arabia, Vietnam, Norway, UK, India, Oman, Philippines)

- **"Research gap" claim vague**: Paper states "notable research gap regarding long-term implications" but doesn't specify what long-term implications are most critical or propose specific research agenda for addressing gaps
