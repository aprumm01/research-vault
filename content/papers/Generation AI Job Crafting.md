# Generation AI: Job Crafting by Entry-Level Professionals

## Summary

This qualitative study conducted at a global consulting firm (pseudonym: InnovaWorld) examines how entry-level professionals (ELPs) use generative AI to craft their jobs. The research identifies three dimensions of job crafting: task crafting, relational crafting, and a novel dimension called "signal crafting" - the strategic management of when and how to disclose GenAI use. The study reveals how ELPs navigate tensions between efficiency, learning, and professional identity in an AI-augmented workplace.

## Key Concepts

- **Job crafting**: Proactive employee behaviors to redesign work tasks and relationships
- **Task crafting**: Using GenAI to modify work tasks (automation, augmentation, reallocation)
- **Relational crafting**: Changing interaction patterns with colleagues due to GenAI use
- **Signal crafting**: Strategic decisions about disclosing or concealing GenAI use (novel contribution)
- **Entry-level professional identity**: How junior employees construct professional identity with GenAI

## Theoretical Framework

- **Job Crafting Theory (Wrzesniewski & Dutton, 2001)**: Framework extended to include GenAI
- Extended to include "signal crafting" as new dimension specific to AI tool use
- Draws on professional identity literature

## Methods

- Qualitative interview study at global consulting firm
- 25+ interviews with entry-level professionals (analysts, associates)
- Snowball sampling within the organization
- Thematic analysis using iterative coding
- Member checking with participants

## Main Arguments

1. **Task crafting with GenAI**: ELPs use GenAI for drafting, research, coding, and analysis - shifting time to higher-value activities
2. **Relational crafting**: GenAI changes how ELPs interact with supervisors and peers; some reduce asking for help
3. **Signal crafting (new dimension)**: ELPs strategically manage visibility of GenAI use based on context:
   - Full disclosure: When GenAI use is normalized or celebrated
   - Selective disclosure: Mentioning GenAI for some tasks but not others
   - Concealment: Hiding GenAI use due to stigma or evaluation concerns
4. **Learning tension**: ELPs worry about skill development when GenAI handles foundational tasks
5. **Generational identity**: Being "Generation AI" shapes professional identity formation

## Limitations & Critiques

- Single organization (consulting) limits transferability
- Self-reported data may be affected by social desirability
- Rapidly evolving GenAI landscape may outpace findings
- Focus on knowledge work may not apply to other sectors
- Western corporate context may not generalize globally
