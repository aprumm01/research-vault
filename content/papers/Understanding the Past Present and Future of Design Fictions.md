# Understanding the Past, Present, and Future of Design Fictions

## Summary
IBM Research University of Colorado Cambridge, MA 02142, USA Boulder, CO 80309 USA.

## Key Concepts
- Design fictions as diverse practices spanning stories, artifacts, dramas, probes, and imaginary abstracts
- Historical roots in participatory design, future studies, scenarios, personas, and Theatre of the Oppressed
- Multiple authorship models ranging from researcher-created to stakeholder co-authorship
- Design fictions as both methods and first-class artifacts requiring evaluation criteria
- Political and epistemic commitments embedded in who creates and interprets design fictions
- Design fictions serving functional, aesthetic, pragmatic, and political communication purposes
- Inclusive taxonomy development for understanding design fiction diversity

## Theoretical Framework
Workshop summary drawing on critical design theory, speculative design, participatory design traditions, and feminist/post-colonial perspectives on authorship and knowledge production in design futures.

## Methods
CHI 2020 workshop proposal outlining agenda for community reflection on design fiction practices, including panels, breakout discussions, and collaborative guideline development for evaluating design fiction submissions.

## Main Arguments
- Design fiction has reached critical mass in HCI requiring reflection and agenda-setting rather than single definition
- Current diversity in design fiction practices stems from different political and epistemic commitments about authorship
- Historical lineages of design fiction extend far beyond commonly cited origins in Dunne and Raby or Sterling
- Evaluation criteria for design fiction submissions remain unclear due to diversity of forms and purposes
- Design fiction outcomes vary from fiction as collaborative outcome, to probe eliciting stakeholder reactions, to methods enabling stakeholder authorship
- Participatory design traditions contain proto-design-fiction practices predating contemporary terminology
- Guidelines for reviewers needed to strengthen design fiction as archival contribution genre

## Limitations & Critiques
- Workshop proposal format limits depth of analysis or resolution of raised questions
- Risk of workshop reinforcing CHI community boundaries rather than opening to broader design fiction traditions
- Limited engagement with science fiction studies scholarship that might inform HCI design fiction practices
- Focus on taxonomy development may privilege categorization over understanding situated practices
- Minimal discussion of power dynamics in who gets workshop access to shape design fiction discourse
- Does not address how design fiction evaluation criteria might exclude non-Western or non-academic practices
- Workshop structure favors verbal discussion potentially marginalizing alternative modes of design fiction engagement
- No clear mechanism for ensuring workshop outcomes reach beyond CHI community participants
