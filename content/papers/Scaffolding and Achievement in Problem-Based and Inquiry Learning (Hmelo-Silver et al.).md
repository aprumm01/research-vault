## Summary
Hmelo-Silver, Duncan, and Chinn respond to Kirschner, Sweller, and Clark's critique of minimally guided instruction, arguing that problem-based learning (PBL) and inquiry learning (IL) are not unguided discovery learning but are, in fact, highly scaffolded approaches. They present evidence that PBL and IL effectively reduce cognitive load through structured support while promoting deep understanding—contradicting the claim that these methods are inherently less effective than direct instruction.

## Key Concepts
- Problem-based learning (PBL)
- Inquiry learning (IL)
- Scaffolding
- Cognitive load
- Constructivist pedagogy

## Theoretical Framework
Cognitive load theory and scaffolding theory are central, deployed to show that well-designed PBL/IL provides appropriate cognitive support rather than leaving learners without guidance.

## Methods
Theoretical response and review of empirical literature; synthesis of evidence from PBL and IL research demonstrating learning outcomes and cognitive engagement.

## Main Arguments
- PBL and IL are not equivalent to unguided discovery learning; they employ extensive scaffolding
- Empirical evidence shows PBL and IL produce robust learning outcomes including conceptual understanding and transfer
- Kirschner et al. conflate minimally guided instruction with all constructivist approaches
- The scaffolding in PBL reduces cognitive overload while maintaining the motivational and conceptual benefits of authentic problem-solving
