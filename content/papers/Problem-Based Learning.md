## Summary
This work addresses problem-based learning (PBL) as a constructivist instructional approach in which learners are presented with authentic, ill-structured problems as the starting point for learning. PBL originated in medical education and has since been applied broadly across professional training contexts, developing students' problem-solving, self-directed learning, and collaborative skills through engagement with real-world scenarios rather than abstract didactic content.

## Key Concepts
- Problem-based learning (PBL)
- Authentic problems
- Self-directed learning
- Constructivist pedagogy
- Medical education origins

## Theoretical Framework
Constructivism and cognitive learning theory support PBL's premise that learning is most effective when situated in authentic problem contexts that activate prior knowledge and motivate inquiry.

## Main Arguments
- PBL develops problem-solving competency by embedding learning in authentic professional challenges
- Students in PBL develop self-directed learning habits that transfer to lifelong professional practice
- The tutor/facilitator role in PBL differs fundamentally from direct instruction, requiring facilitation skills
- PBL produces comparable or superior content knowledge alongside stronger process skills relative to traditional instruction
