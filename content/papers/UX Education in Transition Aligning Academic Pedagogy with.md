This study investigates the misalignment between User Experi- ence pedagogy and evolving industry practices, with a focus on accessible design. Two research questions guide the inquiry: how well current UX pedagogy prepares students for industry needs and what strategies can bridge existing gaps. Methods included a rhetorical analysis of relevant literature and a Fall 2024 survey of Technical and Professional Communication educators. Findings re- veal persistent definitional ambiguity, pedagogical misalignments, and limited academia-industry collaboration. Based on these re- sults, the paper proposes structural recommendations to formalize industry partnerships, recognize faculty engagement efforts, and build sustainable pipelines to enrich UX education. ## Summary
This paper examines the gap between academic UX education curricula and current industry practice as AI transforms UX work, arguing that UX education is in a moment of significant transition requiring deliberate curricular adaptation. Through surveys and interviews with UX educators and industry practitioners, the authors find substantial misalignment: industry increasingly expects AI fluency, adaptive skills, and judgment under uncertainty, while many UX programs still emphasize traditional research methods and static deliverable production. The paper proposes principles for redesigning UX education for an AI-transformed practice landscape.

## Key Concepts
- UX education-industry alignment
- AI fluency in UX curricula
- Adaptive competencies for AI-transformed UX practice
- Curriculum redesign principles for AI era
- Practitioner vs. academic UX knowledge gap

## Theoretical Framework
Design education reform; competency-based education; industry-education alignment research; draws on UX practice literature and higher education curriculum theory.

## Methods
Survey of UX educators and practitioners; in-depth interviews with program directors and industry hiring managers; comparative analysis of curriculum content vs. industry competency expectations.

## Main Arguments
- Current UX education significantly underrepresents AI-related competencies that industry now expects from graduates
- The gap is not just technical (knowing AI tools) but dispositional: industry needs adaptive, judgment-driven practitioners, not method-followers
- UX programs must shift from delivering knowledge to cultivating adaptability, critical thinking, and professional judgment in AI-uncertain contexts
- Industry-academic partnerships are essential for keeping UX education aligned with a practice landscape that is changing faster than curriculum cycles

## Limitations & Critiques
Survey-based alignment research captures stated rather than enacted competency needs; industry expectations themselves are rapidly evolving; the transition recommendations require significant curriculum redesign investment not all programs can make.
