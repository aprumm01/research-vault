## Theoretical Framework
- Practice theory - understanding work through lived experience
- Actor-network theory - workers, AI, and collaborative networks
- Technology appropriation frameworks
- Worker-centric innovation models

## Methods
- Systematic literature review
- Analysis of 23 ACM Digital Library studies
- Focus on practitioner experiences and practices
- Qualitative synthesis of worker perspectives

## Main Arguments
- Need for overarching understanding of worker-driven AI transformation patterns
- Workers actively shape how GenAI is integrated into their practices, not passive recipients
- Common patterns emerge across different contexts of GenAI use
- Lived experiences reveal gaps between design intentions and actual use
- Task restructuring occurs organically through worker experimentation
- Peer collaboration patterns shift when GenAI is introduced
- Worker-driven transformations differ from top-down AI implementation
- Understanding practitioner perspectives essential for successful AI adoption
- Bottom-up patterns provide insights for organizational AI strategies

## Limitations & Critiques
- Limited to 23 studies from single database (ACM Digital Library)
- May not capture full range of worker experiences across industries
- Literature review methodology dependent on what has been published
- Potential bias toward tech-savvy workers who publish in ACM venues
- Temporal limitations - early stage of GenAI adoption
- May not account for workers who resist or reject GenAI
- Limited longitudinal perspective on evolving practices
- Cross-cultural variations in worker practices not fully explored
