# User Experience Design Professionals’ Perceptions of Generative Artificial Intelligence

## Summary
1 Among creative professionals, Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) has sparked excitement over its capabilities and fear over unanticipated consequences How does GenAI impact User Experience Design (UXD) practice, and are fears warranted We interviewed 20 UX Designers, with diverse experience and across c.

## Key Concepts
- GenAI as assistive tool for experienced UX designers maintaining confidence in originality, creativity, and empathic skills
- Human factors of enjoyment and agency as uniquely human with humans remaining arbiters of AI alignment
- Differential impact by experience level with junior designers facing skill degradation, job replacement, and creativity exhaustion
- GenAI capabilities in summarizing briefs, providing best practices, labeling information priority, and offering UI inspiration
- Human-AI collaboration paradigm requiring copyright/ownership clarity, creativity preservation, and AI literacy access
- UX designers' workflows spanning research, ideation, design, prototyping, testing, and development across eight tool categories
- Participatory AI and value-sensitive design approaches for fostering human and creative safeguarding mechanisms
- Breadth-first investigation approach capturing wide range of perspectives across UX field and organizational contexts

## Theoretical Framework
- Hybrid deductive/inductive thematic analysis approach for qualitative data
- Affinity diagramming technique for surfacing sub-themes from statement cards
- Consensus-based validation through researcher workshops rather than statistical inter-rater reliability
- Trustworthiness criteria framework ensuring analytical rigor and credibility

## Methods
Semi-structured interviews with 20 UX designers (12 female, 8 male) across diverse experience levels (11 with >5 years, 2 with 3-5 years, 7 with <3 years) and company sizes (10 in enterprises >10,000 employees, 4 in startups <50 employees). Participants from Europe (Netherlands, Sweden, Romania, Hungary) and United States. 60-minute interviews April-July 2023 including GenAI tool demonstrations via YouTube videos. Five researchers conducted deductive coding producing 415 statement cards, followed by inductive coding and affinity diagramming yielding six major themes with 2-4 sub-themes each.

## Main Arguments
- Experienced designers view GenAI as assistive tool enhancing efficiency without threatening core creative and empathic competencies
- Human agency and enjoyment remain uniquely human factors positioning humans as ultimate decision-makers over AI output
- Junior designers face disproportionate risks including skill atrophy from over-reliance and creativity exhaustion from market saturation
- GenAI excels at summarization, pattern recognition, and aesthetic inspiration but cannot replace human judgment and contextual understanding
- Copyright and ownership ambiguity creates legal and ethical challenges requiring clearer frameworks for AI-generated content
- AI literacy and access inequities risk widening gaps between designers with resources and those without
- Responsible AI development requires participatory approaches incorporating UX practitioner voices in tool design and deployment
- Future of UX design depends on graceful adaptation balancing AI augmentation with preservation of human creativity and agency

## Limitations & Critiques
- Breadth-first approach sacrificing depth for comprehensive perspective without deep dive into specific tools or workflows
- Small sample size of startup participants (N=4) limiting understanding of unique resource constraints and team structures
- Participants watched tool demonstrations rather than hands-on usage potentially missing practical insights from direct experience
- Geographic limitation to Europe and North America restricting cultural and technological landscape generalizability
- Temporal snapshot from April-July 2023 missing rapid GenAI evolution and emergent tool developments
- Majority reliance on ChatGPT and Midjourney experience limiting insights about other GenAI tool categories
- Lack of longitudinal data on skill degradation concerns for junior designers requiring future follow-up studies
- Organizational practice variations between startups and enterprises not deeply explored despite structural differences
- Limited investigation of specific UX products, tools, or workflows preventing detailed use case analysis
- Consensus-based coding approach without statistical inter-rater reliability measures potentially introducing subjective interpretation
