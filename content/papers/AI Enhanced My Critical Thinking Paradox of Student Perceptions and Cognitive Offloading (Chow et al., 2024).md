# AI Enhanced My Critical Thinking: Investigating the Paradox of Student Perceptions and Cognitive Offloading in GenAI Use

## Summary
This mixed-methods study of 188 postgraduate students' authentic GenAI workflows reveals a paradox: students frequently claim AI enhances their critical thinking, yet their actual interaction patterns show widespread offloading of foundational cognitive tasks to AI. The authors introduce a Four-Tiered Cognitive Offloading Framework (4COF) and define a novel concept — "Epistemic Confinement" — where students experience an illusion of competence, believing they are thinking independently while operating entirely within AI-constructed analytical boundaries.

## Key Concepts
- **Four-Tiered Cognitive Offloading Framework (4COF)**: A novel analytical schema categorising cognitive tasks into Tier 1 (Foundational/Sense-Making), Tier 2 (Executive/Decision-Making), Tier 3 (Expressive/Articulation), and Tier 4 (Metacognitive/Self-Correction) — mapping where cognitive agency is retained versus offloaded
- **Epistemic Confinement**: A structural trap where students fully offload initial problem framing (Tier 1) to AI and subsequently fail to critically evaluate this AI-generated foundation; they draw final conclusions believing they made independent decisions, but these conclusions are constrained within an AI-constructed intellectual space
- **Illusion of Competence**: Students conflate the structural fluency and confident presentation of AI outputs with their own conceptual mastery, producing a metacognitive miscalibration
- **Efficiency Trap**: Despite students protecting their executive reasoning (Tier 2) to preserve a sense of intellectual agency, over 95% still fall into patterns of offloading foundational analysis — indicating that avoiding blatant outsourcing is insufficient protection against deep cognitive delegation
- **Metacognitive Miscalibration**: Students rate their critical engagement highly even when behavioural evidence shows they accepted AI-framed problem structures uncritically
- **Developmentally Staged AI Use**: The paper recommends intentionally restricting Tier 1 offloading for novices (to mandate foundational schema building) while scaffolding higher-order GenAI use for advanced learners
- **Gap Analysis Protocol**: The methodological bridge between qualitative self-reporting and quantitative behavioural mapping, anchored in Facione's Delphi consensus framework of critical thinking

## Theoretical Framework
The paper builds on Risko and Gilbert's (2016) cognitive offloading framework and extends Fan et al.'s (2025) metacognitive laziness concept to the specific context of higher-order academic tasks. It uses Facione's (1990) Delphi consensus definition of critical thinking (interpretation, analysis, evaluation, inference, explanation, self-regulation) as the benchmark against which student workflows are coded, enabling systematic comparison of claimed versus enacted critical thinking.

The 4COF framework is the paper's central theoretical contribution: it operationalises cognitive distribution across a four-tier hierarchy rather than treating offloading as binary. This allows the authors to distinguish students who offload only low-level tasks (potentially acceptable) from those who offload the foundational schema-building that enables meaningful higher-order engagement (epistemically risky). The concept of Epistemic Confinement draws on Koriat and Bjork's (2005) work on illusions of competence, applying it to the specific mechanism of AI-mediated problem framing.

## Methods
Sequential mixed-methods exploratory design. 188 authentic reflective essays were collected from postgraduate Information Systems students describing their self-directed GenAI workflows. A gap analysis protocol was developed to bridge qualitative self-reporting and quantitative behavioural mapping, anchored in Facione's Delphi framework. Student workflows were then coded against the 4COF taxonomy, enabling quantitative assessment of where cognitive agency was retained versus delegated across tiers. Thematic analysis was applied to the qualitative strand.

## Main Arguments
- Students strongly believe GenAI enhances their critical thinking, yet behavioural workflow analysis reveals that over 95% of the cohort fall into an "efficiency trap" — offloading foundational sense-making (Tier 1) to AI even while protecting their executive decision-making from overt delegation
- Approximately 17% of the cohort exhibit Epistemic Confinement: they delegate initial problem framing to AI and subsequently operate entirely within that AI-constructed intellectual space, yet experience this as independent high-level reasoning
- The paradox between perceived enhancement and actual engagement is best explained by the structural fluency of AI outputs — well-organised, confident AI text is mistaken for the product of one's own analytical clarity
- The pedagogical impact of GenAI is highly dependent on prior domain expertise: novice learners are most at risk of Epistemic Confinement because they lack the schemas needed to evaluate whether AI-generated frames are appropriate
- Process-oriented assessments and explicit metacognitive training are necessary to make students' cognitive delegation visible and addressable; binary AI use/no-use policies cannot address this nuanced risk
- The 4COF framework offers educators a shared vocabulary and empirical tool for diagnosing cognitive distribution in AI-assisted work, enabling more targeted pedagogical responses

## Limitations & Critiques
The study relies on self-reported reflective essays rather than direct observation of real-time AI interactions, introducing the possibility that described workflows do not accurately capture actual cognitive processes. The sample is limited to postgraduate Information Systems students at a single Australian university, which may not generalise across disciplines or degree levels. The 4COF framework, while theoretically coherent, requires further validation across different task types and domains. The concept of Epistemic Confinement is presented as a novel construct but is closely related to existing literature on anchoring bias and framing effects; the contribution's novelty relative to these traditions merits further discussion.
