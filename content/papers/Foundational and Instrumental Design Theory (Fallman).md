## Summary
Stolterman and Nelson's theoretical framework distinguishes between foundational design theory (explaining what design is) and instrumental design theory (prescribing how to design). They argue that design as a field has been dominated by instrumental approaches at the expense of foundational inquiry, producing a field that knows how to do design but struggles to articulate what design fundamentally is. The work calls for a more philosophically grounded design theory that can speak to design's nature, purpose, and relationship to human judgment.

## Key Concepts
- Foundational design theory (ontological/explanatory)
- Instrumental design theory (prescriptive/methodological)
- Design judgment and phronesis (practical wisdom)
- Ultimate particular (the specific, situated design outcome)
- Design as a synthetic, intentional human activity

## Theoretical Framework
Philosophy of design; draws on Aristotle's practical reasoning (phronesis), phenomenology, and pragmatism to argue that design involves judgment that cannot be fully systematized or codified into rules.

## Methods
Conceptual analysis and philosophical argumentation; no empirical component; synthesizes philosophy of practice, design theory, and systems theory.

## Main Arguments
- The dominance of instrumental design theory has obscured the fundamentally judgmental and value-laden nature of design
- Every design outcome is an "ultimate particular" — uniquely situated and not fully generalizable
- Foundational theory is needed to understand what makes design good, not just how to do it efficiently
- Design education must cultivate practical wisdom, not just method fluency

## Limitations & Critiques
Highly abstract and philosophical; critics note the framework may be difficult to operationalize in design education or practice; the distinction between foundational and instrumental may be less stable in practice than the theory suggests.
