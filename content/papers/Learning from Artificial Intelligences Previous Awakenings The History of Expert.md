# Learning from Artificial Intelligence’s Previous Awakenings The History of Expert Systems Brock, David C. 2018

## Summary
Articles Learning from Artificial Intelligence’s Previous Awakenings: The History of Expert Systems David C Brock n Much of the contemporary moment’s enthusiasms for and commercial interests in artificial intelligence, specificially machine learning, are prefigured in the experience of the artificial intelligence community concerned with expert systems in the 1970s and 1980s This essay is based on an invited panel on the history of expert systems at the AAAI-17 conference, featuring Ed Feigenbaum, Bruce Buchanan, Randall Davis, and Eric Horvitz.

## Key Concepts
- History of expert systems (1970s-1980s)
- AI "awakenings" and cycles
- Expert systems as precursor to modern machine learning
- Commercial interests in AI across eras
- AAAI-17 historical panel perspectives
- Knowledge engineering approaches
- Parallels between expert systems era and contemporary AI
- Lessons from previous AI enthusiasm cycles
- Technological continuity and discontinuity in AI

## Theoretical Framework
- History of technology
- Innovation cycles and hype curves
- Knowledge representation paradigms
- Comparative historical analysis
- Sociotechnical systems evolution

## Methods
- Historical analysis
- Expert panel discussion synthesis
- Oral history approach
- Comparative analysis across AI eras
- Archival research

## Main Arguments
- Contemporary AI enthusiasm and commercial interest prefigured in 1970s-80s expert systems
- History reveals cyclical patterns in AI development and adoption
- Expert systems era offers lessons for current machine learning wave
- Commercial pressures and expectations similar across AI eras
- Both periods saw inflated expectations followed by recalibration
- Technical approaches differ but sociotechnical patterns repeat
- Learning from expert systems history can inform current AI deployment
- Panel of AI pioneers (Feigenbaum, Buchanan, Davis, Horvitz) provides insider perspective
- Understanding previous "awakenings" helps contextualize current moment

## Limitations & Critiques
- Based on invited panel rather than comprehensive historical research
- May reflect retrospective bias from successful AI researchers
- Limited critical distance from subjects (pioneers reflecting on own work)
- Does not fully explore failed expert systems projects
- Technological differences between expert systems and ML may limit comparisons
- Commercial context of AI has shifted significantly since 1980s
- May overemphasize continuity at expense of genuine novelty in current AI
- Limited discussion of social and ethical dimensions across eras
- Geographical focus primarily on US AI development
