# Beyond Replacement or Augmentation: How Creative Workers Reconfigure Division of Labor with Generative AI

## Summary

This CSCW paper challenges the dominant "replacement vs. augmentation" framing of AI in creative work. Through 17 ethnomethodologically-informed interviews with international creative agency workers, Clarke and Joffe demonstrate that the reality is far more nuanced: workers continuously reconfigure their division of labor with AI through situated, reflexive practices.

The study introduces three key contributions: (1) the concept of "reflexive delegation" where workers interpretively configure AI as a type of workplace collaborator; (2) "boundary repair" where workers discursively adjust AI's role in response to breakdowns in trust or quality; and (3) "interpretive templatizing trust" where teams develop strategies to scale automation while maintaining stakeholder confidence.

## Key Concepts

- **Reflexive Delegation**: Creative workers craft AI collaborator personas by relying on typified roles tied to their unique workplace context and assumptions - the AI's role is continuously interpreted and reinterpreted

- **Boundary Repair**: When AI outputs cause problems (intelligibility issues, quality concerns), workers collaboratively adjust AI's job description to maintain trust and mutual understanding

- **Interpretive Templatizing Trust**: Teams develop templated approaches to using AI that help account for and manage tensions related to scaling automation from client stakeholder needs

## Methodology

Ethnomethodologically-informed semi-structured interviews with 17 creative agency workers across international contexts, analyzing how workers make sense of and configure their relationships with generative AI tools.

## Key Findings

1. **Beyond Binary Framing**: The replacement/augmentation dichotomy fails to capture how workers actually negotiate AI's role in ongoing, situated ways

2. **AI as Collaborator Persona**: Workers don't just use AI as a tool - they craft contextual "personas" for it based on workplace roles and expectations

3. **Continuous Reconfiguration**: Division of labor with AI is not set once but continuously renegotiated through daily practice

4. **Trust Management**: Teams develop strategies to maintain stakeholder trust while leveraging AI capabilities

## Relevance to AI and Design Education

This study offers important insights for design education:
- **Agency in AI Use**: Students can learn to reflexively configure AI roles rather than accepting fixed relationships
- **Professional Practice**: Understanding how practicing designers actually work with AI
- **Beyond Tool Metaphor**: AI as something more complex than a simple tool to be used or not
