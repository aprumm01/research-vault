# AI’s Impact on Diversity and Job Displacement in the ICT Industry

## Summary
Artificial intelligence's rapid adoption and technological advancement have impacted the Information Communication and Technology (ICT) industry These advancements also present challenges with workforce diversity and the displacement of jobs Organizations must address these disruptions since they amplify inequities that negatively impact the workforce.

## Key Concepts
- Artificial Intelligence types: ANI (narrow), AGI (general), super-intelligent AI
- Diversity deficit in ICT: Gender and racial underrepresentation in workforce
- Job displacement: Automation impact on workforce composition and roles
- Socio-technical systems: Interdependence of technical and social organizational aspects
- Change management: ADKAR model (Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement)
- Organizational effectiveness: Requires alignment of technical and social factors
- ICT industry: Software development, hardware manufacturing, telecommunications, data management
- Ethical AI practices: Bias mitigation, responsible adoption
- Reskilling and upskilling: Workforce adaptation to evolving AI landscape
- Fourth industrial revolution: Unprecedented technological advancement era

## Theoretical Framework
- Socio-Technical Systems Theory (STST): Organizational systems function effectively only when technical and social aspects treated as interdependent
- Change Management Theory: ADKAR model supporting people through organizational change
- Human-centered technology adoption: Emphasis on ethical, social implications alongside technical capabilities
- Organizational development framework: Aligning technical tools/processes with culture, structure, people
- Cascading effects principle: Changes in one system aspect affect other components

## Methods
survey

## Main Arguments
- AI presents dual nature: enabler of innovation and disruptor of traditional systems
- Findings reject null hypothesis: statistically significant relationship exists between AI integration and diversity
- Alternative hypothesis confirmed: relationship exists between AI integration and job displacement
- ICT industry faces persistent diversity deficit with slow progress despite stakeholder priorities
- Gender and racial disparities remain evident in ICT workforce composition
- Diverse teams yield increased creativity and improved decision-making
- AI adoption can lead to organizational restructuring and changes in job roles
- Policymakers and organizational leaders must proactively mitigate bias in AI systems
- Critical need for reskilling and upskilling programs for evolving workforce
- Public policy requires continuous improvement to address job displacement through workforce assessments
- Ethical AI practices and long-term monitoring strategies essential for successful integration
- STST model provides holistic framework capturing multifaceted nature of AI adoption impacts
- Organizations must navigate AI complexity to benefit both businesses and society
- Successful AI integration depends on aligning technical capabilities with social considerations

## Limitations & Critiques
- External validity concerns: replicability across different industries/contexts may yield different results
- Sample limited to ICT professionals in United States - findings may not generalize globally
- Different AI adoption rates, corporate cultures, legal frameworks across regions limit applicability
- Self-reporting bias: participants may inaccurately report experiences due to social desirability or memory issues
- Participant perception of AI changes as technology matures, affecting internal validity over time
- Privacy concerns and commercial sensitivity may compromise data availability and thoroughness
- Simple random sampling used but still potential for sampling bias if not representative
- Survey questions may be interpreted differently based on demographics, age, gender, background
- SurveyMonkey/Qualtrics platform limitations: participants may overlook questions, complex nuanced questions challenging
- Predefined response options may not capture full range of opinions, oversimplifying complex attitudes
- Cross-sectional data collection (60 days) limits ability to assess AI adoption changes over time or establish causation
- Independent variable (AI Adoption) yielded lower reliability (α = .393) suggesting questions need refinement
- Geographic and industry scope may not reflect broader global trends across different sectors
- Study limited to professionals with 2-3 years AI exposure during generative AI emergence
- Converting percentage scale/rating to Likert format presented survey structure challenges
