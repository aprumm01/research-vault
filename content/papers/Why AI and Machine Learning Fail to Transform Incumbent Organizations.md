# Why AI and Machine Learning Fail to Transform Incumbent Organizations


## Abstract
*Abstract not available — PDF may need OCR or manual entry*

## Summary
This paper examines why new AI and machine learning technologies frequently fail to transform incumbent organizations, analyzing the organizational, strategic, and cognitive factors that prevent established firms from capturing value from technological innovations they often helped create. Drawing on innovation management literature, the paper documents how incumbents' existing processes, capabilities, and business models create structural barriers to AI transformation even when they have access to the technology. The paper offers theoretical explanation for the often-observed pattern of disruption coming from outside rather than inside incumbent organizations.

## Key Concepts
- Incumbent inertia and AI transformation failure
- Organizational barriers to AI adoption
- Innovator's dilemma in AI context
- Capabilities and resources vs. AI transformation
- Disruption and AI in established industries

## Theoretical Framework
Innovation management; disruptive innovation theory (Christensen); organizational learning; draws on Tushman and Anderson's competency-destroying vs. competency-enhancing innovation distinction applied to AI.

## Methods
Theoretical analysis and case study examination; analysis of AI transformation patterns across industries; qualitative case comparison of incumbent vs. new entrant AI adoption.

## Main Arguments
- Incumbents fail to transform with AI not because of lack of access but because AI requires reconfiguring core processes central to current value proposition
- AI transformation is frequently competency-destroying for incumbents
- New entrants adopt AI more readily because they have no prior capabilities to cannibalize
- Successful incumbent AI transformation requires deliberate organizational learning investment and tolerance for internal disruption

## Limitations & Critiques
Case evidence is selective; incumbent/new entrant binary oversimplifies organizational diversity; framework may understate cases where incumbents successfully leveraged AI.
