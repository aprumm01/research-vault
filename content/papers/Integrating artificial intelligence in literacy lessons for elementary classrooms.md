# Integrating Artificial Intelligence in Literacy Lessons for Elementary Classrooms: A Co-Design Approach

## Summary
This study employs a co-design approach to develop AI-powered educational interventions for elementary-level literacy courses in Greek language classrooms. Working collaboratively with 25 in-service elementary teachers, researchers conducted a six-hour workshop where participants created lesson plans integrating multiple AI tools including ChatGPT/Gemini for text generation, Copilot/Ideogram/Muse.ai for image creation, and VideoGen/Fliki/InVideo for video production. The co-designed materials were then piloted with 62 students across four elementary classrooms (grades 1-6) in Cyprus.

The research addresses two questions: how teachers perceive their involvement in the co-design process, and to what extent students perceive AI as enhancing their learning experience. Focus group analysis revealed teachers valued the collaboration, ownership, and professional development opportunities of co-design, with 84% finding the process valuable for understanding AI classroom integration. Student survey results showed positive outcomes, with 71% finding the lesson enjoyable, 67.7% reporting increased interest in Greek language class, and 66.7% finding AI tools helpful for understanding texts. Students particularly enjoyed creating images with AI assistance (31 students), followed by text creation (20 students) and video creation (11 students).

The study demonstrates that co-design provides an effective methodology for developing AI-integrated lesson plans while empowering teachers through ownership and collaborative professional development.

## Key Concepts
- **Co-Design Approach**: A participatory methodology involving teachers and researchers collaboratively developing educational materials, ensuring innovations are tailored to classroom realities and teacher expertise
- **AI Literacy**: Understanding and ability to effectively use AI tools in educational contexts, important for both teachers and students
- **AI-Integrated Lesson Plans**: Structured educational materials that incorporate AI tools (text generation, image creation, video production) to enhance learning objectives
- **Teacher Ownership**: The sense of responsibility and investment teachers develop when involved in creating rather than merely implementing educational interventions

## Theoretical Framework
The study draws on participatory design principles and the co-design methodology tradition in educational technology. It positions teachers as experts on classroom context and student needs, while researchers provide AI knowledge and facilitation. The approach aligns with constructivist learning theory emphasizing active engagement, and recognizes that AI integration requires balancing innovation with ethical considerations and human interaction.

## Methods
- **Participants**: 25 elementary language teachers (22 female, 3 male, ages 29-53) and 62 students (26 boys, 36 girls, mean age 9)
- **Co-Design Workshop**: Six-hour session including presentations on AI in education, hands-on activities, collaborative lesson plan development, peer feedback, and reflection
- **Pilot Implementation**: AI-integrated lessons delivered in four classrooms across different grade levels
- **Data Collection**: Focus groups with teachers (qualitative), 5-point Likert scale questionnaires with students (quantitative)
- **Analysis**: Open coding of focus group transcripts following Saldana's approach; descriptive statistics for student surveys

## Main Arguments
- Co-design empowers teachers as stakeholders in AI integration, fostering ownership and commitment to implementation
- AI tools can enhance literacy education when thoughtfully integrated with clear learning objectives aligned to curriculum standards
- Elementary students respond positively to AI-enhanced lessons, particularly activities involving visual creation
- Professional development for AI integration should be collaborative and practice-oriented rather than passive training
- Early AI exposure helps students develop foundational digital literacy while teachers build confidence in emerging technologies

## Limitations & Critiques
The study acknowledges several limitations: the inclusion criteria requiring basic AI knowledge may have biased toward more technologically comfortable teachers; the six-hour workshop format limited time for deeper discussion and iteration; different classroom contexts (equipment, climate, student needs) made creating universally applicable lessons challenging; the study did not assess language learning outcomes before and after implementation; and the pilot involved a limited sample size. Future research should examine longitudinal impacts on both teacher practice and student learning achievement.
