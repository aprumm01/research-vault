# Design Capability in Social Innovation (Cao Ma Meng Tongji)

## Summary
Cao, Ma, and Meng investigate how design capabilities can be developed and applied in social innovation contexts — situations where design is used not to solve commercial product challenges but to address complex social problems, community needs, and public sector challenges. Drawing on case studies from Chinese social innovation contexts and Tongji University's design programs, the paper identifies distinctive capabilities that social innovation design requires and argues that conventional design education's commercial orientation leaves practitioners unprepared for the specific demands of designing for social change.

## Key Concepts
- Design capability for social innovation
- Social design vs. commercial design
- Co-design in community and public sector contexts
- Design education for social impact
- Systems-level design thinking

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on social innovation theory (Manzini), design thinking literature, and Chinese social policy context; frames social design capability as requiring additional dimensions beyond commercial design competence — community embeddedness, stakeholder facilitation, and systemic thinking.

## Methods
Case study research across social innovation design projects; analysis of design education programs at Tongji University with social innovation orientation; qualitative interviews with designers and community stakeholders.

## Main Arguments
- Social innovation requires design capabilities distinct from commercial product design: empathy at a community scale, political navigation, long time horizons, and willingness to work with distributed agency
- Chinese social context creates specific social innovation design challenges around institutional constraints, community organization, and hybrid public-private collaboration that global frameworks don't fully address
- Design education must explicitly cultivate social innovation capabilities — they do not automatically transfer from commercial design training
- Tongji's social design programs represent an emerging model for embedding social impact orientation into design education infrastructure

## Limitations
- China-specific context limits direct generalizability; social innovation design dynamics differ significantly across political and cultural systems
- Case study methodology limits causal claims about educational program effectiveness
