# Re-evaluating Creative Labor in the Age of AI: A Qualitative Case Study (Öztaş and Arda)

## Abstract

This article explores how the emergence of creative AI technologies transforms creative workers' self-apprehension in the context of critical theory and labor studies. The distinguishing contribution of this study resides in its focus on how creative industry laborers' creativity perception and self-understanding are altered when confronted with AI tools. Drawing on qualitative case study methods, the authors examine how workers in creative industries re-evaluate their labor, skills, and identity in light of AI's expanding capabilities.

## Summary

Öztaş and Arda bring a critical theory and labor studies perspective to the question of how creative AI transforms workers' understanding of their own creativity and labor value. Through qualitative case study methods, they find that AI confronts creative workers with a forced re-evaluation of what is distinctively human about their work — leading to shifts in professional self-conception that are more complex than either acceptance or resistance. Published in AI & SOCIETY, the paper contributes a critical labor studies voice to the predominantly techno-oriented discourse on creative AI.

## Key Concepts
- Creative labor and self-apprehension under AI
- Critical theory applied to technology and work
- Professional identity transformation
- Labor value re-evaluation in creative industries

## Theoretical Framework
Critical theory (Frankfurt School tradition) combined with labor studies; examines how creative AI forces workers to confront the commodification of creativity and re-evaluate their labor value; qualitative case study as methodology.

## Methods
Qualitative case study of creative industry workers; interviews or focus groups with participants in creative professions experiencing AI-driven workplace change; thematic analysis through critical theory lens.

## Main Arguments
- AI forces creative workers to re-evaluate what they understand as distinctively human about their creativity and labor
- This re-evaluation is not a simple acceptance or rejection of AI but a complex identity negotiation
- Critical theory reveals that AI-driven creativity discourse serves to commodify and devalue labor
- Creative workers' self-apprehension is a site of ideological struggle in the age of AI
