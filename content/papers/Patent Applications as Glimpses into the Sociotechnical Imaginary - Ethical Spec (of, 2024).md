# Patent Applications as Glimpses into the Sociotechnical Imaginary - Ethical Speculation on the Imagined Futures of Emotion AI for Mental Health Monitoring and Detection

## Summary
Patent Applications as Glimpses into the Sociotechnical Imaginary: Ethical Speculation on the Imagined Futures of Emotion AI for Mental Health Monitoring and Detection NADIA KARIZAT, University of Michigan, USA ALEXANDRA H VINSON, University of Michigan, USA SHOBITA PARTHASARATHY, University of Michigan, USA NAZANIN ANDALIBI, University of Michigan, USA Patent applications provide insight into how inventors imagine and legitimize uses of their imagined technologies; as part of this imagining they envision social worlds and produce sociotechnical imaginaries Examining sociotechnical imaginaries is important for emerging technologies in high-stakes contexts such as the case of emotion AI to address mental health care.

## Key Concepts
- Sociotechnical imaginaries: Collectively imagined forms of social life and order reflected in technological design
- Emotion AI: Technologies aiming to infer and/or influence individuals' emotions through affective computing
- Ethical speculation: Using imagination about technological futures to proactively anticipate harms
- Patent applications: Windows into inventors' imaginations and potential futures
- Data subjectification: How data subjects are produced and shaped by technologies
- Promissory futures: Visions of technological progress that shape expectations and actions

## Theoretical Framework
- Sociotechnical imaginaries (Jasanoff & Kim): How scientific/technological projects reflect social visions
- Ethical speculation lens: Anticipating implications of imagined futures to prevent harm
- Basic Emotion Theory (critiqued): Six basic emotions model used in most emotion AI systems
- Constructionism approach: Emotions as socially constructed and dynamic
- Science and Technology Studies (STS): Using patents to understand past, present, and future of technologies

## Methods
Qualitative analysis of U.S. emotion AI patent applications (N=58) describing technologies for monitoring/detecting emotions and mental health conditions

## Main Arguments
- Patent applications reveal how inventors legitimize emotion AI as solutions to data accuracy, care provision, patient-provider communication, emotion regulation, and preventing harms
- Imagined futures stigmatize mental health conditions and equate mental health with propensity for crime
- Patents frame individuals with mental health conditions as unpredictable and lacking agency
- Proposed solutions include intensive surveillance, individual responsibility emphasis, and decontextualized interventions
- Emotion AI promises are often "snake oil" - technologies claim capabilities that contradict established critiques of emotion detection
- Patent applications configure rights, responsibilities, and behaviors of users, shaping data subjectification
- Need for ethical speculation in patent review processes before harmful imaginaries become reality

## Limitations & Critiques
- Limited to U.S. patent applications (N=58), may not reflect global emotion AI development
- Patent applications represent intentions not implemented or successful technologies
- Analysis cannot determine which patents will be granted or lead to actual products
- Speculative ethics approach has inherent uncertainties about future outcomes
- May not capture full range of emotion AI mental health applications
- Focus on patents misses other sites of imagination (research papers, marketing, etc.)
- Does not examine patent outcomes or implementation trajectories
- Critique of Basic Emotion Theory well-established but patents continue using it
- Limited discussion of alternative regulatory interventions beyond patent review
- May overemphasize problematic imaginaries at expense of potentially beneficial applications
