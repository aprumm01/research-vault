# AI Failure Loops in Devalued Work the confluence of overconfidence in AI

## Summary
A growing body of literature has focused on understanding and addressing workplace AI design failures However, past work has largely overlooked the role of the devaluation of worker expertise in shaping the dynamics of AI development and deployment In this paper, we examine the case of feminized labor: a class of devalued occupations historically misnomered as “women’s work,” such as social work, K-12 teaching, and home healthcare.

## Key Concepts
- AI Failure Loops in feminized labor contexts
- Occupational devaluation and worker expertise undermining
- Overconfidence in AI capabilities vs. underconfidence in worker expertise
- Feminized labor: social work, K-12 teaching, home healthcare
- Emotional and care-oriented labor devaluation
- Situated, social, and tacit knowledge in work
- Negative feedback loops in AI design and deployment
- Expertise misunderstanding in AI tool development
- Pro-worker futures and labor-centered AI design

## Theoretical Framework
- Occupational devaluation theory
- Feminized labor and gendered work analysis
- Human-computer interaction and work practice studies
- Poor contextual fit and organizational mismatch frameworks
- Science and technology studies (STS) perspectives on labor and power

## Methods
- Focused review of academic and grey literature across three feminized occupations
- Comparative case analysis (social work, home healthcare, K-12 teaching)
- Grounded in team's direct empirical experiences (10+ years collaborative work)
- Field research observations, interviews with workers and leadership
- Iterative concept development through literature synthesis

## Main Arguments
- AI systems in feminized labor fail to complement situated, social, and tacit knowledge underpinning worker expertise
- AI Failure Loops emerge when worker devaluation leads to ineffective AI deployments, which further erode recognition of workers' skills
- Six interwoven failure modes: expertise misunderstanding, poor contextual fit, inadequate governance, misaligned values, flawed automation assumptions, worker exclusion from design
- Devalued workers especially vulnerable to flawed AI deployments resting on reductive understandings of their work
- AI deployments can further obscure and erode visibility of workers' expertise, deepening occupational devaluation
- Recent AI capabilities increases risk accelerating gross under-estimations of worker expertise and over-estimations of AI capabilities
- Current AI development practices sustained by persistent over-estimation of AI and under-recognition of worker complexity/nuance
- Workers in feminized occupations report their experience is misunderstood by those in more powerful decision-making roles

## Limitations & Critiques
- Workplace AI systems frequently fall short of meaningfully supporting workers despite promises of augmentation
- Social services: AI decision support tools withdrawn following frontline worker backlash
- Healthcare: clinicians ignore AI clinical decision-making tools
- Education: AI tutoring systems add to teacher workloads, compromise pedagogical autonomy despite promises to reduce labor
- AI systems fail to reflect complexity of frontline labor in feminized occupations
- Failed deployments create negative feedback loop that deepens occupational devaluation
- Limited worker influence over organizational decision-making perpetuates design failures
- Underexplored dimension: how occupational devaluation shapes AI design, evaluation, and deployment
- Gap in literature on how worker devaluation dynamics contribute to repeated workplace AI failures
- Need for more research on redirecting trajectory toward pro-worker futures
- Systemic issues require addressing power dynamics, not just technical improvements
