# Unlocking training transfer in the age of

## Summary
Park (University of North Carolina Wilmington) examines how AI will reshape training transfer—the application of learned knowledge and skills to the workplace—in an era of rapid technological change. The article is organized around three themes: integrating emerging AI technology in the workplace, strategies for effective and timely training transfer in dynamic environments, and future directions for training transfer research in the AI era. Park proposes AI mentors as a promising mechanism for supporting just-in-time, personalized transfer support, while acknowledging the theoretical and practical challenges of validating AI-mediated transfer.

## Key Concepts
- **Training transfer**: The application of knowledge and skills learned in training to job performance in the workplace
- **AI mentors**: AI systems that provide personalized, context-sensitive guidance and feedback to support skill transfer on the job
- **Just-in-time transfer support**: AI-enabled performance support delivered at the moment of need rather than in scheduled training
- **Rapidly changing work environments**: The challenge of designing for transfer when target job conditions are evolving faster than training cycles
- **Future directions in transfer research**: Questions the field must address as AI mediates both training and transfer

## Theoretical Framework
Training and development research tradition drawing on Baldwin & Ford's (1988) transfer model, situated cognition, and emerging human-AI interaction frameworks. Positions AI not just as a training delivery tool but as an active agent in the transfer process.

## Methods
Conceptual/review article; synthesizes training transfer research and proposes future directions rather than reporting primary empirical findings.

## Main Arguments
1. Traditional training transfer models were designed for stable job contexts; AI-driven change requires new frameworks for transfer to dynamic environments
2. AI mentors offer a scalable mechanism for post-training transfer support that traditional supervisor coaching cannot match in speed or personalization
3. Training researchers must study how AI mediates transfer, not just how it delivers training content
4. Transfer effectiveness in AI-augmented workplaces requires both technical readiness (tools, data) and human readiness (metacognition, AI literacy)

## Limitations
Conceptual paper proposing future directions rather than reporting empirical evidence for AI mentor effectiveness. AI mentor concept is promising but largely untested at the time of writing. Training transfer research historically struggles to isolate training effects from other organizational influences.
