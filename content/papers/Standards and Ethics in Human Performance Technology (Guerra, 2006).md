# Standards and Ethics in Human Performance Technology (Guerra, 2006)


## Summary
Ingrid J. Guerra (2006) provides a comprehensive treatment of standards and ethics as applied to Human Performance Technology (HPT) practice, appearing as Chapter 44 in a foundational HPT handbook. The chapter addresses the ethical dimensions of HPT work through a series of practitioner scenarios (should I tell the truth if it costs a client? should I report misconduct? should profit be the only criterion?), then examines the professional codes and standards that guide behavior. Guerra situates HPT ethics within the field's commitment to systemic, evidence-based performance improvement while acknowledging the value tensions that arise when performance consulting intersects with organizational power, client interests, and social responsibility.

## Key Concepts
- Ethics as practical guide to HPT professional decision-making
- Standards of practice vs. ethical codes in HPT
- Value tensions in performance consulting: truth-telling, client interests, social responsibility
- ISPI (International Society for Performance Improvement) standards and code of ethics
- HPT practitioner obligations to clients, organizations, participants, and society
- Systematic analysis vs. ethical opportunism in performance improvement work

## Theoretical Framework
Professional ethics applied to HPT; draws on ISPI's standards and code of ethics; situates ethics within performance technology's founding commitments to systematic analysis, evidence-based intervention, and results orientation; addresses ethical decision-making in practitioner contexts including consulting, training design, and organizational performance work; chapter in a Handbook of Human Performance Technology (Jossey-Bass, 2006).

## Methods
Conceptual and prescriptive chapter; draws on ISPI standards documents, HPT literature, and practitioner case scenarios; illustrative ethical dilemmas are used to ground abstract principles in recognizable practitioner contexts; no empirical data collection; intended for practicing HPT professionals and students.

## Main Arguments
- HPT practitioners face ethical dilemmas at every stage of the performance improvement process -- from whether to tell a client their requested solution is unsupported by evidence, to how to handle the privacy of data sources and the ethics of performance-based accountability
- Professional ethical codes (particularly ISPI's) provide a structured framework for navigating these dilemmas, covering obligations to clients, employers, professional peers, research participants, and broader society
- The commitment to systematic, evidence-based analysis in HPT is itself an ethical stance: recommending interventions without evidence, overpromising results, or subordinating analysis to client preferences are ethical failures, not merely methodological ones
- HPT professionals must balance competing loyalties -- to clients, to organizational participants, and to broader social values -- and codes of ethics help navigate conflicts among these competing obligations
- Profit and organizational performance metrics cannot be the sole guides for HPT work; practitioner ethics require attention to health, fairness, environmental responsibility, and human dignity

## Limitations & Critiques
As a textbook chapter in a practitioner handbook, the work is prescriptive rather than empirical. The ethical framework draws primarily on US-based professional associations (ISPI) and may not reflect global variations in professional norms, labor law, or organizational cultures. The 2006 publication predates AI-assisted performance analysis, algorithmic management, and other contemporary ethical challenges for HPT professionals. The treatment of ethical dilemmas uses illustrative scenarios rather than analyzed cases, which limits transferability to novel situations.
