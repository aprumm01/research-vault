## Abstract
Recent advancements in AI, particularly those focused on replicat- ing creativity, have

**DOI:** [10.1145/3706599.3719277](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706599.3719277)

## Summary
Jasmin el Kordy at Aalto University examines how generative AI is reshaping the role of designers, framing AI systems as blind algorithms that require human guidance and contextual steering. The extended abstract argues that designers are becoming algorithmic directors — navigating, curating, and directing AI outputs rather than directly creating artifacts. This shift raises questions about what design expertise means when production is delegated to AI systems.

## Key Concepts
- **Generative AI and design roles**: How AI tools are changing the fundamental nature of design work
- **Algorithmic direction**: Designers as curators and directors of AI outputs rather than direct creators
- **Designer as director**: New competency framing for the AI-mediated design workflow
- **Human-AI design collaboration**: The interdependence of human judgment and AI production

## Theoretical Framework
Design theory and human-computer interaction frame the analysis of how generative AI changes the fundamental nature of design work and the competencies required of designers.

## Methods
Conceptual analysis; extended abstract drawing on literature review and theoretical argument; presented at CHI EA 2025 (DOI: 10.1145/3706599.3719277).

## Main Arguments
1. Generative AI shifts designers from direct creators to directors and curators of algorithmic outputs
2. Designers must develop new competencies for steering, evaluating, and contextualizing AI-generated content
3. The blind nature of algorithms — lacking contextual understanding — makes human judgment essential for design quality
4. Design education must evolve to develop algorithmic direction skills alongside traditional design craft
