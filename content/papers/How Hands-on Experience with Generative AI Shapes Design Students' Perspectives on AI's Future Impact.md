# How Hands-on Experience with Generative AI Shapes Design Students' Perspectives on AI's Future Impact


## Summary
Ching-I Chen (National Taipei University of Technology) examines how hands-on experience with generative AI tools shapes industrial design students' attitudes and perceptions of AI's future impact, publishing in the AI-HCI 2025 proceedings (HCII 2025, Springer LNAI). A mixed-methods study of 32 industrial design students in Taiwan combined quantitative survey analysis (ANOVA, Spearman correlation) with thematic analysis of qualitative responses. Results show GenAI is most frequently used during the Ideate stage (87.5%) and least during Empathize (15.6%) and Test (21.9%). Students with higher AI experience perceived GenAI as having a significantly more positive future impact on the design industry, though experience level did not significantly change current attitudes toward using AI in the design process.

## Key Concepts
- Phase-differentiated GenAI adoption: Ideate (highest), Test and Empathize (lowest)
- Experience-differentiated optimism: high-experience users more positive about future impact
- Eight qualitative themes: creative inspiration, saving time, human-centered tool, risks and legal issues, problem identification, prompt engineering needs, future prospects, self-reflection
- Human-AI collaboration as preferred model among students
- Deskilling concerns and design homogenization as risks

## Theoretical Framework
Design Thinking five-phase framework (Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test); Technology Acceptance Model (TAM); situated in design education research and AI-HCI literature; published in LNAI 15821, Springer, as part of the 6th International Conference on Artificial Intelligence in HCI (AI-HCI 2025).

## Methods
Mixed-methods study; 32 industrial design students at National Taipei University of Technology, Taiwan; quantitative analysis using ANOVA and Spearman correlation to compare high-experience (n=9, daily or more than five times per week) vs. low-experience (n=23) groups; qualitative thematic analysis of open-ended responses with two independent coders (Cohen's Kappa = 0.605); 9-question questionnaire covering DT phase usage, attitudes, and future perspectives.

## Main Arguments
- GenAI adoption follows the DT stage structure: Ideate stage sees highest use (87.5%), with Define and Prototype at ~47%; Test and Empathize remain low (21.9% and 15.6%), likely due to their reliance on real user interaction and contextual understanding
- Higher GenAI experience correlates significantly with more positive perceptions of AI's future industry impact (p=.02), but not with current attitudes toward AI in the design process (p=.15)
- Students broadly view GenAI as a supporting tool rather than a replacement; human creativity, judgment, and ethics are seen as irreplaceable
- Key concerns include legal and intellectual property issues, potential deskilling, design homogenization, and over-reliance reducing independent thinking
- Prompt engineering literacy is identified as an emerging educational need; effective GenAI use requires learning to articulate design intentions precisely

## Limitations & Critiques
The sample is limited to 32 students at a single Taiwanese university studying industrial design, restricting geographic and disciplinary generalizability. The small high-experience group (n=9) limits statistical power. Self-reported survey data may not capture actual AI tool behaviors. The study was conducted at a specific moment in GenAI's development and may not reflect rapid tool evolution. The educational and cultural context of Taiwan may not generalize universally.
