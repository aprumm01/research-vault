# Re-evaluating Creative Labor in the Age of Artificial Intelligence (AI & Society, 2025)

## Summary
This 2025 AI & Society paper re-evaluates the concept of creative labor in the age of artificial intelligence, arguing that traditional economic and sociological frameworks for understanding creative work require fundamental revision. The author examines how generative AI challenges the constitutive features of creative labor — originality, skill, authentic expression — and argues that new frameworks must account for the hybrid nature of AI-augmented creative production. The paper draws on labor theory, aesthetics, and AI studies to propose a reconceptualization of creative value and creative work identity.

## Key Concepts
- Creative labor theory in AI context
- Originality and authenticity under AI augmentation
- Hybrid creative production (human + AI)
- Economic valuation of AI-mediated creative work
- Sociological reconceptualization of creative work

## Theoretical Framework
Labor theory (Marxist and post-Marxist); aesthetics; critical AI studies; draws on Becker's "Art Worlds," Lazzarato's immaterial labor concept, and contemporary AI studies to frame the reconceptualization.

## Methods
Theoretical analysis and conceptual argumentation; case analysis of creative industries (art, design, writing, music) under AI pressure; critical review of existing creative labor frameworks.

## Main Arguments
- AI disrupts the traditional bases of creative labor's value: originality is undermined if AI can generate endless variations; skill is devalued if AI can execute without craft development
- Creative workers are developing new value propositions centered on curation, direction, context, and human judgment rather than pure production
- Labor theory must be extended to account for AI as a new kind of productive force that does not fit existing categories of tool or worker
- The economic valuation of AI-mediated creative work is a contested and politically significant site requiring new frameworks and policy attention

## Limitations & Critiques
Primarily theoretical with limited empirical grounding in specific creative labor contexts; the reconceptualization remains at a high level of abstraction; the specific implications for policy and practice need further development.
