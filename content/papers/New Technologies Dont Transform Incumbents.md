# New Technologies Dont Transform Incumbents

## Summary
Why New Technologies Don't Transform Incumbents Sangeet Paul Choudary | Harvard Business Review | February 9, 2026 Overview of the Document Sangeet Paul Choudary is a C-level advisor on AI and platform strategy, a senior fellow at the University of California, Berkeley, and a four-time HBR 10 Must Reads author He received the 2025 Thinkers50 Strategy Award for his book Reshuffle, which focuses on the economic impact of AI That background matters here because Choudary is not writing from inside an academic research tradition.

## Key Concepts
- Unit of work: The basic object of production that an organization manages and hands off
- Coordination mechanism: The pattern by which work moves between people and teams
- Organizational architecture: Formal and informal structures determining how decisions are made and work is divided
- Architectural self-preservation: Resistance to change that protects existing power structures, not mere cultural inertia
- Decomposition of work: Breaking work into smaller pieces that can be recombined and coordinated continuously

## Theoretical Framework
- Coordination theory (Malone & Crowston, 1994): Managing dependencies between activities
- Organizational architecture: Structures determining decision-making, work division, and information flow
- Sociotechnical systems theory: Interdependence of social and technical subsystems in organizations
- HPT gap analysis: Performance gaps caused by structural issues, not behavioral ones

## Methods
Comparative case analysis (Adobe/Figma and Shein versus traditional fashion houses)

## Main Arguments
- Incumbents fail because they apply new technologies to old structures, optimizing efficiency rather than transforming coordination
- Transformation requires simultaneously changing the unit of work and the coordination mechanism
- Adobe treated files as units of work; Figma treated elements within files as units, enabling simultaneous multi-person editing
- Traditional fashion organized around seasons; Shein organized around individual testable product concepts
- Resistance to restructuring is architectural self-preservation, not cultural inertia - people resist because change redistributes influence
- Competitive advantage comes from restructuring enabled by technology, not from the technology itself

## Limitations & Critiques
- Based primarily on two case studies (Adobe/Figma and Shein), limited empirical breadth
- Not written from academic research tradition, lacks rigorous methodology discussion
- May oversimplify complex organizational change processes
- Does not fully address power dynamics and stakeholder interests in restructuring
- Limited discussion of implementation challenges and transition costs
- Success stories may not account for survivorship bias
- Temporal dimension - long-term sustainability of new structures not examined
- Cultural and industry-specific factors underexplored
- Does not address employee welfare implications of continuous restructuring
- May underestimate genuine cultural barriers beyond structural self-preservation
