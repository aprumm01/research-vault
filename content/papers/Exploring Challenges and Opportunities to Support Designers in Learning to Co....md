# Exploring Challenges and Opportunities to Support Designers in Learning to Co...

## Summary
This paper investigates the challenges designers face when learning to effectively co-create with AI systems, identifying specific knowledge, skill, and attitude barriers that prevent practitioners from developing productive human-AI collaboration. Through participatory research with designers at various skill levels, the authors develop a framework of support needs organized around three dimensions: technical understanding of AI (enough to set realistic expectations), creative skill in directing AI outputs, and reflective practice in evaluating AI contributions. The paper offers design implications for tools and educational interventions.

## Key Concepts
- Designer learning to co-create with AI
- Barriers to effective human-AI design collaboration
- AI direction skills (prompt engineering for design)
- Reflective practice in AI-assisted design
- Support framework for designer-AI collaboration

## Theoretical Framework
Participatory design research; learning to collaborate with AI; technology learning theory; draws on reflective practice (Schön) and human-AI teaming literature.

## Methods
Participatory research workshops with designers; qualitative data from collaborative AI design sessions; iterative framework development through analysis of designer difficulties and support needs.

## Main Arguments
- Designers frequently underestimate what they need to know about AI to direct it effectively, leading to frustration and superficial use
- Creative skill in directing AI is learnable but requires practice and reflection that most current tools do not scaffold
- The most significant barrier is not technical knowledge but developing a reflective practice around AI collaboration: when to accept, modify, or reject AI contributions
- Tools and educational programs should scaffold AI direction skills explicitly rather than assuming designers will discover effective approaches on their own

## Limitations & Critiques
Participatory sample may not represent the diversity of design practice; the framework needs validation in real-world professional contexts; the specific AI tools used in workshops may limit generalizability.
