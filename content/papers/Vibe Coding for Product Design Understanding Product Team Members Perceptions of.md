# Vibe Coding for Product Design Understanding Product Team Members Perceptions of AI-Assisted Design and Development

## Summary
Vibe Coding for Product Design: Understanding Product Team Members’ Perceptions of AI-Assisted Design and Development JIE LI, Cake Researcher, Netherlands YOUYANG HOU, Notion Labs, United States LAURA LIN, Collov AI, United States RUIHAO ZHU, Cornell University, United States HANCHENG CAO, Goizueta Business School, Emory University, United States ABDALLAH EL ALI, Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica Utrecht University, The Netherlands Generative AI is reshaping product design practices through “vibe coding”, where product team members express intent in natural language and AI translates it into functional prototypes and code Despite rapid adoption, little research has examined how vibe coding recon gures product development work"ows and collaboration.

## Key Concepts
- **Vibe coding**: Intent-driven workflow where natural language prompts are translated into functional prototypes and code through generative AI
- **Four-stage workflow**: Ideation → Generation → Debugging → Review (distinct from pure prompt-to-product)
- **Intending the right design vs designing the right intention**: Tension between efficiency-driven prototyping and reflective practice
- **AI over-reliance**: New asymmetries in trust, responsibility, and social stigma creating gaps between "AI-literate" and "AI-dependent" practitioners
- **Agentic coding**: Higher degree of autonomous AI involvement than traditional vibe coding
- **Product team members**: Cross-functional professionals including UX/UI designers, front-end engineers, product managers, and startup founders

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on human-AI collaboration theory and responsible AI frameworks. Extends prior work on AI-augmented creativity, workflow transformation, and distributed expertise. Frames vibe coding as a paradigm shift from deterministic to probabilistic intent mediation, where authorship shifts from code execution to intention-setting.

## Methods
- Semi-structured interviews with 22 product team members
- Participants from enterprises, startups, and academia across North America (n=12), Europe (n=8), Asia (n=2)
- 60-minute interviews conducted May-June 2025
- Thematic analysis of workflow integration, collaboration dynamics, and perceptions
- Diverse roles: UX designers, engineers, product managers, hybrid roles
- All had hands-on experience with AI coding agents (Cursor, Replit, Bolt, V0, Lovable)

## Main Arguments
- **Workflow transformation**: Vibe coding accelerates iteration, supports creativity, and lowers participation barriers but involves human-led debugging and verification (not pure automation)
- **Role blurring**: Traditional distinctions between designers, engineers, and product managers are dissolving as vibe coding becomes distributed responsibility
- **Deskilling concerns**: AI over-reliance erodes individual skills while creating new asymmetries in team dynamics and introducing social stigma
- **Dual educational potential**: Can be learning opportunity for those already skilled in coding, but risks creating dependency for others
- **Ownership redefinition**: Authorship tied to intention-setting rather than code execution, raising disclosure and accountability questions
- **Three key tensions**: (1) Efficiency vs reflection, (2) Trust asymmetries within teams, (3) Homogenization vs creative variation
- **Challenges identified**: Code unreliability, integration difficulties, AI over-reliance, erosion of critical thinking

## Limitations & Critiques
- Sample primarily captures early adopters who may be more optimistic or skilled with AI tools
- Interview methodology captures perceptions rather than observed workflow behaviors
- Limited longitudinal data on how practices evolve over time
- May not fully capture experiences of those who abandoned vibe coding tools
- Cross-sectional snapshot during rapid tool evolution (May-June 2025)
- Geographic concentration in North America and Europe with limited Asia representation
