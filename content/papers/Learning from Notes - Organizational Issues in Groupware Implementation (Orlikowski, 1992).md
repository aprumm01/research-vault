## Key Concepts
- Groupware: Collaborative computing tools including communication, coordination, and collaboration features
- Mental models: People's cognitive frameworks about technology and work
- Technological frames: Assumptions and expectations about technology use
- Organizational structure: Policies, norms, reward systems, and cultural properties
- Collaborative nature: Shared effort, cooperation, and collective value creation

## Theoretical Framework
- Organizational cognition: Mental models about technology and work practices
- Structural properties theory: How organizational policies, norms, and reward systems shape technology adoption
- Sociotechnical systems: Interaction between social structures and technical systems
- CSCW (Computer-Supported Cooperative Work): Framework for understanding collaborative technology

## Methods
Exploratory field study of Lotus Notes implementation in one office of a large organization (named Alpha)

## Main Arguments
- People's mental models and organizational structure/culture significantly influence groupware implementation and use
- Without mental models stressing collaborative nature, groupware is interpreted as familiar personal stand-alone technology (like spreadsheets)
- Where organizational culture lacks incentives or norms for cooperation/sharing, groupware alone cannot engender collaboration
- Counter-cultural premises (shared effort vs. competitive individualism) prevent effective collective use
- Technology interpretation depends on existing cognitive frameworks and structural properties
- Groupware becomes another medium for expressing existing organizational values rather than transforming them

## Limitations & Critiques
- Single case study (one office of Alpha organization) limits generalizability
- Lotus Notes specific findings may not apply to contemporary collaborative tools
- 2002 publication predates modern cloud-based collaboration platforms
- Limited discussion of how mental models might be changed through intervention
- Does not fully explore power dynamics in organizational technology adoption
- Temporal snapshot may not capture evolution of use over extended period
- Self-reported data about mental models and perceptions
- May underestimate potential for technology to eventually shift organizational culture
- Limited attention to individual differences in technology adoption
- Does not provide prescriptive guidance for successful groupware implementation
