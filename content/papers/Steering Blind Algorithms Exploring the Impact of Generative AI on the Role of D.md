# Steering Blind Algorithms Exploring the Impact of Generative AI on the Role of Designers

## Summary
CHI EA '25: Extended Abstracts of the CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems April 26 - May 1, 2025 Yokohama, Japan.

## Key Concepts
- Human-centered AI (HCAI): Designing AI to enhance rather than replace human capabilities
- Designer-AI collaboration: Three modes - creative support, tool, collaborator
- Problem-setting vs problem-solving: Defining goals and framing before technical expertise
- Abductive iterative approach: Discovering information through sketching and learning
- Relational expertise: Value manifests through interactions with stakeholders
- Goal communication challenge: AI needs explicit goals vs. designer's iterative refinement

## Theoretical Framework
- Schön's reflective practice: Critique of technical rationality, importance of problem-setting
- Design thinking: Abductive reasoning, iterative approach to ill-defined problems
- Human-centered design (HCD): Ensuring responsible AI development
- Participatory design: Involving end-users as contextual experts

## Methods
Qualitative semi-structured interviews with 17 strategic design professionals in Finland (Spring 2024). Participants included senior designers, heads of design teams across concept design, UX, user research, service design, business strategy, innovation, and AI implementation. Data analyzed using thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke)

## Main Arguments
- Designers confident AI won't replace strategic roles involving stakeholder engagement, problem identification, context-driven decisions
- Three types of AI interaction: creative support (ideation partner), tool (efficiency for established tasks), collaborator (interactive feedback)
- Designer expertise and critical reflection essential for leveraging AI creatively and responsibly
- Problem-oriented approach helps overcome AI hype - understanding underlying drivers before solutions
- HCD key to ethical and useful AI solutions - designers as spokespersons for human needs
- Interdisciplinary skills increasingly valuable for translating between stakeholders and technologists
- AI automates routine tasks, freeing time for complex strategic work perceived as more enjoyable
- True value lies not in artifacts but in generating concepts and ideas
- Contextual awareness, human interpretation, and relational expertise remain irreplaceable

## Limitations & Critiques
- Broad scope provides overview but lacks depth in specific areas
- Focus on senior-level strategic designers may not reflect perspectives of designers at other career stages
- Fast-evolving nature of AI technology means findings may quickly become dated
- Participants have varying understanding of AI's potential which may affect consistency
- Potential bias from participants' concerns about protecting their profession
- Geographic limitation to Finland may limit cultural generalizability
- Sample size of 17 participants limits statistical generalizability
- Self-reported data on AI usage may not reflect actual practices
- Study acknowledges but does not treat participants' personal AI opinions as pure limitation - values/experiences crucial for ethical AI development
