# Steering Blind Algorithms: Exploring the Impact of Generative AI on the Role of Designers

**DOI:** [10.1145/3715336.3735785](https://doi.org/10.1145/3715336.3735785)

## Summary
Qualitative interviews with 17 design professionals focused on strategic design work explore how generative AI tools are affecting their roles and practice. Finds that designers continue to provide irreplaceable value through contextual awareness, relational expertise, and problem-framing, even as AI takes on more execution tasks, and that the iterative, emergent nature of design work conflicts with AI's requirement for explicit goal definitions.

## Key Concepts
- Generative AI in strategic design
- Problem-framing vs. problem-solving
- Relational expertise
- Human-centered AI (HCAI)
- Designer autonomy and agency

## Theoretical Framework
Schön's reflective practitioner critique of technical rationality; Cross's abductive design search model; HCAI framework emphasizing human capabilities over replacement; positions problem-setting as the uniquely human precondition for design.

## Methods
Qualitative semi-structured interviews with 17 strategic design professionals at Aalto University; thematic analysis of perceptions, experiences, and role evolution expectations.

## Main Arguments
- Designers retain primary responsibility for problem-framing; AI handles execution but cannot set goals or navigate ambiguity
- Contextual awareness and relational expertise—built through interaction and experience—remain uniquely human contributions
- AI's requirement for explicit goal definitions conflicts with designers' iterative, emergent process of goal refinement
- Interdisciplinary collaboration and human-centered principles are more important, not less, in an AI-augmented design environment

## Limitations & Critiques
Extended abstract scope with 17 participants limits depth; focused specifically on strategic design professionals, not broader design practice; rapidly evolving AI capabilities may quickly date findings.
