# Method in their madness Explaining how designers think and act through the cognitive co-evolution model

## Summary
Method in their madness: Explaining how designers think and act through the cognitive co-evolution model Philip Cash, Northumbria University, UK Milene Gonçalves, Delft University of Technology (TU Delft), the Netherlands Kees Dorst, University of Technology Sydney, Australia Designers often face situations where the only way forward is through the exploration of possibilities However, there is a critical disconnect between understanding of how designer’s think and act in such situations We address this disconnect by proposing and testing (via protocol analysis) the cognitive coevolution model.

## Key Concepts
- **Cognitive Co-Evolution Model** - Unifies problem-solution space descriptions with cognitive explanations through metacognitive perceived uncertainty mediating knowledge, understanding, and external representations
- **Co-Evolutionary Transitions** - Four types: Problem-to-Problem (PeP), Problem-to-Solution (PeS), Solution-to-Problem (SeP), Solution-to-Solution (SeS) as basic units of design progression
- **Metacognitive Perceived Uncertainty** - Subjective feelings of confidence in knowledge about design task and context, driving monitoring and control of cognitive processes
- **Abductive Reasoning in Design** - "Thinking backwards" from desired VALUE to unknown WHAT (solution space) and HOW (problem space) through emergent exploration
- **Design Emergence** - Non-linear appearance of insights through both "becoming known" (finding solutions) and "bringing into being" (creating solutions) via memory-representation-cognition backtalk
- **Situated Cognition in Design** - Knowledge contextualized by wider understanding (implicit/explicit), with problem/solution elements fluidly switching roles as co-evolution progresses
- **Representation-Cognition Interplay** - External artifacts (sketches, prototypes, notes) linger in context, facilitating non-linear reflection connecting moment-by-moment and longer-term understanding development
- **Relative Criticality** - Extent to which idea outputs impact designer's understanding and generation of subsequent ideas, forming interconnected networks of varying influence

## Theoretical Framework
**Cognitive Co-Evolution Theory**: Integrates computational co-evolution metaphor with human cognitive/metacognitive mechanisms at second-by-second scale. Four-challenge framework addresses: (1) Scale—grounding multi-scale processes in individual cognition, (2) Context—conceptualizing problem/solution spaces as knowledge relative to wider understanding vs. algorithmic state spaces, (3) Direction—explaining transitions via metacognitive monitoring/control vs. a-contextual evaluation, (4) Emergence—accounting for non-linearity through representation-memory-imagination interactions. Unifies behavioral description with cognitive explanation through perceived uncertainty as mediating construct.

## Methods
Protocol analysis reanalysis of Gonçalves et al. (2016) dataset. 31 novice designers (Master students, avg 24yo, 5yrs design education, minimal professional experience) working individually on 45-min think-aloud sketching sessions for children's sleep product design brief. Three conditions: no stimuli (n=10), limited stimuli (n=11), unlimited stimuli (n=10). Analysis focused on 30-min divergent phase. Coded 1617 transcript segments for co-evolutionary transitions (theory-driven segmentation), perceived uncertainty (hedge word counts from Cash & Kreye 2018 dictionary), and relative criticality of idea outputs. Quantitative hypothesis testing using correlational and predictive statistics.

## Main Arguments
- Current co-evolution models describe emergent problem-solution space interactions but lack cognitive explanations for how, when, and what indicates good processes, creating appearance of trial-and-error despite structured underlying thinking
- Metacognitive perceived uncertainty provides missing link between co-evolutionary descriptions and cognitive explanations by connecting knowledge progression, transition direction, and non-linear emergence
- Four critical challenges (scale, context, direction, emergence) in co-evolution theory require explicit grounding in human cognition rather than computational metaphors borrowed from biology
- Co-evolutionary transitions distinguishable by perceived uncertainty levels (H1a supported), with uncertainty changes predicting transition changes (H1b supported)
- Idea output creation correlated with and predicted by specific patterns of perceived uncertainty and transition types (H2a, H2b supported)
- Criticality of idea outputs predictable from perceived uncertainty and transitions (H3 tentatively supported), connecting moment-by-moment cognition to process-level outcomes
- Design cognition characterized by fluid switching where problem elements become solution elements and vice versa, mediated by designer's evolving understanding contextualizing all knowledge
- External representations (sketches, notes) critical for non-linearity by facilitating back-talk between cognition, memory, and imagination across different temporal scales

## Limitations & Critiques
- Homogeneous sample (31 novice Master students, single institution) severely limits generalizability to professional designers, cross-cultural contexts, or varying expertise levels
- Reanalysis of existing dataset precludes control over experimental design; study not originally designed to test cognitive co-evolution hypotheses
- 45-minute individual ideation sessions may not reflect realistic collaborative, multi-day, or iterative design work; artificiality of think-aloud protocol
- Perceived uncertainty operationalized solely through hedge words; syntactical approach may miss non-verbal uncertainty expressions (pauses, tone, gestures) or misclassify context-dependent language
- Relative criticality drawn from Gonçalves & Cash (2021) requires additional validation; construct validity for "impact on understanding" not independently established in current study
- Children's sleep product brief provides single domain/problem type; unclear if findings transfer to ill-defined problems, wicked problems, service design, or systems-level challenges
- Model tested at second-by-second cognitive scale but doesn't explain how findings aggregate to team-level, project-level, or organizational design processes
- Statistical validation focused on internal coherence; external/ecological validity requires future field studies with practicing designers
- Protocol analysis coding requires substantial interpretation despite syntactical dictionary; inter-rater reliability not reported for perceived uncertainty or transition coding
- Doesn't address how motivational, emotional, or socio-cultural factors interact with metacognitive perceived uncertainty to shape design cognition
- Three stimuli conditions not fully exploited in analysis; unclear how external information access moderates cognitive co-evolution patterns
