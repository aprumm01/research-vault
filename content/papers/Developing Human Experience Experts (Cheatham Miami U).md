## Summary
Dennis Cheatham presents a process-centric framework for design education in response to generative AI, arguing that as AI takes over production tasks, designers must develop as "human experience experts" capable of understanding complex human needs. Built on four theoretical foundations—systems thinking, phenomenology, activity theory, and futures studies—the framework is illustrated through course implementations at Miami University (2023–2025), demonstrating how process-oriented pedagogy cultivates judgment over technical skill.

## Key Concepts
- Human experience experts
- Process-centric design education
- Systems thinking
- Phenomenology in design
- Generative AI and design pedagogy

## Theoretical Framework
Activity theory, phenomenology, futures studies, and systems thinking are synthesized into a four-pillar educational framework that foregrounds experiential and reflective dimensions of design work.

## Methods
Design case study; reflective analysis of course implementations at Miami University; includes specific pedagogical interventions such as ripple effect diagrams and specifications grading.

## Main Arguments
- AI's growing capacity for production tasks shifts the designer's value toward deep human understanding and empathy
- Process-centric education develops graduates who can navigate ambiguity that AI cannot resolve
- Systems thinking and futures studies prepare designers for complex sociotechnical contexts
- Phenomenological approaches help students develop sensitivity to the lived experiences of end users
