# Developing Human Experience Experts (Cheatham Miami U)

**DOI:** [10.1007/978-3-031-93233-5_3](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-93233-5_3)

## Summary
Cheatham's work examines how design education programs can develop what she terms "Human Experience (HX) experts" — designers who integrate deep technical competence with empathic, human-centered sensibility and ethical awareness. The paper argues that HX expertise requires pedagogical models that go beyond skill-based training to cultivate reflective practitioners capable of navigating complex sociotechnical contexts. Curricular design implications are drawn for programs preparing the next generation of designers and UX professionals.

## Key Concepts
- Human Experience (HX) expertise
- Empathic design competence
- Reflective practitioner (Schön)
- Sociotechnical competency
- Design curriculum design

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on Schön's reflective practitioner model, Dreyfus skill acquisition stages, and human-centered design philosophy; positions HX expertise as a holistic developmental trajectory rather than a set of discrete skills.

## Methods
Curricular analysis and conceptual framework development; draws on program design experiences and student learning outcome data at Miami University's design programs.

## Main Arguments
- Traditional design education emphasizes artifact production and tool mastery at the expense of developing practitioners' capacity to reason about human experience in context
- HX expertise requires integrating emotional intelligence, systems thinking, and ethical reasoning alongside technical craft
- Studio pedagogy must create authentic, reflective experiences — not just project delivery — to develop this fuller professional capacity
- Industry partnerships and real-world complexity are essential to developing HX expertise at appropriate levels of challenge

## Limitations
- Framework is institution-specific and may require adaptation across different program sizes, resources, and student populations
- Assessment of holistic HX expertise development remains methodologically challenging
