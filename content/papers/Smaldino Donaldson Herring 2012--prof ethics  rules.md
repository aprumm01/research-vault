# Smaldino Donaldson Herring 2012--prof ethics & rules

## Summary
This chapter explores the AECT (Association for Educational Communications and Technology) Code of Professional Ethics that governs professional behavior in the field of instructional design and technology. The code provides guidelines and rules for ethical practice, with ethics included as essential element in the current AECT definition: "Educational technology is the study and ethical practice of facilitating learning and improving performance."

## Key Concepts
- Professional code of ethics as organizational guidelines
- AECT standing ethics committee with power to censure, suspend, or expel members
- Three categories: commitment to individual, society, and profession
- Diversity in learning and instructional design
- Technology and resources selection with health/safety concerns
- Personal rights and privacy
- Professional responsibility to advance the profession

## Theoretical Framework
AECT Definition and field of educational/instructional technology; Professional ethics framework

## Methods
Conceptual analysis of AECT Code of Professional Ethics organized into three sections with specific indicators

## Main Arguments
- Ethical practice essential to professional success in instructional technology
- AECT has been leader since inception in establishing code members expected to adhere to
- IT professionals' responsibility to ensure actions serve profession in practical manner
- Commitment to Individual includes: diversity (cultural influences, various learning paths, avoiding stereotypes), technology/resources (selection, implementation, evaluation, health/safety, ergonomics), personal rights (privacy, integrity, not infringing on others' rights)
- Commitment to Society includes: honest representation of activities/experience, clear attribution of sources, avoiding personal gain at employer/association expense, promoting effective technology use with articulated outcomes
- Commitment to Profession includes: proactive responsible membership, contributing to understanding of ethical practice, appropriate professional interactions, reporting inappropriate behaviors to Ethics Committee
- IT professionals have obligation to make contributions to overall understanding of ethical practice

## Limitations & Critiques
[This appears to be a professional guidelines chapter rather than an empirical research study, so traditional research limitations do not apply. However, implicit challenges include: the difficulty of enforcing ethical codes, the uncomfortable responsibility of reporting colleague violations, balancing personal and professional activities, navigating complex situations where ethical guidelines may conflict with institutional or personal interests]
