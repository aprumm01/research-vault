# Designing Job Characteristics for Hybrid Intelligence (Yang and Jiang)


## Abstract

**DOI:** [10.1108/pr-03-2024-0229](https://doi.org/10.1108/pr-03-2024-0229)
PurposeThe fusion of human intelligence with artificial intelligence is a promising approach for addressing complex challenges, giving rise to the concept of hybrid intelligence. However, principles for guiding the design and management of hybrid intelligence are lacking. This study addresses this gap by exploring the key characteristics of job positions necessary for effective hybrid intelligence. Drawing upon the job characteristic theory (JCT), we identify key job features relevant to hybrid intelligence work, elucidating the challenges and considerations inherent in this domain.Design/methodology/approachThe study conducted 23 semi-structured interviews with middle-level managers from ten service companies in China, exploring their experiences with human–AI collaboration within their organisations. The analysis employed both inductive and deductive methods. Inductive coding was used to identify emerging themes from the participants’ responses, while the deductive approach ensured that these themes aligned with the research questions and Hackman and Oldham’s JCT.FindingsThe results contribute to our understanding of the job positions required for successful hybrid intelligence implementation, offering both theoretical and practical perspectives on job design and JCT applications in the era of intelligent technologies.Originality/valueOur findings illuminate the critical job characteristics essential for integrating hybrid intelligence into organisational systems, highlighting the evolution of job roles and the implications for effective job design in the intelligent era. These insights inform the ongoing discourse on job design and adaptation in the face of rapidly evolving technological advancements, guiding organisations seeking to harness the full potential of hybrid intelligence.

## Summary
Yang and Jiang apply Hackman and Oldham's Job Characteristics Theory (JCT) to the emerging context of hybrid intelligence — human-AI collaboration in organizational work — asking what job characteristics enable effective integration of AI into workers' roles. Drawing on 23 semi-structured interviews with middle managers in Chinese service companies, they identify how JCT dimensions (skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy, feedback) are reconfigured in hybrid intelligence contexts, offering both theoretical extensions of JCT and practical principles for job design in AI-integrated organizations.

## Key Concepts
- Hybrid intelligence (human-AI collaboration)
- Job Characteristics Theory (Hackman and Oldham 1975)
- Skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy, feedback
- Job design for AI-integrated work
- Human-AI task allocation

## Theoretical Framework
Extends Hackman and Oldham's JCT to hybrid intelligence; uses both inductive (emergent themes from interviews) and deductive (JCT framework) coding; frames effective hybrid intelligence as a job design challenge, not merely a technology implementation challenge.

## Methods
Qualitative; 23 semi-structured interviews with middle-level managers from 10 Chinese service companies with direct experience managing human-AI collaboration; thematic analysis combining inductive and deductive coding.

## Main Arguments
- JCT dimensions are all relevant to hybrid intelligence work but their content changes: skill variety now includes AI management skills; autonomy involves deciding when to delegate to AI vs. retain human judgment
- Task identity is challenged when AI handles component subtasks — workers may lose sense of whole-task ownership and meaningful contribution
- Feedback loops must be redesigned when AI is in the loop — workers need feedback on how their AI collaboration choices affect outcomes, not just task outcomes
- Effective job design for hybrid intelligence requires giving workers genuine decision authority over human-AI task allocation

## Limitations
- Chinese service company context; managerial perspective excludes frontline worker experiences; may not generalize across industries or cultures
- JCT was designed for individual jobs; hybrid intelligence creates team-level and system-level dynamics JCT does not capture
