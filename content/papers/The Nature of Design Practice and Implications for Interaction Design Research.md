# The Nature of Design Practice and Implications for Interaction Design Research

## Summary
desired results when used in practice An excellent overview and formulation of this critique is found in Rogers (2004) Rogers presents a thorough analy.

## Key Concepts
- Design complexity as subjective experience differs fundamentally from scientific complexity
- Ultimate particular as core design concept versus universal truth in science
- Designerly way of thinking and acting as distinct intellectual tradition
- Prepared-for-action versus guided-in-action distinction in design education and tools
- Design judgment and repertoire development through reflection-in-action and reflection-on-action
- Rationality resonance between proposed and existing design practice
- Wicked problems and messy situations requiring non-scientific approaches
- Design rigor and discipline distinct from scientific methodological rigor
- Artifacts as theories in design argumentation

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on Schön's reflective practice, Rittel and Webber's wicked problems, Simon's bounded rationality, and pragmatist philosophy to argue design requires distinct intellectual foundations separate from scientific methods.

## Methods
Theoretical perspective paper analyzing Rogers' empirical study of HCI practitioner tool use, combined with philosophical argumentation comparing scientific and design approaches to complexity.

## Main Arguments
- HCI research aimed at supporting practice fails because it lacks sufficient understanding of design practice nature
- Design complexity deals with creating ultimate particulars versus science seeking universal truths about existing reality
- Scientific methods are fundamentally incommensurable with design practice needs despite common misconceptions
- Designers successfully handle complexity through designerly approaches including sketching, parallel alternatives, and iterative whole-details exploration
- Design education should prepare designers for action through repertoire building rather than prescribing step-by-step processes
- Interaction design research must be grounded in design theory and philosophy literature rather than borrowing scientific approaches
- Design rigor exists through judgment, argumentation with artifacts as evidence, and process awareness for specific contexts
- Successful design tools are precise, simple, non-prescriptive frameworks that support designer incorporation into situated practice

## Limitations & Critiques
- Relies heavily on single empirical study by Rogers to support broad claims about practice
- Creates potentially false dichotomy between science and design without acknowledging productive overlaps
- Limited discussion of how design science approaches might be appropriate for certain interaction design contexts
- Assumes universal nature of designerly approach without accounting for cultural differences in design traditions
- Does not address how novice designers might benefit from more structured guidance before developing judgment
- Minimal engagement with design methods that successfully bridge scientific and designerly approaches
- Risk of romanticizing intuition and judgment without sufficient attention to systematic biases
- Lacks concrete guidance for researchers on implementing recommendations beyond reading design theory
