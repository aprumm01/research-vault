# Teaching to Fail: Premortem as a Pedagogical Strategy in HCI Education

## Summary

This EduCHI 2025 paper introduces the premortem technique as a pedagogical strategy for HCI education. Drawing from project management and decision science, the authors adapt the premortem method to help students anticipate and prevent design failures before they occur. The paper argues that teaching students to systematically imagine failure scenarios improves design quality and develops critical thinking skills.

## Key Concepts

- **Premortem Analysis**: Technique assuming a project has failed and working backward to identify causes
- **Prospective Hindsight**: Imagining future outcomes to inform present decisions
- **Design Failure**: When designs do not meet user needs or cause unintended harm
- **Critical Reflection**: Systematic examination of assumptions and decisions
- **Risk Anticipation**: Identifying potential problems before they materialize

## Theoretical Framework

The paper integrates:

1. **Prospective Hindsight Theory** (Mitchell et al.): Future-oriented thinking for better decisions
2. **Reflective Practice** (Schon): Learning through reflection on action
3. **Design Education Theory**: Pedagogical approaches in creative disciplines
4. **Project Management**: Risk identification and mitigation techniques

## Methods

- Adaptation of premortem technique for educational context
- Implementation in HCI courses at Purdue and Indiana University
- Student feedback and course outcome analysis
- Iterative refinement of pedagogical approach

## Main Arguments

1. Students often focus on success scenarios and neglect potential failures
2. Premortem technique makes failure consideration systematic and structured
3. Imagining failure is psychologically easier when framed as already happened
4. The method develops critical evaluation skills transferable beyond specific projects
5. Premortems can be integrated into various stages of design education

## Pedagogical Implementation

- Conducted after concept development, before detailed design
- Students individually brainstorm reasons for imagined failure
- Group discussion to compile and categorize failure modes
- Identification of preventive design decisions
- Integration into design critique and review processes

## Limitations and Critiques

- Limited empirical assessment of learning outcomes
- May be challenging for novice designers with limited experience
- Could potentially discourage risk-taking and innovation
- Effectiveness may vary by project type and student level
- Requires skilled facilitation to avoid negativity
