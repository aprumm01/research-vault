# Prioritizing Human Creativity: A Sequential Approach to Teaching Generative AI in Graphic Design Education

## Summary

This paper examines the integration of generative AI (GenAI) tools into graphic design education through a sequential pedagogical approach. The research presents two case studies from undergraduate courses at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign: a foundational typography course taught without AI tools, and an advanced studio course where students visualized mythical cities using AI-assisted research and image generation. The central argument is that students who develop strong fundamental design skills before engaging with AI tools are better equipped to use them meaningfully while maintaining creative control.

The foundational typography course (16 weeks, 18 students) focused on type construction, conceptualization, and iterative design processes. Students designed custom display typefaces inspired by specific cities, developing skills in letterform anatomy, spacing principles, and layout fundamentals through analog sketching and digital refinement. This course was intentionally taught without GenAI tools, establishing design fundamentals that would later support meaningful AI engagement.

The advanced course (8 weeks, 18 students) had students develop identity systems and landing pages for mythical cities like Atlantis, El Dorado, and Troy. Students used ChatGPT and Gemini for research and Adobe Firefly for image generation. Results showed 83.3% of students reported AI made designing easier, while 72.2% found AI most helpful for generating ideas. However, 52.9% cited "lack of creativity in AI" as a primary challenge, and students expressed ethical concerns about ownership and attribution.

## Key Concepts

- **Sequential Pedagogy**: An approach where design fundamentals are taught before introducing AI tools, mitigating risks of compromising creative processes while supporting development of individual design capabilities
- **Typography**: Extends beyond font selection to include letterform anatomy, technical terminology, spacing principles, and layout fundamentals
- **Conceptualization**: The process where designers develop, refine, and communicate ideas through sketching, iteration, and critique
- **Generative AI (GenAI)**: Systems that produce new content by learning from vast training datasets, capable of generating text, images, and videos that mirror patterns in training data
- **Exploratory vs. Human Creativity**: AI excels at exploratory creativity (iterating on existing knowledge) but lacks potential for human creativity characterized by critical evaluation and emotional complexity

## Theoretical Framework

The paper draws on scholarship distinguishing between exploratory and human creativity, positioning AI as a tool for amplifying rather than replacing human abilities. It references Shneiderman's human-centered AI, Boden's work on creativity and AI, and design education literature on typography's role in building fundamental skills. The framework emphasizes that understanding distinctions between human and machine contributions is crucial for preparing designers to use GenAI responsibly.

## Methods

Mixed-methods case study approach analyzing outcomes from two graphic design courses taught between 2023-2024:
- Course evaluation data from 18 students in foundational typography course (quantitative ratings, qualitative comments)
- Survey data from 18 students in advanced studio course measuring comfort with AI tools, impact on design process, helpful aspects, and primary challenges
- Analysis of student project work and process documentation
- Qualitative insights from student reflections on AI use

## Main Arguments

- Strong foundational skills prepare students to use GenAI tools effectively while retaining creative control
- Typography provides a critical entry point for developing foundational design skills independently of AI
- AI tools serve to amplify and empower human abilities rather than replace them
- Sequencing technical skills before AI integration prevents over-dependence on AI tools
- Ethical considerations including attribution, ownership, and bias must be explicitly addressed in design curricula
- Students should learn to distinguish between human creative contributions and AI-generated material
- Future design education must balance innovation with preservation of fundamental human creativity

## Limitations & Critiques

The study acknowledges several limitations: findings are based on courses at a single institution, limiting generalizability. Technical difficulties with AI tools were noted by 35.3% of students. Ethical concerns about AI training data and ownership remain ongoing challenges. The comfort level with AI tools varied significantly among students (only 38.9% comfortable or very comfortable). Future research should track students longitudinally from foundational through advanced courses and compare different programs' approaches to GenAI integration.
