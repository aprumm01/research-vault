# Ramaul et al. 2025 - Rethinking how we theorize AI in organization and management - A problematizing review of rationality and anthropomorphism

## Summary
Keywords: anthropomorphism, artificial intelligence, management, organization, problematization, rationality, review INTRODUCTION As an emerging digital technology that can learn and perform tasks autonomously, artificial intelligence (AI) has challenged the existing understanding of organizations and management and has quickly, if not abruptly, become a central item on the research Address for reprints: Laavanya Ramaul, Junior Researcher, Business School, LUT University.

## Key Concepts
- AI as root metaphor challenging traditional theorizing approaches in organization and management scholarship
- Problematizing review method enabling selective critical reading of theoretical contributions
- Rationality assumption framing AI capabilities through computational logic and optimization frameworks
- Anthropomorphism assumption attributing human-like qualities to AI systems in organizational contexts
- AI entering previously human-only domains transforming technology conceptualization from tool to actor
- Field assumptions about AI both shared and contested across organization and management literature
- Boundary conditions of rationality and anthropomorphism assumptions requiring contextualized examination
- Need for distinctive organization and management theory scaffolding within broader social science of AI

## Theoretical Framework
The problematizing review surfaces two foundational yet contested assumptions shaping how scholars theorize AI in organizations. The rationality assumption positions AI through computational decision-making frameworks emphasizing optimization, consistency, and data-driven processing. This framing highlights AI's capacity for handling complex calculations and pattern recognition but may obscure sociotechnical embedding, learning dynamics, and unexpected emergent behaviors. The anthropomorphism assumption attributes human-like intelligence, agency, or intentionality to AI systems, enabling theoretical concepts around AI as organizational actor or collaborator. However, this framing risks overstating AI autonomy while understating human roles in design, deployment, and ongoing governance. These competing assumptions create productive tensions requiring scholars to specify organizational boundary conditions rather than treating AI capabilities as context-independent.

## Methods
literature review

## Main Arguments
- Organization and management scholars face distinct theorizing challenge as AI capabilities approach imitating or surpassing human intelligence in specific domains
- Existing literature grounds itself in rationality and anthropomorphism as core but often unexamined assumptions about AI
- Rationality assumption enables theorizing around computational logic but may miss sociotechnical complexities of AI in practice
- Anthropomorphism assumption facilitates concepts of AI agency but risks mischaracterizing AI-human relationships and accountability structures
- Fruitful debates already emerging around both assumptions indicate theoretical vitality but also conceptual confusion
- Organization and management scholarship requires distinctive theory scaffolding beyond importing frameworks from computer science or psychology
- Boundary conditions of AI theorizing demand specifying organizational contexts where assumptions hold versus break down

## Limitations & Critiques
- Problematizing review method inherently selective potentially missing important theoretical contributions outside reviewed journals
- Focus on influential journals may privilege established perspectives over emerging or critical voices
- Analysis identifies assumptions but provides limited guidance on resolving contradictions between rationality and anthropomorphism framings
- Does not address how rapidly evolving AI capabilities (generative models, multimodal systems) challenge or validate existing theoretical assumptions
- Limited engagement with practitioner perspectives on how AI actually functions in organizational settings versus scholarly theorizing
- Unclear whether distinctive organization theory scaffolding needed or if interdisciplinary integration more productive
- May reify rationality/anthropomorphism dichotomy when actual theorizing involves more complex assumption combinations
- Does not fully address power dynamics in whose assumptions about AI shape dominant theoretical paradigms
