# Definition and Terminology Committee of the Association Januszewski Molenda 2008

## Summary
This foundational AECT document defines educational technology as "the study and ethical practice of facilitating learning and improving performance by creating, using, and managing appropriate technological processes and resources." The definition represents a paradigm shift from instruction-centered to learner-centered approaches, reflecting influences from cognitive and constructivist learning theories. Emphasizes technology's role as facilitative rather than controlling, moving from "delivering" instruction to creating learning environments for exploration. Addresses ethical practice as essential to professional success, study as iterative inquiry beyond traditional research, and improvement as effectiveness and efficiency in achieving learning goals. Distinguishes surface learning (information retention) from deep learning (active use and understanding).

## Key Concepts
- **Educational technology definition**: "The study and ethical practice of facilitating learning and improving performance by creating, using, and managing appropriate technological processes and resources"
- **Paradigm shift**: Movement from instruction-centered to learner-centered approaches, from control to facilitation, from behavioral to cognitive/constructivist theories
- **Study as broad inquiry**: Information gathering and analysis encompassing research, reflective practice, disciplined inquiry (quantitative, qualitative, philosophical, historical approaches)
- **Ethical practice**: Foundational approach questioning assumptions, considering learners, environments, societal good, and power positions—not just rule-following
- **Facilitating versus controlling**: Guiding and supporting learning rather than directing it; designing environments, organizing resources, providing tools for exploration
- **Deep versus surface learning**: Active use and application of knowledge vs. information retention for testing (Perkins, Weigel)
- **Inert knowledge**: Information taught but not used outside classroom contexts (Whitehead critique)
- **Reflection-in-action**: Cyclical practice/reflection process improving teaching and learning environments (Schön)
- **Authentic environments**: Real or immersive learning contexts supporting meaningful understanding and knowledge transfer

## Theoretical Framework
Reflects paradigm shift from behavioral to cognitive and constructivist learning theories, changing emphasis from teaching to learning and from controlling to facilitating. Draws on Schön's reflective practice and practice/reflection cycles. References Perkins' learning taxonomy (retention, understanding, active use) and Whitehead's inert knowledge critique. Integrates systems approach with critical theory questioning of power positions. Builds on AECT ethical principles and professional conduct frameworks. Reflects movement from instructional design (prespecified routines) to learning environment design (exploration with electronic support). Grounded in cognitive tools and immersive environments perspectives.

## Methods
Definitional analysis and conceptual framework development by AECT Definition and Terminology Committee. Not empirical research but disciplined inquiry examining field evolution, theoretical foundations, and professional practice standards. Uses historical analysis of prior AECT definitions (Ely 1963 onward) showing evolution from "control" to "facilitate" language. Employs philosophical analysis of key terms and their implications for practice. References AECT Ethics Committee work developing case examples and ethical standards. Synthesis of research literature on learning theories, instructional design, and educational technology practice.

## Main Arguments
- **Educational technology conception is temporary**: Definition represents "snapshot in time" continuously evolving with field development, acknowledging impermanence of any single formulation
- **Paradigm shift from control to facilitation**: Earlier definitions implied direct cause-effect between instruction and learning; current definition recognizes learner as active constructor not passive recipient
- **Technology role is facilitative not causal**: Provides problem space and tools rather than presenting information and drill; enables rather than determines learning
- **Ethical practice is foundational**: Not just following rules but questioning assumptions about efficacy, power distribution, inclusion, and whose interests are served
- **Study encompasses multiple forms**: Traditional research plus theorizing, philosophical analysis, development projects, authentic environment investigations, practitioner voices—all legitimate inquiry forms
- **Learning goals shifting toward deep learning**: Active use, problem-solving, real-world application valued over retention for tests; assessment must match learning goals
- **Improvement means effectiveness and efficiency**: Quality products leading to effective learning plus minimizing wasted time, effort, and expense relative to goals
- **Reflection on practice drives improvement**: Cyclical problem-solving process where practitioners analyze environments, implement changes, and assess results
- **Cognitive and constructivist theories transform practice**: Learners' perspectives, preferences, ownership, and active meaning-making emphasized over information delivery
- **Assessment methods must align with learning types**: Retention tests for recall, paraphrasing for understanding, authentic application for active use—different goals require different measures

## Limitations & Critiques
- **Temporary nature acknowledged**: Definition explicitly positioned as "snapshot" that will continue evolving, limiting permanence and potentially creating instability
- **Efficiency-constructivism tension unresolved**: Contradiction between systematic development's efficiency focus and constructivist emphasis on learner choice and empowerment not fully addressed
- **"Appropriate" left undefined**: No operationalization of what makes technological processes/resources "appropriate," leaving interpretation open to practitioner judgment
- **Ethical framework incomplete in excerpt**: Ethical practice referenced extensively but detailed framework in chapter 11 not included; difficult to assess comprehensiveness
- **Limited guidance on directive approaches**: When facilitative versus more directive methods are justified remains underspecified; not all learning benefits from pure facilitation
- **Causality complexity**: Assumes technology facilitates rather than causes learning but causality relationships remain philosophically and empirically complex
- **Learning dichotomy oversimplification**: Surface vs. deep learning binary may miss spectrum of learning outcomes and legitimate purposes for retention-focused learning
- **Access and equity underaddressed**: Doesn't adequately confront digital divide, resource constraints, or how ethical practice applies to under-resourced settings
- **Practice validation limited**: Focus on theoretical shifts and aspirational practice but limited empirical validation of claims about actual field-wide practice changes
- **Cultural context**: Definition developed in US AECT context; transferability to international educational technology contexts unclear
