# Exploring Resistance and Other Oppositional Responses to AI

## Summary
CSCW 2025 workshop proposal gathering researchers and practitioners who study and/or engage in opposition to AI proliferation. Based on inclusive conceptualization of what counts as AI, workshop assembles diverse perspectives on resistance, avoidance, protest, friction, and other oppositional responses across domains. Six themes: (1) resistance in organizational contexts where workers navigate personal values vs professional/organizational demands, (2) community-based collective resistance through principles like Feminist Data Manifest-No and computer supported collective action, (3) non-voluntary adoption where users are forced to use AI against will, (4) human labor and power distributions including micro-task infrastructure and material supply networks, (5) implications for design including technologies supporting opposition (TrackMeNot, AdNauseam), (6) resistance potentially reifying AI as entity. Workshop produces two outputs: living annotated bibliography and practical guide with tactics for challenging perceived AI inevitability.

## Key Concepts
- Technology resistance - opposition to systems arising from misalignment among personal values, professional responsibilities, and organizational demands
- Non-use - situations where users report being required or forced to use technology against will
- Collective action - practices like Feminist Data Manifest-No identifying refusal practices and commitments to data subjects
- AI as materialist-performative enterprise - Crawford's view of AI as enacted across global networks of power involving natural resources, labor, infrastructures, logistics
- Computer supported collective action - systems facilitating collective action for new data practices and privacy protections
- Community audits - toolkits supporting community examination of local AI policies and acquisition processes
- Resistance and reification - risk that opposition may reinforce AI as prefigured entity rather than challenging its ontological status
- Technologies of opposition - tools like TrackMeNot, AdNauseam, Tor designed to support withholding data and opposing AI proliferation
- Thingness of AI - Suchman's warning against presuming singular system or ontological agent

## Theoretical Framework
**Expansive AI Conceptualization**: Rather than single definition, accommodates varied approaches:
- Traditional: "study of how to make computers do things at which, at the moment, people are better" (Rich, 1983)
- Functional: Prediction (anticipating future events), generation (synthetic content), content moderation (curatorial decisions) (Narayanan & Kapoor)
- Materialist-performative: AI as enacted across global networks of power, made from natural resources, fuel, human labor, infrastructures, logistics, histories (Crawford, 2021)
- Critical: Neither intelligent (intelligence is cultural construct) nor artificial (made from natural materials and human labor)

**Technology Non-Use and Resistance Theory**: Builds on CSCW foundations showing resistance arising from sociotechnical force misalignment, extending to AI contexts where opposition occurs at individual, organizational, and societal levels simultaneously.

**Collective Action Theory**: Drawing on Zong and Matias highlighting multiplicity of collective practices challenging AI and data-oriented systems, including principle enumeration, new social computing systems, and community-oriented interventions.

**Critique and Co-optation**: Foundational theoretical work on how critique can be subsumed into and negated by thing being critiqued, applied to risk that AI resistance reinforces AI as dominant force.

## Methods
**Workshop Format**: Full-day in-person workshop at CSCW 2025 (Bergen, Norway) with hybrid option if sufficient remote interest (10-15% of accepted authors)

**Participant Recruitment**: Position papers (500-1000 words) via mailing lists and social media soliciting:
- Methodological approaches or specific methods for studying/engaging in oppositional practices
- Description of challenge faced in resistance work

**Workshop Activities**:
- Early morning (9:00-10:30): Welcome, participant panel with 3-4 presenters, identify discussion topics
- Late morning (11:00-12:00): Small group discussions on identified topics
- Early afternoon (post-lunch): Continued activities (not fully detailed in excerpt)
- Late afternoon: Synthesis and output generation

**Accessibility**: Video stream for hybrid attendees, designated co-organizer as point person for online participants monitoring channels, consultation on preferred platforms for accessibility needs, coordination with CSCW Accessibility Co-Chairs.

## Main Arguments
- Contemporary sociotechnical imaginaries assume AI inevitability (e.g., OpenAI's assumption superhuman-level AI inevitable) - workshop aims to find means of collectively organizing to demonstrate we actually have choice
- Opposition to AI takes diverse forms beyond simple non-adoption: resistance, avoidance, irritation, protest, friction, dissidence, subterfuge, alternity, destruction across professional software development, higher education, creative pursuits
- Non-use within organizations is multi-dimensional and dynamic, operating simultaneously at individual, organizational, and societal levels - requires understanding fluid, interconnected, constantly evolving sociotechnical factors
- Traditional "implications for design" suggesting modifications to existing AI or creation of "better" AI may fall flat when conflicting with profit motives or for critics believing AI fundamentally harmful
- Design can still be resistance strategy - technologies can be designed to support opposition work (data withholding tools, alternative AI implementations as critical practice)
- Risk exists that resistance may reinforce AI as dominant entity - challenging AI requires not only resisting systems/ideologies but challenging AI's very status as distinct kind of entity
- CSCW approaches to challenging AI should build on and support diverse collective-based practices, noting breadth of potential modes and sites of intervention

## Limitations & Critiques
**Workshop Proposal Scope**: As workshop proposal rather than completed research, limitations relate to planned approach:

**Methodological Concerns**:
- Position paper format (500-1000 words) may limit depth of participant contributions
- Single-day workshop format constrained for building sustained collaborative relationships
- Hybrid format challenges for facilitating equal participation between in-person and remote attendees
- Small group discussion outcomes depend heavily on facilitation quality and participant dynamics

**Conceptual Tensions**:
- Acknowledged but unresolved tension: opposition to AI may reify AI as entity (Section 3.6)
- Expansive definition of AI risks diluting focus and making concrete action difficult
- Balance between welcoming diverse AI conceptualizations and maintaining coherent workshop discussion unclear
- Tension between supporting alternative AI implementations (which accepts AI premise) and fundamental opposition to AI enterprise

**Output Concerns**:
- Living annotated bibliography and practical guide ambitious for single-day workshop
- Sustainability and maintenance of "living" bibliography post-workshop unclear
- Practical guide's context-sensitivity may limit broader applicability
- Risk that outputs document resistance without catalyzing actual change

**Theoretical Gaps**:
- Limited engagement with power asymmetries between individual resisters and well-resourced AI companies
- Insufficient attention to how resistance strategies may differ across global contexts, cultures, and economic positions
- Workshop focuses on CSCW/HCI community - may miss perspectives from other disciplinary approaches to AI resistance
