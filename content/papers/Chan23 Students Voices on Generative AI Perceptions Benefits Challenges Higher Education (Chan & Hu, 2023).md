# Students' Voices on Generative AI: Perceptions, Benefits, and Challenges in Higher Education

## Summary
This study explores university students' perceptions of generative AI technologies, specifically ChatGPT, in higher education through a survey of 399 undergraduate and postgraduate students from various disciplines in Hong Kong. Findings reveal generally positive attitudes toward GenAI in teaching and learning, with students recognizing potential for personalized learning support, writing and brainstorming assistance, and research capabilities. Alongside these benefits, students expressed significant concerns about accuracy, privacy, ethical issues, and impact on personal development and societal values. Published in the International Journal of Educational Technology in Higher Education (2023).

## Key Concepts
- **Student perception of GenAI**: The attitudes, experiences, and evaluations that students form about AI tools in educational contexts, which significantly influence whether and how they engage with these technologies
- **Biggs' 3P model**: Presage-Process-Product model of teaching and learning, which theorizes that student perceptions of learning environment strongly influence their approach (surface vs. deep) and ultimately their outcomes
- **Personalized learning support**: AI's capacity to adapt explanations, feedback, and assistance to individual student needs, knowledge levels, and learning preferences
- **Academic integrity concerns**: Student and institutional anxieties about AI enabling plagiarism, circumventing authentic work requirements, and undermining assessment validity
- **AI literacy**: Students' understanding of how GenAI works, what it can and cannot reliably do, and how to use it responsibly and critically
- **Holistic competency development**: The concern that over-reliance on AI may impede students' development of creativity, critical thinking, and other complex competencies essential for professional success
- **Surface vs. deep learning approaches**: Biggs' distinction between rote, surface-level engagement and deeper meaning-making—AI use may enable either depending on how it is engaged

## Theoretical Framework
The paper applies John Biggs' 3P (Presage-Process-Product) model as its central theoretical framework. According to this model, students' presage characteristics—including their perceptions of the learning environment, their abilities, and teaching strategies—significantly influence their process (approach to learning) and ultimately their product (learning outcomes). Students who perceive GenAI positively and feel confident using it are more likely to adopt a deep approach to learning that uses AI to extend understanding; students who are uncertain or anxious may adopt surface approaches.

The technology acceptance model (Davis, 1989) provides a secondary theoretical lens for understanding how perceived usefulness and ease of use influence student adoption. The paper situates its findings within broader debates about AI in higher education, engaging with research on AI in healthcare, research, and teaching contexts to position student perspectives within the institutional adoption discourse.

## Methods
Cross-sectional survey study with 399 undergraduate and postgraduate students from various disciplines at the University of Hong Kong. The survey instrument addressed: GenAI familiarity and frequency of use; perceived benefits and challenges; attitudes toward ethical issues; and views on effective integration. Both quantitative Likert-scale items and open-ended qualitative questions were used to capture student perceptions. Participants represented diverse disciplinary backgrounds, providing a sample with varied prior AI exposure. The study was published in 2023, making it an early empirical study in the rapidly evolving field of GenAI in higher education.

## Main Arguments
- University students hold generally positive attitudes toward GenAI in teaching and learning contexts, primarily valuing its potential for personalized support, writing assistance, and research acceleration—suggesting readiness for AI integration that institutional policies often lag behind
- Students demonstrate nuanced awareness of both benefits and risks: they recognize GenAI's potential for learning support while expressing substantive concerns about accuracy, privacy, academic integrity, and the erosion of their own skill development
- The Biggs 3P model predicts that students' perceptions of GenAI will meaningfully influence their learning approaches and outcomes, making student voice an essential input to effective AI integration policy rather than a peripheral consideration
- Concerns about AI's impact on holistic competency development (creativity, critical thinking) are prominent among students, suggesting that the pedagogical challenge is not simply technical integration but designing AI-supported learning that still develops these capabilities
- Student familiarity with and confidence in GenAI significantly shapes their perceptions, with more experienced users holding more differentiated views that distinguish appropriate from inappropriate use cases
- Understanding student perceptions is a prerequisite for creating effective institutional guidelines: AI policies developed without student input are likely to be perceived as disconnected from actual practices and to generate non-compliance

## Limitations & Critiques
The study was conducted at a single Hong Kong university in 2023, very early in the ChatGPT adoption cycle, which may limit generalizability to current conditions where student familiarity and institutional policies have evolved significantly. The cross-sectional survey design captures attitudes at a single point rather than tracking how perceptions change with increased AI experience.

The Hong Kong university context—with its particular academic culture, student demographics, and regulatory environment—may not represent student perspectives in other national or institutional contexts. The study also relies entirely on self-reported perceptions rather than behavioral data about actual AI use patterns, introducing social desirability bias, particularly given concerns about academic integrity.
