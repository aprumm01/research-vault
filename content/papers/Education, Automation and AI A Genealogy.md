# Education, Automation and AI: A Genealogy

## Summary
Applies a Foucauldian genealogical approach to trace how education has been mobilized as a governance tool in response to automation and AI from 1955 to 1997, finding that educational policy consistently responds to sociotechnical imaginaries of technological futures. Argues that the relationship between education and automation is not linear but oscillates between periods of techno-utopian adaptation and critical democratic resistance.

## Key Concepts
- Genealogical approach (Foucault)
- Sociotechnical imaginaries
- Educational imaginaries of technology
- Education as governance
- Epistemic anticipations

## Theoretical Framework
Foucauldian genealogy (1984); sociotechnical imaginaries (Jasanoff 2015); concept of "educational imaginaries of technology" developed by Rahm; education as a form of biopolitical governance of anticipated futures.

## Methods
Genealogical analysis of Swedish and broader education policy materials from 1955 to 1997; discourse analysis; no original empirical data collection; draws on archival and digital policy documents.

## Main Arguments
- Education has consistently been mobilized as a governance tool to manage fears and promises surrounding automation and AI since the 1950s
- Educational policies map anticipated technological threats and corresponding solutions, thereby actively producing the futures they claim to prepare for
- The relationship between technology and education is reciprocal: skills needed for innovation also drive what innovations are imagined as possible
- This genealogy of alternative futures reveals that contemporary AI education discourses are not new but replay historical patterns with new devices

## Limitations & Critiques
Primarily focused on Swedish policy context; genealogical method is interpretive and not easily replicable; period 1955–1997 predates the internet and contemporary AI; Foucauldian framing may not be legible to all education research audiences.
