# Lost in Simulation LLM-Simulated Users Are Unreliable Proxies - Seshadri et al - 2026

## Summary
HC] 28 Jan 2026 Agentic benchmarks increasingly rely on LLMsimulated users to scalably evaluate agent performance, yet the robustness, validity, and fairness of this approach remain unexamined Through a user study with participants across the United States, India, Kenya, and Nigeria, we investigate whether LLM-simulated users serve as reliable proxies for real human users.

## Main Arguments
- Agentic benchmarks increasingly rely on LLM-simulated users for scalable evaluation
- Robustness, validity, and fairness of simulated users remain largely unexamined
- Real user study across four countries reveals significant reliability issues
- LLM-simulated users are unreliable proxies for human users
- Scalability benefits do not justify validity costs in many contexts
- Cross-cultural differences expose limitations of universal simulation approaches
- Need for critical examination of simulation-based evaluation practices
- Real human evaluation remains necessary for robust AI assessment
- "Lost in simulation" - over-reliance on synthetic users obscures real user needs
- Geographic and cultural diversity reveals systematic biases in simulated users

## Limitations & Critiques
- Study limited to four countries, not fully globally representative
- Specific to agentic AI evaluation, may not generalize to other AI domains
- User study methodology has inherent limitations and biases
- Rapidly evolving LLM capabilities may improve simulation quality
- Does not provide alternative scalable evaluation approaches
- Sample size and demographic representation within countries not specified
- Temporal validity - findings reflect 2026 LLM capabilities
- May not account for task-specific differences in simulation reliability
- Cost-benefit analysis of alternatives not fully developed
