# herring.scheidt.2004

## Summary
Weblogs (blogs)—frequently modified web pages in which dated entries are listed in reverse chronological sequence—are the latest genre of Internet communication to attain widespread popularity, yet their characteristics have not been systematically described This paper presents the results of a content analysis of 203 randomly-selected weblogs, comparing the empirically observable features of the corpus with popular claims about the nature of weblogs, and.

## Key Concepts
- **Weblog (blog) genre**: Frequently modified web pages with dated entries in reverse chronological sequence
- Systematic description of blog characteristics through empirical observation
- Comparison between empirical blog features and popular claims about blogging
- Latest genre of Internet communication achieving widespread popularity
- Content analysis of randomly-selected weblogs to establish genre characteristics

## Theoretical Framework
- **Genre theory**: Understanding weblogs as distinct communicative genre with identifiable features
- Computer-Mediated Communication (CMC) framework for analyzing blog characteristics
- Empirical validation of popular claims about blog nature and function

## Methods
- Content analysis of 203 randomly-selected weblogs
- Systematic coding of blog features and characteristics
- Comparison of empirical findings with popular claims about blogging

## Main Arguments
- Weblogs represent distinct genre of Internet communication requiring systematic description
- Empirical analysis necessary to validate or refute popular claims about blog characteristics
- Blog features can be systematically identified through content analysis
- Random sampling of 203 blogs provides representative view of genre characteristics
- Gap between popular discourse about blogging and empirical reality of blog practices
- Need for evidence-based understanding of emerging communication genres

## Limitations & Critiques
- 203-blog sample may not capture full diversity of blogging practices
- Rapid evolution of blogging platform and practices may date findings
- Random selection strategy and sampling frame considerations
- Snapshot analysis may miss temporal changes in blog genre
- Need for ongoing research as blogging practices continue to evolve
