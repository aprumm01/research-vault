# The Cultures of Contemporary Instructional Design Scholarship, Part One: Developments Based on Behavioral and Cognitive Science Foundations

## Summary

This Educational Technology article examines four distinct "cultures" or traditions within instructional design (ID) scholarship. Willis argues that the field has evolved from a unified behavioral foundation to multiple competing paradigms. Part One focuses on two cultures: traditional ISD (Instructional Systems Design) based on behavioral theories, and Design-Based Research (DBR) rooted in cognitive science. The paper provides a critical analysis of each approach's strengths and weaknesses.

## Key Concepts

- **Four ID Cultures**: Traditional ISD, Design-Based Research, Constructivist-ID, Critical Theory ID
- **Instructional Systems Design (ISD)**: Linear, systematic approaches like ADDIE
- **Design-Based Research (DBR)**: Iterative research approach in authentic settings
- **Behaviorism**: Learning theory focused on observable behaviors and reinforcement
- **Cognitive Science**: Study of mental processes including memory, problem-solving, learning

## Theoretical Framework

Willis identifies four theoretical foundations:

1. **Behaviorism**: Skinner, stimulus-response, reinforcement
2. **Information Processing**: Cognitive load, schema theory, chunking
3. **Communications Theory**: Message design, media selection
4. **Cognitive Science**: Mental models, problem-solving, expertise

## Methods

- Literature review and synthesis
- Historical analysis of ID theory development
- Critical comparison of ID paradigms
- Analysis of strengths and weaknesses of each approach

## Main Arguments

**Traditional ISD Characteristics:**
- Based on behavioral and positivist foundations
- Linear, sequential design process (waterfall model)
- Emphasis on pre-specified objectives
- Expert designers working top-down
- Focus on efficiency and replicability

**Design-Based Research Characteristics:**
- Grounded in cognitive science and constructivism
- Iterative, contextual design process
- Research conducted in authentic settings
- Integration of design and research
- Focus on understanding and improving practice

## Limitations and Critiques

**ISD Critiques:**
- Too rigid and linear for complex learning
- Oversimplifies learning processes
- Ignores context and individual differences
- Based on outdated behavioral assumptions

**DBR Critiques:**
- Difficulty generalizing findings
- Theory-centric focus can marginalize practitioners
- Time-intensive research approach
- Scalability challenges

## Limitations and Critiques (of the Article)

- Part One only; constructivist and critical perspectives addressed in Part Two
- Author's constructivist sympathies evident in analysis
- Some oversimplification of ISD approaches
- Rapid evolution of field may date some observations
