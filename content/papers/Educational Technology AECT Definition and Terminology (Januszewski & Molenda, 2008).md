# Educational Technology: AECT Definition and Terminology (Januszewski & Molenda, 2008)

## Summary
This chapter presents the 2008 AECT definition of educational technology: "the study and ethical practice of facilitating learning and improving performance by creating, using, and managing appropriate technological processes and resources." It unpacks each term in the definition, traces the evolution of the field from a teaching-centered delivery model to a learner-centered facilitation model, and addresses the ethical dimensions of professional practice. The chapter serves as the canonical definitional statement for the educational technology discipline at the time of publication.

## Key Concepts
- AECT 2008 definition of educational technology
- Ethical practice as a core professional commitment
- Study (research and reflective inquiry) vs. practice distinction
- Facilitation of learning and improvement of performance
- Technological processes and resources (broadly construed)

## Theoretical Framework
Field-defining document drawing on constructivist learning theory, reflective practice (Schon), and the historical evolution of the profession from audiovisual instruction through instructional systems design to learning environment design; situated within AECT's institutional and ethical framework.

## Methods
Conceptual and definitional analysis; each term in the 2008 definition is examined for its intended meaning and contextualized against prior AECT definitions and field developments; not an empirical study.

## Main Arguments
- Educational technology is defined by its ethical orientation and systematic commitment to learning, not merely by the use of media or technological tools
- The field has shifted from a delivery-centered model (instruction as transmission) to a learner-centered model (instruction as facilitation)
- Ethical practice requires educational technologists to consider access, power, and social good alongside efficiency and effectiveness
- The definition intentionally encompasses research, design, development, utilization, management, and evaluation as interrelated professional activities

## Limitations & Critiques
As a definitional document, the chapter reflects consensus positions of the professional body at a specific moment in time; the 2008 definition may not fully anticipate subsequent developments in AI-enhanced learning, open educational resources, and informal digital learning contexts. Definitional work is inherently normative and may not capture the full diversity of practice in the field.
