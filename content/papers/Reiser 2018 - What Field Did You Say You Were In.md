# What Field Did You Say You Were In? Defining and Naming Our Field

## Summary
This chapter from Reiser's textbook traces the historical evolution of definitions for the field of instructional design and technology. The author examines how the field has been labeled differently over the years (audiovisual instruction, educational technology, instructional technology) and how definitions have shifted from focusing on media and hardware to emphasizing systematic design processes.

The chapter presents major definitional milestones including the 1963 definition focusing on media, the 1970 Commission on Instructional Technology definitions (one media-focused, one process-focused), the 1977 AECT definition emphasizing complex integrated processes, the 1994 AECT definition describing theory and practice across five domains, and the 2008 AECT definition highlighting ethical practice, facilitation of learning, and performance improvement. Reiser argues that the field's definition should encompass both systematic design of instruction and the use of media for instructional purposes, while also recognizing the growing influence of human performance technology.

## Key Concepts
- **Instructional Technology**: Originally viewed as media (films, TV, computers), later reconceptualized as a systematic process for designing instruction
- **Instructional Design**: A systematic process involving analysis, design, development, implementation, and evaluation of learning solutions
- **Human Performance Technology (HPT)**: The broader perspective of improving workplace performance through both instructional and non-instructional interventions
- **Educational Technology**: An umbrella term often used interchangeably with instructional technology

## Theoretical Framework
The chapter traces the theoretical evolution from behaviorist-influenced programmed instruction through cognitive and constructivist influences. The field draws from learning theory, systems theory, and communications theory to inform both the design of instruction and the selection/use of instructional media.

## Methods
This is a conceptual/historical analysis chapter that reviews primary source documents including official AECT definition statements from 1963, 1970, 1977, 1994, and 2008.

## Main Arguments
- Definitions of the field have evolved from media-centric to process-centric views
- The goal of the field has shifted from "more effective instruction" to "improving learning and performance"
- The term "instructional design and technology" best captures both the systematic design processes and technological resources that define the field
- Recent definitions appropriately incorporate ethical considerations and emphasize facilitation over control of learning

## Limitations & Critiques
- The author acknowledges that no single definition can capture the full breadth of the field
- Different professionals within the field continue to emphasize different aspects based on their roles and perspectives
- The ongoing debate about the proper name for the field reflects genuine differences in how practitioners conceptualize their work
