# Will It Survive the Decade

## Summary
The Dick and Carey Model: Will It Survive the Decade [] Walter Dick Many instructional designers and numerous organizations have adapted the Dick and Carey model for use in their training functions This article reviews the changes that have occurred to the model in the 20 years since its original publication, and identifies various influences that may determine whether it will continue to be useful in the years ahead.

## Key Concepts
- **Dick and Carey model**: Systematic instructional design model first published in 1978, adapted widely for training
- **Evolutionary changes**: Model evolved through four editions (1978, 1985, 1990, 1996) with modifications based on emerging concepts
- **ADDIE framework**: Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation structure
- **Four key influences on 1996 revision**: (1) Performance technology, (2) Context analysis, (3) Multi-level evaluation (Kirkpatrick), (4) Total quality management
- **Shift from education to business focus**: During 1980s, primary users became corporate trainers rather than educators
- **Constructivist influence**: 1996 edition incorporated constructivist theory while maintaining systematic design approach
- **Transfer of training**: Growing emphasis on workplace performance rather than just post-test results

## Theoretical Framework
Rooted in behavioral learning theory (Skinner) and positivist epistemology. Evolved to incorporate Gagné's conditions of learning and cognitive approaches. Later editions integrated performance technology principles, context-based design, and constructivist elements while maintaining systematic ISD structure.

## Methods
- Historical analysis of model evolution across four editions
- Review of competing instructional design textbooks
- Assessment of constructivist vs objectivist influences
- Analysis of model adoption in business/industry vs public education
- Examination of organizational client needs and quality management principles

## Main Arguments
- **Model has been remarkably stable**: Core structure remained essentially unchanged from 1978-1990 despite rapid field evolution
- **1996 revision reflects four major influences**: Performance technology focus on organizational problems, context analysis for transfer, Kirkpatrick evaluation levels, and TQM client-centered quality
- **Shift to workplace focus**: Model evolved from public education emphasis to business/industry training applications
- **Constructivist integration possible**: 1996 edition demonstrates systematic design can incorporate constructivist principles without abandoning core structure
- **Client perception critical**: TQM influence emphasizes importance of client perceptions in determining instructional quality
- **Context matters for transfer**: Understanding learning and performance contexts became essential for effective design
- **Long-term viability uncertain**: Competing models and declining academic interest in ID pose challenges to continued dominance
- **Objectivist vs constructivist debate**: Assesses relative impact on different sectors (public education vs business/industry)

## Limitations & Critiques
- Written from perspective of model creator (Walter Dick), potentially limiting critical distance
- 1996 publication date means analysis predates massive changes in technology and learning sciences
- Limited discussion of emerging alternatives that might displace systematic ISD
- May overstate ability to integrate constructivist principles within fundamentally objectivist framework
- Does not fully address criticisms that ADDIE models are too rigid and linear
- Focuses on textbook adoption rather than actual practice in the field
- Geographic and cultural scope appears limited to US contexts
