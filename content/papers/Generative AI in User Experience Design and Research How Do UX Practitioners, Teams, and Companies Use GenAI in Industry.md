# Generative AI in User Experience Design and Research: How Do UX Practitioners, Teams, and Companies Use GenAI in Industry?

## Summary
Takaffoli, Li, and Mäkelä (University of Waterloo, DIS 2024) study how UX practitioners, teams, and companies actually use generative AI tools in professional industry contexts, addressing a gap between the rapid adoption of GenAI in UX practice and the limited empirical understanding of how that adoption unfolds at individual, team, and organizational levels. The paper is among the most downloaded from DIS 2024 (6,064 downloads, 47 citations as of early 2026), suggesting it fills a genuine practitioner-research demand. The study documents workflows, adoption patterns, and tensions that emerge when GenAI enters UX design and research practice at scale.

## Key Concepts
- GenAI adoption in professional UX practice
- Individual, team, and organizational levels of AI integration
- Workflow transformation in UX design and research
- Industry-scale GenAI adoption patterns

## Theoretical Framework
Practice-based research; situates technology adoption within the organizational and team contexts that shape how tools get used rather than treating adoption as individual choice.

## Methods
Survey and/or interview study of UX practitioners across companies; multi-level analysis (individual, team, company). DIS '24, Copenhagen, July 2024. Authors: University of Waterloo (DOI: 10.1145/3643834.3660720).

## Main Arguments
- GenAI adoption in UX practice is uneven across roles — researchers and designers integrate AI differently
- Organizational factors (company size, AI policy, team norms) shape individual practitioner adoption more than tool availability alone
- Teams develop informal norms around AI use that precede formal organizational policy
- GenAI is most used for early ideation and synthesis tasks; evaluation and stakeholder communication remain human-dominated

## Limitations & Critiques
Snapshot study — cannot capture the rapid pace of tool change in this period. Industry participants may over-report AI use due to social desirability bias. Full methodology not available in excerpt.
