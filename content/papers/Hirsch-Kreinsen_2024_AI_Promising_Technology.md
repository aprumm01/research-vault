# Artificial Intelligence: A Promising Technology

## Summary

This paper from AI & Society examines artificial intelligence through the lens of "promising technology" theory, analyzing how AI has been promoted and perceived in Germany since the 1970s. Hirsch-Kreinsen argues that AI represents a paradigmatic case of a technology sustained by ambitious promises rather than immediate practical results. The paper traces the cycles of hype and disappointment in AI development, examining how technological promises function socially and politically.

## Key Concepts

- **Promising Technology**: Technologies sustained by future-oriented claims rather than current capabilities
- **Technological Promise Dynamics**: Cycles of expectation, disappointment, and renewed optimism
- **AI Winter**: Periods of reduced funding and interest following unfulfilled AI promises
- **Sociotechnical Imaginaries**: Collectively held visions of desirable technological futures
- **Technology Assessment**: Critical evaluation of technological impacts and trajectories

## Theoretical Framework

The paper employs sociology of technology perspectives:

1. **Promise Theory**: Technologies gain support through compelling narratives about future benefits
2. **Sociology of Expectations**: Expectations actively shape technological development
3. **Science and Technology Studies (STS)**: Technology development is socially embedded
4. **Critical Technology Assessment**: Evaluating technology beyond technical metrics

## Methods

- Historical analysis of AI development in Germany from the 1970s to present
- Document analysis of policy papers, funding proposals, and media coverage
- Examination of institutional responses to AI promises over time
- Comparative analysis of different AI hype cycles

## Main Arguments

1. AI has persistently operated as a "promising technology" despite repeated failures to deliver
2. Technological promises serve important social functions in mobilizing resources and attention
3. The gap between AI promises and reality has remained remarkably consistent over decades
4. AI development is shaped by broader sociotechnical imaginaries and political contexts
5. Critical assessment of AI requires understanding its promissory character

## Limitations and Critiques

- Focus on German context may limit applicability to other national innovation systems
- Recent developments in large language models not fully addressed
- Normative implications of the analysis could be more developed
- Limited engagement with technical AI literature
- The role of commercial versus academic AI development could be explored further
