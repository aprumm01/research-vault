# Spectrum of AI futures imaginaries by AI practitioners in Finland and Singapore - The unimagined speed of AI progress

## Summary
Futures 153 (2023) 103247 Contents lists available at ScienceDirect Futures journal homepage: www com/locate/futures Spectrum of AI futures imaginaries by AI practitioners in Finland and Singapore: The unimagined speed of AI progress Johanna Hautala a, *, Hanna Heino b a b School of Management, University of Vaasa, Vaasa, Finland Finland Futures Research Centre, University of Turku, Turku, Finland A R T I C L E I N F O A B S T R A C T.

## Key Concepts
- Futures imaginaries: Individual practitioners' visions of best, worst, and likely futures
- Sociotechnical imaginaries: Collectively imagined forms of social life reflected in technology
- AI transformative technology: Autonomous learning, real-time interaction, widespread application
- Continuum of futures: Variations between best and worst scenarios
- Agent in control: Questions of who governs AI development and deployment
- Non-linear perspectives: Individual rather than collective imaginary interpretation

## Theoretical Framework
- Futures imaginaries (individual interpretation): Non-linear, practitioner-specific visions
- Sociotechnical imaginaries: Collective vision of social order through technology
- Futures studies methodology: Exploring multiple possible futures

## Methods
Qualitative interviews with 35 AI and robotics practitioners in Finland and Singapore

## Main Arguments
- AI practitioners envision three continuums of likely futures: best, in-between, and worst scenarios
- Decisive questions behind continuums: agent in control, practitioner community relations, justified concentration of power
- Individual practitioners' imaginaries differ from collective societal imaginaries
- Empirical focus on practitioners essential as they create and realize AI futures
- Need for fine-tuning vocabulary to support individual and non-linear perspectives
- Dystopian and utopian imaginaries coexist in practitioner thinking
- Speed of AI progress often unimagined or underestimated
- Practitioners' visions shape actual technological development trajectories

## Limitations & Critiques
- Small sample size (35 interviews) limits generalizability
- Limited gender diversity (only 17% female participants reflects broader AI field issue)
- Geographically constrained to peripheral locations (Finland/Singapore, not Silicon Valley core)
- Practitioners located outside core AI development have limited access to global progress knowledge
- Focus on individual rather than collective imaginaries may miss systemic patterns
- Temporal limitation: practitioners underestimated speed of AI progress (study 2018-2019, pre-ChatGPT)
- Need for more research on female AI practitioners' perspectives globally
- Sample bias toward university and industry practitioners, may miss other stakeholder perspectives
- Limited access to data, algorithms, and discussions by peripheral communities affects imaginary formation
