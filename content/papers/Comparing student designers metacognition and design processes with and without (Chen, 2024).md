# Comparing student designers’ metacognition and design processes with and without generative AI a preliminary study

## Summary
This study investigated the role of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) and metacognitive operations within the design process A qualitative research approach was employed and Retrospective Think-Aloud protocols and semi-structured interviews were utilized to examine the design processes of five students under the Normal design condition (without GenAI use) and four students under the GenAI-assisted condition The findings revealed that the use of GenAI was predominantly concentrated in the early phases of the design process, closely aligning with a divergent orientation.

## Key Concepts
- **Metacognitive operations in design**: Self-monitoring, planning, evaluation, and regulation processes designers use to guide their own cognitive activities during creative problem-solving
- **GenAI-assisted versus normal design conditions**: Comparison of design processes with and without access to generative AI tools to isolate technology's influence on cognitive and creative patterns
- **Retrospective think-aloud protocol**: Method where designers verbalize their thought processes after completing design tasks, revealing metacognitive strategies and decision-making patterns
- **Divergent phase concentration**: Finding that GenAI use primarily clusters in early, exploratory design stages focused on idea generation rather than convergent refinement phases
- **Design process phase analysis**: Examination of how GenAI affects different stages (problem definition, ideation, development, evaluation) of systematic design workflows

## Theoretical Framework
The study draws on metacognition theory (Flavell) examining designers' awareness and control of their own cognitive processes. Connects to design cognition research examining problem-solving strategies, divergent/convergent thinking patterns, and design fixation. Framework likely references dual-process theory (intuitive vs. deliberate thinking) and Schön's reflective practice, examining how GenAI affects designers' reflective monitoring and strategic regulation of creative work.

## Methods
Qualitative comparative study with N=9 student designers: 5 in normal condition (no GenAI) and 4 in GenAI-assisted condition. Data collected through retrospective think-aloud protocols where participants verbalized reasoning after design tasks, plus semi-structured interviews exploring metacognitive strategies and GenAI usage patterns. Analysis examined design process phases, metacognitive operation types, and how GenAI integration affected cognitive strategies and creative workflows.

## Main Arguments
- **GenAI concentrates in divergent early phases**: Students primarily used generative AI during initial ideation and exploration stages, aligning with divergent thinking needs, while later convergent refinement relied more on traditional approaches
- **Metacognitive operations differ by condition**: GenAI-assisted designers showed different self-monitoring and planning patterns compared to normal condition, potentially offloading some metacognitive work to AI tools
- **Early-stage cognitive support patterns**: GenAI functioned as ideation catalyst and creative stimulation in problem-framing phases but less useful for detailed development and evaluation work
- **Potential metacognitive scaffolding effects**: GenAI tools may provide external metacognitive support (prompting reflection, suggesting alternatives) but could also reduce students' internal metacognitive skill development
- **Design process structure maintained**: Despite GenAI integration, fundamental design process phases (define-ideate-develop-evaluate) remained intact, suggesting tools augment rather than transform core design workflows
- **Individual variation in appropriation**: Students showed diverse GenAI integration strategies, with some embracing extensive use and others remaining selective, reflecting personal cognitive and creative preferences

## Limitations & Critiques
- **Very small sample size**: N=9 (5 normal, 4 GenAI) severely limits statistical power and generalizability; findings should be considered exploratory and preliminary
- **Retrospective think-aloud limitations**: Post-hoc verbal reports may not accurately capture in-the-moment cognitive processes; participants may rationalize or misremember actual thinking patterns
- **Unbalanced comparison groups**: 5 vs. 4 participants makes direct comparison challenging; groups may differ in ways beyond GenAI access (skill levels, design approaches, motivation)
- **Missing concurrent think-aloud data**: Retrospective protocol misses real-time cognitive processes; concurrent verbalization during design would provide richer metacognitive insights
- **Short-term snapshot**: Single design task doesn't capture how metacognitive strategies and GenAI integration patterns evolve with extended use and skill development
- **Task specificity**: Findings may depend on particular design brief, domain, constraints; different tasks might show different GenAI integration patterns
- **Student versus professional differences**: Student designers' metacognition and GenAI use likely differs from experienced professionals; findings may not generalize to industry practice
- **Unclear GenAI tool specificity**: Limited detail about which generative AI tools students used and how different tools' capabilities affect metacognitive patterns
