# Why Minimal Guidance During Instruction Does Not - Kirschner

## Summary
EDUCATIONAL PSYCHOLOGIST, 41(2), 75–86 Copyright © 2006, Lawrence Erlbaum Associates, Inc KIRSCHNER, MINIMAL GUIDANCE SWELLER, CLARK Why Minimal Guidance During Instruction Does Not Work: An Analysis of the Failure of Constructivist, Discovery, Problem-Based, Experiential, and Inquiry-Based Teaching Paul A Kirschner Educational Technology Expertise Center Open University of the Netherlands Research Centre Learning in Interaction Utrecht University, The Netherlands John Sweller School of Education University of New South Wales.

## Key Concepts
- **Minimal guidance instruction**: Approaches where learners must discover or construct essential information themselves (discovery, problem-based, inquiry-based, experiential, constructivist learning)
- **Direct instructional guidance**: Providing information that fully explains concepts/procedures plus learning strategy support compatible with cognitive architecture
- **Human cognitive architecture**: Sensory memory, working memory, long-term memory structures and their relations
- **Working memory limitations**: Very limited duration (~30 sec) and capacity (~4±1 elements) when processing novel information; limitations disappear for familiar info from long-term memory
- **Long-term memory as central**: Massive knowledge base that is central to all cognitive activities; aim of instruction is to alter long-term memory
- **Cognitive load**: Problem solving places huge burden on working memory, leaving less capacity for learning
- **Expert-novice differences**: Experts derive skill from extensive long-term memory, not superior problem-solving processes
- **Epistemology ≠ pedagogy**: How experts work in domain is not equivalent to how novices should learn in that area

## Theoretical Framework
Builds on Atkinson-Shiffrin model of human memory. Draws on cognitive load theory (Sweller), expertise research (de Groot, Chase & Simon), and work on schema construction. Critiques constructivist learning theories (Bruner, Papert, Steffe & Gale) using evidence from cognitive psychology. Integrates Shulman's pedagogical content knowledge framework.

## Methods
- Literature review of empirical studies comparing guided vs unguided instruction
- Analysis of cognitive architecture implications for instructional design
- Review of expertise research
- Theoretical critique of constructivist assumptions
- Synthesis of evidence from multiple decades (1950s-2000s)

## Main Arguments
- **Minimal guidance ignores cognitive architecture**: Proceeds as if working memory limitations don't exist or are irrelevant when dealing with novel information
- **Problem solving impairs learning**: Searching problem spaces uses working memory that then isn't available for learning; can search for extended periods with minimal changes to long-term memory
- **Overwhelming evidence favors guidance**: Past half-century of research consistently shows minimal guidance is less effective and efficient than guided instruction
- **Constructivist observation doesn't support constructivist prescription**: While learners do construct knowledge, this doesn't mean withholding information enhances construction - reverse is true
- **Confusion of learning and practicing**: Major fallacy of confusing how experts work (epistemology) with how novices should learn (pedagogy)
- **Guidance advantage recedes with expertise**: Only when learners have sufficient prior knowledge to provide "internal" guidance does advantage of external guidance diminish
- **Cycle of failed approaches**: Each decade sees unguided approach under new name (discovery → experiential → problem-based → inquiry → constructivist) despite consistent evidence of failure
- **Mayer's conclusion**: "Each time, the evidence has favored a guided approach to learning"

## Limitations & Critiques
- May overstate the dichotomy between guided and unguided instruction; most real instruction falls on a continuum
- Focus on novices may not adequately address how to transition learners toward more independent problem-solving
- Limited discussion of motivational factors and learner agency
- Does not fully address domain differences where exploration may be more beneficial
- May conflate different types of minimal guidance approaches that have distinct characteristics
- Strong position may discourage valuable inquiry-based elements in appropriate contexts
- Limited consideration of collaborative learning or scaffolded discovery approaches
