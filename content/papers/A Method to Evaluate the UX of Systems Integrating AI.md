# A Method to Evaluate the UX of Systems Integrating AI

## Summary
SpringerBriefs in Applied Sciences and Technology PoliMI SpringerBriefs Davide Spallazzo · Martina Sciannamè · Mauro Ceconello User Experience + Artificial Intelligence Assessing the Qualities of AI-infused Systems SpringerBriefs in Applied Sciences and Technology PoliMI SpringerBriefs Series Editors Barbara Pernici, DEIB, Politecnico di Milano, Milano, Italy Stefano Della Torre, DABC, Politecnico di Milano, Milano, Italy Bianca M Colosimo, DMEC, Politecnico di Milano, Milano, Italy Tiziano Faravelli, DCHEM, Politecnico di Milano, Milano, Italy Roberto Paolucci, DICA, Politecnico di Milano, Milano, Italy Silvia Piardi, Design, Politecnico di Milano, Milano, Italy.

## Key Concepts
- **AI-infused products as sociotechnical systems**: Physical or digital artifacts embedding ML/DL algorithms characterized by autonomous adaptation, probabilistic operation, and human-AI cooperation across design, deployment, and use contexts
- **Design for imperfection paradigm**: Fundamental shift requiring designers to accept non-optimal AI outputs, define "well enough" thresholds, and design contexts where probabilistic errors are acceptable rather than pursuing Swiss-watch precision
- **AIXE scale (AI user eXperience Evaluation)**: Statistically validated 33-question quantitative assessment tool measuring 12 descriptors across 6 UX dimensions specifically tailored for AI-infused products' unique complexities
- **Four emergent AI-specific UX dimensions**: Intelligence (perceived cognitive capability), trustworthiness (reliability and ethical operation), conversational quality (natural dialogue interaction), and meaningfulness (purposeful value in users' lives) address gaps traditional UX assessment cannot capture
- **Uncertainty as defining characteristic**: AI-infused products tackle ill-defined wicked problems without predetermined solutions, using statistical pattern recognition rather than logical rules, resembling design discipline's approach more than traditional engineering
- **Communication-based opacity challenges**: User frustration stems from insufficient understanding of AI capabilities, misaligned expectations versus reality (e.g., "nest" advertising versus actual limited functionality), and hidden affordances requiring explicit communication strategies
- **Multi-touchpoint ecosystem complexity**: AI-infused products operate through interconnected physical interfaces, conversational modalities, companion apps, and device ecosystems, requiring systemic holistic design approaches beyond individual artifact focus
- **Alterity concept in AI interaction**: Shift from traditional embodiment paradigm where users extend themselves through tools toward treating AI as distinct counterpart entities (otherware) capable of agency, proactivity, and autonomous task delegation

## Theoretical Framework
**Sociotechnical Systems Theory** (van de Poel 2020, Johnson & Verdicchio): AI systems comprise inseparable technical components (algorithms, artifacts, technical norms) and social elements (human agents including programmers, users, deployers, policymakers, plus social rules and cultural conventions). This framework challenges "sociotechnical blindness" that obscures human centrality in AI development, emphasizing that AI systems exist only because humans design objectives and operational conditions, making human-AI cooperation inherent to their purpose.

**Simon's Sciences of the Artificial** (1969): AI-infused products' approach to ill-defined problems parallels design discipline methodology—operating through examples and pattern identification rather than linear pre-determined universal principles, positioning them uniquely to address complex uncertain changing environments. This theoretical grounding legitimizes treating AI as "design material" requiring designerly rather than purely engineering approaches.

**Value-Centered Design Framework** (HLEG on AI 2018, emerging research): Proposed adaptation of design methods to foreground ethical concerns and societal implications as central rather than peripheral considerations. Argues for trustworthy AI requiring not just technical robustness but user trust earned through transparency, accountability, and demonstrable human factor integration throughout development lifecycle.

## Methods
**Multi-stage Mixed-Methods Research Design**: The Meet-AI project employed comprehensive umbrella review, scoping review, and systematic literature review to assess existing UX evaluation methods' applicability to AI-infused products, identifying eight essential UX dimensions (pragmatic, aesthetic, hedonic, affective, intelligence, trustworthiness, conversational, meaningfulness) as foundation for experimental research.

**User-Centered Validation Through Advanced Users**: Phase 1-3 involved expert user surveys, intertwined descriptor analysis, and research workshops to systematically explore AI-related qualities, with findings showing AI-specific dimensions (intelligence, trustworthiness, conversational, meaningfulness) resonating most strongly while traditional dimensions produced modest results.

**Statistical Validation Protocol**: AIXE scale developed through rigorous item and questionnaire elaboration process, generating 33 questions measuring 12 descriptors across 6 final UX dimensions, validated through large-scale quantitative data collection and psychometric analysis to ensure reliability and construct validity.

**Real-World Application Study**: AIXE applied to comparative evaluation of domestic smart speakers using sample of 1600+ respondents from US and UK markets, analyzing performances across demographics, device types, and temporal evolution (2021 versus 2023), demonstrating practical utility for product benchmarking and UX assessment in authentic usage contexts.

## Main Arguments
- **Traditional UX evaluation methods prove inadequate for AI-infused products**: Conventional tools designed for static predictable systems fail to address AI's dynamic complexity—autonomous adaptation means identical inputs yield different outputs over time, unpredictability challenges established mental models, and multi-touchpoint ecosystems defy single-artifact evaluation frameworks. The research demonstrates through systematic literature review that existing methods overlook critical AI-specific qualities like trustworthiness and conversational intelligence, creating urgent need for specialized assessment approaches. Evidence from smart speaker evaluation reveals how standard usability metrics miss opacity issues, skill discoverability problems, and privacy concerns unique to AI systems.

- **Four AI-specific UX dimensions must complement traditional evaluation**: Beyond pragmatic/aesthetic/hedonic/affective dimensions, AI-infused products demand assessment of (1) **intelligence**—perceived cognitive capability matching real-world rather than anthropomorphized expectations, (2) **trustworthiness**—reliability encompassing technical robustness and ethical operation including privacy protection, (3) **conversational quality**—natural dialogue interaction moving beyond keyword triggers to contextual understanding, and (4) **meaningfulness**—purposeful value addressing relevant problems rather than gadget appeal. User validation research confirms these AI-specific dimensions resonate most strongly with advanced users, while traditional dimensions show continued but modest relevance. The AIXE scale operationalizes this expanded framework through 12 descriptors capturing nuanced AI interaction qualities.

- **AI-infused products' defining paradox requires design paradigm shift**: Their probabilistic nature creates fundamental tension—they excel at addressing uncertain ill-defined problems yet simultaneously introduce inherent flaws through statistical error margins. Research reveals 85% of AI innovation projects fail primarily from lack of human-centered design, as technologists showcase sophisticated capabilities (conversing with machines) without UX expertise to create satisfying interactions. Smart speakers exemplify this gap: advertised as homes that "take care of inhabitants" yet reality reveals systems needing constant human assistance, prone to errors, far from J.A.R.V.I.S.-level autonomy. Solution demands "design for imperfection" paradigm—defining "well enough" thresholds, identifying acceptable error contexts (entertainment versus critical applications), and building user mental models accommodating probabilistic rather than deterministic behavior.

- **Agency and proactivity transform interaction from tool use to counterpart engagement**: AI-infused products transcend delegated agency (implementing human intentions) to exhibit genuine agency—finding unanticipated solutions, learning winning strategies through trial-and-error, sensing ahead to anticipate needs (smart thermostats learning habits, Apple devices auto-activating modes based on context). This introduces "alterity" concept replacing traditional embodiment paradigm, positioning AI as distinct entities users converse with, delegate to, or seek inspiration from rather than merely extending human capabilities. Design implications include accounting for time as UX factor (recurring interactions improve accuracy/personalization), managing proactivity to aid discoverability while avoiding creepiness, and developing communication strategies compensating for hidden affordances since virtual/cloud-based operations lack physical interface cues.

- **Systematic multi-method approach enables empirically grounded AI UX assessment**: The AIXE scale's development through umbrella/scoping/systematic reviews, advanced user validation, and statistical verification demonstrates methodological rigor addressing emerging topic challenges—limited existing resources and subjectivity risks. Real-world application to 1600+ smart speaker users across US/UK markets validates practical utility for product benchmarking, temporal tracking (2021-2023 performance evolution), demographic analysis, and competitive comparison. This establishes replicable framework for researchers studying AI-infused products, designers evaluating prototypes with actionable AI behavior insights, and companies assessing products over time, filling critical gap in field lacking standardized tools despite AI's pervasive spread across healthcare, education, governance, entertainment, and domestic domains.

## Limitations & Critiques
**Scope Limitations of Smart Speaker Focus**: While smart speakers represent the most pervasive domestic AI materialization, exclusive focus on this product category may limit AIXE scale generalizability. The research acknowledges need for broader application across diverse AI-infused product types (healthcare systems, autonomous vehicles, creative tools, industrial applications) to validate dimensional relevance beyond conversational assistants. Different AI manifestations may reveal additional UX dimensions or require descriptor modifications.

**Temporal Constraints in Rapidly Evolving Field**: As acknowledged in conclusions, the probabilistic nature and continuous improvement of AI models means findings about output quality and capability limitations represent snapshots rather than permanent characteristics. The 2021-2023 smart speaker comparison shows measurable evolution, but research does not establish frameworks for anticipating or modeling how dimensional importance shifts as AI sophistication advances. AIXE scale may require periodic recalibration as AI capabilities expand.

**Advanced User Sample Limitations**: Phase 1-3 user validation relied on "advanced users" whose expertise and familiarity with AI systems may not reflect mainstream consumer perspectives. This expert-oriented approach risks overweighting technically sophisticated interactions while underrepresenting novice user concerns like basic comprehension, onboarding difficulties, or fundamental mental model construction. Demographic details of validation samples not fully specified.

**Methodological Trade-offs in Emergent Topic Research**: The multi-method approach explicitly chosen to cope with "difficulties posed by an emerging topic—with a consequent limitation of available resources—and dealing with the risk of subjectivity hindering the results." While rigorous, the umbrella/scoping/systematic review strategy inherited limitations from underlying literature, potentially amplifying existing research biases toward technically-oriented rather than UX-focused AI assessment.

**Cross-Cultural and Market Limitations**: Smart speaker validation focused exclusively on US and UK markets, leaving questions about AIXE applicability across different cultural contexts where AI adoption patterns, privacy concerns, interaction expectations, and meaningfulness criteria may vary substantially. The research does not address how cultural differences in trust, anthropomorphization, or technology acceptance might affect dimensional weighting.

**Limited Design Process Integration Guidance**: While AIXE provides robust post-hoc evaluation tool for working prototypes and deployed products, the research offers limited guidance on integrating dimensional assessment into earlier design stages. Gap exists between identifying UX dimensions and operationalizing them as generative design principles, leaving designers with evaluation framework but modest actionable knowledge for anticipatory design interventions.

**Ethical Dimension Operationalization Challenges**: Though trustworthiness dimension addresses ethical concerns (privacy, bias, transparency), the research acknowledges this "quite slippery" matter extends across multiple disciplines. AIXE's quantitative approach may struggle capturing nuanced ethical trade-offs, contextual ethical reasoning, or long-term societal implications that resist standardized measurement, potentially reducing complex ethical questions to simplified rating scales.
