# Reconciling ADDIE and Agile Instructional Design Models: A Case Study

# Study Partner Note: Reconciling ADDIE and Agile Instructional Design Models

## Summary
This paper argues that ADDIE (a linear, structured instructional design model) and Agile (an adaptive, iterative approach) can be successfully combined in hybrid form to create e-learning courses, particularly in dynamic and complex environments. The finding matters because it challenges the assumption that these opposing models are mutually exclusive and offers practical guidance for multinational, geographically dispersed educational projects.

## Key Concepts
- **ADDIE Model**: Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, and Evaluation—a waterfall/cascade approach to instructional design
- **Agile Methods**: Lightweight, iterative processes emphasizing rapid feedback, user involvement, and adaptive change
- **Hybrid Instructional Design**: Combining rigid ADDIE framework with flexible Agile practices
- **Instructional Design (ID)**: Systematic process of improving instruction through analysis of learning needs and development of materials
- **Cultural Awareness e-learning Course**: The practical case study context demonstrating the hybrid approach

## Theoretical Framework
The paper draws on instructional design theory and software engineering principles, adapting Agile methodology—originally from IT development—into educational design contexts. It positions the study within the broader literature examining how ID models can be modified and improved to better serve contemporary e-learning development.

## Methods
This is a qualitative case study analyzing the development of an "Introduction to Cultural Awareness" e-learning course created by a small, geographically dispersed multinational project team working in specific situational constraints.

## Main Arguments
- ADDIE and Agile models, though traditionally defined as opposites, can coexist and complement each other in practice
- Hybrid approaches combining both models produce positive results, particularly in complex, dynamic environments with small distributed teams
- Communication skills and personal qualities are crucial success factors in atypical instructional design projects
- There is limited existing research combining multiple ID models, creating an opportunity for new approaches to emerge

## Limitations & Critiques
The paper provides limited detail on how the hybrid model was operationalized or what specific tensions arose between ADDIE's linearity and Agile's iteration. The qualitative findings from a single case study may have limited generalizability beyond similar multinational, dispersed team contexts, and the paper lacks explicit discussion of when this hybrid approach might *not* be suitable.
