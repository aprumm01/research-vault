# Artificial Intelligence and Work Transformations: Integrating Sensemaking and Workplace Learning Perspectives

## Summary
This conceptual paper integrates sensemaking theory with workplace learning frameworks to understand how workers adapt to AI-driven transformations in their jobs. The authors argue that introducing AI into work contexts triggers sensemaking processes as employees try to understand what AI means for their roles, competencies, and professional identities. They propose a framework connecting individual sensemaking to organizational learning outcomes, emphasizing the importance of both exploitative (refining existing knowledge) and explorative (developing new knowledge) learning modes.

## Key Concepts
- **Sensemaking**: The cognitive process by which individuals construct meaning from ambiguous or novel situations
- **Exploitative Learning**: Learning that refines, extends, and uses existing knowledge and competencies
- **Explorative Learning**: Learning that involves experimentation, variation, and development of new knowledge
- **Work Transformation**: Changes to job tasks, roles, and skill requirements driven by AI adoption
- **Professional Identity**: Workers' sense of self tied to their occupational expertise
- **Organizational Ambidexterity**: Capacity to balance exploitation and exploration

## Theoretical Framework
The paper draws on Karl Weick's sensemaking theory as its primary lens, integrating it with March's exploitation/exploration framework and contemporary workplace learning theories. The authors position sensemaking as the bridge between individual cognitive responses to AI and broader organizational learning dynamics. They also incorporate elements of identity theory to explain how AI challenges workers' professional self-concepts.

## Methods
- Conceptual/theoretical paper
- Literature synthesis across sensemaking, workplace learning, and AI-in-organizations research
- Framework development through theoretical integration
- Illustrative examples from empirical AI adoption studies

## Main Arguments
1. **AI as Sensemaking Trigger**: AI introduction creates ambiguity and uncertainty that activates sensemaking processes in workers
2. **Dual Learning Demands**: Workers must simultaneously exploit existing knowledge while exploring new AI-related competencies
3. **Identity Work**: AI adoption requires workers to reconstruct professional identities when core competencies are automated
4. **Social Nature of Sensemaking**: Understanding AI is not purely individual but occurs through social interactions and shared interpretations
5. **Temporal Dynamics**: Sensemaking about AI evolves over time as workers gain experience and AI capabilities change
6. **Organizational Context Matters**: Organizational culture, leadership, and resources shape how workers make sense of and learn from AI

## Limitations & Critiques
- Conceptual paper without empirical validation
- Framework may be too abstract for practical application
- Does not address power dynamics in organizational AI adoption
- Limited attention to workers who resist or reject AI
- Assumes rational sensemaking processes that may not capture emotional responses
- Does not differentiate between types of AI (assistive vs. autonomous)
- Industry 5.0 framing (human-AI collaboration) may be overly optimistic given job displacement concerns
