## Key Concepts
- Behaviorism: Learning as changes in observable performance through stimulus-response associations
- Cognitivism: Learning as information processing, mental models, and knowledge structures
- Constructivism: Learning as active knowledge construction through experience and reflection
- Instructional design: Translating learning principles into optimal instructional actions
- Mental models: Internal representations of how learning occurs
- Transfer: Application of learned knowledge in new contexts

## Theoretical Framework
- Behaviorism (empiricist tradition): Knowledge from sensory experiences and environmental associations
- Cognitivism (rationalist tradition): Knowledge through mental processing and internal structures
- Constructivism: Knowledge actively constructed by learners through interaction with environment
- Instructional systems design: Bridge between learning theory and educational practice

## Methods
Theoretical comparison and analysis (not empirical study)

## Main Arguments
- Instructional designers must understand multiple learning theories to select appropriate strategies for different tasks
- Learning theories differ in how they explain: learning occurrence, influencing factors, memory role, transfer, and instructional structure
- Behaviorism emphasizes environmental stimuli, observable responses, and reinforcement; suited for discriminations, associations, chaining
- Cognitivism emphasizes mental processing, schema development, meaningful learning; suited for higher-order skills
- Constructivism emphasizes active construction, social negotiation, authentic contexts; suited for complex problem-solving
- Effective instruction requires matching cognitive processing demands of task with appropriate theoretical approach
- Less than 2% of educational technology courses emphasize learning theory
- Understanding deep principles allows extrapolation to specific applications

## Limitations & Critiques
- Theoretical comparison lacks empirical validation of claims
- May oversimplify complex and overlapping learning theories
- Presents theories as more distinct than they are in practice
- Does not fully address contemporary blended/hybrid approaches
- Less than 2% statistic about ed tech courses needs more context
- May not account for implicit theory use by practitioners
- Limited discussion of sociocultural learning theories
- Prescriptive matching of theory to task type may be too rigid
- Does not fully explore contradictions within each theoretical tradition
- Cultural and contextual variations in learning theory applicability underexplored
- Contemporary neuroscience perspectives on learning not integrated
