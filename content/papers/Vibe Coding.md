# Vibe Coding

## Summary
Li et al. examine how "vibe coding," an intent-driven, natural language interaction style with generative AI tools, is reshaping product design and development workflows across enterprises, startups, and academia. Drawing on interviews with 22 product team members, they map a four-stage workflow of ideation, generation, debugging, and review, and find that vibe coding accelerates iteration and lowers participation barriers while introducing new tensions around code unreliability, AI over-reliance, deskilling, ownership, and social stigma. The paper argues for responsible human-AI collaboration frameworks that protect reflection, creativity, and skill development.

## Key Concepts
- Vibe coding
- Human-AI collaboration in product design
- Deskilling and cognitive offloading
- Ownership, disclosure, and social stigma in AI-assisted work
- "Intending the right design" vs. "designing the right intention"

## Theoretical Framework
The paper applies a responsible human-AI collaboration lens, drawing on HCI research on AI over-reliance, deskilling, and sociotechnical systems. It situates vibe coding within broader debates about how generative AI redistributes epistemic labor between humans and machines and reshapes professional role boundaries.

## Methods
In-depth semi-structured interviews with 22 product team members across software engineers, UX/UI designers, product managers, and startup founders. Participants were recruited from enterprises, startups, and academia. Analysis used inductive thematic coding to identify workflow patterns, opportunities, and tensions.

## Main Arguments
- Vibe coding has become a meaningful mediator between human creative intent and product artifacts, following a consistent four-stage workflow
- The technology accelerates prototyping and lowers barriers to participation but introduces unreliability and integration challenges
- New asymmetries in trust and responsibility emerge within product teams, with tensions between efficiency-focused prototyping and deeper reflective design
- AI over-reliance erodes individual skills and creates social stigma between "AI-literate" and "AI-dependent" practitioners
- Responsible vibe coding requires preserving space for critical reflection, skill development, and explicit human oversight

## Limitations & Critiques
The qualitative sample of 22 participants limits generalizability, and the study reflects a moment of rapid tool change, meaning findings may shift quickly as vibe coding tools and professional norms mature. The focus on product teams may not capture how vibe coding plays out differently in more specialized design or engineering contexts.
