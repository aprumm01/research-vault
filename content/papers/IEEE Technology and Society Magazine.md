# Closing the Sociotechnical Gap in AI: Measuring Practitioners' Attitudes and Perceptions


## Abstract
*Abstract not yet found — search by title or check source PDF*

**DOI:** [10.1109/MTS.2024.3392280](https://doi.org/10.1109/MTS.2024.3392280)

## Summary
This IEEE paper examines the "sociotechnical gap" in AI development — the space between what technology can do and what social practice requires — by measuring practitioners' attitudes and perceptions toward AI in their organizational contexts. The study develops and validates an instrument for assessing the sociotechnical gap in AI deployments, finding that gaps are consistently underestimated by technical teams and overestimated by end users, with middle management showing the widest divergence from both. The paper argues for bridging the gap through participatory AI development practices.

## Key Concepts
- Sociotechnical gap in AI systems
- Practitioner attitudes toward AI
- Measurement instrument for sociotechnical AI gaps
- Participatory AI development
- Technical vs. social perception misalignment

## Theoretical Framework
Sociotechnical systems theory; Ackerman's sociotechnical gap concept; technology acceptance; draws on systems theory and participatory design to frame the measurement and bridging of AI sociotechnical gaps.

## Methods
Instrument development and validation study; survey of practitioners across technical, managerial, and end-user roles in organizations deploying AI; psychometric analysis of sociotechnical gap measures; comparative analysis across role groups.

## Main Arguments
- Sociotechnical gaps in AI are systematic and predictable, reflecting consistent misalignment between technical capability and social practice requirements
- Technical teams consistently underestimate sociotechnical complexity; end users consistently overestimate what AI customization is possible
- Closing the gap requires institutional mechanisms for practitioner co-design and continuous feedback, not just technical improvement
- The measurement instrument enables organizations to diagnose sociotechnical gaps before deployment rather than discovering them through failure

## Limitations & Critiques
Instrument validation in specific organizational contexts; cross-sector generalizability requires further study; the measurement approach may not capture rapidly shifting sociotechnical gaps as AI capabilities and expectations evolve.
