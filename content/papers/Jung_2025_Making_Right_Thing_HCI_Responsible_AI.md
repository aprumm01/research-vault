# Making the Right Thing: Bridging HCI and Responsible AI in Early-Stage AI Concept Selection

## Summary

This DIS 2025 paper from Carnegie Mellon University addresses the gap between HCI design processes and Responsible AI (RAI) practices during early-stage AI concept selection. The authors developed and evaluated a set of tools to help design teams systematically consider ethical implications when selecting among AI product concepts. Through participatory design with industry practitioners, they created materials including Concept Cards, Individual Rating Sheets, Team Response Overviews, and Risk-Benefit Matrices.

## Key Concepts

- **Responsible AI (RAI)**: Ethical frameworks and practices for AI development
- **Concept Selection**: Early design phase choosing among alternative product ideas
- **Risk-Benefit Analysis**: Systematic evaluation of potential harms versus benefits
- **Value Sensitive Design**: Design approach considering human values throughout
- **AI Ethics**: Moral considerations in AI system development and deployment

## Theoretical Framework

The paper integrates:

1. **Human-Centered Design**: User-focused iterative design processes
2. **Responsible AI Frameworks**: Fairness, accountability, transparency principles
3. **Design Methods**: Concept generation and selection techniques
4. **Participatory Design**: Stakeholder involvement in design decisions

## Methods

- Participatory design workshops with industry practitioners
- Iterative development and testing of design tools
- Evaluation with AI/UX design teams
- Qualitative analysis of tool usage and feedback
- Comparison with existing RAI practices

## Main Arguments

1. Current RAI practices engage too late in the design process
2. Early concept selection is a critical but neglected intervention point
3. Structured tools can help teams surface ethical considerations earlier
4. Both individual and collective assessment are needed
5. Bridging HCI and RAI requires practical, usable methods

## Tools Developed

- **Concept Card**: Summarizes AI concept for evaluation
- **Individual Rating Sheet**: Personal assessment of risks and benefits
- **Team Response Overview**: Aggregates individual assessments
- **Risk-Benefit Matrix**: Visual tool for comparing concepts on ethical dimensions

## Limitations and Critiques

- Tools tested with limited number of teams
- Long-term organizational adoption not studied
- May add overhead to fast-moving design processes
- Does not address power dynamics in team decision-making
- Effectiveness dependent on team expertise and motivation
