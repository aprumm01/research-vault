# Creative Ambiguity and Cognitive Tension in Generative AI Tools (Dalsgaard)

## Summary
Dalsgaard reframes unexpected, misaligned outputs from generative AI tools not as failures but as sources of creative ambiguity and cognitive tension that can catalyze reflection and creative reframing. Through five cross-modal cases (writing, design, music, code, prototyping), the paper demonstrates how cognitive tensions—drawing on theories of cognitive dissonance, frame conflict, and prediction error—function as generative forces in human-AI interaction, arguing for design that works with friction rather than eliminating it.

## Key Concepts
- Creative ambiguity (semantic, aesthetic, intentional)
- Cognitive tension as generative force
- Productive misalignment
- Frame conflict and reframing
- Prediction error in AI interaction
- Reflection-in-action with AI tools

## Theoretical Framework
Integrates cognitive dissonance theory (Festinger), frame analysis (Goffman, Schön), predictive processing models, and theories of ambiguity in design (Gaver et al.). Draws on pragmatist design theory and insight-driven cognition research to position misalignment as epistemic friction that triggers creative shifts.

## Methods
Cross-modal case analysis examining five examples spanning text, image, sound, code, and form generation. Uses theoretical analysis grounded in cognitive ergonomics and creativity research, presented at ECCE 2025 (European Association of Cognitive Ergonomics).

## Main Arguments
- Misalignment between user intent and AI output should be treated as provocation, not error
- Creative ambiguity stimulates meaning-making and conceptual expansion
- Cognitive tension (dissonance, frame conflict, prediction error) motivates reframing and insight
- Productive disruption requires interpretive labor but enables new creative pathways
- Design should scaffold users' ability to navigate ambiguity rather than eliminate it
- Surprise and tension sustain attention and invite playful exploration

## Limitations & Critiques
Ambiguity is not universally beneficial—without interpretive resources, domain expertise, or time, it becomes derailment rather than insight. Framework requires users willing and able to "stay with the tension." Limited empirical validation; primarily theoretical with illustrative cases. Does not address how to distinguish fruitful ambiguity from obstructive noise systematically.
