# Powerful Futures - How a Big Tech Company Envisions Humans and Technologies in the Workplace of the Future

## Summary
Powerful Futures: How a Big Tech Company Envisions Humans and Technologies in the Workplace of the Future EUNJEONG CHEON, School of Information Studies, Syracuse University, USA Big tech companies have had increasing control over how we work with technologies and how technologies define the work we do In this paper, I identify the sociotechnical futures that Amazon—one of the big tech companies—envisions and the future of work that the company is moving toward I explore the future of fulfillment centers through an analysis of the patents on fulfillment center technologies which Amazon may turn into reality one day.

## Key Concepts
- Corporate imaginaries shaping sociotechnical futures of work through technology development
- Patent analysis as methodology for revealing big tech companies' visions of future work arrangements
- Human labor configured within automation systems rather than replaced by them in Amazon's vision
- "Stepping in" moments where humans operate, monitor, and intervene in automated fulfillment systems
- Fulfillment center technologies revealing design assumptions about human-technology relationships
- Big tech control over both working with technologies and how technologies define work itself
- Patents as material artifacts encoding future workplace configurations before implementation
- Sociotechnical interventions as means to negotiate or resist corporate technological imaginaries

## Theoretical Framework
The research employs sociotechnical futures analysis to examine how Amazon's patents encode specific configurations of humans and technologies in fulfillment centers. Drawing on Science and Technology Studies (STS) perspectives on imaginaries, the framework treats patents not as neutral technical documents but as performative artifacts that prefigure social arrangements. The analysis focuses on where and how humans are positioned within automated systems, revealing design assumptions about human capacities, responsibilities, and value. This approach enables identifying "stepping in" points where human labor is deemed necessary despite automation goals. The framework emphasizes that understanding tech companies' imaginaries is prerequisite for effective intervention to shape technology uptake democratically.

## Methods
patent analysis, document analysis

## Main Arguments
- Amazon's fulfillment center patents reveal systematic vision of humans as operators within automation systems rather than autonomous workers
- Humans are configured to handle exceptions, monitor system performance, and perform tasks deemed uneconomical to automate
- Patent analysis provides unique methodological access to tech companies' pre-implementation visions unavailable through other methods
- Big tech companies exercise increasing control over work futures through technology development and implementation power
- Current automation trajectory maintains human labor while fundamentally reconfiguring its nature and autonomy
- Understanding corporate imaginaries encoded in patents enables proactive sociotechnical interventions before widespread deployment
- CSCW research should engage with patents to anticipate and shape future workplace technologies rather than only studying existing systems

## Limitations & Critiques
- Patents represent aspirational visions that may never be implemented, limiting claims about actual future workplaces
- Analysis focuses on single company (Amazon) limiting generalizability about broader big tech imaginaries
- Methodology cannot access informal design decisions, rejected alternatives, or implementation challenges shaping actual systems
- Patents written for legal audiences may obscure or misrepresent actual technical and social design intentions
- Limited examination of worker perspectives on proposed configurations or their agency in resisting implementations
- Does not address economic factors constraining Amazon's automation choices beyond technological feasibility
- Unclear how insights translate to actionable interventions given asymmetric power between workers and tech companies
- May overstate coherence of corporate vision given diverse patent authors and evolving strategic priorities
