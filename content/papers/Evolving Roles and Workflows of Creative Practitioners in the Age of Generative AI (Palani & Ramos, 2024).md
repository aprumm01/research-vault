# Evolving Roles, Workflows and Design Opportunities: A Study of Creative Practitioners Interacting with and Orchestrating Generative AI

## Summary

Through triangulated methods (10 interviews, 17 YouTube videos, 31 survey responses), this study examines how creative practitioners integrate GenAI into their workflows across domains like UI/UX design, architecture, writing, and video production. The findings reveal practitioners conceptualizing themselves as "project managers" orchestrating AI tools, operating at project-level and artifact-level orchestrations while navigating tradeoffs between benefits and challenges.

## Key Concepts

- **Creativity Support Tools (CSTs)**: Digital tools supporting creative processes (e.g., Illustrator, now including GenAI)
- **Project-Level Orchestration**: Managing information, learning model capabilities, defining goals, planning projects, specifying context
- **Artifact-Level Orchestration**: Direct work with AI on creative outputs - diverge, transform, reflect, critique, converge
- **Mixed-Initiative Co-Creativity**: Framework describing human-AI initiative sharing in creative tasks
- **Design Double Diamond**: Four-phase creative process model (Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver)

## Theoretical Framework

Builds on HCI research on creativity support tools and human-AI interaction. Extends prior frameworks (ideate, constrain, produce, suggest, select, assess, adapt) with GenAI-specific actions. Positions practitioners' evolving relationship with AI within changing conceptions of creative agency and tool use.

## Methods

- **Interviews**: 10 semi-structured interviews with creative professionals (3-27 years experience) across 6 domains
- **YouTube Videos**: 17 videos of practitioners demonstrating GenAI workflows
- **Survey**: 31 additional practitioners rating and ranking observed factors
- **Analysis**: Open and focused coding, thematic clustering, inter-rater reliability checks (79-96% agreement)
- **GenAI Tools Studied**: Midjourney, ChatGPT, Codex, Stable Diffusion, Dall-E

## Main Arguments

**Perceived Roles:**

1. **Human as Project Manager** (14/27 sources): Orchestrating information, managing context across models and sessions
2. **Human as Ideator** (5/27 sources): Using AI as brainstorming partner, maintaining creative vision
3. **AI as Tool** (12/27 sources): Viewing GenAI as high-variance search, scratchboard, exploration device
4. **AI as Collaborator** (10/27 sources): Partnership dynamic, back-and-forth refinement

**Three Key Challenges:**
- C1: Articulating creative goals in specialized vocabulary
- C2: Lack of memory across fragmented workflows/models
- C3: Aligning stochastic outputs with intentions

**Three Key Benefits:**
- B1: Helps with cold start and goal development
- B2: Streamlines process through automation
- B3: Accelerates alternative generation

**Two Orchestration Levels:**

*Project-Level:*
- P1: Gather information
- P2: Learn model capabilities/limitations
- P3: Define goals
- P4: Project planning
- P5: Specify relevant context

*Artifact-Level:*
- A1: Diverge (generate alternatives)
- A2: Transform (iterate, expand)
- A3: Reflect (assess state)
- A4: Critique (evaluate, get feedback)
- A5: Converge (curate, combine)

## Limitations

- Rapidly evolving field means findings may date quickly
- YouTube data lacks richness of direct interviews
- Self-report may not capture all unconscious practices
- 19 creative domains represented but not exhaustive
- Western-centric sample
- Focus on individual practice, less on team collaboration
- Survey sample (n=31) relatively small for quantitative claims
