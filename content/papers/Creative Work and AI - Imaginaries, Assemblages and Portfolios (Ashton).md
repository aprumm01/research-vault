# Creative Work and AI: Imaginaries, Assemblages and Portfolios (Ashton)

## Abstract

This article analyses how the impacts of AI technologies on creative work have been identified and constructed. The concept of imaginaries is used as a methodological and analytical approach to analyse a variety of grey literature sources published in the UK. The analysis highlights three interconnecting risk imaginaries in which creative occupations are differentiated from other occupations — they are safe and/or are being complemented, but are not being replaced by automation. The construction and implications of these imaginaries are questioned, and the concept of assemblages is introduced to examine how creative workers manage AI uncertainty through portfolio strategies.

## Summary

Ashton uses the concept of "imaginaries" — socially shared frameworks for understanding technology's role — to analyze how creative workers, industries, and policy discourse construct narratives about AI's threat to or enrichment of creative labor. Rather than evaluating whether AI will replace creative workers, the paper examines how risk is framed and managed, finding that creative workers construct their resilience through "assemblages" of skills, practices, and portfolio positioning. The contribution is theoretical: it shows how narrative framings of AI risk shape professional identity strategies.

## Key Concepts
- Imaginaries (sociotechnical expectations shaping technology perception)
- Assemblages (heterogeneous configurations of practices, tools, identities)
- Portfolio strategies for professional resilience
- Risk construction in creative industries discourse

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on social studies of technology's concept of sociotechnical imaginaries (Jasanoff) and Deleuzian assemblage theory to analyze how grey literature constructs and normalizes particular views of AI's impact on creative occupations.

## Methods
Analysis of UK grey literature (policy documents, industry reports, trade press); discourse analysis approach using imaginaries as an analytical framework.

## Main Arguments
- Dominant narratives frame creative work as uniquely safe from AI displacement — a construction that deserves scrutiny
- Three risk imaginaries differentiate creative occupations: complementarity, safety, and non-replaceability
- These imaginaries obscure real vulnerabilities and shape professional responses
- Portfolio strategies are a key mechanism through which creative workers manage AI-related uncertainty
