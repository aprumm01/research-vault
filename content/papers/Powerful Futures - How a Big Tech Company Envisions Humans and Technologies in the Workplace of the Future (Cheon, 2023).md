# Powerful Futures: How a Big Tech Company Envisions Humans and Technologies in the Workplace of the Future

## Summary

This CSCW paper by EunJeong Cheon examines Amazon's patents on fulfillment center technologies to understand how big tech companies envision the future relationship between humans and automation. Through careful analysis of patents, Cheon reveals that Amazon imagines a future where humans "step in" to support AI and automation systems rather than being augmented by them.

The paper argues that studying tech companies' "imaginaries" - their visions of future work configurations - is essential because these companies have outsized power to shape actual futures. By making these imaginaries visible, researchers and workers can launch more effective sociotechnical interventions.

## Key Concepts

- **Sociotechnical Imaginaries**: Collectively held visions of desirable futures that are attainable through advances in science and technology - here, corporate imaginaries of work

- **Human Infrastructure**: Following Wajcman and Mateescu & Elish, the idea that human labor often becomes invisible "infrastructure" supporting automated systems

- **Patent Analysis as Method**: Using patents as public documents that explicitly articulate how companies envision technology-enabled futures

- **Amazonification**: The tendency for other industries to emulate Amazon's fulfillment and logistics approaches

## Methodology

Qualitative analysis of Amazon fulfillment center patents, examining how they configure the roles of humans, robots, AI, and other technologies in future work arrangements.

## Key Findings

1. **Humans Supporting Machines**: Amazon's patents envision humans primarily as support for automated systems, "stepping in" when automation fails rather than being central actors

2. **Invisible Human Labor**: The patents often obscure the role of human workers while foregrounding automated capabilities

3. **Future as Fait Accompli**: Patents present technological futures as inevitable, masking the choices and values embedded in these visions

4. **Power Asymmetry**: Big tech companies have disproportionate power to turn their imaginaries into reality

## Relevance to AI and Design Education

This paper is highly relevant for understanding the broader context in which AI tools are being developed:
- **Critical Awareness**: Students should understand whose interests shape AI tool development
- **Alternative Imaginaries**: Education can cultivate alternative visions of human-AI collaboration
- **Design Responsibility**: Designers participate in constructing futures through their work
