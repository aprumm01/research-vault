# Kirkpatrick Plus Evaluation and Continuous Improvement with a Community Focus Watkins et al.

## Summary
Kirkpatrick Plus: Evaluation and Continuous Improvement with a Community Focus [] Ryan Watkins Doug Leigh Rob Foshay Roger Kaufman For almost 40 years, Donald Kirkpatrick's framework for evaluation has been used as a basic model for the identification and targeting of training-spec~ic interventions in business, government, military, and industry alike By approaching evaluation from four d~ferent perspectivesmreaction, learning, behavior, and resultsmthe model has provided a solid basis for the examination of training's impact on the organization Despite the current practice of measuring one's success according to the success of one's clients, proposed changes in the model have not beenfrequently adopted.

## Key Concepts
- Kirkpatrick's four-level evaluation framework (reaction, learning, behavior, results)
- Organizational Elements Model (OEM) - inputs, processes, products, outputs, outcomes
- Societal value-added and external client focus
- Proactive vs reactive evaluation approaches
- Continuous improvement orientation over summative evaluation
- Costs-consequences analysis across evaluation levels
- Not Quite Measurable Objectives (NQMOs) and their limitations
- Alignment of evaluation with strategic planning and needs assessment
- Mega-level results (societal contributions)
- Process and resource separation at Level 1

## Theoretical Framework
- Organizational Elements Model (OEM) by Kaufman - links inputs, processes, products, outputs, and outcomes
- Kirkpatrick Plus framework - expands Kirkpatrick's original four levels to include societal value-added (Level 5)
- Strategic planning and needs assessment alignment
- Quality management principles applied to training evaluation

## Methods
- Conceptual framework development
- Comparative analysis of evaluation models
- Integration of OEM with Kirkpatrick framework
- Costs-consequences analysis methodology

## Main Arguments
- Traditional Kirkpatrick framework is insufficient for today's organizational paradigm that demands societal value-added
- Organizations must demonstrate value to communities and external clients, not just internal metrics
- Evaluation should be proactive (built into planning) rather than reactive (post-implementation)
- Level 1 should separate process acceptability from resource quality/availability
- A fifth level (Mega) addressing societal contributions is essential and missing from original framework
- Upper-level evaluations (3, 4, 5) are not necessarily more difficult than commonly assumed
- Costs-consequences analysis can be applied at all levels, not just as ROI at top level
- NQMOs hinder effective evaluation by lacking measurable indicators
- Beginning evaluation at Level 5 (societal) and rolling down ensures alignment

## Limitations & Critiques
- Acknowledges that only 2% of organizations evaluate at Kirkpatrick Level 4, suggesting adoption challenges
- Admits difficulty in obtaining useful metrics for "opportunity costs"
- Does not provide extensive empirical validation of the Kirkpatrick Plus framework
- Limited discussion of implementation barriers in organizations resistant to change
- Costs-consequences analysis provides "coarse-grain approximations" rather than detailed accounting
- May underestimate practical challenges of measuring societal impact
- Relies heavily on case study examples rather than broad empirical research
- Does not fully address why proposed changes to Kirkpatrick have not been widely adopted despite being available
