## Summary
Clark and Mayer's textbook "e-Learning and the Science of Instruction" applies cognitive learning science principles to the design of e-learning materials, translating research on multimedia learning into practical guidelines for instructional designers. The book introduces principles such as the coherence principle, signaling principle, contiguity principle, and modality principle, each grounded in Mayer's cognitive theory of multimedia learning. It is the foundational applied reference for evidence-based e-learning design.

## Key Concepts
- Multimedia learning principles (coherence, signaling, contiguity, modality, etc.)
- Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning (Mayer)
- Evidence-based e-learning design
- Worked examples and practice principles

## Theoretical Framework
Mayer's Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning (CTML); grounded in cognitive psychology research on working memory, dual-channel processing, and schema formation in multimedia environments.

## Methods
Applied synthesis of cognitive science research for instructional design practice; translates experimental findings into design guidelines; textbook format with principles, examples, and case studies.

## Main Arguments
- Effective e-learning design must account for cognitive architecture — working memory limits and dual-channel processing
- Eleven evidence-based multimedia principles guide effective e-learning design
- Many common e-learning practices (e.g., adding background music, decorative graphics) are counterproductive
- The science of instruction can be reliably applied to improve learner outcomes in digital learning environments
