# Pershing 2006--HPT fundamentals

## Summary

## Key Concepts
- Human Performance Technology (HPT) as a systematic approach to improving productivity and competence
- Performance analysis as the foundation for identifying gaps between actual and desired performance
- Intervention selection based on root cause analysis rather than default training solutions
- Systems thinking in performance improvement emphasizing organizational context
- Results-focused orientation measuring business outcomes rather than learning outputs
- Environmental factors (incentives, resources, feedback) as primary performance drivers
- Knowledge and skill deficits as only one category of performance barriers
- Systematic evaluation of interventions tied to business metrics
- Stakeholder engagement throughout the performance improvement process

## Theoretical Framework
The HPT Model integrates multiple theoretical foundations including systems theory, behavioral psychology, cognitive science, and instructional systems design. The framework positions performance improvement as a systematic process involving: (1) performance analysis to identify gaps and causes, (2) intervention design and development based on root cause findings, (3) implementation with attention to organizational change factors, and (4) evaluation measuring business impact. This approach draws on Gilbert's Behavior Engineering Model which distinguishes environmental factors from individual repertory as performance influences.

## Main Arguments
- HPT provides a more comprehensive approach to workplace problems than training-centered models by addressing multiple performance variables
- Performance gaps typically stem from environmental barriers (unclear expectations, inadequate resources, poor feedback systems) more often than knowledge deficits
- Systematic front-end analysis prevents the misapplication of training as a default solution to non-training problems
- Business language and metrics are essential for HPT credibility and securing organizational support for interventions
- The HPT process model ensures alignment between interventions and strategic business goals
- Cross-functional collaboration improves intervention effectiveness compared to isolated training department efforts
- Measurement of business results (productivity, quality, cost) validates HPT value beyond satisfaction ratings

## Limitations & Critiques
- Implementation challenges when organizational culture resists systematic analysis in favor of quick training fixes
- Requires substantial time and resources for thorough performance analysis that clients may view as delaying solutions
- HPT practitioners need both performance consulting skills and business acumen that exceed traditional training expertise
- Difficulty establishing clear causal links between interventions and business outcomes due to confounding variables
- Risk of scope creep when performance analysis reveals systemic organizational issues beyond initial problem
- Limited applicability in crisis situations requiring immediate response rather than systematic analysis
- Potential resistance from training departments viewing HPT as threatening to traditional L&D roles
- Challenge of maintaining stakeholder engagement throughout extended systematic process
