# AI Industry Expectations and Underperforming Imaginaries (Stanusch et al., 2024)

## Summary
Panel proceedings document from the 25th Annual Conference of the Association of Internet Researchers (AoIR 2024, Sheffield, UK, October 30 -- November 2, 2024), edited and introduced by Natalia Stanusch, Richard Rogers, Nancy Baym, Chuncheng Liu, Ryland Shaw, Christian Katzenbach, Vanessa Richter, Sigrid Kannengießer, Anne Mollen, Saba Rebecca Brause, Heng Yang, Mike Schäfer, Jing Zeng, and Anastasia Glawatzki. The panel investigates AI industry imaginaries and counter-imaginaries across five papers, organized around the thesis that AI imaginaries are "underperforming" -- corporations like Big Tech dominate imaginative visions of AI futures, but their claims systematically outpace technical delivery while structuring society regardless. The panel takes up AoIR's theme of how industry pre-mediates internet technology futures and examines what alternative and counter-imaginaries look like in practice.

## Key Concepts
- "No AI without Big Tech" (AI Now Institute): AI industry imaginaries are inseparable from corporate power concentration
- Underperforming imaginaries: AI narratives that reshape institutions, resources, and social order even when technical predictions fail to materialize
- Issue preclusion and premediation: Big Tech absorbs and forecloses alternative futures through anticipatory discursive dominance
- Counter-imaginaries: civil society, civil tech, and alternative AI companies attempt to construct competing visions; they remain structurally disadvantaged
- Copilot as insider imaginaries: Microsoft employees construct and contest AI imaginaries about their own products even within industry

## Theoretical Framework
Sociotechnical imaginaries (Jasanoff & Kim 2013, 2015); counter-imaginaries (Markham 2021); issue and controversy mapping (Latour; Rogers; Venturini); premediation and anticipatory media (Grusin); AI as new media concentration (Crawford; Kak & West); algorithmic imaginaries (Bucher); platform imaginaries; science communication and public communication about technology.

## Methods
Panel proceedings with five contributing papers using mixed methods: (1) Richter et al.: field survey + longitudinal Twitter analysis + semi-structured interviews in US and Germany; (2) Brause et al.: LDA topic modelling + thematic analysis of newspaper coverage (US, China, Germany); (3) Rogers et al.: digital methods issue mapping of the Sam Altman firing/reinstatement on LinkedIn and X/Twitter (retweet networks + co-hashtag analysis); (4) Baym et al.: semi-structured interviews with 36 Microsoft Copilot early-access employees across 14 countries, conducted in two waves (Sept 2023--Apr 2024); (5) sustainability paper: close reading of Big Tech and alternative AI company websites.

## Main Arguments
- AI industry imaginaries are underperforming in technical terms but overperforming in institutional and social terms: visions of AI transformation restructure work, policy, and investment regardless of whether the predictions materialize
- Big Tech's control over AI imaginaries operates through both premediation (anticipating and foreclosing alternative futures) and preclusion (absorbing controversial issues into their own framing) -- illustrated empirically by the Sam Altman firing/reinstatement controversy as a moment when AI futures were visibly at stake
- Even within industry, AI imaginaries are contested: Microsoft Copilot early users construct both dominant industry imaginaries (AI as collaborative friend, puppy, partner) and counter-imaginaries (AI as surveillance tool, labor replacement, overconfident advisor)
- "Alternative" AI companies claiming to offer sustainability or justice-oriented alternatives are primarily still driven by technological progress imaginaries rather than genuinely justice-centered ones -- demonstrating the limits of counter-imaginary from inside the industry

## Limitations & Critiques
Panel proceedings format with five abbreviated papers rather than one developed study; the "underperforming" frame is theoretically generative but empirically underspecified in the introduction; panel heavily weighted toward European and US research perspectives; Copilot study is single-company and single product; the sustainability analysis covers few organizations; AoIR 2024 short paper format limits depth of each contributing paper.
