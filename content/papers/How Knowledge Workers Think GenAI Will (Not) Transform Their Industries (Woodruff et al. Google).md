# How Knowledge Workers Think GenAI Will (Not) Transform Their Industries (Woodruff et al., Google)

## Abstract

This research-article investigates how knowledge workers across industries think about the transformative potential of generative AI. Rather than asking whether AI will transform work, the authors examine workers' own predictions, hopes, and fears about AI's industry-level impact, finding that knowledge workers are neither uniformly optimistic nor pessimistic but hold nuanced, context-sensitive views about which aspects of their work AI will and will not change.

## Summary

Woodruff et al. conduct a large-scale empirical study with Google researchers examining how knowledge workers across industries imagine GenAI's transformative potential for their fields. The study finds that workers distinguish carefully between tasks they expect AI to transform and core professional practices they believe will remain human — a nuanced view that contradicts both techno-optimist and techno-pessimist narratives. The paper is significant for understanding how workers are making sense of AI's potential, which has implications for both organizational AI adoption strategy and education.

## Key Concepts
- Knowledge worker perspectives on AI transformation
- Industry-specific AI adoption narratives
- Worker agency in AI sense-making
- Techno-optimism vs. techno-pessimism in practice

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on technology adoption and sensemaking literature; uses survey/interview methods to elicit workers' mental models of AI transformation, grounded in their industry experience and professional identity.

## Methods
Large-scale empirical study (likely mixed methods combining survey and qualitative components) with knowledge workers across multiple industries; published at CHI 2024 with Google LLC and CSULB support.

## Main Arguments
- Knowledge workers hold nuanced, domain-specific views about AI's transformative potential, not uniform optimism or pessimism
- Workers distinguish between transformable tasks (routine, codifiable) and durable human competences (judgment, creativity, relationships)
- Industry context significantly shapes workers' predictions about AI transformation
- Workers' own perspectives should inform AI deployment and training strategies
