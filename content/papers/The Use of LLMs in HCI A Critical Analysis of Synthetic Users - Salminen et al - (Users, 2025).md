# The Use of LLMs in HCI A Critical Analysis of Synthetic Users - Salminen et al - 2025

## Summary
CCS Concepts While many user researchers remain skeptical of synthetic users generated by large language models (LLMs), their adoption is growing in industry This conceptual article investigates the root causes driving synthetic user adoption, maps potential use cases, and identifies key risks Our inquiry reveals that while synthetic users emerge from legitimate pressures in user research, they present fundamental methodological and epistemological challenges.

## Key Concepts
- Synthetic users generated by LLMs
- Validation and circularity problems
- Convincing mimicry and normative bias
- Pull factors (convenience) and push factors (resource constraints)
- ROI measurement challenges in user research
- Principle of least effort
- Serendipity elimination

## Theoretical Framework
Conceptual analysis examining synthetic users as symptom of systemic pressures in user research practice, grounded in understanding tensions between convenience and research validity in HCI.

## Methods
Conceptual analysis integrating literature review, industry reports analysis, and critical evaluation of synthetic user services and their marketed capabilities. No primary empirical data collection.

## Main Arguments
- Synthetic users emerge from legitimate pressures: resource constraints (64% time, 46% budget, 31% recruitment difficulty), privacy regulations, and IRB requirements
- Pull factors include convenience and "great quotes" for presentations despite suboptimal research outcomes
- Four key issues: (1) validation remains proprietary black box, (2) circularity - LLMs cannot produce insights beyond training data patterns, (3) convincing mimicry creates dangerous perceived/actual reliability disparity, (4) normative bias toward progressive values and positivity
- Possible use cases limited to: early ideation, competitive analysis with public data, usability testing for well-known patterns, pilot testing research plans
- Should NOT replace traditional user research for novel products, marginalized populations, or discovering unexpected needs
- Weakest link principle: flawed synthetic user information can compromise overall research quality through intractability and anchoring biases
- HCI community should understand synthetic users as revealing shortcomings in conventional user research practices

## Limitations & Critiques
- Conceptual analysis lacks primary empirical evidence from actual synthetic user deployments
- Relies heavily on industry marketing materials and reports without independent verification
- Does not examine actual user research outcomes comparing synthetic vs. real participants
- Limited discussion of technical variations in synthetic user implementations (RAG vs. internet knowledge)
- No analysis of how different organizations or contexts affect synthetic user validity
- Guidelines provided are tentative without empirical testing
- Does not address how to demonstrate value of conventional user research to combat synthetic user appeal
