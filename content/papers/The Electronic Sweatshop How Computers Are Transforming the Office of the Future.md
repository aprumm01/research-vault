# The Electronic Sweatshop How Computers Are Transforming the Office of the Future into the Factor - Review by Dominick A. Pisano 1992

## Summary
The Electronic Sweatshop: How Computers Are Transforming the Office of the Future into the Factory of the Past by Barbara Garson; Science, Technology, and Social Progress by Steven L Goldman; Technology and the Pursuit of Economic Growth by David C Mowery; Nathan Rosenberg Review by: Dominick A.

## Key Concepts
- Computer automation and workplace dehumanization
- Management control through technology
- Deskilling and worker displacement
- Service industry transformation
- Electronic surveillance and monitoring

## Theoretical Framework
Critical analysis of technology's impact on labor relations, drawing on labor process theory and workplace sociology to examine how computer systems reshape power dynamics between management and workers.

## Methods
Qualitative investigation using extensive personal interviews with workers across various industries (McDonald's, American Airlines, Procter & Gamble) to document firsthand experiences with computer automation.

## Main Arguments
- Computer automation eliminates workers' important decision-making incentives, dehumanizing the workplace
- Technology is controlled undemocratically by management who use it to discipline, deskill, and displace workers
- Computer systems place similar controls on service industry workers as industrial automation places on factory workers
- Managers consistently adopt technologies that enhance their power and minimize challenges to authority

## Limitations & Critiques
- One-sided perspective focusing exclusively on negative impacts without exploring potential positive effects of computer technology
- Limited source base relying primarily on interviews and a handful of secondary materials
- Lacks balanced analysis that would reduce polemical tone
- Does not adequately address worker agency or resistance strategies
