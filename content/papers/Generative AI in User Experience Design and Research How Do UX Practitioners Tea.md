# Generative AI in User Experience Design and Research How Do UX Practitioners, Teams, and Companies Use GenAI in Industry

## Summary
Industry interview study (n=24 UX practitioners from 8 countries, multiple companies, varying roles/seniority) investigated how UX professionals, teams, and companies actually use GenAI in practice and what challenges they face. Key findings: 1) Significant lack of formal GenAI company policies—companies informally advise caution or leave responsibility to individuals; 2) UX teams lack team-wide practices; practitioners use GenAI individually for writing-based tasks but note limitations for design-focused activities (wireframing, prototyping); 3) Practitioners struggle with prompt engineering and output quality assessment, voicing concerns about over-reliance. Study provides recommendations for GenAI integration addressing policy gaps, training needs, and tool support deficiencies. Published DIS '24 (47 citations, 6064 downloads by Feb 2026).

## Key Concepts
- **Design with AI vs. Design for AI**: Study focuses on "design with AI" (AI as component in design process) rather than "design for AI" (AI as component in designed product)
- **Policy Vacuum**: Absence of formal company policies on GenAI usage, creating uncertainty and leaving risk management to individual practitioners
- **Task-Based Adoption Pattern**: GenAI widely used for research-focused, writing-based tasks; limited utility for design-focused activities requiring spatial/visual reasoning
- **Individual vs. Team Practices**: GenAI adoption primarily individual-level with significant lack of collaborative practices or team-wide integration
- **Prompt Engineering Challenge**: Practitioners identify difficulty writing effective prompts and assessing output quality as critical skill gaps

## Theoretical Framework
**Human-AI Collaboration in UX** - Integration of AI into user-centered design activities:
- AI-enhanced tools for ideation, sketching, wireframing, prototyping, user testing
- AI as collaborative partner fostering self-reflection and creativity among designers
- Machine learning as design material requiring new practitioner literacies

**Industry Practice Investigation Framework** - Gap between proposed AI tools/methods vs. actual adoption:
- Distinction between research proposals and real-world implementation
- Company, team, and individual levels of analysis required for comprehensive understanding

## Methods
**Semi-Structured Online Interviews** (n=24)

**Participant Diversity Criteria**:
- **Geographic**: 8 different countries ensuring cross-cultural perspectives
- **Seniority**: Varying experience levels from junior to senior practitioners
- **Roles**: UX designers, UI designers, UX researchers, UX managers—diverse skill sets
- **Company Types**: Start-ups, small businesses, large corporations (all different employers)
- **Goal**: Rich insights and inclusion of different perspectives on GenAI adoption

**Data Collection**: Online interviews conducting qualitative content analysis

**Analysis Framework**: 10 themes categorized under 3 major dimensions:
1. GenAI Policies and Adoption in Companies (RQ1)
2. GenAI Adoption and Practices in UX (RQ2)  
3. Challenges and Needs Regarding GenAI in UX (RQ3)

**Research Questions**:
- RQ1: How are companies allowing GenAI use in UX departments and what policies are in place?
- RQ2: How are UX practitioners and teams integrating GenAI into work practices?
- RQ3: What are current challenges and needs regarding GenAI integration?

## Main Arguments
1. **Policy Gap Creates Risk and Uncertainty**: Absence of formal GenAI company policies forces practitioners to self-regulate, creating inconsistent practices, legal/ethical risks, and individual burden for responsible use decisions without institutional guidance

2. **Individual Adoption Without Team Integration**: Despite widespread individual GenAI use, teams lack collaborative practices and shared workflows—missing opportunity for collective learning, standardization, and leveraging GenAI's full potential at organizational level

3. **Task Asymmetry Pattern**: GenAI proves highly effective for text generation, research synthesis, and documentation but shows significant limitations for core UX design activities (wireframing, prototyping, graphic design)—revealing current tools optimized for linguistic not spatial/visual tasks

4. **Training Imperative for Effective Use**: Practitioners call for better training on prompt engineering and output quality evaluation—successful GenAI integration requires new literacies beyond traditional UX skills, particularly critical assessment of AI-generated content

5. **Over-Reliance Concerns Among Practitioners**: Professionals worry colleagues may over-depend on GenAI, potentially degrading skills especially among junior designers—echoing broader anxiety about automation's impact on professional development and expertise maintenance

6. **Generic Output Problem Persists**: AI tools generate outputs deemed "too generic" without considering designer's specific domain or company's style—similar to Lu et al. (2021) findings, suggesting fundamental limitation in current GenAI's contextual understanding

## Limitations & Critiques
**Research Limitations**:
- 24 participants from different companies provides breadth but limits depth per organization
- Online interview format may miss observational insights from watching actual GenAI use in situ
- Self-reported practices may not fully reflect actual behavior or unconscious biases
- Snapshot study cannot track evolution of practices as GenAI tools and policies mature
- No quantitative metrics on actual usage frequency, time savings, or productivity impacts

**Scope Constraints**:
- Study focuses on "design with AI" not "design for AI"—does not address how practitioners design AI-enhanced products
- Focuses on recent GenAI wave (ChatGPT, DALL-E, Midjourney) not earlier ML/AI tools
- Geographic diversity noted but cultural differences in adoption not systematically analyzed
- Company size, industry sector, and organizational culture variations not deeply explored

**Practitioner Concerns Documented**:
- Risk of skill degradation, particularly among junior designers
- Potential unemployment concerns as AI handles more tasks
- Generic outputs lacking domain-specific or company-style consideration
- Difficulty assessing quality and trustworthiness of AI-generated content
- Ethical concerns about AI bias, data privacy, and responsible use
- Workers' anxiety and public skepticism about AI in design process

**Gaps Acknowledged**:
- Surprisingly little research on actual AI-supported UX industry practices despite wealth of proposed tools
- Most existing research focused on other AI/ML types, not GenAI specifically
- Prior work almost exclusively focused on individual practices, not team/organizational levels
- Gap between academic proposals for AI tools/methods and industry adoption patterns
