# Bradshaw, Amy - Reconsidering Design and Tech TechTrends 2018

## Summary
design and technology (IDT) and concurrent events in the broader social context was engaged as part of an effort to understand why social justice issues seem to be perceived as irrelevant to and disconnected from professional work in IDT A brief review of IDT is presented, including an example reinforcing tendencies toward mechanistic thinking, and key moments when the field shifted in response to attention being called to areas previously neglected Several interesting juxtapositions found in the combined timeline are then presented.

## Key Concepts
- **Social justice in IDT**: The examination of how systemic inequity, privilege, and marginalization intersect with instructional design and technology field development and practice
- **Timeline methodology**: Juxtaposing major events in IDT field history with concurrent broader social context events to reveal connections and blind spots
- **Mechanistic thinking**: The field's tendency toward efficiency, standardization, and technical rationality inherited from military and industrial contexts, often at the expense of social-contextual considerations
- **Culture of ignorance**: The perpetuation of disconnection between IDT professional work and social justice issues through lack of critical attention to historical and cultural contexts
- **Privileged perspectives**: The concentration of relatively privileged positionalities in the field leading to a narrow mainstream and limited diversity of thought
- **Field shifts**: Key moments when IDT responded to attention being called to previously neglected areas, demonstrating capacity for evolution

## Theoretical Framework
The paper employs **social construction frameworks** (Bruner, Vygotsky) combined with **critical theory perspectives** on social justice to analyze IDT field development. The approach draws on:

1. **Historical-contextual analysis**: Examining how social, historical, and cultural contexts shape field development, practices, and dominant paradigms
2. **Critical pedagogy lens**: Questioning assumptions about neutrality in design and foregrounding issues of equity, inclusion, and justice
3. **Positionality and privilege theory**: Analyzing how designers' and technologists' social positions influence what problems are deemed important and how solutions are conceptualized

The theoretical stance challenges the field's technical-rational traditions by demonstrating how apparently neutral design decisions are embedded in broader systems of power and inequality.

## Methods
Historical timeline analysis combining IDT field milestones with concurrent social justice events. The methodology involved creating a dual timeline to identify juxtapositions, patterns, and blind spots. The paper presents a conceptual-philosophical inquiry rather than empirical research, using historical comparison and critical reflection to examine field assumptions and trajectories.

## Main Arguments
- **IDT's historical disconnection from social justice**: The field developed primarily through military and industrial contexts emphasizing efficiency and standardization (Pavlov's conditioning, Bobbitt's curricula, Skinner's programmed instruction), creating mechanistic orientations that overlooked social justice dimensions

- **Social context shapes field culture**: Major social justice movements and events occurred parallel to IDT developments, but the field rarely engaged critically with these contexts, perpetuating a culture of ignorance regarding systemic injustice that gets reinforced in design work

- **Timeline juxtapositions reveal gaps**: When IDT milestones are placed alongside civil rights movements, feminist activism, disability rights advocacy, and other social justice events, the disconnection becomes visible—showing what the field neglected and how dominant perspectives limited scope

- **Narrow mainstream limits effectiveness**: Concentration of privileged perspectives in IDT creates blind spots in understanding diverse learner needs, contexts, and barriers. This homogeneity constrains the field's ability to design equitable and inclusive learning experiences

- **Field can shift when attention is called**: IDT has demonstrated capacity to evolve when previously neglected areas receive focused attention (e.g., increasing attention to accessibility, cultural responsiveness). This history suggests intentional focus on social justice could transform practice

- **Critical reflection is necessary for change**: Without examining our own schemas, positionalities, and assumptions, IDT professionals risk assuming their perspectives are universal and continuing to overlook or reinforce systemic injustices in design work

## Limitations & Critiques
- **Limited empirical evidence**: As a conceptual piece using timeline methodology, the paper does not provide empirical research demonstrating how social justice integration would improve design outcomes or learner experiences

- **Broad scope without specifics**: While effectively arguing for social justice attention, the paper provides limited concrete guidance on how IDT practitioners should operationalize these concerns in daily design work

- **Potential oversimplification**: The timeline approach risks oversimplifying complex historical relationships and may imply more direct causation between contextual events and field blind spots than actually exists

- **US-centric perspective**: The analysis focuses primarily on US IDT history and social context, limiting applicability to international contexts where different social justice issues and field development trajectories exist

- **Implementation challenges unaddressed**: The paper does not engage with potential tensions between social justice goals and other design constraints (time, budget, stakeholder requirements, learning objectives) that practitioners face

- **Defensive reactions anticipated**: Critical examination of field assumptions and privilege may trigger defensiveness among practitioners, but the paper provides limited strategies for productive engagement with these difficult conversations
