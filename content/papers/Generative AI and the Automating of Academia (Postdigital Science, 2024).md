# Generative AI and the Automating of Academia (Postdigital Science, 2024)

## Summary
Surveys 284 UK academics about their use of generative AI tools, finding that the neoliberal audit culture and overwork epidemic in British universities predates and shapes how GAI is adopted—and that GAI tools extend rather than alleviate the dysfunctions of this system. Argues that academics' use of GAI could nonetheless be harnessed as "positive disruption" toward slow scholarship and scholarly craftsmanship.

## Key Concepts
- Generative AI (GAI) in academia
- Neoliberal audit culture
- Work intensification
- Slow scholarship
- Scholarly craftsmanship

## Theoretical Framework
Critical higher education studies; postdigital theory; frames GAI adoption within pre-existing structural conditions of overwork, precarity, and audit culture rather than treating it as a standalone technological disruption.

## Methods
Survey of n=284 UK academics about their use of GAI tools; quantitative and qualitative analysis; contextualizes findings within critical literature on academic working conditions.

## Main Arguments
- UK academics already work under epidemic levels of overwork, precarity, and surveillance before GAI
- GAI tools do not alleviate this workload; they extend and deepen the logic of productivity maximization and audit culture
- The "industrialization of academic labor" is accelerated, not interrupted, by GAI adoption
- Paradoxically, widespread academic GAI use could be a catalyst for reclaiming scholarly craftsmanship and "slow scholarship" if directed intentionally

## Limitations & Critiques
UK-specific context with strong neoliberal HE restructuring; survey-based with self-selection bias; findings may not transfer to other national higher education systems; the "positive disruption" argument is aspirational rather than empirically supported.
