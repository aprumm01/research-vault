# Shaping-the-Future-Workforce-Students -Perceptions-on-AI-and-Human-Centric-Technologies-in-Industry-5.0

## Summary
This paper aims to analyse how university students perceive the role of Artificial Intelligence and human-centric technologies in shaping the future workforce within the context of Industry 5 By focusing on students as future professionals, the study explores their expectations, ethical concerns, and readiness for AIdriven transformation in a developing country context Design / Methodology / Approach.

## Key Concepts
- Industry 5.0: Human-centric industrial model emphasizing human-AI collaboration over automation
- Education 5.0: Integration of AI and IoT into learning to prepare workforce
- Explainable AI (XAI): Transparency and interpretability in AI decision-making
- Workforce empowerment: AI as tool to enhance rather than replace human value
- Algorithmic fairness: Ensuring equitable outcomes and preventing biases

## Theoretical Framework
- Technology Readiness and Acceptance Model (TRAM): Integrating readiness traits with perceived usefulness
- Trust in Automation Theory (Lee & See, 2004): Trust as function of reliability, transparency, ethical alignment
- Industry 5.0 paradigm: Resilience, sustainability, and human-centric values
- Education 5.0: Preparing workforce through technical and interpersonal skills

## Methods
Quantitative survey of 344 university students (231 Bachelor's, 113 Master's) from Albania: 40% IT, 35% Computer Engineering, 25% Business Management. Data analyzed using Spearman's correlation and descriptive statistics (Cronbach's α = 0.94)

## Main Arguments
- Students recognize AI's transformative potential but skeptical about enhancing human traits like creativity and emotional intelligence
- Positive correlation between AI knowledge and perceptions of industrial transformation (H1 supported)
- Students with ethical concerns emphasize need for transparency and explainability (H2 supported)
- IT/Computer Engineering students more optimistic than Business Management students
- Awareness of Industry 5.0 concept is low across all groups (M=2.238)
- Ethical concerns center on job displacement, algorithmic bias, data privacy
- Education 5.0 must foster both technical skills and critical thinking/creativity
- Human-AI collaboration requires continuous reskilling and policy frameworks
- Developing country context (Albania) shows distinct challenges in workforce readiness

## Limitations & Critiques
- Student perceptions may not reflect actual professional experience with AI
- Survey methodology captures stated attitudes not necessarily behaviors
- Developing country context specified but geographic details may be limited
- Cross-sectional snapshot may not capture evolving perceptions
- Industry 5.0 remains largely aspirational, limiting empirical grounding
- May not account for variation across disciplines and career paths
- Self-selection bias in survey participation possible
- Ethical concerns may be influenced by course content and media coverage
- Long-term career trajectories and actual AI adoption not examined
- Limited comparison with student perceptions in developed country contexts
