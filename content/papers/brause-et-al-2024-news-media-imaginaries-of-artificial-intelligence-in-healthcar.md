# brause-et-al-2024-news-media-imaginaries-of-artificial-intelligence-in-healthcare-a-qualitative-analysis-across-china

## Summary
Artiﬁcial intelligence (AI) has attracted much public interest, inspiring both hopes and fears As countries deﬁne pathways for developing and implementing AI, healthcare is emerging as a priority sector Sociotechnical imaginaries, which can mobilize public support and attract resources for realising sociotechnical visions, play an important role in the trajectories of emerging technologies.

## Key Concepts
- **Sociotechnical imaginaries (SIs)**: Collectively held, institutionally stabilized, and publicly performed visions of desirable futures (or resistance against undesirable ones), animated by shared understandings of social life attainable through advances in science and technology (Jasanoff, 2015)
- **Context-specificity of imaginaries**: SIs are culturally particular, temporally situated, materially bound, and spatially anchored, developing differently based on sociopolitical and sociocultural contexts
- **Public communication of technology (PCT)**: The competition between different speakers, frames, and discursive positions about technologies in the public sphere, where imaginaries emerge from competition for visibility
- **News media as arena**: Media serve as central sites for negotiation, construction, and promotion of sociotechnical imaginaries, disseminating information and shaping trajectories of technological developments
- **Most-different-systems design**: Comparative methodology exploring whether similar imaginaries emerge despite systemic differences across countries in healthcare systems, technology outlooks, and media systems
- **AI in healthcare roles**: Two powerful cross-national imaginaries—enhancing healthcare (all three countries) and managing pandemics/epidemics (China and US, with outlier presence in Germany)

## Theoretical Framework
The paper integrates two primary frameworks:

1. **Sociotechnical Imaginaries Theory (Jasanoff, 2015)**: Explains how technologies develop depending on their sociopolitical and sociocultural contexts. Key elements include the future role prescribed to technology, specific AI technologies involved, and desirability evaluations (whether visions are seen as desirable/undesirable, which entities are affected, and rationales for these assessments)

2. **Public Communication of Technology (PCT) Scholarship**: Accounts for specificities of news coverage by formalizing competition between speakers, frames, and discursive positions in the public sphere (Ferree et al., 2002; Gerhards & Schäfer, 2006). Adds a speaker element to investigate who gets to express views and contribute to imaginary construction

The integrated framework examines six elements: envisioned role of AI, specific technologies involved, desirability evaluation, affected entities, rationale for desirability, and contributing speakers.

## Methods
Qualitative thematic analysis of newspaper articles from China, Germany, and USA (2012-2021). Corpus: 15 newspapers total (5 per country) selected for national scope, wide circulation, domestic headquarters, and viewpoint diversity. Search terms: "artificial intelligence" (English), "Künstlich* Intelligen*" (German), "人工智能" (Chinese) in title or lead paragraph. Initial corpora: 2,370 US articles, 3,422 German articles, 3,786 Chinese articles. 

LDA topic modeling identified healthcare topics, filtered for minimum 50% topic fit and strong AI focus. Final analyzed corpora: 26 US articles, 25 German articles, 38 Chinese articles. Sampling continued to theoretical saturation (14 US, 16 German, 15 Chinese articles). Coded in MaxQDA using analytical framework's six elements. US/German articles coded by first author, Chinese by second author, with memos and interpretation discussions.

## Main Arguments
- **Two powerful cross-national imaginaries emerge despite systemic differences**: "Enhancing healthcare with AI" appeared across all three countries (2017-2021), while "using AI to manage pandemics/epidemics" fully developed in China and US coverage, present only as outlier in Germany. This demonstrates how collectively held visions transcend different healthcare systems, technology outlooks, and media structures

- **Lower-level divergences reflect national contexts**: While high-level imaginaries were similar, specific manifestations reflected systemic differences—US coverage emphasized private system dynamics and market competition; Chinese coverage reflected state control and development priorities; German coverage showed technological hesitancy and ethical concerns

- **AI technologies and roles vary by healthcare system structure**: US privatized system emphasized AI for efficiency and cost savings; German universal system focused on addressing aging population and workforce shortages; Chinese state-led system highlighted AI for distributing quality care between urban centers and rural areas, addressing practitioner skill gaps

- **Speaker composition reveals power dynamics**: Industry actors (Big Tech, med-tech, pharma) strongly present across all countries, reflecting what Ozalp et al. (2022) call "digital colonization" of healthcare. Policy makers, healthcare providers, and patients differentially represented based on media system characteristics

- **Desirability framing predominantly positive but ambiguous**: AI portrayed as improving patient outcomes, reducing practitioner workload, and addressing systemic challenges, but also raising concerns about faulty AI, patient discrimination, loss of agency, deskilling of practitioners, and overreliance on technology

- **Pandemic imaginary demonstrates temporal and spatial anchoring**: The pandemic management imaginary emerged during COVID-19 context, showing how SIs are temporally situated. Its full development in China and US but not Germany reflects different risk perceptions and political priorities

## Limitations & Critiques
- **Limited temporal scope may miss evolution**: 2012-2021 timeframe captures AI's rise in public discourse but may miss earlier formative developments and cannot capture post-2021 shifts (especially post-ChatGPT generative AI era)

- **Newspaper focus excludes other media**: Analysis limited to traditional newspapers excludes social media, broadcast media, specialized health publications, and direct stakeholder communications that may construct different or competing imaginaries

- **Sample size constraints**: While theoretical saturation was reached, relatively small final analyzed corpora (14-16 articles per country) may not capture full range of imaginaries, especially minority or emerging perspectives

- **Translation and cultural interpretation challenges**: Cross-linguistic analysis (English, German, Chinese) introduces potential for subtle meaning loss or cultural nuance misinterpretation despite bilingual coding team

- **LDA topic modeling filtering risks**: Pre-filtering through LDA topic modeling with 50% minimum fit may have excluded articles with significant but non-dominant healthcare content, potentially missing hybrid or transitional imaginaries

- **Most-different-systems design limitations**: While demonstrating cross-national similarities, the design privileges identifying convergences over explaining causal mechanisms behind imaginary formation and diffusion

- **Speaker analysis depth**: While identifying who contributes to imaginaries, the analysis provides limited examination of power dynamics, agenda-setting processes, or how different actors' contributions are weighted in public discourse

- **Desirability evaluation complexity**: The ambiguous and sometimes contradictory desirability assessments suggest imaginaries may contain internal tensions not fully explored in the thematic analysis
