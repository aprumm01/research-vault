Generative artificial intelligence (AI) has expanded its role in design processes, making it essential for design students to develop the skills needed to navigate AI’s opportunities while also critically reflecting on its risks. We used a two-part quantitative and qualitative survey to understand the attitudes of communication and architectural design students towards AI and assess their AI literacy. Students expressed positive and negative viewpoints on AI and rated their AI literacy skills as moderate. ## Summary
This paper investigates design students' AI literacy levels and their attitudes toward AI tools in design education, using survey and assessment instruments to map the relationship between AI understanding and AI adoption willingness. The study finds that students with higher AI literacy report more nuanced attitudes — neither blanket enthusiasm nor rejection — and demonstrate more sophisticated strategies for AI tool evaluation and use. The paper argues that AI literacy education is essential in design programs not just for technical competence but for cultivating the critical attitudes necessary for responsible AI use.

## Key Concepts
- AI literacy framework (awareness, use, evaluation, ethics)
- Student attitudes toward AI in design
- AI literacy assessment in design education
- Critical AI adoption vs. naive adoption
- AI education integration in design curricula

## Theoretical Framework
AI literacy frameworks (Long & Magerko; Touretzky et al.); technology acceptance theory; draws on digital literacy education research to contextualize AI literacy in design education.

## Methods
Survey study with design students; AI literacy assessment instruments; quantitative analysis of relationships between literacy scores and attitude measures; descriptive statistics and correlation analysis.

## Main Arguments
- AI literacy significantly predicts the sophistication of student attitudes toward AI in design — more literate students show more nuanced, critical stances
- Most design students have high AI use interest but low AI evaluation skills, creating a literacy gap that education must address
- AI literacy in design contexts includes design-specific competencies (evaluating AI aesthetics, understanding bias in generated images) beyond general AI literacy frameworks
- Design programs need explicit AI literacy education, not just AI tool integration

## Limitations & Critiques
Self-reported literacy measures may not accurately capture actual capabilities; attitudes toward AI are rapidly shifting as tools evolve; the design student sample may not generalize across design disciplines or geographic contexts.
