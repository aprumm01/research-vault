# Patent Applications as Glimpses into the Sociotechnical Imaginary (Karizat et al., 2024)

## Summary
Analyzes 58 U.S. patent applications for emotion AI in mental health monitoring and detection as windows into sociotechnical imaginaries—examining how inventors envision social worlds and legitimize uses through the act of patenting. Finds that emotion AI patents encode assumptions about emotional universalism and clinical certainty that outpace scientific evidence, embedding ethically fraught imaginaries about surveillance, mental health, and AI authority into intellectual property law before products exist.

## Key Concepts
- **Emotion AI patents**: Patent applications for AI systems claiming to detect or monitor emotional and mental states
- **Ethical speculation**: Prospective ethical analysis of technologies not yet deployed, using imagined use cases in patents
- **Emotional universalism**: The embedded assumption that emotions are universal, legible, and quantifiable across contexts

## Main Arguments
1. Patent applications are underutilized but rich data sources for studying sociotechnical imaginaries
2. Emotion AI patents embed scientifically unsubstantiated claims about emotional detection into legally protected intellectual property
3. Prospective ethical analysis using patents can identify harms before deployment, rather than after
