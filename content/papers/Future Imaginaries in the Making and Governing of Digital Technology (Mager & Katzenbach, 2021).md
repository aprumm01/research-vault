# Future Imaginaries in the Making and Governing of Digital Technology (Mager & Katzenbach, 2021)

## Summary
Introduces a special issue of New Media & Society theorizing how visions of technological futures are not merely predictive but actively constitutive of digital development—multiple in form, contested across actors, and increasingly commodified by platform and tech industry interests. Advancing the concept of future imaginaries as performative tools, the paper argues that governing digital technology requires governing the imaginaries that precede, justify, and shape it.

## Key Concepts
- **Multiple imaginaries**: The co-existence of competing visions of digital futures across different institutions and communities
- **Contested imaginaries**: Sites where different actors struggle over whose vision of the future governs development
- **Commodified imaginaries**: Future visions that are packaged and sold as market propositions by platform actors
- **Performativity of imaginaries**: How visions of the future bring about the futures they claim to merely anticipate

## Main Arguments
1. Future imaginaries are not neutral forecasts but performative instruments that shape the technologies they describe
2. Digital governance requires analysis of the imaginaries embedded in platform business models and policy discourse
3. Imaginaries are plural and contested—no single vision of digital futures holds stable authority
