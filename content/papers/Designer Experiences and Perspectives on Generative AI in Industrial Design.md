# Designer Experiences and Perspectives on the Role of Generative AI in Industrial Design

## Summary
This paper investigates human-AI co-creation in industrial design through workshops and focus group discussions with 32 industrial designers and design students. Participants reflected on incorporating GenAI into their workflows via hands-on experiences with AI-based image generators including Midjourney, Vizcom, DreamStudio, DALL-E, and ControlNet. Through thematic analysis triangulated with observation notes and AI-generated works, the research maps factors influencing GenAI adoption in product design, examining the interplay of communication, automation, and designer autonomy.

The findings reveal first-hand experiences regarding AI tool performance, pragmatic uses for design tasks, impacts on design performance and creativity, and anticipated effects on the profession. Key themes emerged around image consistency and quality limitations, reliance on training data, designer-AI communication challenges, and the tension between augmenting versus restricting creativity. Designers valued AI for creating variations, quick visualization, and inspiration for initial ideas, but expressed concerns about functional dependency, loss of ownership, employment risks, and the potential for AI to establish unrealistic design standards.

The authors propose a conceptual model mapping factors affecting GenAI adoption: AI adoption influences professional norms; optimizing designer-AI communication enables task automation; automation expands creative space but also poses employment risks; all factors interact with improved AI performance and ultimately affect enhanced designer agency and ownership. Recommendations for designer-friendly GenAI systems include supporting effective communication, fostering ownership through iteration control, enhancing competence via automation of mundane tasks, and positioning AI as a collaborative partner.

## Key Concepts
- **Designer-AI Communication**: The critical interface challenge of effectively conveying design intent to generative AI systems, often frustrated by text-based prompting that doesn't align with designers' visual thinking
- **Functional Dependency**: The risk of AI becoming an integral tool that designers turn to throughout the design process, potentially limiting independent creative exploration
- **Sense of Ownership**: The psychological connection designers feel (or lose) when AI-generated content becomes part of their designs; iteration and refinement help maintain ownership
- **Unexpected Possibilities**: The creative advantage of AI producing ideas designers might not have conceived, particularly valuable in divergent design phases
- **Controlled Divergence**: Designers' desire for AI that provides creative variation within defined design constraints rather than unconstrained randomness

## Theoretical Framework
The study draws on Self-Determination Theory (SDT) to analyze designer acceptance of GenAI, examining how autonomy (creative control), competence (skill enhancement), and relatedness (professional community connection) shape adoption attitudes. The authors also engage literature on human-AI co-creation, design cognition, and computational design to contextualize findings within broader design research traditions.

## Methods
- **Participants**: 32 individuals - 20 junior/senior industrial design students and 12 professional designers from Turkey
- **Design**: Five workshops combining hands-on AI exploration with focus group discussions
- **Materials**: Strategy cards categorizing AI features (text prompts, image prompts, image editing) across five platforms
- **Procedure**: Presentation, free exploration (45 min), guided exploration with strategy cards (1 hour), group discussion (30 min)
- **Analysis**: Inductive thematic analysis using MAXQDA; triangulation with observation notes and AI-generated artifacts
- **Duration**: Each session approximately 3 hours

## Main Arguments
- GenAI tools exhibit both strengths (rapid visualization, creating variations, overcoming creative blocks) and limitations (inconsistency, lack of domain-specific understanding, low manufacturability awareness)
- Industrial design presents unique challenges for GenAI compared to other design fields due to requirements for 3D form-giving, material considerations, and manufacturing constraints
- Career stage shapes adoption attitudes: students view GenAI as natural digital extension while professionals express more concern about expertise devaluation
- Effective use of AI tools will emerge as a critical professional skill, potentially reshaping designer roles toward curation and strategic planning
- AI should amplify designer creativity and abilities rather than replace them; a human-centered approach prioritizing designer agency is essential
- Professional anxieties about AI relate to both direct employment risks and indirect effects on design value and professional recognition

## Limitations & Critiques
The study acknowledges limitations in sample diversity, focusing on Turkish designers which may limit cross-cultural generalizability. The rapid evolution of GenAI tools means findings may quickly become dated. The workshop format provided controlled exploration but may not fully capture naturalistic workflow integration. Students and professionals were analyzed together rather than as separate cohorts, though both groups work on similar industrial design tasks. The research did not assess actual design outcomes or client/stakeholder perspectives on AI-generated work.
