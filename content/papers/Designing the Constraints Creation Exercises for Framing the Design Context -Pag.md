# Designing the Constraints Creation Exercises for Framing the Design Context -Pages from Analysing Design Thinking(2017) - Claudia Eckert Martin Stacey

## Summary
Developing an understanding of a design problem by exploring the context in which the new product will be marketed and used is often a crucial part of the design process but has been little studied outside the fashion and textiles industries. A user experience design team in a European car company sought to understand the interests and values of potential Chinese customers by carrying out a co-creation exercise with a set of representative Chinese consumers, in order to understand how to design accessory products and services for them. This paper compares the co-creation exercise, which produced accounts of the consumers’ values in verbal narrative form, to the constraint gathering research phase of artistic design processes, which typically produce sets of constraints, usable design features and desirable emergent properties to express the space of possible designs in visuospatial form as mood boards.

## Methods
- Transcript analysis of 22 audio recordings (v1-v22) from co-creation workshops
- Comparative case study methodology linking automotive UX design to fashion/textile design processes
- Protocol analysis highlighting constraint-setting moments
- Interpretive analysis of designer discourse and reframing behaviors

## Main Arguments
- Co-creation exercises function as constraint-finding activities analogous to mood board creation in artistic design, not true collaborative design
- Designers actively constructed the design problem through continuous reframing and constraint management rather than co-creating with users
- User narratives served as "verbal mood boards" that encapsulated values in story form, parallel to visual mood boards in fashion design
- The designers treated participants as passive sources of inspiration to be selected and interpreted, similar to how fashion designers browse objects in shops
- Constraint management occurs through three sources: the problem itself, the design process, and the emerging solution
- Designers made constraint decisions without explicit negotiation, often unconsciously shaping what users could produce
- The exercise successfully revealed Chinese consumer values (status, eco-consciousness, family care, "me-time") while generating few concrete product ideas
- Cross-cultural design requires understanding not just functional requirements but the aesthetic preferences and self-understanding of target customers

## Limitations & Critiques
- Participant selection criteria (extroverted, English-speaking, "lead users") likely biased results away from typical Chinese consumers
- Designers’ constant reframing and rephrasing of themes introduced interpretation bias without explicit acknowledgment
- Limited reflexivity about how their constraint-setting choices shaped workshop outcomes
- The "co-creation" framing masked a fundamentally extractive research process where designers maintained full control
- Time constraints (10 hours total) forced reliance on role play and provocative techniques that may produce inauthentic insights
- Language barriers required translators, potentially losing nuance in consumer expressions
- Designers used cultural stereotypes and clichés to interpret user behavior with minimal evidence
- No discussion of how pragmatic choices (budget, format, participant selection) affected validity of findings
- Designers rarely challenged each other’s interpretations, creating echo chamber dynamics
- The dismissive treatment of participants as "subjects to be studied" rather than co-designers contradicts stated co-creation goals
