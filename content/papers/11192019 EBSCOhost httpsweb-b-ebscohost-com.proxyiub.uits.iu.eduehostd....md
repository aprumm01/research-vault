## Summary
Bradshaw's 2018 TechTrends perspective piece calls for educational technology and instructional design to fundamentally reconsider their foundational assumptions about the relationship between design, technology, and learning in the face of AI's growing capabilities. The paper argues that AI forces the field to confront questions it has deferred: What is the distinctive value of human instructional designers? What aspects of learning technology design require human judgment that AI cannot provide? And how should educational technology prepare learners for AI-transformed work? The piece is an early call for the field to engage AI as an intellectual challenge, not just a technical development.

## Key Concepts
- Reconsidering foundational assumptions in educational technology
- Human designer value in AI age
- Learning technology design and AI capabilities
- AI preparedness in educational technology
- Instructional design identity in AI context

## Theoretical Framework
Field reflection and critique; draws on educational technology intellectual history and AI capabilities analysis to challenge the field's assumptions about design and technology relationships.

## Methods
Perspective piece and conceptual argument; no empirical data; reflective analysis of the field's situation relative to AI capabilities.

## Main Arguments
- Educational technology and instructional design have not adequately engaged the implications of AI for their foundational assumptions
- The field must articulate what human instructional designers distinctively contribute that AI cannot replicate
- Learning technology must shift focus from content delivery to developing learners' capacity to work productively with AI
- The field's future depends on proactively engaging AI as an intellectual challenge rather than treating it as a tool to be adopted incrementally

## Limitations & Critiques
Perspective piece without empirical grounding; written in 2018 when specific AI capabilities were less clear; the recommendations remain general and call for further research rather than offering specific solutions.
