# Computer-Mediated Discourse Analysis: An Approach to Researching Online Behavior

## Summary
This chapter presents Computer-Mediated Discourse Analysis (CMDA) as a methodological approach for researching online interactive behavior. CMDA applies methods adapted from language-focused disciplines such as linguistics, communication, and rhetoric to the analysis of computer-mediated communication (CMC). What defines CMDA at its core is the analysis of logs of verbal interaction—characters, words, utterances, messages, exchanges, threads, and archives—grounded in empirical, textual observations. The approach views online behavior through the lens of language, informed by linguistic perspectives including conversation analysis, interactional sociolinguistics, pragmatics, text analysis, and critical discourse analysis.

The chapter provides a detailed overview of the CMDA approach based on the "coding and counting" paradigm of classical content analysis, illustrated through the problem of analyzing "virtual community" in two professional development sites—the Linguist List (a text-based listserv) and the Inquiry Learning Forum (a multimodal website). Herring identifies five conceptual skills necessary for carrying out CMDA research: articulating research questions, selecting appropriate data samples, operationalizing key concepts, selecting and applying analytical methods, and interpreting results at multiple levels. The chapter demonstrates how abstract concepts like "community" can be operationalized through observable discourse behaviors.

CMDA can address both micro-level linguistic phenomena (word formation, lexical choice, sentence structure) and macro-level social phenomena (coherence, community, gender equity, identity). The potential of CMDA lies in its ability to enable questions of broad social and psychological significance to be investigated with fine-grained empirical rigor, making otherwise intractable notions accessible to systematic analysis.

## Key Concepts
- **Computer-Mediated Discourse Analysis (CMDA)**: A methodological approach applying language-focused methods from linguistics, communication, and rhetoric to analyze computer-mediated communication; grounded in empirical analysis of textual logs of verbal interaction.
- **Virtual Community**: An abstract concept that can be operationalized through discourse behaviors including participation patterns, shared history/culture, solidarity/support, criticism/conflict resolution, group self-awareness, and emergence of roles/hierarchy.
- **Operationalization**: The process of defining abstract concepts in concrete, textual terms so they can be directly observed, coded, and counted; essential for CMDA's empirical rigor.
- **Four Domains of Language Analysis**: Structure (typography, orthography, syntax), Meaning (speech acts, macrosegments), Interaction (turn-taking, coherence, topic development), and Social Behavior (conflict, power, identity, play).
- **Structural vs. Semantic Phenomena**: Structural phenomena (emoticons, abbreviations, syntactic patterns) can be coded automatically; semantic phenomena (speech acts, social meanings) require human interpretation.

## Theoretical Framework
CMDA is grounded in three core theoretical assumptions from linguistic discourse analysis: (1) discourse exhibits recurrent patterns that may be produced consciously or unconsciously; (2) discourse involves speaker choices conditioned by cognitive and social factors; and (3) computer-mediated discourse may be, but is not inevitably, shaped by the technological features of CMC systems. The approach is not a theory itself but rather a methodological toolkit providing theoretical lenses through which to make observations and interpret empirical results. It allows diverse theories about discourse and computer-mediated communication to be entertained and tested while rejecting a priori technological determinism.

The framework draws on five discourse analysis paradigms: Text Analysis (classification, description, texture of texts), Conversation Analysis (interaction as jointly negotiated accomplishment), Pragmatics (language as doing things with words), Interactional Sociolinguistics (role of culture in shaping interaction), and Critical Discourse Analysis (discourse as a site where power and meaning are contested).

## Methods
CMDA employs language-focused content analysis that may be purely qualitative (observations illustrated and discussed) or quantitative (phenomena coded, counted, and statistically analyzed). The methodology involves five key steps:

1. **Research Questions**: Must be empirically answerable from available data, non-trivial, hypothesis-motivated, and open-ended.
2. **Data Selection**: Samples should be appropriate in nature and size; sampling techniques include random, thematic, temporal, by phenomenon, by individual/group, and convenience sampling. Temporal sampling preserves the richest context.
3. **Operationalization**: Abstract concepts must be defined in terms of observable discourse features; concepts exist on a continuum from more operationalizable (external, directly observable) to less operationalizable (internal, subjective states).
4. **Analytical Methods**: Selection from structural/descriptive linguistics, text analysis, conversation analysis, pragmatics, interactional sociolinguistics, and critical discourse analysis based on research questions.
5. **Interpretation**: Conducted at three levels—close to data (summarizing patterns), close to research question (answering hypotheses), and broader implications (theoretical/methodological/practical).

## Main Arguments
- Online behavior is fundamentally constituted through discourse; language is doing in the performative sense on the Internet where physical bodies are absent
- CMDA enables empirical rigor on otherwise slippery social and psychological concepts by grounding them in observable textual evidence
- The approach is particularly suited for analyzing naturally-occurring online discourse data logged from archives rather than experimentally elicited
- Both qualitative and quantitative methods have value; quantitative CMDA based on content analysis is well-suited for systematic comparisons, while qualitative/ethnographic approaches better capture complex, ambiguous phenomena
- Virtual community can be studied empirically by operationalizing it through discourse behaviors (participation, solidarity, conflict, self-awareness, roles) rather than treating it as purely metaphorical
- The technological and social properties of CMC systems may influence but do not deterministically shape online discourse; this is an empirical question for investigation

## Limitations & Critiques
- CMDA can only directly analyze discourse behavior; larger social and cognitive formations (perceived group identity, internal states) must be inferred indirectly
- Text-based analysis is limited in what it can reveal about participants' thoughts, feelings, and motivations—these require supplementary methods like interviews or experiments
- Operationalizations of abstract concepts are inevitably somewhat arbitrary and involve reduction/distortion of the original concept
- The coding and counting approach may not be optimal for complex, ambiguous, or scalar phenomena that resist discrete categorization
- Quantitative content analysis risks distortion when forcing interacting or continuous phenomena into artificial discrete categories
- The approach is currently limited to text-based analysis; methods for analyzing multimodal CMC (graphics, video, audio) need development
- Demonstrating that an online group constitutes a "virtual community" based on discourse evidence alone may require too great an interpretive leap given the abstractness of "community"
- Causal indeterminacy is common when comparing samples that differ on multiple dimensions simultaneously
