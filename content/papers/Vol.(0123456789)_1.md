# Perceptions and Integration of Generative AI in Creative Practices and Industries

## Summary
This paper examines creative professionals' perceptions of and integration with generative AI in creative practices and industries, providing a cross-sectoral overview of how AI is being integrated into photography, illustration, graphic design, video production, and other creative fields. Through survey and interview research, the paper documents the diversity of integration strategies, the cultural and professional debates about AI in creative industries, and the emerging norms around AI disclosure and attribution. The paper contributes a broad empirical landscape of AI-creative industry integration.

## Key Concepts
- Generative AI in creative industries
- Creative professional AI integration patterns
- AI disclosure and attribution norms
- Cross-sectoral comparison of AI adoption
- Creative culture and AI resistance/acceptance

## Theoretical Framework
Creative industries research; sociology of cultural production; technology adoption; draws on Becker's art worlds and digital economy research to frame AI integration in creative sectors.

## Methods
Cross-sectoral survey and interview study with creative professionals in multiple creative industries; thematic analysis of adoption patterns and cultural attitudes; comparative analysis across sectors.

## Main Arguments
- Creative industry AI integration is highly uneven: some sectors (stock photography, marketing design) are deeply transformed while others (fine art, craft) show principled resistance
- Disclosure and attribution norms are forming unevenly across creative industries, creating ethical gray zones
- Creative professionals who develop distinctive AI direction expertise maintain competitive advantage over those who use AI generically
- Cultural debates about AI authenticity in creative work are shaping adoption as much as practical utility considerations

## Limitations & Critiques
Cross-sectoral breadth comes at the cost of depth in any single creative industry; sampling across creative sectors introduces significant methodological heterogeneity; the pace of AI development makes cross-sectional findings quickly time-bound.
