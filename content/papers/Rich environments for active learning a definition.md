# Rich environments for active learning a definition

## Summary
Rich environments for active learning: a definition R Scott Grabinger and Joanna C Dunlap University of Colorado at Denver Rich Environments for Active Learning, or REALs, are comprehensive instructional systems that evolve from and are consistent with constructivist philosophies and theories.

## Key Concepts
- Rich Environments for Active Learning (REALs) as comprehensive constructivist instructional systems
- Authentic contexts for study and investigation reflecting real-world complexity
- Student responsibility, initiative, decision-making, and intentional learning development
- Collaboration among students and teachers as core pedagogical principle
- Dynamic interdisciplinary generative learning activities promoting higher-order thinking
- Assessment within authentic contexts using realistic tasks and performances
- Response to conventional knowledge-transfer practices producing inert knowledge
- Continuous collaborative process of building and reshaping understanding through experience

## Theoretical Framework
REALs operationalize constructivist learning theory through five integrated attributes that collectively create learning environments where students actively construct knowledge. The framework contrasts with transmission models positioning teachers as knowledge sources and students as passive recipients. Constructivist foundations emphasize: (1) learning as meaning-making through experience rather than information absorption, (2) knowledge as constructed through social interaction and context-dependent rather than universal truths, (3) authentic tasks providing genuine problems motivating inquiry, (4) learner agency and self-regulation as essential for deep understanding, and (5) assessment as embedded in learning process rather than separate summative judgment. REALs synthesize these principles into coherent instructional design promoting problem-solving and complex knowledge structure development rather than superficial memorization.

## Methods
literature review, theoretical framework development

## Main Arguments
- Conventional teacher-to-student knowledge transfer produces inert knowledge inaccessible in authentic application contexts
- REALs provide alternative promoting active construction of rich complex knowledge structures through authentic engagement
- Five core attributes (authentic contexts, student responsibility, collaboration, generative activities, authentic assessment) collectively enable constructivist learning
- Learning activities must engage students in continuous reshaping of understanding as natural consequence of experience
- Authentic contexts essential for developing knowledge that transfers to real-world situations beyond classroom
- Higher-order thinking develops through dynamic interdisciplinary activities rather than isolated skill practice
- Assessment integrated into authentic tasks provides formative feedback supporting learning-to-learn development

## Limitations & Critiques
- Implementation complexity of REAL attributes may prove overwhelming for teachers accustomed to traditional instruction
- Authentic contexts and open-ended problems require substantial preparation time and resources
- Limited guidance on managing diverse student needs and abilities within collaborative REAL environments
- Assessment within authentic contexts may lack reliability and validity of standardized measures for accountability purposes
- Does not address how to sequence REAL experiences with necessary foundational knowledge acquisition
- Collaboration emphasis may disadvantage students lacking social skills or facing peer group challenges
- Authentic task design difficulty may lead to superficial activities labeled "authentic" without genuine complexity
- Framework assumes sufficient time for deep engagement conflicting with curriculum coverage pressures
