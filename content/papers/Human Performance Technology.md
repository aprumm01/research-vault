# Human Performance Technology

## Summary
Human Performance Technology (HPT) is a systematic, evidence-based approach to improving human performance in organizations by identifying and closing performance gaps through interventions that may be instructional or non-instructional. Rooted in the behaviorist tradition (Skinner, Gilbert, Harless), HPT distinguishes itself from pure training by emphasizing analysis first: performance gaps are diagnosed before solutions are prescribed, and solutions are evaluated against business outcomes. In this research vault, HPT represents the applied performance improvement tradition that frames several papers on instructional design theory, professional ethics, evaluation frameworks (Kirkpatrick), and the ADDIE design process.

## Key Concepts
- Performance gap analysis as the starting point of HPT
- Instructional vs. non-instructional interventions: training is not always the answer
- Gilbert's Behavior Engineering Model (BEM): environmental vs. individual causes
- Systems thinking: HPT treats organizations as interconnected systems
- Evaluation: linking training outcomes to business results (Kirkpatrick, Phillips ROI)
- ISPI standards and professional ethics as governance frameworks for HPT practice

## Theoretical Framework
Applied behavioral psychology (Skinner, Gilbert); systems thinking; instructional systems design; ISPI professional tradition; connected in this vault to ADDIE Model, Kirkpatrick Evaluation Model, Instructional Design Theory, and professional ethics frameworks.
