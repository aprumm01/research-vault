# The HPT Model Applied to a Kayak Company's Registration Process

## Summary
This case study describes the step-by-step application of the traditional Human Performance Technology (HPT) model at Premier Kayaks, a kayak tour and surf lesson business located on the coast of North Carolina. The HPT model was applied to address lost revenues related to three specific business issues: misinformed customers, dissatisfied customers, and guides not showing up for tours or lessons. The case demonstrates how performance technology methods can be applied to small business contexts.

The analysis revealed that the company's greatest weakness was its communication network, which was causing revenue losses of approximately $4,249 annually due to overbooking, customer confusion about tour times and locations, and scheduling conflicts with guides. The performance gap was traced primarily to environmental factors - inadequate tools and information systems - rather than individual worker deficiencies. The company relied on a single paper reservation book that could only be in one location at a time, creating an "information blackout" when the book was being transported between the shop and managers' homes.

The authors recommend three information systems interventions: (1) a common database accessible to all employees, (2) a decision support system for viewing and organizing information, and (3) a new transaction processing system to handle reservations from multiple locations simultaneously.

## Key Concepts
- **Human Performance Technology (HPT)**: A systematic process that helps performance technologists bridge the gap between what is and what should be in human performance systems
- **Performance Gap**: The difference between current (actual) workforce performance and desired workforce performance
- **Environmental Analysis**: Examination of organizational environment, work environment, and worker factors affecting performance
- **Cause Analysis**: Using tools like the PROBE model to determine whether performance gaps stem from environmental or individual factors
- **Information Systems (IS) Intervention**: Technology-based solutions including databases, decision support systems, and transaction processing systems

## Theoretical Framework
The paper applies the traditional HPT process model from the International Society for Performance Improvement (ISPI, 2000), which includes:
- Performance Analysis (identifying needs or opportunities)
- Organizational Analysis (vision, mission, values, goals, strategies)
- Environmental Analysis (organizational, work, and worker factors)
- Gap Analysis (comparing actual to desired performance)
- Cause Analysis (using Gilbert's PROBE model)
- Intervention Selection, Design, and Development
- Intervention Implementation and Change
- Evaluation (formative, summative, confirmative, meta-evaluation)

## Methods
- **Case Study Approach**: Detailed analysis of a single small business
- **Performance Analysis**: Documentation of business problems and their financial impact
- **Process Mapping**: Visual representation of reservation workflows (online, phone, and in-person)
- **Gap Analysis**: Comparison of current vs. desired reservation system performance
- **Cause Analysis**: Application of Gilbert's PROBE model to identify environmental vs. individual causes
- **Fishbone Diagram**: Illustration of cause-and-effect relationships (Machine, Methods, Materials factors)
- **Financial Analysis**: Quantification of lost revenue ($1,160 total: ~$320 from no-show guides, ~$840 from misinformed customers)

## Main Arguments
- The traditional HPT process is an effective guideline for analyzing and improving performance in small business contexts
- Most performance problems at Premier Kayaks stemmed from environmental factors (inadequate tools and information systems) rather than individual worker deficiencies
- The company's reliance on a single paper reservation book created systematic communication failures
- Information needs to be accessible to managers, guides, instructors, secretaries, and customers simultaneously from various locations
- Three IS interventions are recommended: (1) a common database using free tools like Google Documents, (2) a decision support system allowing employees to compare and organize information, and (3) a transaction processing system enabling real-time booking from multiple locations
- The ideal reservation system would prevent overbooking, provide error-checking, manage confirmation communications, and make scheduling information available 24/7
- Investment in communication infrastructure would improve not only operations but also customer relations, scheduling, organization, workflow, and access to information

## Limitations & Critiques
- The case study focuses on a single small seasonal business, which may limit generalizability
- The company name was changed to maintain anonymity, limiting verification
- Financial impact calculations ($4,249 annual loss) are based on 2007 data and may not reflect current conditions
- The recommended solutions (e.g., Google Documents) reflect 2009 technology options and would need updating
- The study does not include implementation results or evaluation of the proposed interventions
- The seasonal nature of the business (March-November) creates unique constraints not present in year-round operations
- Worker turnover due to seasonal employment is acknowledged but not fully addressed in the intervention recommendations
