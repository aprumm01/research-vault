## Summary
Brown's doctoral dissertation investigates the correlational relationship between AI adoption and both diversity outcomes and job displacement in the ICT industry, using quantitative methods across a large sample of ICT organizations. The study finds that AI adoption correlates with increased job displacement, particularly affecting diverse and lower-skilled worker populations, raising concerns about AI's differential impact on ICT workforce equity. Published March 2025, this is one of the first large-scale quantitative dissertations specifically examining AI's equity effects in the ICT sector.

## Theoretical Framework
Quantitative organizational research framework; uses correlational design to establish statistical relationships between AI adoption rates and workforce diversity/displacement outcomes across ICT organizations.

## Methods
Correlational study; dissertation research submitted to National University School of Business; quantitative survey and/or secondary data analysis across ICT industry organizations; March 2025.

## Main Arguments
- AI adoption in the ICT industry correlates with measurable job displacement
- Displacement effects are not evenly distributed — diverse worker populations face greater risk
- The ICT industry, despite being AI-native, is not immune to AI-driven workforce disruption
- Policy interventions are needed to mitigate AI's differential equity impacts in the ICT sector
