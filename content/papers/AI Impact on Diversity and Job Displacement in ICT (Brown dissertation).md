# AI Impact on Diversity and Job Displacement in ICT (Brown dissertation)

## Summary
Brown's 2025 doctoral dissertation investigates the relationship between AI integration, workforce diversity, and job displacement in the ICT industry using a quantitative correlational study. Surveying 25-question instruments administered to ICT professionals in leadership roles, the study finds statistically significant evidence that AI integration correlates with both reduced workforce diversity and increased job displacement. Grounded in Socio-technical Systems theory and change management frameworks, the study argues for proactive policy and organizational strategies to mitigate bias and support reskilling.

## Key Concepts
- AI and workforce diversity in ICT
- Job displacement (AI-driven automation)
- Socio-technical Systems (STS) theory
- Quantitative correlational research design
- Reskilling and upskilling policy

## Theoretical Framework
Uses Socio-technical Systems theory as the primary lens, emphasizing interdependence of technical and social dimensions in organizational effectiveness; secondary framework from change management (Lewin, Kotter) to analyze organizational adaptation.

## Methods
Quantitative correlational study; online survey instrument (25 questions) administered to ICT industry professionals with leadership experience and AI familiarity; statistical analyses to explore variable relationships and identify disparities.

## Main Arguments
- AI integration in ICT is associated with reduced diversity — compounding pre-existing inequities in a sector already demographically skewed
- Job displacement is a measurable correlate of AI adoption, not merely a future risk; ICT leaders must engage with it as a present challenge
- Organizations that treat AI adoption as purely technical without social and workforce dimensions will amplify inequality while undermining long-term organizational sustainability
- Policy must move beyond principles to workforce assessments, training programs, and accountability mechanisms

## Limitations
- Correlational design cannot establish causality; many confounding variables in ICT labor markets
- Survey sample limited to leadership roles; frontline workers most affected by displacement are underrepresented
- Single sector study; findings may not generalize beyond ICT
