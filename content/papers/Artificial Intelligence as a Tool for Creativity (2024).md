## Abstract

Integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in the design process is a growing area of research interest. Three years after its public launch in 2022, AI has already established itself as the most disruptive tool revolutionizing how designers conceptualize, iterate and innovate. As AI technologies continue to evolve, it is pertinent that design students are acquainted with the potential of the technology and how it can be integrated in their professional practice. The objective of this paper is to explore the role of AI as a conceptualization and research tool in interior design. We aim to examine its effectiveness in enhancing the ideation process and facilitating collaboration and knowledge sharing in intercultural design teams. ## Summary
This paper investigates generative AI as a tool for supporting both individual and collaborative creativity in design education, examining how students and faculty use AI during ideation, iteration, and concept development. Through case studies of AI-integrated design courses, the authors find that AI tools open new creative possibilities and lower barriers to visual ideation, but also raise concerns about the homogenization of student work and the erosion of individual creative voice. The paper argues for pedagogical frameworks that cultivate students' capacity to use AI creatively while preserving distinctive design perspectives.

## Key Concepts
- AI as creativity tool (individual and collaborative)
- Design ideation with generative AI
- Creative homogenization risk
- Individual creative voice in AI-mediated design
- Collaborative creativity with AI mediation

## Theoretical Framework
Creativity theory; collaborative cognition; design education pedagogy; draws on componential model of creativity (Amabile) and social creativity frameworks.

## Methods
Multiple case studies of design courses integrating AI for creative tasks; qualitative analysis of student work, reflections, and interviews about AI's role in their creative process.

## Main Arguments
- AI tools significantly lower the barrier to visual ideation, enabling students to generate more concepts faster
- Collaborative creativity benefits from AI when it serves as a shared ideation partner rather than an individual shortcut
- Without pedagogical intervention, AI use tends to converge student outputs toward aesthetically similar results
- Design education must develop students' capacity to use AI creatively — including knowing when not to use it

## Limitations & Critiques
Case study design limits generalizability; the homogenization risk may be tool-specific and evolve as AI models improve; measuring "individual creative voice" empirically remains challenging.
