# Exploring Challenges and Opportunities to Support Designers in Learning to Co-create with AI-based Manufacturing Design Tools

## Summary
Gmeiner, Yang, Yao, Holstein, and Martelaro (CMU HCI Institute, ~2024–2025) study how trained engineering and industrial designers learn to work effectively with AI-based manufacturing design tools — specifically tools that take on agentic "co-creator" roles rather than acting as passive instruments. Through an observational study watching designers work with two AI-based tools on actual design tasks, they document the skills, strategies, and breakdowns involved in learning to collaborate with agentic AI systems. Their key insight is that working with AI design tools requires fundamentally different competencies than working with traditional CAD software.

## Key Concepts
- Agentic AI design tools as co-creators
- Learning to collaborate with AI vs. learning to use AI as a tool
- Metacognitive strategies for AI-assisted design
- Breakdown and recovery in human-AI design collaboration
- Manufacturing design as AI-augmented domain

## Theoretical Framework
Learning science and HCI; observational methodology draws on situated learning theory — skills are revealed through actual task performance rather than self-report.

## Methods
Observational study of trained designers learning to use two AI-based manufacturing design tools on real design tasks; qualitative analysis of observed strategies and breakdowns. CMU HCI Institute (5 authors, 20 pages, ~2024–2025).

## Main Arguments
- Agentic AI tools require designers to develop skills of specification, evaluation, and direction — not just operation
- Designers frequently misapply CAD-tool mental models to AI tools, causing collaboration breakdowns
- Learning curves for AI design tools are steep and qualitatively different from software learning curves — the challenge is collaborative, not operational
- Successful designers develop metacognitive strategies: monitoring AI outputs, recognizing when to intervene, and knowing when to trust AI suggestions

## Limitations & Critiques
Specific tools are not publicly identified. Observational study in a controlled setting; naturalistic workplace deployment may differ. Small sample typical of qualitative observational work.
