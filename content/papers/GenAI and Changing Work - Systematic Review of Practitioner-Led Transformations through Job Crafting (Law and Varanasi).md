# GenAI and Changing Work: Systematic Review of Practitioner-Led Transformations through Job Crafting (Law and Varanasi)

## Abstract

Widespread integration of Generative AI tools is transforming white-collar work, reshaping how workers define their roles, manage their tasks, and collaborate with peers. This has created a need to develop an overarching understanding of common worker-driven patterns around these transformations. To fill this gap, Law and Varanasi conducted a systematic literature review of 23 studies from the ACM Digital Library focused on workers' lived experiences with GenAI, analyzed through the theoretical lens of job crafting.

## Summary

Law and Varanasi conduct a systematic review of 23 ACM studies on how workers adapt to GenAI through job crafting behaviors — proactively modifying tasks, relationships, and cognitive orientations. The review synthesizes practitioner-led transformation patterns, finding consistent themes of task restructuring (offloading routine work), relational reconfiguration (managing human-AI collaboration), and identity crafting (reframing professional value). The paper provides the field with a consolidated framework for understanding worker agency in GenAI adoption.

## Key Concepts
- Job crafting as framework for understanding worker adaptation to GenAI
- Practitioner-led work transformation (vs. organization-imposed change)
- Systematic review of ACM GenAI workplace literature
- Task, relational, and cognitive job crafting dimensions

## Theoretical Framework
Job crafting theory (Wrzesniewski & Dutton; Tims & Bakker) as primary lens; applied to GenAI adoption in white-collar professional contexts; draws on 23 empirical ACM studies of practitioner experiences.

## Methods
Systematic literature review of 23 studies from the ACM Digital Library; studies focused on workers' lived experiences with GenAI tool integration; thematic synthesis using job crafting dimensions as coding framework.

## Main Arguments
- Workers across professions are actively crafting their roles in response to GenAI rather than passively accepting AI-determined workflows
- Three consistent patterns emerge: task crafting (restructuring AI-appropriate work), relational crafting (renegotiating human-AI collaboration), and cognitive crafting (reframing professional identity)
- Job crafting provides a more accurate account of AI adoption than top-down change management models
- Organizations should support and not suppress practitioner-led adaptation to GenAI
