# Cho Jo Park Kang Chen 2011--current state of HPT

## Summary
The Current State of Human Performance Technology: A Citation Network Analysis of Performance Improvement Quarterly, 1988–2010 Yonjoo Cho, PhD, Sung Jun Jo, PhD, Sunyoung Park, Ingu Kang, and Zengguan Chen If you want to make the ﬁeld ﬁrm and tight and hard and crystalline you have to play with your peers and keep on the ball by citing their recent work de Solla Price (1970, p 22) T This study conducted a citation network analysis (CNA) of human performance technology (HPT) to examine its current state of the field.

## Key Concepts
- **Human Performance Technology (HPT)**: Field of practice focused on engineering systems that allow people and organizations to perform in ways all stakeholders value, heavily practice-oriented but requiring theory-practice balance
- **Citation Network Analysis (CNA)**: Methodology using social network analysis (SNA) to examine relational and network patterns in citation data, providing distinctive lens beyond traditional citation analysis for observing scholarly community evolution
- **Theory-practice gap**: HPT pioneers created numerous models but lacked empirical research determining effectiveness, with "too many professionals trying to build personal brand" using different models without evidence base
- **Structural attributes**: Network characteristics including cohesiveness (density of connections), centralization (concentration around key nodes), out-degree citation frequency, and relational patterns among scholars
- **Cohesive subgroups**: Five identified topic clusters—performance, instructional design, performance support, organization/workplace, and transfer of training—showing key research areas in field
- **Interdisciplinary nature**: HPT cites heavily from related areas (psychology, management, education) but tendency to cite own articles most raises concerns about "replicating outdated theories and methodologies"

## Theoretical Framework
The paper employs **Social Network Analysis (SNA)** as theoretical framework for understanding HPT field structure:

1. **Network theory**: Examines structures and patterns of relationships among scholarly works through citation patterns, showing how authors situate work relative to others and how fields self-organize through larger citation patterns

2. **Pasteur's Quadrant (Stokes, 1997)**: Borrowed from management studies, suggests scholars should pursue fundamental understanding with goal of tackling complex real-world problems, advocating theory-driven practices combining theory and practice

3. **Field development theory**: Compares HPT's evolution to established disciplines (management, sociology) that identified themselves by mirroring other fields and developing robust theoretical frameworks

4. **Citation relationship analysis**: Explores relationships among scholarly work that traditional citation analysis overlooks, focusing on network structure, influential articles/scholars, and cohesive subgroups

The framework positions CNA as superior to traditional methods (content analysis, survey, Delphi, citation analysis) because it provides theoretical lens for analyzing relational propensities in citation data.

## Methods
Citation network analysis of Performance Improvement Quarterly (PIQ) articles from 1988-2010. Database construction: (1) collected 647 peer-reviewed articles from 86 journal issues, coding title, authors, year, volume/issue, article type in Excel; (2) coded 575 citation links by checking reference lists. Cross-checked coding for reliability. Created two text files in nodelist format for UCINET network analysis software. Used Pajek and NetDraw for visualization. Compared PIQ network structural attributes to HRD network (Jo et al., 2009) as reference. Analysis identified: structural attributes, most influential articles/scholars (top 10), frequently co-cited articles showing strong relationships, and five cohesive subgroups representing key topics.

## Main Arguments
- **PIQ network shows loose, decentralized structure**: Compared to HRD network, PIQ demonstrated more widely but loosely connected and less centralized structural attributes. Only 56.7% (367/647) of articles cited one or more times, with mean out-degree citation frequency 0.89 (SD=1.76), indicating fragmented scholarly community

- **Ten most influential articles identified**: Network analysis revealed key foundational works driving field development, providing evidence-based understanding of which scholarship shapes HPT thinking and practice—information not available through traditional review methods

- **Frequently co-cited articles reveal strong intellectual relationships**: Articles cited together by subsequent researchers demonstrate recognized conceptual linkages, showing how field organizes knowledge and which works are understood as theoretically or empirically connected

- **Five cohesive subgroups define field scope**: Network clustering identified key topic areas (performance, instructional design, performance support, organization/workplace, transfer of training), providing empirical evidence of HPT's substantive domains and their relative prominence

- **Theory-practice imbalance persists**: Despite field's practical orientation since 1970s, lack of empirical research and excessive proliferation of HPT models without validation evidence demonstrates that "balance between theory and practice was rarely achieved" (Cho & Yoon, 2010)

- **Self-citation tendency risks insularity**: HPT's habit of citing own journals most frequently (Huglin, 2009a; Johnsen et al., 2008) suggests field may be "replicating outdated theories and methodologies" rather than engaging interdisciplinary collaboration despite drawing from psychology, management, and education

## Limitations & Critiques
- **Single journal limitation**: Analysis restricted to PIQ excludes other HPT outlets (Performance Improvement, Improving Human Performance Quarterly), potentially missing important developments published elsewhere or in books, conference proceedings, dissertations

- **No causal or qualitative insight**: While CNA reveals network patterns and relationships, it cannot explain why certain articles are influential, what makes subgroups cohesive, or whether citations indicate agreement vs. critique. Network structure doesn't reveal conceptual substance

- **Citation as imperfect influence measure**: Citation frequency doesn't distinguish between positive citations (building on work) and critical citations (disagreeing with work), methodological citations (using tools) and theoretical citations (adopting frameworks), or self-citations vs. independent validation

- **Temporal aggregation masks evolution**: Analyzing 1988-2010 as single network obscures how field evolved over 22 years—which topics declined, which emerged, whether centralization increased/decreased, how influential scholars' positions changed over time

- **Comparison to HRD may be arbitrary**: Using HRD network as sole reference point assumes HRD represents optimal or typical field structure, but different fields may legitimately exhibit different network characteristics based on their nature, maturity, and scope

- **Missing author-level analysis**: While identifying influential articles, analysis doesn't fully explore author networks—which scholars collaborate, institutional affiliations, whether influence concentrates in few universities, or whether network periphery represents emerging scholars vs. marginal contributors

- **Subgroup interpretation underspecified**: Paper identifies five cohesive subgroups by topic labels but doesn't explain clustering algorithm used, statistical criteria for subgroup boundaries, or degree of overlap between groups. Topics may be researcher-imposed rather than empirically emergent

- **Practice-theory balance assumption**: Critique that field needs better theory-practice balance assumes equal weighting is optimal, but "field of practice" definition might justify practice orientation. What constitutes appropriate balance left undefined

- **Publication bias not addressed**: PIQ editorial policies, reviewer preferences, and submission patterns shape what gets published, but analysis treats published articles as representative of field without considering selection biases

- **Limited actionable recommendations**: While identifying network characteristics and influential works, paper provides limited specific guidance for researchers on how to address identified issues (fragmentation, insularity, theory-practice gap) beyond calling for more empirical research
