## Summary
Investigates AI as a conceptualization and research tool in interior design through a Collaborative Online International Learning (COIL) project between the University of Monterrey (Mexico) and Wayne State University (USA). Student surveys and reflection writings confirm that AI tools enhanced ideation, cross-cultural communication confidence, and decision-making in the early design phase.

## Key Concepts
- AI literacy (Long & Magerko framework)
- COIL (Collaborative Online International Learning)
- Ideation and conceptualization
- Intercultural design teams
- Human-AI collaboration

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on Long and Magerko's (2020) AI literacy framework and Markauskaite et al.'s (2022) cognitive-humanistic-social orientation model for human-AI capabilities; positions AI as a cognitive teammate.

## Methods
COIL case study with interior design students across two universities; surveys and reflection writings collected to gauge creativity, idea exchange, and decision-making; documentation of all AI-generated design variations.

## Main Arguments
- AI tools are most effective in early-stage ideation, enabling rapid exploration of design possibilities
- Students developed greater self-confidence communicating within culturally diverse teams when AI handled initial visual generation
- A significant training gap exists: 60% of industry professionals have received no formal AI training
- Design education must explicitly address AI literacy to prepare students for evolving professional demands

## Limitations & Critiques
Single COIL project in a narrow disciplinary context (interior design); no control group; findings from two specific institutions may not generalize to other design disciplines or cultural settings.
