# methodology-legend-and-rhetoric-the-constructions-of-ai-by-academia-industry-eynon-young-2020

## Summary
Artificial intelligence (AI) is again attracting significant attention across all areas of social life One important sphere of focus is education; many policy makers across the globe view lifelong learning as an essential means to prepare society for an “AI future” and look to AI as a way to “deliver” learning opportunities to meet these needs AI is a complex social, cultural, and material artifact that is understood and constructed by different stakeholders in varied ways, and these differences have significant social and educational implications that need to be explored.

## Key Concepts
- **AI as social, cultural, and material artifact** constructed differently by stakeholders
- **Methodology, legend, and rhetoric** in AI constructions
- Academia vs industry constructions of AI
- AI in education and lifelong learning policy discourse
- **Varied stakeholder understandings** with significant social and educational implications
- AI future as policy imperative vs AI as delivery mechanism for learning
- Complex and contested meanings of AI across contexts

## Theoretical Framework
- **Social construction of technology (SCOT)**: Different stakeholder groups construct AI differently
- **Sociotechnical systems theory**: AI as material-cultural-social assemblage
- **Discourse analysis**: Rhetorical and narrative strategies in AI construction
- Sociology of scientific knowledge

## Methods
interview

## Main Arguments
- AI is constructed in varied ways by academia and industry with significant implications
- Methodology, legend, and rhetoric shape how AI is understood and promoted
- Educational policy frames AI as both future to prepare for and solution to deliver learning
- Differences in AI constructions have important social and educational consequences
- Understanding stakeholder constructions essential for responsible AI development
- Academic and industry framings of AI serve different interests and purposes
- Need to explore how varied AI constructions shape educational practice and policy

## Limitations & Critiques
- Published 2020 (Eynon-Young), may not capture post-2020 generative AI developments
- Focus on academia and industry may miss other important stakeholder voices
- Interview methodology captures stated constructions but not all implicit assumptions
- Need for ongoing analysis as AI constructions evolve rapidly
- Geographic and cultural specificity of findings requires consideration
- Balance between describing constructions and evaluating their validity
