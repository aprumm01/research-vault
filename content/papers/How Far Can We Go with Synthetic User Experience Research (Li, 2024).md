# How Far Can We Go with Synthetic User Experience Research? (Li, 2024)

## Summary
Jie Li (EPAM) writes a practitioner perspective column in ACM Interactions (May-June 2024) examining the limits of synthetic UX research -- AI-generated substitutes for field studies, user interviews, and usability testing. Drawing on interviews with HCI researchers and practitioners (Lowies, Bouzit, Dong, Rizk, Ishii, Kunze), Li presents a nuanced picture: synthetic UXR has genuine value for augmenting minority data, pilot testing, and automating low-level analysis tasks, but 70% of AI-generated data lacks the contextual depth of real user engagement, misses novel insights, and produces averaged results. Li concludes with a practical taxonomy of what GenAI can and cannot do across standard UX research methods.

## Key Concepts
- 70% of AI-generated UXR data lacks contextual depth compared to real user research (practitioner observation)
- Synthetic data value for minority data augmentation and bias reduction in data-scarce domains
- LLMs interpolate from known data; humans extrapolate to novel contexts (Kunze distinction)
- GenAI cannot replicate field study's authentic connections, observation, and emotional resonance
- Practical taxonomy: what GenAI can do (templates, basic thematic coding, pattern identification) vs. cannot do (contextual fieldwork, genuine empathy, novel insight generation)
- "There is no such thing as an average user" (Ishii) as the philosophical anchor

## Theoretical Framework
Practitioner-oriented perspective; draws on expert interviews with named HCI researchers and practitioners; Interactions magazine column format; no formal methods; positioned as a critical counterpoint to industry hype around synthetic UX platforms; published in ACM Interactions (May-June 2024).

## Methods
Expert interview synthesis: Li interviewed six researchers and practitioners (Lowies, Bouzit, Dong, Rizk, Ishii, Kunze) and synthesized their perspectives; personal practitioner reflection grounded in field experience (crowd management studies, MotoGP broadcasting, outdoor product evaluation); practical taxonomy of GenAI capabilities mapped against standard UX research methods.

## Main Arguments
- The hype around synthetic UXR ("user research without the user") is overblown: 70% of AI-generated data is too shallow and averaged to capture the nuanced, contextual, and unexpected insights that make user research valuable
- The value of synthetic UXR is real but narrow: it can augment minority group data, reduce bias in data-scarce contexts, automate routine analysis tasks, and generate templates -- but it cannot replace the authentic human connection, field observation, or genuine empathy that drives insight generation
- The key epistemological limit is that LLMs interpolate within their training data while humans extrapolate beyond known categories -- and user research is most valuable precisely when it reveals what no one yet knows
- Practitioners and researchers should ask how much they want AI involved in research -- as tool, companion, or collaborator -- rather than framing the question as AI replacement vs. human preservation

## Limitations & Critiques
Column-format piece with no systematic methodology; expert sample is convenience-based and may not represent the field broadly; practitioner observations (70% figure) are anecdotal; the taxonomy of what GenAI can and cannot do reflects 2024 capabilities and may need updating; the author's personal field research framing is vivid but may not generalize.
