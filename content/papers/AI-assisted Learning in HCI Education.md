## Summary
This paper examines how AI-assisted learning tools are being integrated into HCI education specifically, investigating how AI tutors, code assistants, and feedback systems affect HCI learning outcomes, student engagement, and the development of human-centered design skills. The study finds that AI assistance in HCI courses can support technical skill development effectively but shows mixed results for developing the empathy, contextual reasoning, and critical reflection that are central to HCI practice. The paper calls for HCI-specific AI learning tool design that preserves the discipline's distinctive pedagogical values.

## Key Concepts
- AI-assisted learning in HCI courses
- AI tutoring for technical HCI skills
- Empathy and contextual reasoning in AI-mediated HCI education
- HCI-specific pedagogical values and AI tools
- Learning outcome assessment in AI-assisted HCI courses

## Theoretical Framework
HCI pedagogy; AI in education; constructivist learning; draws on HCI learning science research and AI tutoring literature.

## Methods
Empirical study of AI-assisted learning tools in HCI courses; comparison of learning outcomes with and without AI assistance; student and instructor surveys; qualitative analysis of AI-assisted design work quality.

## Main Arguments
- AI assistance improves technical HCI skill development (prototyping, usability evaluation, coding) but shows limited benefit for developing empathic user research skills
- The efficiency gains from AI assistance in HCI courses may come at the cost of the reflective struggle that builds deeper design reasoning
- HCI educators must be intentional about which course activities benefit from AI support and which must remain unaugmented
- AI learning tools designed for HCI must preserve the discipline's user-centered epistemology, not just optimize for information transfer

## Limitations & Critiques
Single-discipline study may not generalize; learning outcome measures may not capture tacit HCI skill development; the rapid evolution of AI tools makes specific findings time-sensitive.
