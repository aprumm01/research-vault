## Key Concepts
- **Conversational AI for UX evaluation**: Using dialogue-based AI assistants (chatbots, voice interfaces) to support user experience research and usability testing processes
- **Voice versus text modality comparison**: Examining how question-asking medium (spoken vs. typed) affects UX evaluation quality, user comfort, and information elicitation
- **Question formulation strategies**: How UX practitioners craft questions for AI assistants to extract useful evaluation insights, including prompt engineering and conversational flow design
- **AI-mediated user research**: Integration of conversational AI into traditional UX research methods, potentially automating or augmenting aspects of user interviews, think-aloud protocols, or heuristic evaluation
- **Natural language interaction for research**: Leveraging conversational interfaces' accessibility and naturalistic interaction patterns for UX data collection

## Main Arguments
- **Modality significantly affects UX evaluation quality**: Voice and text interactions with conversational AI produce different question formulation patterns, evaluation depth, and information richness in UX research contexts
- **Voice enables more natural exploratory inquiry**: Spoken interactions may facilitate free-flowing, exploratory questioning patterns that better support qualitative UX research needs compared to text entry's friction
- **Text provides greater control and precision**: Written questions allow more careful formulation, editing, and specification, potentially producing more focused and systematic UX evaluations
- **Question formulation requires new skills**: UX practitioners need training in prompting conversational AI effectively; traditional interview skills don't directly translate to AI collaboration contexts
- **AI assistants complement rather than replace**: Conversational AI tools best serve as augmentation for UX researchers rather than autonomous evaluation agents, requiring human judgment for interpretation
- **Accessibility implications**: Voice and text modalities create different inclusion/exclusion patterns, affecting which UX practitioners can effectively leverage conversational AI assistance

## Limitations & Critiques
- **AI capability limitations**: Conversational AI assistants' current limitations (hallucinations, context understanding, domain expertise) may produce misleading UX insights that practitioners must identify
- **Ecological validity questions**: Laboratory studies of AI-assisted UX evaluation may not reflect real-world constraints, stakeholder pressures, or multi-method integration challenges
- **Expertise level confounds**: Novice versus expert UX practitioners may show different patterns in leveraging voice vs. text AI assistance, but studies may not adequately account for experience effects
- **Missing longitudinal perspective**: Short-term studies miss how practitioners develop skills, preferences, and effective strategies with extended conversational AI use over time
- **Context specificity**: Findings may depend on specific AI systems tested (ChatGPT, custom tools), UX evaluation types (usability testing vs. expert review), or product domains
- **Question quality metrics unclear**: Difficult to objectively assess "good" versus "poor" questions without seeing downstream effects on actual UX evaluation outcomes and design improvements
- **Voice transcription challenges**: Spoken interactions depend on speech recognition accuracy, accents, environment noise—factors that may affect voice modality usability but aren't fundamental to the approach
- **Ethical considerations underexplored**: Limited attention to data privacy, consent, or appropriateness of AI-mediated interactions with research participants in sensitive UX contexts
