# Willis-CulturesContemporaryInstructional-2011

## Summary
The Cultures of Contemporary Instructional Design Scholarship, Part One: Developments Based on Behavioral and Cognitive Science Foundations Author(s): Jerry Willis Source: Educational Technology, January-February 2011, Vol 1 (January-February 2011), pp Published by: Educational Technology Publications, Inc Stable URL: https://www.

## Key Concepts
- **Four cultures of ID scholarship**: (1) Traditional behavioral ISD/ADDIE, (2) Cognitive science/learning sciences (including Design-Based Research), (3) Constructivist ID, (4) Critical theory/critical pedagogy
- **Design culture**: Broader framework for thinking about design as integral component of culture (Nelson & Stolterman)
- **Evolutionary development**: Behavioral to cognitive transitions were evolutionary, not revolutionary
- **Design-Based Research (DBR)**: New cognitive ID model that cannot be easily integrated into traditional ADDIE/ISD models
- **Gagné's nine events of instruction**: Converting abstract learning theory into concrete instructional guidance
- **Reductionist approach**: Breaking content into small units taught discretely through task/content analysis
- **Positivist epistemology**: Assumption that scientific research can discover laws of behavior to guide design

## Theoretical Framework
Examines ID scholarship through lens of competing theoretical traditions. Traces evolution from behavioral foundations (Skinner) through cognitive science (information processing theory, communications theory) to constructivist and critical approaches. Uses concept of "cultures" to frame different paradigms as related but distinct scholarly communities.

## Methods
- Historical analysis of ID field development from 1960s to 2011
- Literature review across four theoretical traditions
- Analysis of canonical texts and models (Dick & Carey, Gagné, ADDIE/ISD)
- Examination of journal emergence (e.g., Instructional Science launched 1972)
- Comparative analysis of theoretical influences and epistemological foundations
- Part one of two-part article series

## Main Arguments
- **Four distinct cultures exist**: ID scholarship operates across four families with different theoretical foundations and assumptions
- **Not Either-Or alternatives**: These four foundations need not be treated as mutually exclusive paradigms requiring rejection of others
- **Behavioral and cognitive are fellow travelers**: Despite differences, both share reductionist approach, positivist epistemology, and focus on discrete skills
- **Cognitive revolution gradual**: Shift from behavioral to cognitive theories was evolutionary, not disruptive to core ID models
- **Recent cognitive work more radical**: Learning sciences and DBR represent more fundamental challenges to traditional ISD that cannot be easily integrated
- **Gagné's lasting influence**: Nine events of instruction translated theory into practice and influenced virtually all subsequent ID models for decades
- **Theoretical diversity increasing**: Field moving beyond single dominant paradigm toward multiple coexisting approaches
- **ADDIE remains influential despite criticism**: Linear ISD models persist even as alternatives emerge

## Limitations & Critiques
- 2011 publication means analysis predates recent AI and learning technology developments
- Part one only covers two of four cultures (behavioral and cognitive), limiting complete picture
- May oversimplify complex theoretical distinctions within each "culture"
- Limited discussion of how practitioners actually navigate multiple paradigms in practice
- Focus on academic scholarship may not fully reflect practitioner realities
- Geographic and cultural scope appears US-centric
- Does not fully address power dynamics between competing cultures in academic institutions
