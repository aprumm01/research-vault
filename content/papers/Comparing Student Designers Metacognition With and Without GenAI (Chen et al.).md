# Comparing Student Designers' Metacognition With and Without GenAI (Chen et al.)

## Abstract

This study investigated the role of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) and metacognitive operations within the design process of student designers. By comparing design processes conducted with and without GenAI assistance, the research examines how the presence of GenAI tools affects the frequency, type, and quality of metacognitive behaviors exhibited by design students at National Cheng Kung University's Industrial Design department.

## Summary

Chen et al. conduct a comparative study of student designers' metacognitive processes when working with vs. without GenAI tools, finding that AI assistance changes how and when students self-monitor, evaluate, and regulate their design thinking. The paper is significant for design education because it provides empirical evidence about cognitive consequences — not just output quality — of GenAI tool use. The study raises concerns about whether GenAI use may reduce the metacognitive effort that is essential to developing design judgment.

## Key Concepts
- Metacognition in design (self-monitoring, self-regulation, self-evaluation)
- GenAI-assisted vs. unassisted design processes
- Design thinking stages and cognitive operations
- Preliminary/exploratory study methodology

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on metacognition theory (Flavell) applied to design cognition, using process-tracing methodology to observe when students engage in planning, monitoring, and evaluating their own design work relative to AI assistance availability.

## Methods
Preliminary empirical study at National Cheng Kung University; comparative within-subjects or between-subjects design with student designers completing design tasks with and without GenAI tools; verbal protocol or think-aloud analysis of metacognitive operations.

## Main Arguments
- GenAI availability alters the pattern of metacognitive operations in student design processes
- AI tools may reduce metacognitive load in ways that hinder deep learning
- Design education must attend to cognitive process effects of GenAI, not just artifact quality
- Preliminary findings call for longitudinal studies on skill development with AI assistance
