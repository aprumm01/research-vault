# Designers Articulation of Design Judgements in Cross-Cultural User Research

## Summary
This paper on designers' articulation and activation of instrumental design judgements in cross-cultural user research (published in CoDesign) examines how design researchers make and communicate judgments in complex, cross-cultural research contexts. The research investigates how designers articulate their reasoning during cross-cultural user research activities, documenting the tacit and explicit knowledge that professional designers activate when working across cultural boundaries. The paper contributes to understanding of design judgment in international and cross-cultural design practice.

## Key Concepts
- Instrumental design judgment in cross-cultural contexts
- Design researcher cognition and decision-making
- Cross-cultural user research practice
- Tacit knowledge in design practice
- CoDesign and participatory research across cultures

## Theoretical Framework
Design cognition; reflective practice; cross-cultural design research; draws on design judgment theory (Stolterman) and cross-cultural research methodology.

## Methods
Case study of cross-cultural user research; qualitative analysis of designer judgment episodes; documentation of articulation and activation of design knowledge across cultural contexts.

## Main Arguments
- Cross-cultural design contexts require designers to articulate and adapt instrumental judgments that are typically tacit in single-culture research
- Successful cross-cultural design research requires metacognitive awareness of one's own cultural assumptions about appropriate research methods
- Design researchers develop culturally adaptive competencies through experience that are difficult to transmit through method instruction alone
- Cross-cultural design practice surfaces the situatedness of design judgment that universalistic design method frameworks obscure
