# Perceptions and Integration of Generative Artificial Intelligence in Creative Practices and Industries: A Scoping Review and Conceptual Model

## Summary
This scoping review examines how creative professionals perceive and integrate generative AI (GenAI) technologies across four domains: visual art and design, writing and literature, performing arts, and environmental and spatial design. The authors analyzed 57 empirical papers (2022-2025) following PRISMA-ScR guidelines to map current applications, professional attitudes, and integration patterns. They found universal patterns of ambivalence across all creative fields, with career stage emerging as a critical factor: entry-level professionals demonstrate enthusiasm and view GenAI as a natural extension of digital tools, while senior practitioners express skepticism about expertise devaluation.

The authors propose a conceptual framework positioning creative professionals' attitudes along two dimensions: knowledge codifiability (tacit/embodied to explicit/codifiable) and output materiality (physical/permanent to digital/ephemeral). They identify an inverse relationship between integration levels and traditional notions of "pure" creativity: fields prioritizing embodied practice and cultural authenticity (fine arts, literary fiction, classical music) show the most significant resistance, while commercially oriented domains embrace higher adoption for efficiency gains. Key tensions include balancing productivity with meaningful engagement, volume with distinction, precision with serendipity, and individual expression with collective intelligence.

## Key Concepts
- **Knowledge Codifiability**: The spectrum from tacit/embodied knowledge (resisting formalization) to explicit/codifiable knowledge (systematizable)
- **Output Materiality**: The spectrum from physical/permanent artifacts to digital/ephemeral outputs
- **Definitional Capture**: Redefining a broad, multidisciplinary practice as a narrow, execution-focused one
- **Hybrid Methodologies**: Approaches that combine AI capabilities with traditional methods while preserving human judgment in convergent creative phases

## Theoretical Framework
The framework draws on Polanyi's tacit-explicit knowledge spectrum and cultural theories of materiality (Benjamin, Latour). The authors examine how disciplinary epistemologies shape creative agency through divergent-convergent frameworks, temporal relationships, and cultural practices. They connect integration patterns to Bourdieu's field theory and rules of art, explaining how symbolic capital and authenticity concerns influence adoption.

## Methods
The authors conducted a scoping review following PRISMA-ScR guidelines, searching multiple databases for empirical studies on professional creative practitioners' experiences with GenAI. They extracted data on professional perceptions, integration patterns, integration levels, domain-specific applications, and impacts on creative practice from 57 included papers.

## Main Arguments
- Career stage dramatically shapes attitudes: entry-level professionals embrace GenAI while senior practitioners fear expertise devaluation
- All fields position GenAI in early-stage conceptualization rather than final production, preserving human judgment for convergent phases
- Integration levels follow an inverse relationship with proximity to "pure" creativity and embodied practice
- Creative fields are experiencing three fundamental shifts: from creation to curation/meta-creation, from mechanical skill to ideation proficiency, and emergence of new literacies like prompt engineering
- Practitioners develop hybrid methodologies to resist standardization while leveraging AI capabilities
- Ethical concerns include algorithmic bias, cultural homogenization, and "neocolonization" through Western-centric AI systems

## Limitations & Critiques
- The rapid pace of GenAI advancement may quickly date findings
- Studies are predominantly small-scale and qualitative, relying on self-reported data
- Participant representation skews toward Western/Northern, digitally-connected practitioners
- The categorical separation of creative fields may miss hybrid practices and cross-disciplinary pollination
- Limited attention to Global South perspectives and issues of unequal access
