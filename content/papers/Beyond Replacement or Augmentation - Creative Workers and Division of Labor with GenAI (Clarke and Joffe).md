# Beyond Replacement or Augmentation: Creative Workers and Division of Labor with GenAI (Clarke and Joffe)

## Abstract

The introduction of generative AI tools such as ChatGPT into creative workplaces has sparked highly visible but binary replacement/augmentation debates. This study reframes the argument by examining how creative professionals re-specify a division of labor with these tools. Through 17 ethnomethodologically informed interviews with international creative agency workers, the authors demonstrate how roles are assigned to generative AI tools, how their contributions are modified and reframed, and how creative identity is maintained or renegotiated in the process.

## Summary

Clarke and Joffe argue that the replacement/augmentation binary is inadequate for describing how creative workers actually integrate GenAI tools — instead, workers actively reconfigure divisions of labor, assigning AI specific roles while preserving their own creative authority. Drawing on ethnomethodologically informed interviews with creative agency professionals, the study shows that the process is dynamic and negotiated rather than fixed. The key contribution is a more granular, practice-oriented account of human–AI collaboration in creative workplaces.

## Key Concepts
- Division of labor reconfiguration (assigning roles to AI tools)
- Ethnomethodology (studying members' methods for practical action)
- Creative identity maintenance under AI integration
- Role specification and boundary-setting with GenAI

## Theoretical Framework
Uses ethnomethodology as both a method and analytical lens to examine how creative professionals make sense of and manage AI tool roles in their everyday work, foregrounding members' practical reasoning rather than structural or economic analysis.

## Methods
17 semi-structured, ethnomethodologically informed interviews with creative agency workers from international contexts; inductive thematic analysis of how participants described and enacted labor divisions with GenAI tools.

## Main Arguments
- The replacement vs. augmentation framing misrepresents how creative workers experience GenAI
- Workers actively construct and renegotiate the AI's role in their workflows
- Creative identity is preserved through deliberate boundary-setting with AI contributions
- "Division of labor" is a more precise analytical frame than augmentation/replacement
