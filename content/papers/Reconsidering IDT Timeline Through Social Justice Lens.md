# Reconsidering the Instructional Design and Technology Timeline Through a Lens of Social Justice

## Summary
This reflective article challenges the traditional narrative of Instructional Design and Technology (IDT) history by juxtaposing canonical field events with concurrent social justice events in the United States. Bradshaw argues that the typical IDT timeline presents a sanitized, decontextualized history that reinforces ignorance about systemic injustice and the field's complicity in oppressive structures. By placing events like Bloom's taxonomy and Gagné's contributions alongside the Civil Rights Movement, she reveals troubling blind spots in how the field understands itself and its responsibilities.

## Key Concepts
- **IDT Timeline**: The standard chronology of significant events and figures in instructional design and technology
- **Social Justice Blind Spot**: The field's collective failure to engage with issues of equity, inclusion, and systemic oppression
- **Epistemologies of Ignorance**: Theoretical framework for understanding how knowledge systems perpetuate not-knowing
- **Praxis**: Freire's concept of reflection and action to transform the world
- **Positionality**: Recognition that scholars' social positions shape their perspectives and research
- **AECT Definition**: The Association for Educational Communications and Technology's official definition of the field

## Theoretical Framework
Bradshaw draws on:
- **Critical pedagogy** (Freire) - education as liberation vs. domestication
- **Epistemologies of ignorance** (Alcoff, Code, Harding, Mills) - how ignorance is socially produced
- **Social construction** (Bruner) - how knowledge and culture shape each other
- **Critical race theory** - implicit in the analysis of white-dominated field history

## Methods
- Historical timeline analysis
- Critical juxtaposition of parallel chronologies
- Reflective/autobiographical inquiry
- Drawing on critical and feminist epistemologies

## Main Arguments
1. **Decontextualized History**: Standard IDT timelines present events in isolation from social context, creating false neutrality
2. **White Male Dominance**: The field's canon consists almost entirely of white men whose work developed during periods of explicit racial segregation
3. **Military Origins Unexamined**: IDT's roots in military training (Gagné, WWII) occurred during institutional racism (Executive Order 9066, segregated military)
4. **Concurrent Injustice**: Major IDT contributions coincide with civil rights struggles entirely absent from field narratives
5. **Structural Ignorance**: Not knowing about social context is not innocent but actively produced through field socialization
6. **Ethical Practice Requires Context**: The AECT definition's inclusion of "ethical practice" cannot be realized without engaging social justice
7. **Field Culture Shapes Practice**: A field that ignores systemic injustice will produce instructional systems that perpetuate it

## Limitations & Critiques
- Primarily U.S.-focused; does not address global contexts
- Calls for change but offers limited specific guidance for curriculum or practice
- May oversimplify the political views of historical figures
- Does not address how international IDT developments complicate the U.S.-centered critique
- Brief treatment of timeline events may lack nuance
- Does not propose alternative pedagogical approaches in detail
- Risk of presentism - judging past figures by contemporary standards
