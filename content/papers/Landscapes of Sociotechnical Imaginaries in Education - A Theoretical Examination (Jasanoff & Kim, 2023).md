# Landscapes of Sociotechnical Imaginaries in Education-A Theoretical Examination of Integrating Artificial Intelligence in Education

## Summary
The vision of integrating artificial intelligence in education is part of an ongoing push for harnessing digital solutions to improve teaching and learning Drawing from Jasanoff and Hasse, this paper deliberates on how sociotechnical imaginaries are interrelated to the implications of new technologies, such as AI, in education Complicating Hasses’s call for the development of Socratic ignorance to consider our predispositions about new technologies and open new prospects of thought, this paper revisits postphenomenology and Feenberg’s critical constructivist theories.

## Key Concepts
- Sociotechnical imaginaries (Jasanoff and Kim)
- Artificial intelligence integration in educational contexts
- Socratic ignorance as methodological stance
- Postphenomenology and technological mediation
- Critical constructivism (Feenberg)
- Digital solutionism in education
- Technological predispositions and assumptions
- Future visions of AI-enhanced learning
- Collectively held visions of desirable futures
- Technology as socially constructed

## Theoretical Framework
- Sociotechnical imaginaries framework (Jasanoff and Kim) - collective visions of technology and society
- Postphenomenology - examining human-technology relations
- Critical constructivism (Feenberg) - technology as socially shaped and political
- Socratic ignorance (Hasse) - questioning assumptions about new technologies

## Methods
- Phenomenological analysis
- Theoretical examination
- Critical analysis of discourse around AI in education
- Conceptual framework development

## Main Arguments
- AI integration in education reflects broader sociotechnical imaginaries about technology and learning
- Current push for AI in education driven by unreflective digital solutionism
- Need for Socratic ignorance to critically examine predispositions about AI's educational role
- Postphenomenology reveals how AI mediates and transforms educational relationships
- Critical constructivism exposes political dimensions of AI implementation decisions
- Sociotechnical imaginaries shape what is considered possible and desirable in educational AI
- Questioning taken-for-granted assumptions opens new possibilities for AI in education
- Educational AI implementations embody particular visions of teaching, learning, and human development

## Limitations & Critiques
- Primarily theoretical rather than empirical examination
- Limited concrete examples of alternative AI educational practices
- May not provide actionable guidance for practitioners
- Phenomenological approach may not capture structural/institutional constraints
- Does not fully address power dynamics in shaping educational imaginaries
- Limited attention to student perspectives and agency
- May underestimate practical pressures driving AI adoption
- Could benefit from more comparative analysis across educational contexts
