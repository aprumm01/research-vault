# Epistemic Insights AI Ethics via Constructionism (Lin and Dai CUHK)

## Summary
Lin and Dai present a constructionist pedagogical approach for developing epistemic insights into AI ethics among upper primary students (ages 10-12). They design three theory-informed activities in which students build and interact with rule-based, ML-based, and generative AI systems, using mathematical reasoning to represent AI decision-making and reflect on fairness, bias, and transparency. Findings show students developed not only foundational ethical principles but also genuine epistemic insight — understanding how AI knowledge construction differs from human cognition.

## Key Concepts
- Constructionist pedagogy (Papert)
- Epistemic insights into AI (how AI acquires/processes knowledge)
- K-12 AI ethics education
- Rule-based, ML-based, and generative AI systems as learning objects
- Fairness, bias, and transparency as ethical concepts

## Theoretical Framework
Grounded in constructionism (learning by making) combined with an interdisciplinary approach spanning computer science, mathematics, philosophy, and humanities; distinguishes between AI literacy (broad) and epistemic insight (specific understanding of how AI differs from human knowing).

## Methods
Design-based curriculum development and implementation with upper primary students (AAAI 2025); three hands-on activities with guided reflection; qualitative analysis of student reasoning and artifacts.

## Main Arguments
- Epistemic insight — understanding how AI acquires and represents knowledge — is a distinct and underdeveloped component of AI literacy
- Constructionist making (building AI models) provides a concrete foundation for ethical reflection that abstract instruction alone cannot achieve
- Students who build AI systems develop more nuanced understandings of AI limitations than those who only use them
- Interdisciplinary curriculum design is necessary because AI ethics is genuinely interdisciplinary — neither purely technical nor purely humanistic

## Limitations
- Short-term intervention; long-term effects on ethical reasoning and AI literacy development are unknown
- Upper primary context is specific; scaling challenges for younger or older age groups
- Relies on guided teacher facilitation which may be difficult to replicate without significant professional development
