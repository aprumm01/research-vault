# Exploring the Role of AI in UX Research: Challenges, Opportunities, and Educational Implications

## Summary
This ASCILITE 2024 conference paper explores how AI can address challenges faced by UX researchers and assesses their readiness to adopt AI tools. Through in-depth interviews with five early-career UX researchers in Melbourne, Australia, the study identifies key pain points in the UX research process and examines AI's potential to mitigate them.

The findings reveal that UX researchers face significant challenges in two main phases: (1) collecting, interpreting, and synthesizing qualitative data, and (2) translating user needs into design concepts. Specific challenges include missing important follow-up questions during interviews, failing to capture insights in real-time, managing large volumes of qualitative data, and accurately transforming user goals into effective design solutions.

The study found mixed readiness among UX researchers to adopt AI tools. While some saw potential for AI to broaden options and stimulate divergent thinking, others preferred traditional human-centric approaches and expressed concerns about AI stifling creativity. The researchers conclude that there is a significant gap in AI literacy among HCI students, and they recommend integrating fundamental AI concepts and hands-on practice with AI tools into HCI curricula.

## Key Concepts
- **AI Literacy**: Understanding of AI capabilities, limitations, and ethical considerations needed to effectively use AI in professional practice
- **UX Research Process**: Four key phases including planning, data collection/synthesis, translating needs to design, and validation/refinement
- **Proactive AI Assistants**: AI tools that can provide real-time suggestions during interviews and UX evaluation processes
- **Multimodal AI Applications**: Using AI beyond text generation to include visual organization and immersive experiences

## Theoretical Framework
The study positions AI integration within the broader context of HCI education and UX research practice, drawing on literature about AI literacy competencies and the evolving role of AI tools in design processes.

## Methods
Qualitative research methodology using semi-structured interviews with five early-career UX researchers (ages 21-25) with recent HCI education and relevant project experience. The retrospective cued recall (RCR) approach enabled participants to review and comment on past UX projects. Thematic analysis and affinity mapping identified recurring patterns in responses.

## Main Arguments
- AI is currently underutilized in UX research, primarily limited to routine tasks like transcription and note-taking
- AI has significant potential to support qualitative data synthesis and translation of user needs into design concepts
- Many UX researchers lack the AI literacy skills needed to fully leverage AI's potential
- Researchers are often unaware of critical ethical issues like privacy, bias, and misinformation
- HCI education must integrate AI literacy and hands-on AI tool experience to prepare students for industry demands

## Limitations & Critiques
- Small sample size of five early-career researchers limits generalizability
- Geographic limitation to Melbourne, Australia
- Focus on early-career researchers may not capture perspectives of more experienced practitioners
- Rapidly evolving AI technologies may quickly change the relevance of specific findings
- Study provides initial insights but calls for further research on multimodal AI applications in UX research
