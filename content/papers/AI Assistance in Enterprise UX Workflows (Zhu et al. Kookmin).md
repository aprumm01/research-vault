# AI Assistance in Enterprise UX Workflows (Zhu et al. Kookmin)


## Abstract

**DOI:** [10.3389/frai.2024.1404647](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2024.1404647)
The study explores the impact of AI tools on the daily tasks of designers in corporate environments, with a focus on the creation and evaluation processes of design briefs. Given ChatGPT’s advanced natural language processing capabilities and its potential to meet the complex communication and analysis needs of design work, this tool was selected to investigate its application in designers’ workflows. Through expert interviews, experimental testing, and third-party expert evaluations, we collected and analyzed data to understand the impact of AI on work processes. The findings indicate that AI tools significantly enhance both operational experience and subjective perceptions across most tasks. Additionally, the study provides a visual comparison of the testing process through a user experience map, highlighting AI’s positive influence on work efficiency, information retrieval, verification, analysis, communication, and decision-making. However, challenges remain in ensuring information authenticity, protecting content copyright, and maintaining professional identity. The primary objective is to gain a comprehensive understanding of the current state of AI application in business contexts and its impact on designers’ roles. By analyzing real-world feedback, the research aims to identify the strengths and weaknesses of AI solutions in enterprises and offer practical recommendations. The study underscores the importance of integrating AI thinking into workflows and adopting a human-centric approach for the future development of corporate work environments.

## Summary
Zhu, Lee, Pan, and Cai examine how AI tools — specifically ChatGPT — affect designers' workflows in corporate enterprise environments, focusing on the creation and evaluation of design briefs. Through expert interviews, experimental testing, and third-party evaluations, they find that AI significantly enhances operational efficiency and subjective experience across most tasks: supporting information retrieval, analysis, communication, and decision-making. However, challenges remain around information authenticity, copyright, and professional identity maintenance. The paper advocates for human-centric AI integration frameworks.

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on human-computer interaction research, design management theory, and UX workflow analysis; uses user experience mapping as an analytical tool to compare pre/post AI integration experiences.

## Methods
Mixed methods: expert interviews with designers, experimental testing of ChatGPT in design brief tasks, and third-party expert evaluations of output quality; UX map visualization of the testing process.

## Main Arguments
- AI tools provide genuine operational value in enterprise UX contexts, particularly for information-intensive phases like brief creation, where domain gaps and communication complexity create friction
- Designers' subjective experience of AI assistance is largely positive, but concerns about accuracy and authenticity require ongoing critical engagement with AI outputs
- Professional identity challenges arise when designers feel AI encroaches on their expert judgment — organizations must manage this through cultural norms and training
- Effective integration requires designers to develop "AI thinking": understanding what AI does well and structuring workflows to leverage it appropriately

## Limitations
- Single institution study (Kookmin University, Seoul); enterprise contexts vary significantly in tools, norms, and design brief complexity
- Focus on brief creation is a narrow slice of full UX workflow; findings may not generalize to later design phases requiring more tacit judgment
