# Generative AI Integrated Educational Model for User-Centered Design

## Summary
This study developed an educational model integrating generative AI tools (ChatGPT and Midjourney) into user-centered design curriculum, based on the Double Diamond design thinking framework. An empirical study with 48 third-year Visual Communication students at Jimei University tested the model through a travel app UI design project. Results showed the AI-integrated curriculum enhanced students' creativity and problem-solving skills, with 2024 students achieving higher average scores (86.15) compared to 2023 non-AI students (82.12). System usability scores indicated ChatGPT (78.975) was more usable than Midjourney (68.4), though students required more personalized instruction to achieve full confidence with both tools.

## Key Concepts
- **Generative AI in Education**: Integration of ChatGPT (large language model) and Midjourney (text-to-image generator) as design tools throughout the entire user-centered design process, from research through prototyping
- **Double Diamond Model**: Four-phase design process (Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver) combining divergent and convergent thinking with iterative development, providing structured framework for AI tool integration across design stages
- **AI-Assisted Design Process**: Generative AI enhances divergent phase breadth through rapid idea generation and convergent phase efficiency through quick refinement, enabling expanded exploration and accelerated iteration cycles
- **AI Literacy**: Critical competency for design students to evaluate AI-generated content, avoid over-reliance, and maintain human judgment in decision-making, requiring explicit instruction beyond tool operation
- **Human-AI Collaboration**: Balance between traditional design methods (empathy, observation, collaboration) and AI tools to cultivate both technical skills and design sensitivity without displacing essential human-centered practices

## Theoretical Framework
**Design Thinking / Double Diamond Model** (British Design Council, 2004): Four-phase iterative framework—Discover (divergent user/market research), Define (convergent problem framing), Develop (divergent solution ideation), Deliver (convergent prototype refinement)—providing pedagogical structure for systematically integrating AI tools at appropriate design stages based on divergent versus convergent thinking requirements.

**User-Centered Design** (Norman, 1980s-present): Design philosophy emphasizing active user involvement to understand requirements, iterative design and evaluation cycles, and multidisciplinary collaboration, establishing foundational principles that AI tools must support rather than replace for maintaining design quality and appropriateness.

## Methods
**Empirical Comparative Study Design**: Two cohorts of third-year Visual Communication students at Jimei University completing travel app UI design project—2023 cohort (n=24) using traditional methods, 2024 cohort (n=24) using AI-integrated curriculum—enabling before-after comparison of learning outcomes, design quality, and student experiences.

**AI-Integrated Curriculum Implementation**: Systematic integration of ChatGPT for research assistance, persona development, idea generation, and content refinement across all Double Diamond phases, plus Midjourney for mood board creation, visual exploration, and design iteration in Develop phase, with explicit instruction on tool capabilities, limitations, and appropriate use contexts.

**Multi-Method Assessment**:
- **Quantitative**: Final project scores comparing cohort averages (2024 M=86.15 vs. 2023 M=82.12)
- **Usability Evaluation**: System Usability Scale (SUS) scores for ChatGPT (M=78.975) and Midjourney (M=68.4)
- **Qualitative**: Student surveys and reflections on learning experiences, tool utility, confidence development

## Main Arguments
- **Structured AI integration across design phases enhances learning outcomes**: Results showing 4-point average score improvement for AI-integrated cohort suggests thoughtful AI tool incorporation throughout design process—not just ad hoc usage—produces measurable educational benefits. Double Diamond framework provided pedagogical structure ensuring AI used appropriately at each stage: ChatGPT for research synthesis and ideation, Midjourney for visual exploration, both supporting rather than replacing foundational design thinking. This structured approach contrasts with unguided AI access potentially creating dependency without skill development.

- **AI tools differentially effective across design phases requiring phase-appropriate pedagogy**: ChatGPT's higher usability (78.975 SUS) versus Midjourney (68.4 SUS) reflects different interaction models and learning curves—language-based versus visual generation. More importantly, tools showed distinct utility across Double Diamond phases: ChatGPT particularly valuable in Discover/Define (research, problem framing) and Midjourney in Develop (visual ideation). Effective AI-integrated education requires teaching students which tools appropriate for which design activities rather than generic "use AI" guidance.

- **AI enhances creativity through expanded exploration capacity not autonomous generation**: Study frames AI as tool enabling students to explore broader design space—generating more personas, more mood boards, more interface variations—rather than replacing creative thinking. AI augments divergent thinking by reducing production friction, allowing students engage more ideas. However, human judgment remains central for convergence: selecting promising directions, evaluating appropriateness, refining toward user needs. This positions AI as amplifier of human creativity rather than substitute.

- **AI literacy requires explicit instruction on evaluation and critical engagement**: Despite productivity gains, study emphasizes students needed personalized instruction to achieve confidence and appropriate tool use. Learning to generate AI outputs relatively easy; developing judgment about output quality, recognizing limitations, knowing when AI guidance misleading requires dedicated pedagogical attention. AI-integrated curriculum cannot assume tool access sufficient—must explicitly teach evaluation frameworks, bias recognition, and when to trust versus question AI suggestions.

- **Successful AI integration preserves rather than replaces human-centered methods**: Study deliberately maintains traditional design practices—user observation, empathy mapping, collaborative critique, manual sketching—alongside AI tools. This both/and approach ensures students develop fundamental competencies (user understanding, design judgment, interpersonal collaboration) that AI cannot provide while leveraging AI for appropriate efficiency gains. Wholesale replacement of traditional methods with AI risks producing technically proficient but conceptually weak designers.

## Limitations & Critiques
**Non-randomized cohort comparison challenges causal claims**: Comparing sequential year cohorts (2023 vs. 2024) introduces confounds beyond AI integration—different students, potentially different instruction quality, evolving curriculum elements, instructor learning effects—making difficult to isolate AI impact from other improvements or differences.

**Limited sample size and single institution context**: Study with 48 students at one Chinese university limits generalizability across different educational contexts, student populations, cultural settings, institutional resources, and disciplinary focuses (findings in visual communication may not transfer to interaction design, industrial design, etc.).

**Outcome measures focus on grades not skill development**: While final project scores provide convenient measure, grades reflect complex combination of factors and may not directly measure design skill acquisition, critical thinking development, or long-term capability—deeper learning outcomes remain unmeasured.

**Insufficient attention to potential negative effects**: Study emphasizes positive outcomes without adequately examining possible drawbacks: students becoming dependent on AI, surface-level engagement with design challenges, reduced struggle leading to less deep learning, or uneven benefits favoring already-strong students.

**Short-term evaluation only**: Assessment captures single project performance but cannot determine whether AI-supported learning produces equivalent long-term skill development, knowledge retention, or professional practice capabilities compared to traditional methods requiring more initial struggle.

**Usability as proxy for pedagogical effectiveness**: SUS scores indicate tool ease-of-use but not necessarily learning effectiveness—tools being usable doesn't mean they support learning goals appropriately. Higher ChatGPT usability versus Midjourney doesn't necessarily indicate superior pedagogical value.

## Methods
**Mixed Methods Design** - 48 students divided into 12 teams of 4 members
- **Observation**: Student design behavior throughout each phase
- **Document Analysis**: Fabrie design journals tracking team workflow
- **Interviews**: Group interviews at course conclusion
- **Questionnaire**: System Usability Scale (SUS) for ChatGPT and Midjourney; student views on AI-assisted design
- **Comparative Analysis**: Final project scores compared between 2024 (with AI) and 2023 (without AI) cohorts using independent samples t-test

**Research Setting**: User Interface Design curriculum, June-July 2024, Chengyi College, Jimei University

## Main Arguments
1. **AI Optimizes Design Process Structure**: Generative AI significantly increases divergent phase breadth by providing multifaceted, interdisciplinary information and increases convergent phase efficiency by rapidly generating and testing design options

2. **Complementary Tool, Not Replacement**: AI serves as supplementary design assistance - students maintain dominant position in decision-making, judgment, and critical evaluation of AI-generated content which can be predictable or stereotypical

3. **Enhanced Learning Outcomes**: AI-integrated curriculum fostered creativity and problem-solving skills, with students achieving 4.03 point higher average scores (statistically significant, p < 0.001)

4. **Balance Traditional and AI Methods**: Traditional empathy approaches (focus groups, in-depth interviews, on-site observation) remain essential for gaining authentic user insights and developing interpersonal abilities that AI cannot replicate

5. **Design Strategist Development**: Human-AI interaction requires continuous reflection and evaluation, transforming students into system thinkers and strategy makers rather than passive tool users

## Limitations & Critiques
**Identified by Researchers**:
- Students need to verify ChatGPT responses as some content is inaccurate or contains stereotypes
- AI-generated design concepts can be mediocre and predictable due to rational data processing lacking unexpected characteristics
- AI shows limitations judging human factors feasibility (e.g., irrational functionality arrangements in wireframes expose deficiency in interaction logic)
- AI lacks adaptability and controllability for detailed design requiring specialized CAD tools (Figma, Adobe XD)
- Low student confidence in fully controlling AIGC systems due to content ambiguity and uncertainty
- Personalized instruction needed for students to master tools and produce high-quality content

**Practical Challenges**:
- Risk of student over-reliance on AI-generated content
- Traditional brainstorming atmosphere with team members still preferred for collaborative inspiration
- Need for refined assessment strategies to track AI collaboration in design journals
- Further evaluation methods needed across various design majors

**Research Limitations**:
- Single course context (UI Design) limits generalizability
- Same instructors and project topic enables comparison but constrains ecological validity
- Short study duration (June-July 2024) provides limited longitudinal insights
- Small sample size (48 students) for quantitative analysis
