# Competences for the Modern Designer: Systematic Literature Review (2026)

## Summary
Silver and Ruokamo conduct a systematic literature review (PRISMA methodology, 20 articles selected from 281) examining what future competences designers need and how design education should respond. The review covers peer-reviewed journal articles from 2018 to 2024 and finds that the shift from Industry 4.0 (digitalization) to Industry 5.0 (sustainable, human-centric, resilient industry) is now the primary driver of competence change. The paper argues that current design curricula address digital skills adequately but fail to prepare designers for wicked problems, planetary design, and systemic complexity. The findings are mapped against the EU DigComp digital competence framework with supplemental competences identified.

## Key Concepts
- Industry 5.0 as a driver of designer competence transformation
- Transferable skills: ability to adapt and apply knowledge across contexts
- Meta-competences: critical thinking, systemic thinking, scientific thinking, innovative thinking
- Planetary design paradigm and eco-systems design
- Co-creation pedagogy and industry-academia collaboration
- DigComp framework as benchmark for digital competence

## Theoretical Framework
Systematic literature review methodology (PRISMA); situated within design education research and European educational policy (DigComp framework, EU Industry 5.0 report); draws on scholarship by Friedman, Meyer and Norman, Wilde, Manzini, and others working at the intersection of design education and societal transformation; focuses especially on service designers whose roles have grown in complexity.

## Methods
Systematic literature review following PRISMA guidelines; seven databases searched (ScienceDirect, ProQuest, Taylor and Francis, Wiley, EBSCO, Scopus, Web of Science); keywords: design education, higher education, digitalization, competence; 281 initial results reduced to 20 included articles through abstract screening and full-text eligibility checking; deductive content analysis with selective codes (design education, competence, working life, digitalization).

## Main Arguments
- Industry 5.0, not merely digitalization, is the organizing concept for future designer competence: it demands sustainability thinking, eco-systems design, and planetary-scale responsibility beyond current curricula
- Future designer competences are transferable and meta-cognitive: critical, systemic, scientific, and innovative thinking; interdisciplinary collaboration; and the ability to apply knowledge in novel and rapidly changing contexts
- Design education must shift from performance-based (technical, craft) to systemic and global challenges, with most programs currently failing to address the latter two adequately
- Co-creation pedagogy, close industry-academia collaboration, and communities of practice are the most effective educational approaches for developing these competences
- The DigComp framework can be supplemented with deeper design-specific competences: multi-disciplinary thinking, participatory and empathic design, planetary design, sustainability expertise, and complex problem-solving with multiple stakeholders

## Limitations & Critiques
Search terms were limited to a fixed keyword set, which may have excluded relevant studies. The review was finalized in early 2024 and may not reflect the most recent publications. The analysis was conducted by one author, introducing potential single-rater bias. The empirical grounding is in Finnish higher education, which may not generalize universally; the SLR findings are directive rather than prescriptive and require further empirical validation.
