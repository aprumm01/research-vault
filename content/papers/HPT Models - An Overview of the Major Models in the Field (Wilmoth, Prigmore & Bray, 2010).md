# Wilmoth, Prigmore, & Bray (2010). HPT Models--cleaner

## Summary
E1C01_1 10/14/2009 5 S CHAPTER ONE S HPT Models An Overview of the Major Models in the Field Frank S Wilmoth Christine Prigmore Marty Bray A.

## Key Concepts
- **Human Performance Technology (HPT)**: Systematic approach to improving productivity and competence through analysis, intervention, design, development, implementation, and evaluation
- **Performance gap**: Bridging difference between what is and what should be in human performance systems
- **Four HPT pioneers**: Gilbert (father of HPT, focus on environmental barriers), Harless (front-end analysis, ADDIE model), Mager (performance objectives with performance/conditions/criterion), Rummler (organizational ecosystems, nine performance variables)
- **ADDIE model**: Analysis, Design, Development and Testing, Implementation and Evaluation (originated from Harless)
- **Performance objectives components**: Performance (what learner should do), Conditions (situations under which), Criterion (standards for acceptable performance)
- **Three organizational levels**: Organizational, process, job/performer levels (Rummler's interrelated framework)
- **Four intervention categories**: Selection/assignment, motivation/incentive, environment, information/skills

## Theoretical Framework
Builds on instructional design foundations but evolved to address complex business environments. Integrates Gilbert's environmental focus, Harless's diagnostic approach, Mager's objectives framework, and Rummler's systems thinking. Emphasizes that epistemology of discipline ≠ pedagogy, and that organizational performance differs from individual performance.

## Methods
- Historical analysis of HPT model development
- Literature review of foundational works by Gilbert, Harless, Mager, Rummler
- Examination of model evolution from linear instructional design to complex business process models
- Comparative analysis of major HPT models
- Overview chapter providing conceptual and communication tools for visualizing and managing performance improvement processes

## Main Arguments
- **HPT migrated from instructional design**: Early practitioners came from instructional technology, so modeling practices evolved from ID principles
- **Linear models inadequate for business**: Early attempts to use linear ID models failed to describe sophisticated, multifaceted business environments
- **Performance before instruction**: Focus shifted from what instructors teach to what learners can do, and from learning to performance results
- **Environmental barriers first**: Gilbert's principle that improving performance begins with identifying and resolving environmental barriers before addressing individual skills
- **Front-end analysis critical**: Harless's belief that understanding cause of problem should drive solution
- **Clear measurable standards essential**: Mager's model introduced notion that performance must have definable conditions and measurable standards
- **Three levels must be simultaneous**: Rummler's framework requires considering organizational, process, and job/performer levels simultaneously across different functions
- **Multiple models reflect diversity**: Large number of models emerged from different perspectives and backgrounds of pioneers, still evolving
- **Modeling enables communication**: Models provide conceptual and communication tools for visualizing, directing, and managing complex processes

## Limitations & Critiques
- 2010 publication predates recent developments in performance support technology and AI
- Chapter is overview/introduction, may lack depth on individual models
- Focus on pioneering models may not reflect current cutting-edge HPT practice
- Limited discussion of challenges in applying models across different organizational contexts
- May not fully address critiques of systematic approaches similar to those faced by ADDIE in ID
- Geographic and cultural scope appears limited to North American contexts
- Does not extensively discuss how to choose between competing models for specific situations
