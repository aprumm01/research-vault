# Reflecting on the Integration of Generative AI in Design Education

## Summary

This testimonial article presents the author's reflections on integrating generative AI across three design education experiences: personal creative exploration (AI+Poetry+Design project), a faculty learning community (DigiFab at Appalachian State University), and an undergraduate digital design course. The paper uses constructivist and experiential learning frameworks to analyze how GenAI can enhance creativity, democratize access to creative tools, and foster interdisciplinary collaboration while raising ethical concerns about authorship, over-reliance, and equity.

## Key Concepts

- **AI+Poetry+Design**: Creative workflow combining text-to-image AI with poetry and fashion design
- **Faculty learning community**: Interdisciplinary group exploring AI tools for creative education
- **Democratization of creativity**: AI lowering technical barriers to creative expression
- **Human-AI co-creation**: AI as collaborative partner rather than replacement for human creativity
- **Critical AI literacy**: Teaching students to evaluate and ethically use AI-generated content

## Theoretical Framework

- **Constructivism (Vygotsky, Piaget)**: Learning through active engagement and social interaction
- **Experiential Learning (Kolb)**: Concrete experience, reflective observation, conceptualization, active experimentation
- **Reflective Practice (Schon)**: Reflection-in-action and reflection-on-action
- **TPACK Framework**: Integration of technology, pedagogy, and content knowledge

## Methods

- Autoethnographic/testimonial approach
- Three case experiences documented:
  1. Personal AI+Poetry+Design project using ChatGPT, DALL-E, Midjourney
  2. Faculty learning community (12-15 members) exploring Midjourney for 3D model generation
  3. Undergraduate digital design course (400+ students over multiple semesters)
- Reflective analysis connecting experiences to learning theory

## Main Arguments

1. **Catalyst for creativity**: GenAI serves as powerful catalyst for ideation and creative exploration
2. **Democratization**: AI tools enable participation by those without traditional technical skills
3. **Iterative workflow**: GenAI encourages rapid iteration and experimentation in design process
4. **Collaborative learning**: AI integration fosters interdisciplinary exchange and community learning
5. **Over-reliance risk**: Students may become passive consumers rather than critical creators
6. **Ethical complexity**: Authorship, copyright, environmental impact, and bias require structured discussion
7. **Equity concerns**: Access disparities to paid AI tools may exacerbate educational inequalities

## Limitations & Critiques

- Testimonial format limits generalizability
- Single institution (public university in southeastern US)
- Creative/design disciplines may not transfer to other fields
- Limited quantitative evidence for learning outcomes
- Author's dual role as instructor and researcher may introduce bias
- Rapidly evolving AI tools may outpace observations
