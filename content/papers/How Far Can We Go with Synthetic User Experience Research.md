# How Far Can We Go with Synthetic User Experience Research?

## Summary
This column article in ACM Interactions explores the emerging debate around synthetic user experience research—the use of generative AI tools to simulate user interactions, preferences, and needs. Li, an HCI researcher at EPAM, reflects on her extensive field research experience and raises critical questions about how far AI can go in augmenting or replacing traditional UXR methods. Despite the hype around GenAI and synthetic UXR platforms, she argues that human researchers remain essential for the nuanced understanding, empathy, and contextual awareness that define quality user research.

Li surveys expert opinions from HCI and UXR professionals, revealing divided perspectives. Skeptics like Cher Lowies note that 70% of AI-generated data lacks the depth of human-conducted studies, producing generalized themes rather than nuanced insights. Ye Dong emphasizes the "human touch" (renwei) that synthetic data cannot replicate—the context-rich, unexpected, personal and emotional experiences unique to real users. However, others like Hamada Rizk and Sara Bouzit see value in using synthetic data to augment minority data and reduce bias in training datasets, particularly in domains like medical imaging where annotated data is scarce.

The article presents a framework mapping what GenAI can and cannot do across four UXR stages: Discover, Explore, Test, and Listen. While AI excels at providing templates, transcription, pattern identification, and automating categorization, it cannot capture emotional journeys, provide nuanced critiques like human testers, or replace strategic decision-making derived from deep analysis of user feedback.

## Key Concepts
- **Synthetic User Experience Research**: Using GenAI to simulate or augment user research activities, including generating personas, interview responses, and usability feedback
- **Human Touch (Renwei)**: A Chinese concept referring to human warmth, personality, and authenticity that AI cannot replicate in research contexts
- **AI Interpolation vs. Human Extrapolation**: AI provides predictions within the range of existing data (interpolation), while humans can extrapolate beyond known patterns
- **Empathy vs. Sympathy in UXR**: The distinction between empathetically connecting with users (potentially requiring distance for objectivity) versus sympathetically experiencing alongside them
- **Four-Stage UXR Framework**: Discover, Explore, Test, and Listen—with different AI capabilities and limitations at each stage

## Theoretical Framework
Li draws on motivation theory to discuss how researchers find meaning in their work, distinguishing between extrinsic motivations (efficiency, treating technology as a tool) and intrinsic motivations (enjoyment of the research process itself). She also references the ongoing community debate about AI's appropriate role in research, positioning the article within broader HCI discussions about human-AI collaboration.

## Methods
This is a reflective column piece rather than empirical research. Li synthesizes expert interviews with researchers and professors in HCI and UXR, including Cher Lowies, Sara Bouzit, Ye Dong, Hamada Rizk, Hiroshi Ishii, and Kai Kunze. She also draws on her personal field research experiences and survey data showing 77.1% of UX researchers use AI in some capacity.

## Main Arguments
- Synthetic UXR cannot fully replace the nuanced understanding, empathy, and contextual awareness that human researchers provide
- AI-generated data often produces generalized themes and averaged results that miss the complexity and diversity of real user interactions
- AI excels at specific tasks: providing templates, transcribing audio, identifying patterns, automating usability assessments, and categorizing feedback
- AI cannot: collaborate with stakeholders for validation, capture full emotional journeys, provide nuanced critiques like experienced human testers, or make strategic decisions based on deep analysis
- The joy and meaning of connecting with real users, conducting field research, and engaging in thematic analysis should not be "overshadowed" by AI-generated insights
- AI tools could help researchers maintain a balanced approach to empathy—providing enough distance for objective evaluation while supporting user understanding
- Relying solely on synthetic data to guide strategic product development and predict the future is risky

## Limitations & Critiques
As a column piece, this article represents the author's perspective and expert interviews rather than systematic empirical research. The framework of what GenAI can/cannot do may quickly become outdated as AI capabilities evolve. The article does not address specific methodological approaches for integrating AI into UXR workflows. The dichotomy between synthetic and traditional research may oversimplify hybrid approaches that combine both. The concern about AI replacing researchers may not fully account for how roles might evolve rather than disappear.
