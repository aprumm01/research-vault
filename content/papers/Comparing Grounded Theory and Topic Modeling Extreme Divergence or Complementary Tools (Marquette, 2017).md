# Comparing Grounded Theory and Topic Modeling: Extreme Divergence or Complementary Tools? (Marquette, 2017)

## Summary
This methodological paper compares grounded theory and topic modeling as approaches to qualitative data analysis, examining their epistemological assumptions, strengths, weaknesses, and appropriate contexts of use. The authors find that despite surface similarities (both generate categories from data without a priori frameworks), grounded theory and topic modeling reflect fundamentally different epistemologies: grounded theory seeks substantive theory about social processes; topic modeling seeks statistical patterns in text. The paper offers guidance for researchers choosing between and potentially combining the two approaches.

## Key Concepts
- Grounded theory (Glaser, Strauss, Charmaz traditions)
- Topic modeling (LDA and structural topic models)
- Epistemological comparison of qualitative and computational methods
- Theory generation vs. pattern discovery
- Mixed-methods implications for textual data analysis

## Theoretical Framework
Research methodology meta-analysis drawing on constructivist grounded theory (Charmaz), computational text analysis, and qualitative research methodology literature; compares two approaches at the level of epistemological assumptions and procedural requirements rather than outcomes alone.

## Methods
Methodological review and comparative analysis; applies both grounded theory and topic modeling to the same dataset (nonuse of social media survey responses); examines epistemological assumptions, procedural requirements, and output types of each approach; DOI: 10.1002/asi.23786.

## Main Arguments
- Grounded theory and topic modeling are not interchangeable despite both being inductive and data-driven; they reflect fundamentally different epistemological commitments
- Grounded theory produces substantive theoretical accounts of social processes with interpretive richness; topic modeling produces statistical descriptions of textual patterns
- The choice between approaches should be driven by the research question's epistemological requirements, not by sample size or convenience alone
- Under appropriate conditions, the two approaches can be productively combined: topic modeling for initial pattern identification and grounded theory for theory-building on those patterns

## Limitations & Critiques
The comparison does not cover all variants of either approach and the demonstration case may not generalize. The epistemological framing may be perceived as privileging interpretive over computational perspectives by some readers. The article focuses on information science contexts and may not address all disciplinary applications of these methods.
