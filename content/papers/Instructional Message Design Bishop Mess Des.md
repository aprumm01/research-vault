# Instructional Message Design Bishop Mess Des

## Summary
Instructional message design explores how various media and delivery systems might be used more effectively to help optimize instructional communications within context-specific instructional situations and learner needs But use of the term appears to have fallen out of favor over the years since the mid-1990s A review of the historical and theoretical foundations of instructional message design reveals that, while instructional design generally has shifted from objectivist to more constructivist perspectives on learning theory, the instructional message design field remains firmly rooted in early “transmission oriented” communications models.

## Key Concepts
- **Transmission-Oriented Communications**: Field remains rooted in Shannon-Weaver model viewing communication as information passage from source to destination, despite philosophical shifts elsewhere
- **Instructional Communications System**: Teaching-learning problems viewed as communication problems with interrelated parts working together to produce learning
- **Message Redundancy Balance**: Effective communication requires optimal balance between redundancy (to overcome noise) and new information (avoiding inefficiency)
- **Linking Science**: Original broad view positioned instructional message design as bridge between learning theory research and instructional practice
- **Media Attributes Focus**: Narrower modern view emphasizes specific media attributes (text, sound, color, images) and their cognitive processing affordances
- **Cognitive Load Optimization**: Design aims to minimize extraneous processing, manage essential processing, and foster generative processing during learning
- **Conversation Metaphor**: Emerging view that good instruction serves conversation metaphor rather than transmission theory
- **Systems Philosophy Complexity**: Educational communication systems have too many interacting variables for linear cause-effect relationships

## Theoretical Framework
- **Shannon-Weaver Mathematical Communication Theory**: Transmission model with source, transmitter, channel, receiver, destination; communication perfection occurs when receiver effects match source intentions
- **Berlo's S-M-C-R Model**: Sender-Message-Channel-Receiver framework emphasizing shared experience, feedback, and message attributes (content, treatment, code) at communication center
- **Atkinson-Shiffrin Information-Processing**: Three-stage model (sensory register, short-term store, long-term store) with encoding as relationship-building process
- **Conversation Theory**: Recommended framework positioning instruction as dynamic social reciprocal transaction rather than unidirectional transmission
- **Cognitive Load Theory**: Framework examining how multimedia message attributes impact learner processing through dual channel, limited capacity, and active processing principles

## Methods
Historical-theoretical review examining instructional message design literature from field's origins through mid-1990s decline to current state. Database searches revealing sparse recent scholarship beyond Fleming & Levie's foundational texts. Comparative analysis of philosophical shifts (behaviorism to cognitivism to constructivism) against stagnant communication theory foundations.

## Main Arguments
- **Philosophical Mismatch Crisis**: While instructional design shifted from objectivist to constructivist perspectives, message design remains stuck in transmission-oriented communications models creating fundamental incompatibility
- **Definitional Ambiguity Problem**: Field suffers from lack of precise meaning, oscillating between broad "linking science" vision and narrow media attributes focus without clear boundaries
- **Generalization Limitations**: Research findings provide guidelines for what won't work in cognitive processing but seldom illuminate what designers should actually do in particular learning contexts
- **Systems Complexity Challenge**: Educational communication systems cannot be reduced to laboratory-controlled independent variables; understanding requires holistic examination of element relationships
- **Research-Practice Gap**: Despite wealth of empirical principles, little applied work translates specifications into tangible message attributes, failing to serve designers' decision-making needs
- **Reestablishment Requirements**: Reviving field requires catching up with communication theory shifts, adjusting definitions beyond cognitive processing optimization, and adopting systems view of inquiry
- **Conversation Over Transmission**: Future relevance depends on repositioning message design around conversation metaphor rather than knowledge transmission model

## Limitations & Critiques
- **Terminal Decline Acknowledged**: Author notes field terminology "fallen out of favor" since mid-1990s with database searches "more-or-less empty" beyond historical references
- **Theoretical Stagnation**: Underlying concepts remained essentially unchanged for 50 years despite dramatic shifts in communications/learning theory and interactive technologies
- **Predictive Impossibility**: Cannot hope to predict instructional message consequences because systems governed by culturally-diffused meanings and context-dependent rules, not cause-effect contingencies
- **Experimental Validity**: Generalization from controlled multimedia research to "real world" learning contexts should be viewed with "considerable caution" per Rieber
- **Methodological Narrowing**: Increasing focus on highly constrained comparison studies of media attributes produces limited actionable guidance for design decisions
- **Incomplete Constructivist Integration**: Field failed to adapt when Jonassen and others argued learner thinking matters more than message representation or presentation technologies
- **Over-Reliance on Pre-1995 Work**: Recent compilations of design principles still depend principally on empirical work done before major philosophical shifts
- **Meaning Control Delusion**: Assumption that meaningfulness can be determined in advance and transmitted successfully to all learners with right message cue combinations
