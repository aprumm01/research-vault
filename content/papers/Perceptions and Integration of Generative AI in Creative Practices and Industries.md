# Perceptions and Integration of Generative AI in Creative Practices and Industries

# Study Partner Note: GenAI in Creative Practices

## Summary
This scoping review examines how creative professionals across visual art, writing, performing arts, and environmental design perceive and integrate generative AI into their work. The paper matters because it maps complex patterns of adoption and resistance that will reshape creative education, professional practice, and debates about authorship and creativity itself.

## Key Concepts
- **Knowledge codifiability**: The spectrum from tacit/embodied knowledge to explicit/codifiable knowledge—a key dimension shaping GenAI adoption
- **Output materiality**: Whether creative work is physical/permanent vs. digital/ephemeral, affecting integration patterns
- **Meta-creation & curation**: Shift from direct creation toward curatorial and higher-level conceptual roles when using GenAI
- **Career stage as moderator**: Entry-level professionals embrace GenAI; senior practitioners resist due to expertise devaluation concerns
- **Hybrid methodologies**: GenAI positioned for early conceptualization, with human judgment preserved for final/convergent phases

## Theoretical Framework
The authors employ a two-dimensional conceptual framework positioning creative professionals' attitudes along knowledge codifiability and output materiality axes. This allows them to theorize how different creative fields' relationships to embodiment, materiality, and cultural authenticity shape their resistance or adoption of GenAI.

## Methods
Scoping review following PRISMA-ScR guidelines analyzing 57 empirically-based papers (2022–2025) across multiple databases, focusing on professional creative practice in four domains.

## Main Arguments
- **Career stage matters**: Entry-level professionals see GenAI as natural digital extension; senior practitioners fear expertise erosion
- **Domain-specific integration patterns**: Fine arts, literary fiction, and classical music show most resistance; commercially-oriented fields adopt more readily
- **Inverse relationship with "pure" creativity**: Fields prioritizing embodied practice and cultural authenticity resist most strongly
- **Field-specific challenges**: Visual artists face "erasure by obscurity"; writers struggle with coherence/cliché; performers embrace computational unpredictability; architects worry about cultural homogenization
- **Persistent tensions**: Productivity vs. meaning, volume vs. distinction, precision vs. serendipity, individual vs. collective intelligence

## Limitations & Critiques
The review lacks longitudinal data on actual skill impacts over time and underrepresents Global South perspectives and non-Western creative traditions. It also does not deeply examine emerging forms of creative resistance or AI-native practices developing outside traditional frameworks.
