# When "The Design Process Is Dead" Is a Sales Pitch (Prumm, 2026)

## Summary
This critical essay analyzes a podcast episode in which a Head of Design at an AI company argued that the traditional design process is dead, contending that the argument exemplifies a pattern the author terms "definitional capture" — the strategic narrowing of what "design" means to make displacement claims appear more complete than they are. By reducing design to visual execution and layout, the speaker renders invisible the analytical, ethical, and advocacy functions of UX research that AI cannot yet replicate. The essay argues that incomplete truths function socially as complete ones: when executives hear "the design process is dead," they hear "cut your UX team," regardless of the speaker's intent. The essay also flags structural conflict of interest inherent in vendor-as-prophet positioning and the compounding risk of displacing the human judgment layer from product development precisely as AI systems require more scrutiny, not less.

## Key Concepts
- **Definitional capture**: The rhetorical move of narrowing the definition of "design" to mean only visual/execution work, making AI displacement claims appear total when they are actually partial
- **Vendor-as-prophet**: Structural conflict of interest when an AI company employee argues that AI replaces human designers — the speaker profits from the claim regardless of accuracy
- **Audience mismatch**: The same message lands differently for designers (nuanced) vs. executives (budget justification for cuts); speakers may optimize for one audience while the other internalizes the message literally
- **Compounding risk**: Displacing human judgment (UX research, ethics, advocacy) from product development at the moment when AI systems most require human scrutiny
- **UX vs. visual design distinction**: UX research — user interviews, empathy, ethical review, stakeholder advocacy — is the function AI cannot replicate and the function omitted from the displacement narrative

## Main Arguments
1. The claim "the design process is dead" is accurate only if "design" is redefined to mean visual execution — a definitional move that is invisible to most audiences
2. AI can replicate layout and visual generation, but not the empathy, ethical judgment, and advocacy functions of UX research
3. Vendor-as-prophet positioning creates a structural incentive to overstate AI capability regardless of evidence
4. Executives do not parse nuance: incomplete truths about AI capability function as complete ones in budget and staffing decisions
5. Removing UX researchers from product teams increases risk at precisely the moment that AI-integrated products require more human oversight, not less
6. Definitional capture works because it is invisible — the narrowed definition feels natural once established

## Positionality Note
The author acknowledges a dual stake: as a designer and design educator whose professional position is implicitly critiqued by displacement claims, and as a user of Anthropic's Claude (an AI product) to analyze a claim made by an Anthropic employee. The essay treats this irony as evidence of the complexity of the moment, not a disqualifying conflict.
