# lebovitz-et-al-2022-to-engage-or-not-to-engage-with-ai-for-critical-judgments-how-professionals-deal-with-opacity-when

## Summary
conduct knowledge work by augmenting their capabilities for making professional judgments We know little, however, about how human-AI augmentation takes place in practice Yet, gaining this understanding is particularly important when professionals use AI tools to form judgments on critical decisions.

## Key Concepts
- **Human-AI augmentation** in professional knowledge work and judgment-making
- **AI opacity** and its impact on professional decision-making
- Critical decisions and professional judgments with AI support
- Augmenting capabilities vs replacing professional judgment
- How professionals deal with opaque AI systems in practice
- Engagement strategies when using AI for critical judgments
- Professional practice with AI tools lacking transparency

## Theoretical Framework
- **Professional work theory**: Understanding knowledge work and expertise
- **Human-AI collaboration**: Augmentation rather than automation frameworks
- **Opacity and transparency** in algorithmic systems
- **Critical judgment**: High-stakes professional decision-making processes

## Methods
- Empirical study of professionals using AI for critical judgments
- Examination of human-AI augmentation in practice
- Analysis of engagement strategies with opaque AI systems

## Main Arguments
- Little is known about how human-AI augmentation actually takes place in professional practice
- Understanding human-AI augmentation particularly important for critical decisions
- Professionals face challenges engaging with AI tools when systems are opaque
- AI augmentation of professional judgment differs from AI automation of routine tasks
- Question of whether to engage or not engage with AI reflects fundamental tensions in professional work
- Opacity creates barriers to effective human-AI collaboration in high-stakes contexts
- Professional knowledge work requires different approach than task automation

## Limitations & Critiques
- Study context limited to specific professional domains and critical judgment types
- Generalizability across different professions and AI systems requires investigation
- Opacity as construct may vary across different AI technologies and applications
- Need for longitudinal studies tracking professional engagement strategies over time
- Balance between understanding current practices and prescribing best practices
- Professional norms and institutional contexts shape engagement possibilities
