# Optimizing Design Education Using ArtiﬁcialIntelligence

## Summary
May 16, 2025 15:59 Journal – IJHSES 2540617 Int com by INDIANA UNIVERSITY @ BLOOMINGTON on 07/13/25 Re-use and distribution is strictly not permitted, except for Open Access articles.

## Key Concepts
- Outcome-Based Education (OBE): Student-centered pedagogical framework prioritizing predefined competencies
- Intelligent color platforms: Digital tools (e.g., Coolors) providing algorithmic color generation, harmony analysis, and iterative optimization
- BP Neural Networks: Backpropagation networks for predictive analytics of learning outcomes
- Chromatic composition: Color as expressive medium and communicative tool in design
- Closed-loop adaptive system: Integration of OBE objectives, real-time feedback, and predictive analytics

## Theoretical Framework
- Outcome-Based Education (OBE): Competency-driven structure with explicit learning outcomes
- Coordination theory: Managing dependencies between educational components
- Predictive analytics: BP neural networks processing educational data for outcome forecasting
- Sociotechnical integration: Synchronizing technology affordances with pedagogical structures

## Methods
Quasi-experimental design with 120 visual communication students from three universities in Chengdu, randomly assigned to experimental (n=60) and control (n=60) groups. Experimental group used OBE-aligned instruction with Coolors platform; control group used traditional teaching. Assessment via design project scores, theoretical performance tests, and student surveys.

## Main Arguments
- Traditional color education has three limitations: overemphasis on pigment theory, inadequate technological integration, disconnection from industry workflows
- Effective AI integration requires tripartite framework: OBE competency mapping, intelligent platform feedback, BP neural network prediction
- Experimental group showed statistically significant improvements: 7.83 points higher design scores (p<0.01), 6.27 points higher theoretical performance (p<0.01)
- BP neural network achieved 2.62% mean error in learning outcome forecasts
- 71.7% student approval for digital tool integration
- Transformation requires synchronizing real-time diagnostics with curricular adaptations, not just tool adoption

## Limitations & Critiques
- Limited to 120 students from three universities in Chengdu, geographic specificity limits generalizability
- Quasi-experimental design lacks full randomization control
- Short-term assessment may not capture long-term learning retention
- Focus on color education may not extend to other design domains
- Coolors platform specific findings may not apply to other AI tools
- 71.7% student approval suggests 28.3% had reservations not fully explored
- BP neural network 2.62% error rate still leaves room for improvement
- Does not examine potential negative effects of over-reliance on AI tools
- Industry workflow alignment claimed but not empirically validated
- Limited discussion of pedagogical training required for instructors
- May not account for variation in student prior technological literacy
