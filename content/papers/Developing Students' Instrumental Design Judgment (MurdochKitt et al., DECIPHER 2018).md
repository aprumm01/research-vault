# Developing Students' Instrumental Design Judgment (MurdochKitt et al., DECIPHER 2018)

# Study Partner Note: Developing Students' Instrumental Judgment Capacity for Design Research Methods

## Summary
This paper argues that design educators must move beyond simply teaching design methods by instead cultivating students' **instrumental judgment**—the capacity to select, adapt, or create appropriate research approaches for specific design problems. The authors contend this matters because teaching methods from textbooks alone is insufficient; students need systematic, scaffolded experiences across the curriculum to develop judgment about *when and why* to use particular methods.

## Key Concepts
- **Instrumental judgment**: capacity to choose, adapt, or create appropriate design research approaches
- **Integrated studio**: a curricular model blending research, critique, history, ethics, and psychology across multiple semesters
- **Spiraling curriculum**: cascading courses that revisit and deepen research skills progressively
- **Mindset toward methods**: a reflective stance that helps students understand connections between methods and design contexts
- **Research-through-making and research-informed making**: two approaches to embedding inquiry in design practice

## Theoretical Framework
The paper draws implicitly on constructivist and experiential learning principles, emphasizing that students develop judgment through reflection, experimentation, and failure rather than passive knowledge transfer. The authors position design education as needing to bridge the research/practice divide by making methodological reasoning explicit and reflexive.

## Methods
The paper presents a case study describing the implementation of an integrated studio model across five semesters in a UX Design undergraduate program at Purdue University, with a curriculum table showing research and analysis methods introduced at each level.

## Main Arguments
- Simply assigning design methods books or asking students to select methods does not cultivate judgment
- Students need substantial space for experimentation and failure to learn when approaches work or don't work
- Educators must create opportunities for students to understand *connections* between methods and context-specific appropriateness
- A spiraling, integrated studio model across multiple semesters systematically develops instrumental judgment by revisiting and deepening research skills progressively

## Limitations & Critiques
The paper does not provide empirical evidence or assessment data demonstrating that the integrated studio model actually improves students' instrumental judgment compared to traditional approaches. Additionally, the authors do not address how this model might scale or transfer to different institutional contexts, student populations, or design disciplines beyond UX.
