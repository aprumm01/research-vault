# E1C01_1 10/14/2009 5

## Summary
Wilmoth, Prigmore, and Bray provide an overview of the major models in the Human Performance Technology (HPT) field, tracing their conceptual foundations and practical applications. The chapter surveys foundational HPT models including Gilbert's Behavior Engineering Model, Rummler and Brache's performance system model, and the ISPI HPT model, examining how each conceptualizes the relationship between human performance, environmental factors, and organizational systems. The authors argue that HPT models share a commitment to systematic analysis, cause diagnosis, and evidence-based intervention selection that distinguishes HPT from ad hoc training approaches.

## Key Concepts
- **Gilbert's Behavior Engineering Model (BEM)**: Framework distinguishing environmental supports (information, resources, incentives) from individual repertoire (knowledge, capacity, motivation) as causes of performance gaps
- **Rummler-Brache Performance System Model**: Three-level model (organization, process, job/performer) for understanding performance in context
- **ISPI HPT Model**: The International Society for Performance Improvement's comprehensive process model for performance improvement
- **Performance analysis**: The diagnostic process of identifying gaps between actual and desired performance
- **Systematic intervention selection**: Matching solutions to diagnosed causes rather than defaulting to training

## Theoretical Framework
Behavioral and systems theory foundations; HPT models integrate behavioral psychology (Skinner, Gilbert), systems engineering, and organizational development. The shared commitment is to empirical analysis rather than theoretical advocacy.

## Methods
Literature review and conceptual synthesis of major HPT models; practitioner-oriented analysis of each model's structure, assumptions, and application domains.

## Main Arguments
1. HPT models share a commitment to systematic diagnosis before intervention—the field-defining move that distinguishes HPT from training-first approaches
2. Gilbert's BEM remains the most influential diagnostic framework because it directs attention away from individual deficit toward environmental causes
3. Rummler-Brache contributes the insight that performance problems are often process and system problems, not individual performance problems
4. The proliferation of HPT models reflects the field's maturation and the diversity of contexts in which performance improvement is practiced

## Limitations
The overview necessarily simplifies models that have substantial complexity in full application. The field has continued to evolve since publication; more recent frameworks integrating technology, learning analytics, and AI are not covered.
