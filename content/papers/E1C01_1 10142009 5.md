## Summary
Wilmoth, Prigmore, and Bray provide a comprehensive overview of the major models in Human Performance Technology (HPT), examining the theoretical and practical foundations that have shaped the field. They review models from leading practitioners and examine how HPT bridges the gap between current and desired performance through systematic analysis, intervention design, and evaluation. The chapter contextualizes HPT's evolution within broader organizational improvement frameworks.

## Key Concepts
- Human Performance Technology (HPT) models
- Performance gap analysis
- Intervention design
- Organizational efficiency
- Systematic performance improvement

## Theoretical Framework
HPT draws from behavioral psychology, systems theory, and instructional design to create a process for diagnosing and improving human performance in organizational settings.

## Methods
Conceptual review; analysis and comparison of major HPT models including Gilbert's Behavior Engineering Model and the ISPI HPT model.

## Main Arguments
- HPT is a systematic approach to improving productivity through analysis, intervention, and evaluation
- Multiple HPT models exist but share core commitments to data-driven diagnosis and systemic intervention
- Successful HPT implementation requires organizational alignment between performance goals and intervention design
- The field bridges training, management, and systems engineering disciplines
