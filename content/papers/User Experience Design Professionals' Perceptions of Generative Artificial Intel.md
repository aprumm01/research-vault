## Summary
Interviews 20 UX designers across companies from startups to large enterprises to understand how GenAI impacts UX design practice, finding that experienced designers are confident in their originality and empathic skills and see GenAI as assistive. However, junior designers face heightened risks of skill degradation, job replacement, and creativity exhaustion, and broader issues of copyright, AI literacy access, and human agency remain unresolved.

## Key Concepts
- GenAI in UX design
- Responsible and participatory AI
- Human creative agency
- Skill degradation
- AI alignment and copyright

## Theoretical Framework
Responsible AI and participatory AI frameworks; emphasizes that creative professionals—not just technical experts—hold crucial knowledge for the responsible development of AI; foregrounds "enjoyment" and "agency" as irreducible human factors.

## Methods
Qualitative semi-structured interviews with 20 UX designers from varied company sizes and experience levels; thematic analysis; purposive sampling for diversity of contexts.

## Main Arguments
- Experienced designers view GenAI as a productivity assistant rather than a threat, confident in their irreplaceable human creativity and empathy
- Junior designers face real risks: skill degradation from bypassing foundational learning, potential job displacement, and creativity exhaustion
- Humans remain the arbiters of "AI alignment"—the judgment of whether AI output meets user and business needs
- Unresolved issues: copyright ownership of AI-assisted work, disparate AI literacy access, and the risk of homogenized design output

## Limitations & Critiques
20 participants cannot capture the full diversity of UX roles and contexts; qualitative sample may skew toward practitioners with strong opinions; findings represent a specific moment in GenAI's rapid evolution.
