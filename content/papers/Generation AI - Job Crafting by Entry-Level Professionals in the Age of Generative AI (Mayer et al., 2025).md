# Generation AI: Job Crafting by Entry-Level Professionals in the Age of Generative AI

## Summary
This qualitative study examines how entry-level professionals (ELPs) in knowledge work reshape their jobs in response to the adoption and integration of Generative AI (GenAI) in the workplace. Drawing on job crafting theory and insights from a case study at InnovaWorld (a pseudonym for a major global consulting firm in the Netherlands), the research shows that ELPs proactively leverage GenAI to (re)craft their work tasks and relationships.

The study identifies a novel third dimension of job crafting specific to the GenAI era: "signal crafting," where ELPs tailor the signals they send to stakeholders about the distinctiveness and value of their work. This emerges because GenAI's widespread availability makes it increasingly difficult for managers to distinguish between contributions stemming from AI versus human expertise. ELPs engage in signal crafting by becoming "GenAI pioneers" who showcase prompt engineering skills, "GenAI educators" who teach others, and by emphasizing their own contributions through transparent use of AI.

The findings reveal both positive consequences (perceived acceleration of career progress, enhanced work meaningfulness) and negative consequences (skill stagnation, threat to job relevance, reduced mentorship opportunities) arising from GenAI-induced job crafting behaviors.

## Key Concepts
- **Job Crafting**: The proactive modifications professionals make to their job tasks and relationships to better align work with their skills, motivations, and career aspirations
- **Signal Crafting**: A newly identified behavioral dimension where ELPs actively manage the visibility and image of their work and role in others' eyes to demonstrate value despite GenAI democratization
- **Task-Agnostic Versatility**: GenAI's capability to fluidly switch between diverse tasks (writing, coding, analysis) without predefined constraints, distinguishing it from earlier AI systems
- **Conversational Interface**: GenAI's dialogue-based interaction that enables reflective, exploratory work processes unlike rigid automation systems

## Theoretical Framework
The study applies Wrzesniewski and Dutton's (2001) job crafting framework, which distinguishes between task crafting (changing the scope of job tasks) and relational crafting (changing interactions with others). The authors extend this framework by identifying signal crafting as a third behavioral dimension particularly relevant to GenAI contexts, while noting that cognitive crafting was excluded as it manifests more slowly and is harder to observe.

## Methods
- **Design**: Inductive qualitative research using grounded theory methods
- **Setting**: DBI department (technology strategy consulting) at InnovaWorld, Netherlands, February-September 2024
- **Data Collection**: 27 semi-structured interviews (14 ELPs, 5 senior consultants, 8 managers) plus 57 days of field observations
- **Analysis**: Three rounds of iterative coding following Gioia et al. (2013), moving from open codes to second-order themes to aggregate dimensions

## Main Arguments
- ELPs engage in three forms of job crafting in response to GenAI: task crafting, relational crafting, and signal crafting
- Task crafting manifests as automating repetitive tasks, shifting to higher-order tasks, and developing new skills through GenAI interaction
- Relational crafting includes using GenAI as a work confidant, career mentor, and networking tool, often bypassing traditional senior mentorship
- Signal crafting emerges because GenAI democratizes expertise, creating pressure on ELPs to differentiate their contributions from AI outputs
- Consequences include both accelerated career progress and risks of skill stagnation, over-reliance, and diminished mentorship

## Limitations & Critiques
- Single case study within one organization (consultancy sector) limits generalizability to other industries and organizational contexts
- Data collected during "novelty phase" of GenAI adoption (~15 months after ChatGPT release); longer-term effects remain unknown
- Focus on behavioral dimensions of job crafting excludes cognitive crafting, which may reveal deeper identity and meaning-making changes
- Organization's tolerant AI policy may not reflect contexts with more restrictive GenAI governance
- Perceptions captured at a specific moment; longitudinal research needed to track evolving job crafting patterns
