# Integrating AI into Instructional Design: A Case Study on Digital Photography Education in Higher Education

## Summary
This qualitative case study investigates the impact of AI-supported education in higher education by examining its integration into a digital photography course in Turkey. The study involved 38 students and one instructor, with data collected through semi-structured interviews and analyzed using content analysis. The findings indicate that AI enhances educational processes by facilitating individualized learning, improving instructional effectiveness, supporting digital content development, and advancing academic language proficiency.

The instructor integrated AI tools throughout the course design using the MRK instructional design model and a constructivist pedagogical approach. Students used AI tools like ChatGPT and Gemini to analyze their photographs and receive immediate feedback on technical elements such as lighting, composition, and the rule of thirds. The study found that AI contributed to students' technical skill development and promoted active engagement in learning. However, concerns emerged about potential risks including decreased engagement, learner passivity, and exposure to misinformation or contradictory content from AI systems.

The research highlights the importance of integrating AI within a sound pedagogical framework rather than as a standalone tool. Drawing from both student and instructor experiences, the study suggests that AI-supported educational models can enhance learning efficiency while emphasizing the need for critical thinking skills and information verification practices.

## Key Concepts
- **AI-Supported Education**: Integration of artificial intelligence tools into educational settings to enhance personalized learning, feedback mechanisms, and content delivery
- **MRK Instructional Design Model**: A task-centered, flexible instructional design framework that supports iterative revision and integration of modern technologies including AI
- **Flipped Classroom with AI**: Combining the flipped classroom pedagogical model with AI tools to enhance student engagement and AI literacy
- **AI Hallucinations**: Instances where AI generates non-existent or misleading information, requiring human oversight in educational contexts
- **Technological Infrastructure Barriers**: The dependency of AI-supported education on robust technical infrastructure and equitable access to digital tools

## Theoretical Framework
The study draws on the Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge (TPACK) framework, which emphasizes the integration of technology, pedagogy, and content knowledge for effective instruction. Additionally, the research is grounded in constructivist learning theory (Vygotsky), viewing learners as active agents constructing knowledge through interaction, reflection, and experiential learning. The MRK instructional design model provides the practical framework for course design, offering flexibility and adaptability to AI integration.

## Methods
- **Design**: Qualitative case study examining AI integration in a university-level Digital Photography in Nature course
- **Participants**: 38 associate degree students (25 female, 13 male, ages 20-40+) and one instructor with 8+ years experience
- **Sampling**: Criterion sampling from students who successfully completed the course
- **Data Collection**: Semi-structured interviews via Zoom (40 min average for students; 80 min for instructor)
- **Analysis**: Content analysis guided by Lincoln and Guba's (1985) trustworthiness criteria; triangulation using MAXQDA 2020 and manual coding
- **Validation**: Peer debriefing with independent researchers; 25% of data blind-coded by external PhD researcher

## Main Arguments
- AI integration enhances instructional design by enabling personalized feedback, adaptive learning pathways, and more objective assessment processes
- Students demonstrated improvements in critical evaluation skills, technological adaptability, and academic language proficiency through AI-supported learning
- The instructor's pedagogically-informed approach positioned AI as an integral component of instruction rather than a supplementary tool
- Infrastructure limitations (connectivity, device access, premium AI tool costs) present significant barriers to effective AI integration
- AI hallucinations and misinformation risks require instructors to maintain human oversight and teach critical evaluation of AI outputs
- Students perceived AI in multiple roles: assistant, guide, teacher, and tool for fostering critical thinking
- Successful AI integration depends on continuous professional development and willingness to engage in iterative experimentation

## Limitations & Critiques
The study focuses on a single course at one Turkish university, limiting generalizability across different educational contexts and disciplines. The case study design, while providing rich contextual understanding, does not support causal claims about AI's impact on learning outcomes. The study relies primarily on self-reported interview data rather than objective measures of learning achievement. The rapid evolution of AI tools means findings may quickly become dated as new capabilities emerge. The research does not address long-term effects of AI-supported learning on student independence and critical thinking development.
