# Beyond the hype: evidence-based

## Summary
This paper provides an evidence-based critique of the "AI hype" surrounding AI integration in workplace learning, arguing that many claims about AI's transformative potential for organizational learning are significantly overstated and that practitioners need more rigorous evidence standards for evaluating AI learning tools. The authors review the available empirical evidence on AI in workplace learning contexts and find a substantial gap between marketing claims and research-backed outcomes. The paper proposes criteria for responsible AI integration in workplace learning based on existing evidence rather than hype cycles.

## Key Concepts
- Evidence-based AI integration in workplace learning
- AI hype critique and evidence assessment
- Responsible AI adoption criteria
- Workplace learning technology evaluation
- Research-to-practice gap in AI learning

## Theoretical Framework
Evidence-based practice; educational technology evaluation; responsible AI adoption; draws on systematic review methodology and technology adoption critique literature.

## Methods
Evidence review of published research on AI in workplace learning; gap analysis between marketing claims and research outcomes; synthesis of evaluation criteria from the strongest evidence.

## Main Arguments
- Most claims about AI's transformative potential for workplace learning are not supported by rigorous empirical evidence
- Practitioners are making AI integration decisions based on vendor claims and tech media rather than research
- The evidence that does exist shows conditional effectiveness: AI learning tools work in specific, bounded contexts that are frequently overgeneralized
- Responsible AI integration requires practitioners to ask evidence-based questions about specific tools in specific contexts before adoption

## Limitations & Critiques
Evidence reviews are limited by the quality and availability of published research; the rapidly evolving AI landscape means the evidence review has a short shelf life; the critique of "hype" may be perceived as AI skepticism rather than balanced evaluation.
