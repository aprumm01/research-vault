# Experimenting with Generative AI Tools and their Implications:

## Summary
This paper reports on a design course in which students and faculty systematically experimented with generative AI tools (image generators, text AI, design assistants) in studio contexts, documenting the implications for design process, creative ownership, and skill development. The authors find that AI tools accelerate certain phases of design while creating new challenges around authorship, evaluation, and the development of foundational design competencies. The paper argues for purposeful, critically reflective AI integration in design curricula rather than wholesale adoption or blanket rejection.

## Key Concepts
- Generative AI tools in design studio
- Creative authorship and AI co-creation
- Skill development and AI-mediated shortcuts
- Critical AI literacy in design education
- Curriculum integration strategies

## Theoretical Framework
Design pedagogy; human-AI collaboration frameworks; critical technology studies applied to design education; reflective practitioner tradition (Schön).

## Methods
Action research in a design course; qualitative analysis of student design processes, reflections, and artifacts when using generative AI tools over a semester.

## Main Arguments
- Generative AI can accelerate ideation and asset creation but risks bypassing the skill-building friction that makes design education valuable
- Students who use AI without critical reflection tend to converge on aesthetically similar outputs, reducing diversity of design exploration
- Effective AI integration requires explicit metacognitive instruction about when and why to use AI tools
- Design educators must develop new frameworks for evaluating creative work in AI-mediated contexts

## Limitations & Critiques
Single course context; the tools examined are rapidly evolving, making specific findings time-sensitive; the study does not track long-term skill development outcomes.
