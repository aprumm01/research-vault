# Unlocking training transfer in the age of artificial intelligence

## Summary
development programs is more urgent than ever The biggest challenge to training research stems from the advancement of technology such as artificial intelligence This article is organized into three sections.

## Key Concepts
- Training transfer challenges in rapidly evolving AI-driven work environments
- Microlearning as response to cognitive load constraints and shortened transfer intervals
- AI-enabled personalized and adaptive learning systems for individualized training
- Rapid needs assessment frameworks for accelerated training design cycles
- Pre and posttraining interventions including reverse mentoring and AI mentors
- Long-term strategies focusing on agentic behaviors and self-directed learning
- Communities of practice for navigating complex AI-related training transfers
- Technology acceptance model applied to AI tool adoption in workplace learning
- Closed skills versus open skills distinction in AI-related training content
- Workforce analytics and generative AI applications in learning and development

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on Baldwin and Ford's training transfer model, cognitive load theory, technology acceptance model (TAM), and Bandura's human agency theory to address training effectiveness in AI era.

## Methods
Conceptual article synthesizing existing training transfer research with emerging AI technologies, offering recommendations based on literature review and future-oriented analysis rather than empirical data collection.

## Main Arguments
- Traditional training transfer timelines are becoming obsolete due to rapid technological change requiring accelerated transfer intervals
- Microlearning addresses cognitive load constraints while enabling faster deployment of training content
- AI tools including chatbots, workforce analytics, and adaptive learning systems can personalize training experiences
- Organizations must shift from teaching specific AI tools to developing open skills for AI collaboration
- Pre and posttraining interventions need rapid execution through frequent SWOT analyses and AI-assisted needs assessments
- Long-term training strategies should prioritize agentic behaviors, problem-solving, and decision-making skills over tool-specific knowledge
- Continuous collaboration across education, industry, academia, and government sectors necessary for AI workforce pipeline
- Communities of practice both internal and external to organizations facilitate complex AI training transfer discussions

## Limitations & Critiques
- Purely conceptual with no empirical validation of proposed training transfer strategies
- Assumes universal access to AI tools and infrastructure across organizations regardless of size or resources
- Limited discussion of resistance to AI adoption or legitimate concerns about AI replacing human jobs
- Does not address equity issues in who benefits from personalized AI learning systems
- Overemphasis on speed and efficiency may undervalue deeper learning and critical reflection
- Reverse mentoring and AI mentors presented without evidence of effectiveness for training transfer
- Assumes linear technological progress without considering regulatory constraints or AI limitations
- Minimal attention to ethical considerations of AI-driven workforce development and surveillance
