# The Cultures of Contemporary Instructional Design Scholarship, Part One: Developments Based on Behavioral and Cognitive Science Foundations

## Summary
This article examines the current state of instructional design scholarship through the lens of four distinct "cultures" or theoretical traditions that have shaped the field. Willis argues that while instructional design emerged in the 1960s primarily from behavioral theories of learning and positivist epistemology, the field has since diversified into multiple competing paradigms. The four cultures identified are: (1) Traditional ISD based on behaviorism; (2) Design-Based Research drawing on cognitive science; (3) Constructivist Instructional Design; and (4) Critical Theory and Critical Pedagogy approaches.

The article traces the historical development from early behaviorist models like ADDIE through the incorporation of cognitive science perspectives, noting how each theoretical shift has brought different assumptions about learning, knowledge, and appropriate research methods. Willis examines how Robert Gagne's "nine events of instruction" translated behavioral learning theories into concrete instructional guidance, while later cognitive approaches introduced concepts like schema theory, information processing, and situated learning. The transition from purely behavioral to cognitive foundations was initially evolutionary rather than revolutionary, as early cognitive additions could be incorporated without abandoning behaviorist foundations.

This first article in a two-part series focuses specifically on Traditional ISD and Design-Based Research, leaving Constructivist and Critical Theory approaches for the companion piece. Willis positions himself as someone trained in behaviorism who later shifted to constructivist and interpretivist perspectives.

## Key Concepts
- **Four Cultures of ID**: Traditional ISD (behavioral), Design-Based Research (cognitive), Constructivist ID, and Critical Theory/Pedagogy approaches representing distinct theoretical foundations
- **ADDIE Model**: The dominant traditional instructional systems design model (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation) based on behavioral principles and positivist epistemology
- **Design-Based Research (DBR)**: An approach integrating design and research within authentic educational contexts to both create resources and develop theoretical knowledge
- **Gagne's Nine Events of Instruction**: A framework translating cognitive learning theory into instructional design guidance
- **Epistemological Foundations**: The underlying beliefs about knowledge that shape each approach - positivist for Traditional ISD and DBR, interpretivist for Constructivist ID

## Theoretical Framework
The article uses the concept of "cultures" to frame different ID traditions as distinct communities with their own epistemologies, methods, and values. Willis draws on Nelson and Stolterman's notion of "design culture" while examining how theoretical foundations from behavioral psychology, cognitive science, constructivism, and critical theory have each shaped different approaches to instructional design practice and scholarship.

## Methods
This is a theoretical/conceptual article that synthesizes existing literature rather than presenting original empirical research. Willis employs historical analysis and comparative examination of different ID traditions, drawing on extensive citations from foundational works in each tradition.

## Main Arguments
- Instructional design is not a unified field but comprises distinct theoretical cultures with different foundational assumptions
- Traditional ISD/ADDIE models remain dominant in practice but face increasing criticism for being too linear, rigid, and based on outdated positivist assumptions
- Design-Based Research offers a promising alternative that integrates design practice with theory development in authentic contexts
- The choice between ID approaches is not merely technical but involves fundamental epistemological and value commitments
- Practitioners should understand the theoretical foundations underlying different approaches rather than simply following procedural steps
- The field is in transition from a relatively unified discipline to one with "real choices" between fundamentally different approaches

## Limitations & Critiques
Willis acknowledges his own theoretical biases, having shifted from behaviorism to constructivism over his career. The article presents Traditional ISD somewhat critically, which may not fully represent the perspective of its proponents. As Part One of a two-part series, it does not address constructivist and critical approaches in depth. The article also notes that DBR, while promising, has its own limitations including difficulty engaging practitioners and maintaining a theory-centric focus despite claims of valuing practice.
