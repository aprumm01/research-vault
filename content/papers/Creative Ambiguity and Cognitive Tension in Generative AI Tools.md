# Creative Ambiguity and Cognitive Tension in Generative AI Tools

## Summary
Dalsgaard reframes unexpected, misaligned outputs from generative AI tools not as failures but as sources of creative ambiguity and cognitive tension that can catalyze reflection and creative reframing. Through five cross-modal cases (writing, design, music, code, prototyping), the paper demonstrates how cognitive tensions—drawing on theories of cognitive dissonance, frame conflict, and prediction error—function as generative forces in human-AI interaction, arguing for design that works with friction rather than eliminating it.

## Key Concepts
- **Creative ambiguity**: Outputs that resist easy interpretation, offering incomplete, contradictory, or semantically open-ended cues (semantic, aesthetic, intentional types)
- **Cognitive tension**: Interpretive conflict when user's internal model collides with system output
- **Productive misalignment**: Surprises and inconsistencies that catalyze reinterpretation and conceptual expansion
- **Frame conflict and reframing**: Disruption requiring renegotiation of what the task/problem actually is
- **Prediction error**: Violation of expectations triggering cognitive adaptation
- **Reflection-in-action**: Schön's concept of improvising and adjusting in response to unexpected situations
- **Interpretive labor**: Deep engagement required to make sense of ambiguous outputs

## Theoretical Framework
Integrates multiple traditions: **cognitive dissonance theory** (Festinger) on inconsistency as motivator; **frame analysis** (Goffman, Schön) on problem-setting and reframing; **predictive processing models** (Clark, Friston) on prediction error driving cognitive adaptation; **ambiguity in design** (Gaver et al.) as provocation for meaning-making; **pragmatist design theory** (Schön, Dewey) on reflection-in-action; **insight-driven cognition** (Kounios & Beeman, Smith) on breaking representational frames. Positions misalignment as epistemic friction that triggers creative shifts rather than error to be eliminated.

## Methods
**Cross-modal case analysis** examining five examples spanning text, image, sound, code, and form generation. Uses theoretical analysis grounded in cognitive ergonomics and creativity research. Presented at ECCE 2025 (European Association of Cognitive Ergonomics). Cases demonstrate how ambiguity and tension co-produce creative shifts through interpretive engagement. Not empirical study but conceptual framework with illustrative examples showing pattern across creative domains.

## Main Arguments
- Misalignment between user intent and AI output should be treated as provocation for reflection, not error to optimize away
- Creative ambiguity (semantic, aesthetic, intentional) stimulates meaning-making and conceptual expansion rather than representing absence of meaning
- Cognitive tension operates through three mechanisms: dissonance motivating rethinking; frame conflict requiring problem redefinition; prediction error triggering adaptation
- Productive disruption requires interpretive labor but enables new creative pathways when users are prepared to "stay with the tension"
- Design should scaffold users' ability to navigate ambiguity rather than eliminate it entirely
- Surprise and tension sustain attention and invite playful exploration in creative processes
- Shift needed from optimization toward reflection, designing to work with friction not minimize it
- Emotional dimension matters: reactions from frustration to fascination are part of creative experience
- When users have interpretive resources, domain expertise, and time, ambiguity leads to insight; without these, becomes derailment

## Limitations & Critiques
Ambiguity is not universally beneficial: without interpretive resources, domain expertise, or time, it becomes derailment rather than insight. Framework requires users willing and able to "stay with the tension," which is not always feasible in constrained workflows or for novice users. Limited empirical validation; primarily theoretical with illustrative cases rather than systematic user studies. Does not provide operational criteria for distinguishing fruitful ambiguity from obstructive noise in practice. Assumes creative contexts where exploration is valued; may not apply to efficiency-focused or high-stakes domains. Does not address how to design systems that adaptively scaffold ambiguity navigation based on user state. Lacks examination of when cognitive tension tips from productive to overwhelming.
