## Summary
Januszewski and Molenda's AECT definitional work establishes the 2008 official definition of educational technology as "the study and ethical practice of facilitating learning and improving performance by creating, using, and managing appropriate technological processes and resources." The text unpacks each element of this definition, tracing the historical development of the field from visual instruction to audiovisual communication to the broader conception of instructional technology. The definition grounds the field's professional identity and scope.

## Key Concepts
- Educational technology definition (AECT 2008)
- Facilitation vs. instruction (shift in conceptualization)
- Performance improvement alongside learning
- Ethical practice as a core professional obligation
- Technological processes and resources (broad conception)

## Theoretical Framework
Professional/disciplinary definitional framework; draws on the historical evolution of AECT's prior definitions (1963, 1972, 1977, 1994) to situate the 2008 revision within the field's intellectual development.

## Methods
Conceptual and historical analysis; committee-based definition development typical of professional association standard-setting; comparative analysis of prior AECT definitions.

## Main Arguments
- The field has expanded from focusing narrowly on audiovisual media to encompassing all processes and resources for learning and performance improvement
- "Ethical practice" was explicitly added in 2008, reflecting the profession's responsibility beyond technical effectiveness
- Facilitating learning (not just delivering instruction) represents a student-centered epistemological commitment
- The definition must balance precision (guiding practice) with breadth (capturing the field's full scope)

## Limitations & Critiques
Definitional consensus documents necessarily smooth over genuine theoretical disagreements within the field; the 2008 definition has been critiqued for underemphasizing sociocultural and critical perspectives on technology.
