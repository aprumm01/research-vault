# AI Rivalry as a Craft: Writing Professionals (Varanasi et al. NYU)

## Summary
Varanasi, Wiesenfeld, and Nov report on how 25 writing professionals navigate generative AI through the lens of Job Crafting theory, identifying four distinct strategies: some resist GAI to maximize human potential and preserve identity, while others embrace it to enhance workflows and take on new AI-managerial labor. The paper shows that GAI adoption is not binary acceptance or rejection but a complex, ongoing process of role reshaping that has identity implications alongside practical workflow effects. It contributes empirical richness to debates about professional futures in the age of GAI.

## Key Concepts
- Job Crafting theory (Wrzesniewski & Dutton)
- GAI resistance and embracing as strategic choices
- Professional identity and credibility preservation
- AI-managerial labor (new work created by managing AI outputs)
- Invisible work amplification through AI

## Theoretical Framework
Job Crafting theory — the idea that workers proactively shape their job boundaries, tasks, and relationships — applied to explain how writing professionals actively restructure their roles in response to GAI pressure rather than passively adapting.

## Methods
25 semi-structured interviews with writing professionals who adopted and/or resisted GAI (screenwriters, paralegals, journalists, marketing writers, etc.); thematic analysis using Job Crafting as interpretive framework.

## Main Arguments
- Writing professionals employ four GAI-response strategies: maximize human potential, reinforce identity, carve niche, preserve credibility (resistance strategies) vs. enhance workflow, minimize mundane tasks, manage AI outputs (adoption strategies)
- GAI resistance is an active, strategic practice, not merely technophobia
- Embracing GAI creates new invisible labor (managing, prompting, verifying AI outputs) that is not yet recognized or compensated
- Professional identity is as much a driver of AI adoption/rejection as practical utility

## Limitations & Critiques
Sample recruited via snowball/purposive sampling and may overrepresent writers who are already reflective about their GAI practices; the study's qualitative nature limits claims about prevalence of each strategy across the broader profession.
