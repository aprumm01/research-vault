## Summary
Varanasi, Wiesenfeld, and Nov study how writing professionals at NYU frame their relationship to GenAI as a matter of craft identity — either embracing AI as a tool that extends their craft or resisting it as a threat to professional distinctiveness. The paper finds that craft identity serves as a key psychological and professional resource through which writers negotiate AI's encroachment on their domain, with both resistance and embracement strategies reflecting craft values. This contributes a craft theory framework to professional identity research under AI disruption.

## Theoretical Framework
Craft identity theory applied to creative professional work; draws on professional identity literature and technology adoption research to frame how writers' self-conception as craftspeople mediates their relationship to AI tools.

## Methods
Empirical qualitative study with writing professionals; likely interviews or survey with thematic analysis; NYU Tandon School of Engineering and Stern School of Business; published in ACM or related venue.

## Main Arguments
- Writing professionals frame AI rivalry through craft identity — as craftspeople, not just employees
- Both AI resistance and embracement can be craft-consistent strategies, not opposites
- Craft identity provides professional resilience resources in the face of AI disruption
- Understanding professional identity is essential for predicting how creative workers will adapt to AI
