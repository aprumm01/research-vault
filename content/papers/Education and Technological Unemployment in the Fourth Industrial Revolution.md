# Education and Technological Unemployment in the Fourth Industrial Revolution

## Summary
Explores the rapidly growing body of research on technological unemployment in the context of the Fourth Industrial Revolution, arguing that educational solutions grounded in human capital theory and "homo economicus" are inadequate. Proposes a non-supercessionist model of education for the 4IR centered on "homo collaborans"—the collaborative, creative human—that fundamentally rethinks work, education, and research in an automated economy.

## Key Concepts
- Technological unemployment (4IR)
- Homo economicus vs. homo collaborans
- Non-supercessionist education
- Cognitive capitalism
- Open/creative digital university

## Theoretical Framework
Cognitive capitalism theory; critique of human capital theory; non-supercessionist approach that rejects simple replacement narratives; blends digital university models with post-work economic thinking.

## Methods
Theoretical/conceptual chapter; review of economic and educational literature on technological unemployment; normative argument for a new educational framework; no original empirical data.

## Main Arguments
- Technological unemployment may become the rule rather than the exception, but human capital education models are structurally unable to address this
- Homo economicus assumptions underlie most current education policy and must be replaced with homo collaborans: humans as collaborative, creative, and relational
- A non-supercessionist approach avoids both techno-utopianism (machines replace humans) and techno-dystopianism (humans resist machines)
- The open/creative digital public university is proposed as an institutional model for this reorientation

## Limitations & Critiques
Highly normative and theoretical; the "homo collaborans" framework is prescriptive rather than empirically grounded; the proposed educational model is aspirational with limited practical implementation guidance; debate about the actual scale of technological unemployment remains unresolved.
