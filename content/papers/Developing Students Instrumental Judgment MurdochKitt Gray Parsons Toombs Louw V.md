# Developing Students Instrumental Judgment MurdochKitt Gray Parsons Toombs Louw VanGent

## Summary
This paper explores how design educators can develop students’ instrumental judgment capacity for design research methods. Instrumental judgment includes the capacity to choose appropriate approaches to design problems, decide from established options, or create new approaches. The authors present a case study of an integrated studio model at Purdue University that systematically develops instrumental judgment through spiraling curriculum across five semesters, where students learn research methods while practicing them on industry projects.

## Key Concepts
- Instrumental judgment - capacity to choose appropriate approaches to design problems, decide from array of established options, or create new approaches to design challenges
- Integrated studio model - educational approach where students learn across multiple content strands in each session, practicing design activities and critique while blending research, history, ethics, and psychology
- Spiraling development - cascading set of studios across five semesters where students learn, build, and deepen skills in user research, prototyping, evaluation, and design philosophy
- Dual-strand studio experience - learning studios for skill development in heightened reality paired with industry experience studios for real-world practice on cross-cohort teams
- Methods mindset - going beyond owning design methods book to understanding what connects methods, why certain methods useful in specific contexts, where new methods or combinations needed
- Design rigor - unique to design discipline, lies in designer’s character, identity, and sense of competence rather than borrowed scientific approaches
- Research-through-making - creative inquiry process integrating making activities with research investigation
- Research-informed making - design practice drawing on research insights to guide creative decisions
- Professional judgment - designers’ unique skill sets bound up in capacity for judgment, particularly regarding research methods selection and application
- Weak methods teaching - presenting methods as prescriptive procedures without intentional accounting for what aspects of human experience to explore or what analytic lens to employ

## Theoretical Framework
- Nelson and Stolterman’s (2012) Design Way framework - informing approach to intentional change and design competence development
- Gray’s (2016) mindset toward methods - conceptualizing methods knowledge as dispositional rather than purely procedural

## Methods
- Conference workshop activity with design educators (DECIPHER 2018)
- Paired brainstorming using worksheets adapted from Nelson and Stolterman framework
- Participants reflected from student and faculty perspectives on four questions: how approach work, what know, what able to do, what able to facilitate
- Case study documentation of integrated studio model at Purdue University UX Design program
- Synthesis and categorization of participant responses across reflection prompts

## Main Arguments
- Owning design methods book insufficient for cultivating judgment - educators must create opportunities for students to understand connections between methods, contextual appropriateness, and when new approaches needed
- Integrated studio overcomes course-based instructional constraints by enabling learning across content strands with reflexive spiraling of skills over five semesters
- Dual-strand approach essential: learning studios provide heightened reality for skill development while industry experience studios enable cross-cohort real-world practice
- Methods often weakly taught when viewed instrumentally without commitment to unique rigor of design lying in designer’s character, identity, and competence
- Designers need descriptive, exploratory, and generative approaches valuing lived experience and individual student knowledge rather than prescriptive method application
- Design discipline better served by describing designers’ unique skill sets rather than adding "sciences" to work for credibility with other disciplines
- Students require substantial space for experimentation and failure to learn when approaches not working or when different methods would serve better
- Program-wide continuity essential: students must continuously learn and practice research skills with attention to progression and deepening over time, not just semester-by-semester skill acquisition
- Tension exists between engaging with methods texts pragmatically while developing higher-order skills like professional judgment and contextual sensitivity
- Design research teaching must help students identify relationships between design theory and concrete design activities, bridging abstraction and application

## Limitations & Critiques
- Case study based on single program (Purdue UX Design) limits generalizability to other design disciplines, institutional contexts, or pedagogical approaches
- Workshop activity provided limited time for establishing baseline understanding of instrumental judgment concept among diverse participants
- No empirical assessment of student outcomes: paper describes curriculum design but doesn’t measure whether integrated studio actually develops superior instrumental judgment compared to traditional approaches
- Table 1 shows methods distribution but doesn’t explain selection rationale or how decisions made about which methods taught in which semester
- Missing student voice: case study presents faculty perspective on curriculum design without examining how students experience or perceive instrumental judgment development
- Dual-strand model requires significant resources (program-wide experience studio, industry partnerships, cross-cohort coordination) that may be inaccessible to smaller programs
- Workshop synthesis presents participant responses without critical analysis of tensions or contradictions between different educators’ conceptions of instrumental judgment
- Paper acknowledges "key themes and tensions emerged" but provides limited depth on what those tensions were or how they might be resolved
- No longitudinal data tracking graduates: unclear whether instrumental judgment developed during program transfers to professional practice or persists over time
- Critique of "weak methods teaching" lacks specificity about what constitutes strong methods pedagogy beyond avoiding prescriptive approach
