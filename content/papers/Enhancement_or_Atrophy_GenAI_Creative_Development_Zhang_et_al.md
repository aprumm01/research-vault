# Enhancement or Atrophy? Examining How Generative AI Reshapes Creative Development in Design Education

## Summary
This quasi-experimental study with 45 Chinese design students empirically measures GenAI's differential effects across the four TTCT dimensions of creative thinking (fluency, flexibility, originality, elaboration) using expert evaluations, questionnaires, and interviews. The central finding is an "efficiency-originality divergence": AI significantly improved elaboration and fluency but had negligible effects on originality. The paper also identifies systematic "perception-performance gaps" where students dramatically overestimated their AI-enhanced creativity, and introduces the concept of "Creative Subjectivity Negotiation" to explain identity reconstruction in human-AI collaboration.

## Key Concepts
- **TTCT (Torrance Tests of Creative Thinking)**: The four-dimensional framework for measuring creativity: fluency (quantity of ideas), flexibility (diversity of categories), originality (novelty), and elaboration (detailed development)
- **Efficiency-Originality Divergence**: The core finding that AI enhances output quantity and detail (elaboration d=1.97, fluency d=0.84) but has negligible impact on originality (d=0.22, not significant)
- **Perception-Performance Gap**: The systematic discrepancy between students' self-reported creativity improvements (Creative Behavior d=2.01) and expert-rated actual gains—attributed to capability illusions and novelty effects
- **Creative Subjectivity Negotiation**: A theoretical framework explaining how students reconstruct creative identity when working with AI, navigating tensions between technological dependency and creative autonomy
- **Optimism Bias**: Students' tendency to overestimate AI-enhanced capabilities, potentially masking skill development gaps and delaying awareness of cognitive dependency
- **Agency Negotiation**: The capacity to maintain creative ownership and intentionality when AI generates content—identified as the core competency design education must cultivate
- **Distributed Creativity Theory**: Glaveanu's framework treating creativity as emerging from interactions between individuals, tools, and cultural contexts, used to theorize human-AI creative collaboration

## Theoretical Framework
The study draws on Guilford's Structure of Intellect Model and Torrance's TTCT framework for measuring divergent thinking, combined with Distributed Cognition Theory and Glaveanu's Distributed Creativity Theory to theorize how AI enters the human-tool-environment system. The paper's key theoretical contribution is "Creative Subjectivity Negotiation"—a framework for understanding identity reconstruction across four dimensions: temporal (short-term vs. long-term capability development), skill-based (which competencies atrophy vs. develop), identity-based (designer identity in relation to AI), and evaluative (capacity to critically assess AI outputs).

The explanatory sequential mixed-methods design (quantitative phase followed by qualitative phenomenological interviews) reflects a commitment to integrating objective performance metrics with subjective creator experiences—a methodological gap the authors identify in prior literature.

## Methods
A 5-week quasi-experimental study with 45 third-year Visual Communication Design undergraduates at Guangzhou Huali College. Pre-test (O1): participants completed a 180-minute illustration task using traditional methods only. Intervention (Weeks 2-3): systematic AI training covering ChatGPT-4 for concept generation, Midjourney v6.1 and Adobe Firefly v3.0 for visual generation, and AI ethics. Post-test (O2): equivalent illustration task with AI tools encouraged. Three experts evaluated works using TTCT framework under double-blind conditions (ICC ≥ 0.70 required). A 27-item questionnaire measured self-perception, and 20 students completed 30-45 minute semi-structured phenomenological interviews.

## Main Arguments
- AI significantly enhanced elaboration (d=1.97, large effect) and fluency (d=0.84, medium effect), indicating it accelerates output volume and detail but this comes at the cost of depth and originality
- Originality showed negligible improvement (d=0.22, not statistically significant), providing empirical evidence that AI's greatest weakness is precisely the creative dimension most valued in professional design practice
- Self-reported improvements dramatically exceeded expert-rated gains across all dimensions, revealing systematic optimism bias that may impair students' capacity to accurately assess their own development
- Most students oscillated between "technological dependency" (outsourcing creative judgment to AI) and "cognitive disorientation" (uncertainty about authorship and identity); only a minority maintained creative autonomy
- The Creative Subjectivity Negotiation framework identifies four negotiation dimensions and argues that successful human-AI collaboration requires "mature negotiation strategies" that reposition AI as "dialogue partner" rather than "creative generator"
- Design education must shift from tool-centric training (how to use AI efficiently) toward cultivating agency negotiation competencies (how to maintain creative subjectivity while collaborating with AI)

## Limitations & Critiques
The single-group quasi-experimental design lacks a true control group, limiting capacity to isolate AI intervention effects from maturation, practice effects, or task differences. The 5-week timeframe cannot distinguish transient novelty effects from sustained trajectories. The study is conducted entirely in China, raising questions about cultural generalizability given China's specific AI education context and cultural norms around creativity. The small sample (N=45) limits statistical power for detecting effects in the originality dimension.

The paper's theoretical concept of "Creative Subjectivity Negotiation," while generative, is based on a relatively small interview sample (N=20) and requires further validation across different design disciplines, AI tools, and cultural contexts.
