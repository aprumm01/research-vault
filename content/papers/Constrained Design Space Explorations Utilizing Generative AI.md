## Summary
This paper examines faculty and student perspectives on utilizing generative AI in design education, drawing on survey data and interviews to understand current adoption patterns, attitudes, and concerns. The study finds wide variation in how design educators approach AI integration: some embrace it as a new design material and studio tool, others resist it as a threat to skill development, and many are navigating uncertainty about appropriate use. The paper argues for discipline-wide dialogue about AI's role in design education rather than ad hoc individual responses.

## Key Concepts
- Faculty attitudes toward AI in design education
- Student AI adoption patterns
- Design skill development in the age of AI
- Curriculum policy for AI tools
- AI as design material vs. AI as productivity tool

## Theoretical Framework
Design education research; technology adoption theory; draws on surveys of attitudes toward educational technology and emerging AI in education literature.

## Methods
Mixed-methods study; survey of design faculty and students; follow-up interviews; descriptive statistical analysis and qualitative thematic analysis.

## Main Arguments
- Design faculty hold heterogeneous views on AI, ranging from enthusiastic adoption to principled resistance, without shared pedagogical frameworks
- Students are adopting AI tools regardless of faculty stance, creating a gap between formal instruction and actual practice
- The field needs shared frameworks for distinguishing between productive AI use (enhancing design thinking) and counterproductive use (bypassing design skill development)
- Institutional policies on AI in design courses are lagging behind student and faculty practice

## Limitations & Critiques
Survey response rates and sample representativeness may limit generalizability; the rapid pace of AI tool development means the findings may be partially outdated; disciplinary variation within design is not fully captured.
