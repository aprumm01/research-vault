# Navigating a New Direction in HCI Education Challenges of Teaching More-Than-Human Perspectives

## Summary
This paper follows up on the challenges we encountered when we initially began teaching more-than-human perspectives in humancomputer interaction (HCI) and technology design Since then, we have developed a collection of teaching activities and conducted pilots with nearly 800 students, exploring how these perspectives can be taught in various settings Although we think that our teaching activities address some of the initial challenges, several challenges are still open, and new ones have emerged such as the gap between what we are teaching and the industry, and the feelings of hopelessness that students may feel when confronted with the notion that technology may not fix all problems.

## Main Arguments
- Teaching more-than-human perspectives in HCI presents ongoing challenges
- Initial challenges partially addressed through developed teaching activities
- New challenges emerge: gap between curriculum and industry expectations
- Students experience hopelessness when confronting technology's limitations
- Important to teach that technology may not fix all problems, despite student discomfort
- Various educational settings require adapted approaches
- Nearly 800 students provide evidence of scalability and recurring patterns
- Need to bridge critical perspectives with practical design skills
- More-than-human thinking requires fundamental shift from human-centered design
- Pedagogical evolution continues as field develops

## Limitations & Critiques
- Focus on challenges may underemphasize successes and student learning outcomes
- "Nearly 800 students" provides scale but limited depth per student experience
- Industry gap suggests potential employability concerns for students
- Hopelessness response may indicate pedagogical framing issues
- Limited discussion of assessment methods for more-than-human thinking
- May not fully address how to prepare students for industry while teaching critique
- Cross-setting research limits depth of contextual analysis
- Does not provide clear resolution to identified challenges
- Long-term impacts on student practice and thinking not examined
- Cultural and institutional variations in adoption underexplored
