# integrating-ai-into-instructional-design-a-case-study-on-digital-photography-education-in-higher-ed

## Summary
Received: 7 Feb 2025 This study investigates the impact of artificial intelligence (AI)-supported education in higher education, specifically examining its integration into a digital photography course and its effects on both students and instructors A qualitative research methodology was employed, and participants were selected through purposive sampling The study involved one instructor and 38 students, with data collected through semi-structured interviews and analyzed using content analysis within a qualitative case study design.

## Key Concepts
- **AI-supported education** in higher education context (digital photography course)
- Impact on both students (38) and instructor (1)
- Qualitative case study examining AI integration effects
- Purposive sampling for participant selection
- Semi-structured interviews for data collection
- Content analysis methodology
- AI integration into specific disciplinary course (photography) rather than general context

## Theoretical Framework
- **Technology integration in higher education**: Frameworks for understanding educational technology adoption
- Instructional design principles for AI-enhanced learning
- Case study methodology for understanding educational interventions

## Methods
case study, interview

## Main Arguments
- AI integration into digital photography course produces measurable impacts on teaching and learning
- Both student and instructor perspectives essential for understanding AI-supported education
- Qualitative case study reveals nuanced effects not captured by quantitative measures alone
- AI support in higher education requires examination at course-specific level
- Integration effects differ for students (n=38) versus instructor, requiring separate analysis
- Semi-structured interviews provide rich data on AI integration experiences
- Digital photography as case reveals discipline-specific considerations for AI integration

## Limitations & Critiques
- Single course case study limits generalizability to other disciplines
- One instructor limits understanding of instructor-level variation
- Purposive sampling may not represent full range of student experiences
- Snapshot study may miss longer-term effects of AI integration
- Need for comparison with non-AI-supported photography instruction
- Content analysis approach and coding reliability considerations
- Study received Feb 2025 reflects early-stage AI integration in education
