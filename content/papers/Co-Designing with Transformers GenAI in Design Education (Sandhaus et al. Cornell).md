# Co-Designing with Transformers: GenAI in Design Education (Sandhaus et al. Cornell)

## Summary
Sandhaus, Hegde, and colleagues at Cornell report on integrating large language model-based tools into design education courses, documenting how students and instructors navigate the introduction of transformer-based AI into design studio workflows. The paper examines student attitudes, adoption patterns, and the pedagogical challenges that emerge when AI becomes a studio collaborator. The authors identify a need for new frameworks for evaluating AI-assisted design work and for helping students develop AI literacy alongside design skills.

## Key Concepts
- Transformer-based AI in design studio
- AI literacy in design education
- Co-design with AI systems
- Pedagogical adaptation to AI tools
- Evaluation challenges for AI-assisted design

## Theoretical Framework
Design pedagogy; human-AI collaboration; constructivist learning; draws on studio-based learning theory and emerging AI literacy frameworks.

## Methods
Case study of AI integration in Cornell design courses; qualitative data from student reflections, instructor observations, and design artifact analysis; action research approach.

## Main Arguments
- Students rapidly adopt AI tools for ideation but struggle to critically evaluate and iterate on AI outputs
- Instructors face significant challenges in adapting evaluation criteria to account for AI's role in student work
- AI literacy — understanding what AI can and cannot do and making principled decisions about its use — must be explicitly taught
- Design education's value lies increasingly in the judgment students bring to AI-assisted work, not just production skills

## Limitations & Critiques
Single institution case study; the rapid evolution of AI tools means specific findings may have short validity; the study does not include long-term follow-up on student design practice.
