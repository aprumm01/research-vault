# Katz 2017 - Manufacturing an artificial intelligence revolution

## Summary
While the term “Artificial Intelligence” (AI) was coined in the 1950s, in recent years AI has become a focus of attention in mainstream media Yet the forces behind AI’s revival have been unclear I argue here that the “AI” label has been rebranded to promote a contested vision of world governance through big data.

## Key Concepts
- **AI rebranding**: "Artificial Intelligence" label rebranded since 1950s origins
- **Manufactured revolution**: Forces behind AI's recent revival and mainstream media focus
- **Big data governance vision**: AI label used to promote contested vision of world governance
- Distinction between historical AI and contemporary rebranding
- Revival of AI terminology in recent years (pre-2017)
- Political and economic dimensions of AI discourse
- AI as tool for legitimating particular governance approaches

## Theoretical Framework
- **Science and Technology Studies (STS)**: Social construction of technological revolutions
- **Critical discourse analysis**: Examination of how AI terminology is deployed
- **Political economy of technology**: AI as instrument of governance and power
- History of AI as field and concept

## Methods
- Historical analysis of AI terminology evolution since 1950s
- Critical discourse analysis of AI revival in mainstream media
- Examination of political and economic forces behind AI rebranding

## Main Arguments
- "AI revolution" has been manufactured rather than emerging organically from technical breakthroughs
- AI label has been strategically rebranded to serve particular political and economic interests
- Recent AI revival driven by forces beyond technical advancement
- Big data governance vision promoted through AI terminology
- Contemporary AI discourse differs significantly from historical origins
- Mainstream media focus on AI reflects manufactured rather than natural phenomenon
- Contested visions of governance embedded in AI promotion

## Limitations & Critiques
- Published 2017, predates current wave of generative AI developments
- Need to examine how manufacturing thesis applies to post-2017 AI developments
- Balance between critical analysis and recognition of genuine technical progress
- Specificity of "big data governance vision" requires further elaboration
- May underestimate role of actual technical capabilities in AI revival
- Manufacturing thesis may not account for all drivers of AI prominence
