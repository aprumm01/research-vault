# Shaping Human Capital Standards - Exploring the intersections of the future of work and AI

## Summary
Shaping Human Capital Standards: Exploring the Intersections of the Future of Work and Artificial Intelligence AUTHORS Tania Saba Anne-Marie Hubert Myriam Bernet January 2025 Authors Tania Saba PH , CRHA Distinction Fellow Full Professor , School of Industrial Relations, Faculty of Arts and Science, Université de Montréal Chairholder, Chaire BMO en diversité et gouvernance Director Equity, diversity and inclusion - Obvia.

## Key Concepts
- Human capital: Skills, knowledge, experience, health, well-being, diversity, and collective capabilities
- Sustainable finance: Integration of social considerations into financial systems
- IFRS Foundation: International accounting and sustainability standards
- AI-driven transformation: Reshaping how skills and talents are utilized in organizations
- Workforce diversity and inclusion: Strategic aspects leveraging varied perspectives for innovation

## Theoretical Framework
- Becker's human capital theory (1964): Skills and knowledge as productive investments
- Sustainable Development Goals: Framework for global sustainability integration
- International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB): Framework for transparency and disclosure
- Social and environmental dimensions: Integration into financial and organizational systems

## Methods
Literature review and analysis of 31 reports from international institutions (OECD, ILO, IMF, WEF, Eurofound, European Commission, Future Skills Centre) from 2022-2024

## Main Arguments
- AI reshapes human capital through productivity increases, new skill development, but also job polarization and inclusivity challenges
- Six key AI opportunities: resource optimization, enhanced decision-making, workforce transformation, innovation acceleration, social equity, sustainability
- Major challenges: AI-specific skill gaps, STEM education deficits, gender gaps, pandemic-induced inequities, workforce autonomy strain
- AI-driven monitoring erodes privacy and autonomy, contributing to worker stress
- Green jobs require interdisciplinary skills many workers lack
- Global disparities in AI access limit developing nations' ability to build talent
- Need for lifelong learning, reskilling programs, and cross-sector collaboration
- Ethical AI integration requires transparency, fairness, and alignment with sustainability goals

## Limitations & Critiques
- Literature review limited to 2022-2024 publications (31 reports); rapid AI evolution may date findings quickly
- No primary empirical research; relies entirely on secondary analysis of institutional reports
- Focus on global institutions (OECD, ILO, IMF, WEF) may miss localized or non-Western perspectives
- Social sustainability dimensions acknowledged as lagging behind environmental standards in frameworks/indicators
- Report identifies problems and frameworks but provides limited implementation guidance for organizations
- Human capital redefinition comprehensive but operationalization challenges not addressed
- Sector-specific analysis brief without deep case studies demonstrating successful AI integration
- Ethical AI considerations mentioned but concrete governance mechanisms underspecified
