# Chatbot Research and Design CONVERSATIONS 2023 - Folstad et al (eds) - 2023

## Summary
LNCS 14524 Asbjørn Følstad · Theo Araujo · Symeon Papadopoulos · Effie L Law · Ewa Luger · Morten Goodwin · Sebastian Hobert · Petter Bae Brandtzaeg (Eds ) Chatbot Research and Design 7th International Workshop, CONVERSATIONS 2023 Oslo, Norway, November 22–23, 2023 Revised Selected Papers Lecture Notes in Computer Science Founding Editors Gerhard Goos Juris Hartmanis Editorial Board Members Elisa Bertino, Purdue University, West Lafayette, IN, USA.

## Key Concepts
- **Large language model integration**: 2023 marks watershed moment with ChatGPT's November 2022 public launch creating new research challenges, changing user expectations, and enabling improved conversational interactions for chatbots
- **Conversational AI applications**: Seven key research areas—user insight, user experience, chatbot frameworks/platforms, human-chatbot collaboration, chatbots for all, ethical implications, and leveraging AI advances
- **Chatbot design and research themes**: Four overarching domains—understanding conversational interactions, LLM-driven design/analysis, ethical perspectives and bias, and complementary perspectives on gaming, abuse, and future needs
- **Double-blind peer review process**: 35 submissions (25 full papers, 6 position papers, 2 project presentations, 2 groupwork proposals); 48% acceptance rate with 12 full papers accepted after minor/major revisions
- **Hybrid workshop format**: International meeting place for researchers and practitioners, conducted as two-day free event with 147 participants from 23 countries (39 on-site, remainder online)
- **Dark patterns and bias**: Emerging concerns about conversational design manipulations, language ideology bias, gender identity considerations, and user abuse of conversational agents

## Theoretical Framework
As an edited conference proceedings volume, the paper integrates multiple theoretical perspectives across contributed papers:

1. **Conversational interaction theory**: Papers examining user perspectives on specific applications (voting assistants, customer service, open government data guides) drawing on conversation analysis and repair mechanisms

2. **LLM-driven design frameworks**: Contributions leveraging large language models for human-like conversations (Furhat social robot), user simulation for design feedback, and lexical alignment analysis

3. **Critical and ethical perspectives**: Papers applying critical design lens to examine dark patterns, language ideology bias, gender considerations in conversational AI—drawing on ethics of persuasion and critical algorithm studies

4. **User experience and anticipatory design**: Design fiction methodology for understanding future chatbot needs, game character embodiment studies, and literature synthesis on user-agent abuse patterns

The volume reflects field transition from rule-based chatbots to LLM-powered conversational AI, requiring new theoretical and methodological approaches.

## Methods
Conference proceedings compilation from CONVERSATIONS 2023 workshop (7th in series), November 22-23, 2023, hosted by University of Oslo and Centre for Research on Media Innovations. Double-blind peer review with three independent reviewers per submission, meta-reviews by organizer review leads, and dedicated acceptance meeting. Accepted papers underwent minor or major revisions with compliance checks. Workshop featured six paper sessions with 21 presentations plus two keynote speakers (Nena van As on conversational AI in customer service, Finn Myrstad on generative AI consumer perspectives). Best paper award through juried process among top-scoring nominees.

## Main Arguments
- **ChatGPT launch represents field-defining moment**: November 2022 public launch created substantial advancement in chatbot research, applications, and design, fundamentally shifting landscape from previous rule-based approaches to LLM-powered systems with new capabilities and challenges

- **Active meeting places essential during rapid change**: Given rapid technological evolution, workshop series like CONVERSATIONS provides critical open and inclusive arena for sharing research, applications, and design across academic researchers and practitioners

- **Four thematic research domains emerge**: Contemporary chatbot research clusters around (1) understanding/enhancing conversational interactions in specific applications, (2) leveraging LLMs for design and analysis, (3) addressing ethical perspectives and bias, and (4) exploring complementary issues like gaming, abuse, and future needs

- **Ethical and bias concerns intensify with LLMs**: As conversational AI becomes more sophisticated, issues of dark patterns in design, language ideology bias, gender considerations, and user abuse patterns require urgent attention from research and practice communities

- **Interdisciplinary collaboration necessary**: Workshop brings together diverse disciplines (computer science, HCI, communication studies, consumer advocacy) reflecting that chatbot challenges cannot be addressed from single disciplinary perspective

- **Quality standards through rigorous review**: 48% acceptance rate with mandatory revisions and compliance checks ensures proceedings maintain high research standards despite rapid technological change

## Limitations & Critiques
- **Proceedings format limits depth**: As edited collection of diverse papers rather than single coherent research study, depth and integration across themes necessarily limited. Individual papers may have methodological limitations not addressed in overview

- **Temporal snapshot problem**: Conference proceedings from November 2023 capture moment in rapidly evolving field; findings may become outdated quickly given pace of LLM advancement (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini developments)

- **Geographic and linguistic bias**: While 23 countries represented, workshop hosted in Oslo with English-language proceedings may underrepresent perspectives from non-Western contexts or non-English speaking communities where chatbot adoption differs

- **Selection bias in accepted papers**: 48% acceptance rate and focus on LLM-related contributions may create publication bias toward certain methodologies, applications, or findings, potentially missing important alternative perspectives

- **Limited industry participation details**: While workshop aims to include practitioners, proceedings don't clearly specify industry vs. academic participant ratios or whether industry perspectives adequately represented in research contributions

- **Hybrid format limitations**: 108 of 147 participants attended online; quality of participation, networking opportunities, and collaborative outcomes may differ significantly between on-site and remote attendees

- **Ethical review gaps**: While ethical perspectives form one thematic section, unclear whether proceedings papers underwent institutional ethics review for human subjects research, particularly concerning user studies of vulnerable populations

- **Generalizability across chatbot types**: Papers focus on specific applications (voting assistants, customer service, gaming NPCs); findings may not generalize across diverse chatbot contexts (healthcare, education, government services)

- **Missing longitudinal perspectives**: Snapshot of 2023 research lacks longitudinal tracking of how user expectations, design patterns, or ethical concerns evolve as LLM-powered chatbots become normalized

- **Implementation gap**: Academic research presented at conference may not reflect practical implementation challenges, regulatory constraints, or economic factors shaping real-world chatbot deployment
