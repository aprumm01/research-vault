# Usability Inspection Methods

## Summary

This CHI'95 tutorial overview by Jakob Nielsen provides a comprehensive introduction to usability inspection methods - a family of cost-effective techniques for evaluating user interfaces. Nielsen argues that while empirical user testing is valuable, inspection methods offer a practical alternative when resources are limited or rapid evaluation is needed.

The paper surveys the major inspection methods available at the time and discusses their relative strengths, weaknesses, and appropriate use cases. Nielsen emphasizes that the best results typically come from combining multiple evaluation approaches.

## Key Concepts

- **Usability Inspection**: The generic name for a set of methods based on having evaluators inspect an interface, as opposed to testing with users

- **Heuristic Evaluation**: The most informal method where usability specialists judge whether elements follow established usability principles ("heuristics")

- **Cognitive Walkthroughs**: A more explicit procedure simulating a user's problem-solving process at each step through the dialogue

- **Formal Usability Inspections**: Structured six-step procedure combining heuristic evaluation and cognitive walkthroughs

- **Pluralistic Walkthroughs**: Group meetings where users, developers, and human factors people step through scenarios together

## Inspection Methods Covered

1. **Heuristic Evaluation**: Informal, based on usability principles, inspected by single evaluator at a time
2. **Cognitive Walkthroughs**: Step-by-step simulation of user problem-solving
3. **Formal Usability Inspections**: Six-step procedure with defined roles
4. **Pluralistic Walkthroughs**: Group scenario walkthroughs
5. **Feature Inspection**: Lists sequence of features to accomplish tasks
6. **Consistency Inspection**: Multiple designers inspect interface for consistency
7. **Standards Inspection**: Expert checks interface for compliance

## Key Findings

1. **Cost-Effectiveness**: Inspection methods are highly cost-effective compared to full user testing
2. **Complementary Approaches**: Inspection and testing find different problems - both are needed
3. **Evaluator Expertise**: Results depend on evaluator skill; combining evaluators improves coverage
4. **Method Selection**: Different methods suit different evaluation goals and constraints

## Relevance to AI and Design Education

This foundational work on evaluation methods is increasingly relevant as AI tools enter design practice:
- **Evaluating AI Outputs**: Students need skills to inspect and critique AI-generated designs
- **Human Judgment**: Inspection methods emphasize human expertise that AI cannot replace
- **Systematic Evaluation**: Frameworks for evaluating design quality remain essential
- **Critical Skills**: The evaluative judgment required for inspection is a core human capability
