# The Effects of Generative AI on Design Fixation and Divergent Thinking

## Summary
This paper provides rare empirical evidence against the common assumption that AI image generators enhance creativity. Through a between-participants experiment (N=60), participants in a visual ideation task were assigned to three conditions: no support, Google Image Search, or Midjourney. The study found that AI support led to higher design fixation, fewer ideas, less variety, and lower originality compared to the baseline condition. Qualitative analysis reveals that fixation arises through both prompt creation and response to AI images, and identifies a new phenomenon called "fixation displacement" where the focus of fixation shifts from an initial exemplar onto AI outputs.

## Key Concepts
- **Design Fixation**: "Blind adherence to a set of ideas or concepts limiting the output of conceptual design"—the tendency for exposure to examples to anchor and bias subsequent ideas, restricting exploration of the design space
- **Divergent Thinking Measures**: Four metrics used to assess ideation output quality: fluency (number of ideas), variety (number of distinct categories), originality (novelty rated by judges), and design fixation score (similarity to an initial example)
- **Fixation Displacement**: A new phenomenon identified by the authors—when using AI, the focus of fixation shifts from an initial given exemplar onto the AI's outputs, meaning AI outputs become the new anchoring source rather than liberating designers from the original example
- **Prompt Creation Fixation**: The finding that the act of formulating prompts for AI causes designers to fixate—articulating a prompt in words commits the designer to a specific conceptual framing before ideation has fully opened up
- **Between-Participants Experiment**: The study's methodological contribution—a controlled comparison of AI support vs. image search vs. no support in an equivalent ideation task, providing stronger causal evidence than survey or case study methods
- **Co-ideation with AI**: The practice of using AI image generation as an active partner in ideation rather than a lookup tool—here shown to have different, often more fixating effects than passive reference image searches
- **Prompt Strategy Effects**: Qualitative finding that participants' chosen approach to prompt creation significantly moderated fixation intensity—open, exploratory prompting strategies correlated with less fixation than directed, convergent prompts

## Theoretical Framework
The paper is grounded in the extensive design fixation literature, building particularly on Jansson and Smith's classic experimental paradigm, and integrating research on how modality, fidelity, diversity, and novelty of external stimuli affect fixation intensity. The authors position their study within the creativity support tools literature, responding to claims (e.g., Chiou et al.'s co-ideation work) that AI can open conceptual spaces and promote divergent thinking—providing empirical counter-evidence under controlled conditions. Bayesian statistical methods are used for analysis, providing posterior probability distributions rather than binary significance testing, and enabling more nuanced interpretation of effect sizes.

## Methods
Between-participants experiment with N=60 participants (design students and practitioners) assigned to three conditions: No Support (n=20), Image Search via Google (n=20), or AI Image Generator via Midjourney (n=20). All participants completed a visual ideation task—sketching ideas for a chatbot avatar. Dependent variables included: design fixation score (similarity of sketches to an initial example), fluency (number of distinct ideas), variety (category diversity), and originality (expert-rated novelty). NASA Task Load Index measured cognitive load. Post-task qualitative interviews explored interaction strategies and fixation experiences. Analysis used Bayesian regression models.

## Main Arguments
- AI image generation support led to higher design fixation compared to both no support and Google Image Search conditions—participants who used Midjourney produced sketches more similar to the initial given example, despite AI generating ostensibly "new" images
- Fluency, variety, and originality were all lower in the AI-supported condition compared to the baseline (no support) condition, providing empirical evidence that AI tools do not automatically enhance divergent thinking and may actively suppress it
- Fixation displacement explains the mechanism: AI's outputs become the new anchor, replacing the given exemplar—rather than liberating designers from fixation, AI introduces a new, higher-fidelity fixation source that is harder to recognize and resist
- Prompt creation itself induces fixation by requiring verbal commitment to a specific design direction before divergent exploration is complete—the act of articulating a prompt conceptually narrows the ideation space
- Prompt strategy moderates fixation: participants who used exploratory, open-ended prompts experienced less fixation than those using directive, convergent prompts, suggesting that prompt literacy training should include divergent prompting strategies
- The paper does not conclude AI is useless for ideation—it argues for designing AI ideation tools that explicitly counteract fixation mechanisms, and for incorporating these findings into ideation workflow design and pedagogy

## Limitations & Critiques
The ideation task (chatbot avatar sketching) is relatively narrow and may not generalize to more complex, open-ended design problems. The between-subjects design controls for learning effects but requires large samples to detect interactions; with N=20 per condition, some effects may be underpowered. The study used Midjourney specifically—different AI tools with different output characteristics might produce different fixation effects. Participants' varying levels of design expertise and prior AI experience were not fully controlled.

The ecological validity of a single-session experiment is limited—real design work involves extended, iterative engagement with AI tools that may develop different dynamics than initial exposure. The Bayesian analysis provides nuanced effect estimates but may be less familiar to design education audiences.
