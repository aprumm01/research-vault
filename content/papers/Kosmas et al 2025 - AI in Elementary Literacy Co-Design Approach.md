# Integrating Artificial Intelligence in Literacy Lessons for Elementary Classrooms: A Co-Design Approach

## Summary
This study examines a co-design approach to developing AI-integrated lesson plans for elementary literacy education in Cyprus. Twenty-five in-service teachers participated in a six-hour co-design workshop with researchers to create lesson plans integrating AI tools (ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Ideogram, Muse.ai, VideoGen, Fliki, Invideo AI) into the national Greek language curriculum for grades 1-6. The co-designed materials were then piloted with 62 elementary students across four classrooms. The study addresses two research questions: how teachers perceive their involvement in the co-design process, and how students perceive AI as enhancing their learning experience.

Teachers' focus group data revealed four benefits of co-design: interaction with colleagues and experts, sense of ownership over materials, professional development opportunities, and high-quality collaborative outcomes. Challenges included preparation time, varied familiarity with AI tools, and difficulty designing for diverse classroom contexts. Student survey results showed positive engagement: 42% found the AI-integrated lessons "very enjoyable" and 29% "somewhat enjoyable," 45% found Greek class "very interesting" with AI, and most students found creating images with AI particularly engaging (31 of 62 students selected this as their favorite activity).

## Key Concepts
- **Co-Design Approach**: A collaborative method where teachers and researchers work together to develop educational materials, ensuring resources are innovative yet tailored to specific classroom needs
- **AI Literacy**: Knowledge and skills needed to understand, use, and critically evaluate AI tools in educational and professional contexts
- **AI-Integrated Lesson Plan**: A structured instructional approach embedding AI tools (text generation, image creation, video production) within curriculum-aligned activities

## Theoretical Framework
The study draws on participatory design principles (Muller & Kuhn, 1993) and co-creation frameworks for educational innovation (Roschelle & Penuel, 2006). The authors position AI integration within the broader context of 21st-century skills development and digital literacy education, emphasizing that AI tools can support critical thinking, problem-solving, and engagement when thoughtfully integrated.

## Methods
Mixed methods design including: (1) a six-hour co-design workshop with 25 teachers; (2) qualitative focus groups with teachers using open coding analysis (Saldana, 2009); (3) pilot implementation in four elementary classrooms (62 students, grades 1-5); and (4) student questionnaires using 5-point Likert scales with emoji icons. The lesson plans focused on the thematic unit "Summer" from the national curriculum.

## Main Arguments
- Co-design empowers teachers by fostering ownership, knowledge-sharing, and professional development
- AI tools can enhance elementary literacy education by supporting text comprehension, creative writing, image generation, and video creation
- Teacher involvement in designing AI-integrated materials is essential for creating practical, classroom-ready resources
- Students respond positively to AI-enhanced lessons, particularly activities involving creative image generation
- Early introduction to AI tools can help students develop AI literacy skills alongside traditional literacy competencies
- Challenges include varying teacher familiarity with AI, time constraints, and diverse classroom needs

## Limitations & Critiques
- Small sample size limits generalizability (25 teachers, 62 students)
- Single geographic context (Cyprus) with Greek language focus
- No pre-post assessment of learning outcomes or language abilities
- Short pilot duration prevents assessment of long-term impacts
- Student questionnaires may not fully capture learning effects
- Technical challenges (Wi-Fi, device compatibility) affected some implementations
