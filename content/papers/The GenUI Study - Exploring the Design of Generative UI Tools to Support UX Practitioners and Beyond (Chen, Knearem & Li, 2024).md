# The GenUI Study: Exploring the Design of Generative UI Tools to Support UX Practitioners and Beyond

## Summary

This formative study by Chen, Knearem, and Li investigates how UX-related professionals would adopt and use Generative UI (GenUI) tools in their work. Through a week-long diary study with 37 professionals from four roles (UX designers, UX researchers, product managers, and software engineers), the study surfaces findings about GenUI adoption workflows, cross-role support opportunities, and existing gaps.

The study's central finding is that GenUI provides a "good first draft, tough last mile" - it can accelerate early-stage ideation and prototyping but struggles with the refinement, accessibility, and standards compliance needed for production work.

## Key Concepts

- **Generative UI (GenUI)**: AI models that can create high-fidelity UI mockups based on high-level text descriptions, distinct from image generation or code generation

- **Role-Specific Support**: Different professional roles have different needs and opportunities for GenUI support - not just designers

- **First Draft vs. Last Mile**: GenUI excels at generating initial concepts but struggles with the detailed refinement needed for production

- **Cross-Functional Communication**: GenUI can help non-designers communicate visual ideas to design teams

## Methodology

Week-long diary study where 37 professionals used a state-of-the-art GenUI tool for role-specific tasks, followed by semi-structured interviews about their experiences, expectations, and concerns.

## Key Findings

### How Different Roles Use GenUI

1. **UX Designers (n=11)**: Early ideation, rapid exploration of alternatives, client communication
2. **UX Researchers (n=7)**: Stimulus creation for research, visualizing concepts for testing
3. **Product Managers (n=7)**: Communicating requirements visually, aligning stakeholders
4. **Software Engineers (n=12)**: Understanding design intent, exploring UI possibilities

### Key Themes

1. **Good First Draft**: GenUI accelerates early exploration and democratizes visual prototyping
2. **Tough Last Mile**: Accessibility, brand compliance, and component standards remain challenges
3. **Team-Level Support Needed**: Current tools focus on individual use; team workflows need attention
4. **Quality vs. Fidelity Gap**: High visual fidelity doesn't equal production-ready quality

## Relevance to AI and Design Education

Critical implications for how we teach design with AI tools:
- **Skill Development**: If GenUI handles "first drafts," what skills should students develop?
- **Critical Evaluation**: Students need skills to assess and refine AI-generated outputs
- **Accessibility Literacy**: AI tools don't ensure accessible design - human expertise remains essential
- **Collaborative Practice**: Understanding how AI affects team dynamics and workflows
