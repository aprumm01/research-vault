# Principles of Instructional Design

## Summary
PRINCIPLES OF INSTRUCTIONAL DESIGN Third Edition Robert M Wager Florida State University HOLT, RINEHART AND WINSTON, INC Fort Worth Chicago San Francisco Philadelphia Montreal Toronto London.

## Key Concepts
- **Conditions of learning**: Gagné's framework identifying internal (learner readiness, prior knowledge, cognitive processes) and external (instructional events, environmental factors) conditions necessary for learning to occur, varying across different learning outcome types
- **Nine events of instruction**: Systematic instructional design framework prescribing sequence of teaching activities—gaining attention, informing objectives, stimulating recall, presenting content, providing guidance, eliciting performance, providing feedback, assessing performance, enhancing retention/transfer—applicable across learning contexts
- **Five categories of learning outcomes**: Taxonomy differentiating intellectual skills (procedural knowledge), cognitive strategies (learning-to-learn skills), verbal information (declarative knowledge), motor skills, and attitudes, each requiring different instructional approaches and assessment methods
- **Hierarchical learning structures**: Concept that complex skills built on prerequisite simpler skills through hierarchical relationships, requiring instructional sequencing from foundational to advanced capabilities and systematic task analysis to identify prerequisite knowledge
- **Instructional design as systematic process**: Approaching teaching/training design through rational systematic methodology involving needs analysis, goal specification, task analysis, instructional strategy development, materials creation, and evaluation rather than intuitive or experience-based approaches

## Theoretical Framework
**Information Processing Theory of Learning**: Cognitive psychology framework conceptualizing learning as information processing through stages (attention, encoding, storage, retrieval) with instructional design optimizing each stage through appropriate external conditions supporting internal cognitive processes.

**Behavioral and Cognitive Integration**: Synthesizing behaviorist attention to observable performance and reinforcement with cognitive psychology's focus on internal mental processes, recognizing both external conditions and internal cognitive states as essential for effective instruction design.

## Methods
**Instructional design model development**: Prescriptive framework construction drawing on learning theory research, experimental studies of instruction, and systematic analysis of teaching practices to identify principles and procedures for effective instruction across contexts.

**Task analysis methodology**: Systematic procedures for decomposing complex learning goals into component skills, prerequisite knowledge, and hierarchical relationships to inform instructional sequencing, content selection, and objective specification.

## Main Arguments
- **Effective instruction requires systematic alignment of external events with internal learning processes**: Learning outcomes determined not by chance or instructor intuition but by deliberate design of instructional conditions supporting cognitive processes appropriate to learning goals. Different learning outcomes (intellectual skills versus verbal information versus attitudes) require different instructional strategies because internal processes differ. Systematic design methodology—analyzing learning requirements, identifying prerequisite skills, selecting appropriate instructional events, designing assessments—produces more effective instruction than unsystematic approaches relying on tradition or personal teaching style.

- **Learning hierarchies demand careful instructional sequencing**: Complex intellectual skills cannot be taught directly but only through mastery of prerequisite simpler skills in hierarchical relationship. Attempting to teach advanced skills before foundational prerequisites results in learning failure or superficial understanding. Effective instruction requires: (1) task analysis identifying skill hierarchies, (2) assessment of learner entry capabilities, (3) sequencing instruction from prerequisites upward, (4) ensuring mastery at each level before advancing. Violating hierarchical learning principles explains much instructional failure attributed to learner deficiencies.

- **Nine events of instruction provide generalizable design framework across contexts**: Despite learning context diversity (classroom, workplace training, self-paced materials, technology-mediated), certain instructional functions consistently promote learning: activating prior knowledge, presenting new content with appropriate organization, providing practice with feedback, supporting application to new situations. Nine events framework operationalizes these functions as concrete instructional activities adaptable to any delivery mode. Framework not rigid prescription but flexible template ensuring essential instructional functions addressed systematically.

- **Learning outcome differences demand differentiated instructional approaches**: Intellectual skills (applying procedures/rules), verbal information (stating facts), cognitive strategies (managing own learning), motor skills, and attitudes each involve different internal processes therefore requiring different instructional conditions. Teaching procedure requires demonstration-practice-feedback; teaching facts requires meaningful organization and retrieval cues; developing attitudes requires credible models and positive experiences. Generic teaching approaches treating all learning identically produce suboptimal outcomes—effective instruction tailors strategy to outcome type.

- **Instructional design is applied science not intuitive art**: While acknowledging teaching involves interpersonal skills and contextual judgment, core instructional design functions amenable to systematic methodology grounded in learning science research. Effectiveness not mysterious talent but learnable through applying systematic analysis, design, development, implementation, evaluation processes. Professionalizing education/training requires replacing folklore and intuition with evidence-based design principles, though implementation always requires contextual adaptation.

## Limitations & Critiques
**Cognitive-behaviorist paradigm limitations**: Framework reflects 1970s-80s cognitive psychology with limited attention to social-constructivist learning theories, situated cognition, community of practice perspectives, or socio-cultural dimensions of learning that later became prominent, potentially overemphasizing individual cognitive processes while underplaying social interaction and contextual factors.

**Instructional control assumptions**: Nine events framework positions instructor/designer in control determining objectives, sequencing, activities, and assessments with limited attention to learner agency, self-direction, or negotiated learning goals—reflecting training/direct instruction orientation potentially inappropriate for contexts valuing learner autonomy, discovery learning, or democratic education.

**Limited attention to motivation and affect**: While including attitudes as outcome category, insufficient integration of motivational psychology, emotional dimensions of learning, interest development, or identity formation that contemporary learning science recognizes as essential, treating learning primarily as cognitive information processing problem.

**Context and culture boundedness**: Instructional principles derived primarily from Western educational settings with particular cultural assumptions about teaching/learning relationships, individual versus collective orientation, direct versus indirect communication—may not transfer effectively to different cultural contexts with alternative pedagogical traditions.

**Simplified task analysis assumptions**: Hierarchical learning model works well for procedural skills with clear prerequisite structures but less applicable to ill-defined domains, creative thinking, complex problem-solving in messy real-world contexts where learning not straightforward accumulation of discrete skills but development of adaptive expertise and flexible knowledge application.

**Technology-neutral limitations**: While claiming generalizability across delivery modes, framework predates significant educational technology innovations (collaborative online learning, learning analytics, adaptive systems, social media) that enable instructional approaches not easily accommodated in nine events framework designed for instructor-led synchronous instruction.
