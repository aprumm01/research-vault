# AI Imaginaries, Design Education, and the Transformation of Design Practice (Prumm, 2025)

## Summary
This literature review traces how sociotechnical imaginaries about AI have shaped design education and professional design practice across five historical waves (1943–present). Drawing on Jasanoff & Kim's framework of sociotechnical imaginaries, the review synthesizes scholarship from STS, HCI, instructional design, human performance technology, and design studies to argue that misalignment between industry AI imaginaries and academic program responses produces structural gaps in how designers are prepared for AI-integrated practice. The review introduces an "imaginaries lifecycle" model describing how industry visions propagate through academic programs, student expectations, and back into the workforce.

## Key Concepts
- **Sociotechnical imaginaries**: Collectively held visions of AI-enabled futures that shape institutional decisions before the technology matures (Jasanoff & Kim)
- **Imaginaries lifecycle**: The propagation pathway from industry imaginary → academic program → student expectation → workforce → industry feedback
- **Underperforming imaginaries**: AI transformation narratives that reshape labor and practice even when predictions fail to materialize (Stanusch et al., 2024)
- **Design education gap**: The lag between AI capability shifts and curricular adaptation in design programs

## Theoretical Framework
Sociotechnical imaginaries (Jasanoff & Kim); Science and Technology Studies; sociology of professions; instructional design theory; human performance technology.

## Methods
Systematic literature review synthesizing sources across HCI, STS, design studies, AI ethics, labor economics, and instructional technology.

## Main Arguments
1. Each wave of AI development has been accompanied by a distinct imaginary that restructures how design education is justified, organized, and evaluated
2. Design programs have repeatedly lagged behind industry imaginaries, producing graduates misaligned with actual AI-integrated practice
3. The current wave (generative AI) presents a three-way contested imaginary — AI as tool, collaborator, or threat — with no institutional consensus
4. HPT and instructional design frameworks offer systematic approaches to closing the imaginary-practice gap
