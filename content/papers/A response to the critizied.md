# A response to the critizied

## Summary
Instructional Design and Creativity: A Response to the Criticized Author(s): Gordon Rowland Source: Educational Technology , September-October 1995, Vol 5 (SeptemberOctober 1995), pp 17-22 Published by: Educational Technology Publications, Inc.

## Key Concepts
- Creativity in instructional design processes and products
- Systematic instructional design (ISD) methods critique
- Well-defined versus ill-defined (wicked) design problems
- Objectivist versus constructivist epistemological assumptions
- Design as problem-setting rather than problem-solving
- Effectiveness and efficiency as design criteria
- Reflective skepticism in design practice
- Soft systems thinking in educational design contexts

## Theoretical Framework
- Constructivist epistemology (versus objectivist assumptions)
- Situated cognition and situated learning theory
- Wicked problems framework (Rittel, 1972)
- Reflective practice in design (Schön tradition)
- Soft systems methodology (Checkland)

## Methods
Scholarly response article; employs argumentative analysis and philosophical critique of ISD methods
- Deconstruction of assumptions underlying systematic design claims
- Comparison of design problem types (well-defined vs. ill-defined)
- Analysis of epistemological foundations of design approaches

## Main Arguments
- ISD methods' claims of predictable results assume objectivist epistemology and well-defined problems, which may not fit most authentic design situations
- Effectiveness and efficiency as criteria are biased toward transmissive models of knowledge and inappropriate for constructivist approaches
- "Creativeness" in instruction is misunderstood; creativity better applies to design process (problem-setting, inventing forms) than product features
- Designers increasingly face ill-defined/wicked problems requiring reflective skepticism and transformative approaches, not rule application
- Following systematic procedures does not guarantee quality or creativity when problems are complex and context-dependent
- Critiques of ISD are not arguments "for creative instruction" but challenges to epistemological assumptions and problem-framing approaches
- Balance of criteria matters, but appropriate criteria depend on underlying assumptions about knowledge and learning

## Limitations & Critiques
- ISD methods work for well-defined, routine situations but are increasingly less applicable as workplace complexity grows
- Overconfidence in systematic methods for situations requiring design judgment and invention
- False dichotomy between systematic approaches and creativity obscures deeper epistemological debates
- ISD methods artificially separate logic from imagination and problems from solutions
- Judging constructivist-informed designs by objectivist criteria (effectiveness, efficiency) is philosophically incoherent
- Gap between prescriptive ISD models and actual expert designer practices
- Limited development of alternative design approaches for ill-defined problems in educational technology
- Need for design methods that maintain reflective skepticism throughout process rather than front-loading analysis
- Distinction between technical problem-solving and authentic design activity underexplored in the field
