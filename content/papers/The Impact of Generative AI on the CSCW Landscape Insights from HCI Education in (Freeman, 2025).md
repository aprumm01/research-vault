# The Impact of Generative AI on the CSCW Landscape Insights from HCI Education industry dynamics and funding perspectives

## Summary
Cliff Lampe University of Michigan Ann Arbor, Michigan, USA cacl@umich.

## Key Concepts
- Generative AI impact on CSCW research landscape beyond specific research directions
- HCI/CSCW education transformation in response to AI capabilities
- Tech industry trajectories and shifting job prospects for HCI graduates
- Academia-industry collaboration challenges in the age of generative AI
- Funding landscape shifts toward AI priorities and national AI initiatives
- Community preparedness for potential post-AI era scenarios
- Curriculum development needs for next-generation CSCW researchers
- AI's dual role as facilitator and potential replacement for creative workflows
- Infrastructure and resource intensification requirements for generative AI
- Policy and regulatory frameworks shaping AI research directions

## Theoretical Framework
Panel discussion format with no single theoretical framework, drawing on perspectives from HCI education, industry dynamics, and funding agency viewpoints to examine systemic impacts on the CSCW field.

## Methods
Panel discussion at CSCW 2025 conference featuring academic leaders and industry experts, structured around key questions addressing education, industry collaboration, and funding perspectives with audience participation.

## Main Arguments
- CSCW community must address generative AI's impacts holistically rather than focusing only on specific research agendas
- Next generation of CSCW researchers need new skillsets beyond traditional HCI training to remain relevant
- Tech industry AI competition is accelerating resource requirements, potentially affecting HCI graduate job prospects
- Growing AI focus may reshape funding priorities, favoring AI-related CSCW research over other topics
- Academia-industry collaboration models need reconfiguration to address AI-driven industry transformations
- Community must prepare for potential AI bubble burst and post-AI era scenarios
- Education institutions should help both students and faculty adapt research agendas to AI realities
- National and international AI policies are directly influencing research funding landscapes

## Limitations & Critiques
- Panel format provides discussion framework but limited empirical evidence for claims
- Focus on US and European contexts may not represent global CSCW community experiences
- Assumes linear trajectory of AI development without considering alternative technological paths
- Limited discussion of resistance strategies or non-AI-centric CSCW research preservation
- Potential bias toward industry perspectives given panelist composition from major tech companies
- Does not address power dynamics between well-resourced AI research and smaller CSCW projects
- Framing around "AI bubble" may create unnecessary alarm without substantive analysis
- Minimal attention to how smaller institutions without AI resources might navigate changes
