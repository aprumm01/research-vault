# Poets Over Quants: Automation and AI Threats Increase the Value People Place on Creativity

## Summary

Through nine studies (N=2,320), this research demonstrates that when people perceive automation and AI as threats to employment, they increase the value they place on creative skills relative to analytical/quantitative skills. This effect occurs because people believe creativity has low substitutability (hard to automate) and high complementarity (works well alongside automation) with AI.

## Key Concepts

- **Automation Threat**: Perception that technology will replace human jobs
- **Skill Substitutability**: Degree to which a skill can be replaced by automation
- **Skill Complementarity**: Degree to which a skill enhances or is enhanced by automation
- **Creative Skills**: Abilities related to originality, innovation, and artistic expression ("poets")
- **Analytical Skills**: Abilities related to quantitative reasoning, logic, and systematic processing ("quants")
- **Lay Theories**: Everyday beliefs people hold about how the world works

## Theoretical Framework

Draws on labor economics theories of skill-biased technological change and psychological research on threat responses. Central mechanism: People hold lay theories about which skills automation can and cannot replace, leading to strategic revaluation of skills perceived as "automation-proof."

## Methods

**9 Studies with Mixed Methods:**

- **Studies 1-3**: Correlational surveys linking automation threat perception to skill valuation
- **Studies 4-5**: Experimental manipulation of automation threat salience
- **Studies 6-7**: Testing mediating mechanisms (substitutability, complementarity)
- **Studies 8-9**: Real-world behavioral measures (course enrollment, actual skill development choices)
- **Total Participants**: N=2,320 across all studies
- **Measures**: Skill valuation scales, automation threat scales, career choice scenarios

## Main Arguments

1. **Main Effect**: Automation threat increases valuation of creative relative to analytical skills across diverse samples and methods

2. **Dual Mechanism**: Effect driven by:
   - Low perceived substitutability of creativity (hard for AI to replace)
   - High perceived complementarity of creativity (enhances human-AI collaboration)

3. **Behavioral Consequences**: Effect translates to actual choices:
   - Preference for creativity-focused education
   - Career path selections favoring creative roles
   - Investment in developing creative skills

4. **Strategic Response**: Skill revaluation represents adaptive response to technological change - people seek to maintain labor market value

5. **Asymmetric Effect**: Threat specifically elevates creativity; it doesn't simply devalue analytical skills absolutely

6. **Robustness**: Effect holds across demographics, occupations, and countries

## Limitations

- Most studies use self-report measures subject to social desirability
- Short-term experimental manipulations may not capture long-term belief changes
- Western-centric samples (primarily US) limit generalizability
- Actual accuracy of lay theories about AI capabilities not tested
- Long-term behavioral consequences not tracked
- May not account for how AI capabilities change over time (creativity becoming more automatable)
- Selection effects in behavioral studies
