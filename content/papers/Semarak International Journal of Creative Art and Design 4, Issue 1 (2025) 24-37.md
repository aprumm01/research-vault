# The Integration of AI in Design Thinking for Enhancing Student Creativity and Critical Thinking in Digital Media Learning (2025)

## Summary
This 2025 paper examines how integrating AI into design thinking frameworks enhances student creativity and critical thinking in digital media learning contexts. Through quasi-experimental research comparing AI-augmented and traditional design thinking pedagogy, the authors find that AI integration — when structured around design thinking's empathize-define-ideate-prototype-test cycle — produces measurable gains in creative output diversity and critical thinking skills. The paper argues for a structured AI-design thinking integration model as a framework for digital media education.

## Key Concepts
- AI-augmented design thinking
- Creativity enhancement through AI tools
- Critical thinking in digital media education
- Design thinking stages and AI integration points
- Quasi-experimental pedagogy research

## Theoretical Framework
Design thinking framework (IDEO/Stanford d.school); creative cognition theory; AI-augmented learning; draws on creativity research and educational technology effectiveness studies.

## Methods
Quasi-experimental study; pre/post comparison of student creative output and critical thinking assessment in AI-integrated vs. control design thinking courses in digital media education.

## Main Arguments
- AI tools integrated at specific design thinking stages (particularly ideation and prototyping) produce measurably more diverse and elaborated creative outputs
- Critical thinking gains emerge when students must evaluate and curate AI outputs, not just generate them
- The structured design thinking framework provides the scaffolding needed to make AI integration pedagogically productive
- Instructors need training in how to facilitate AI-augmented design thinking, not just how to use the tools

## Limitations & Critiques
Quasi-experimental design cannot fully isolate the effect of AI integration from instructor enthusiasm, novelty effects, or contextual differences; creativity assessment instruments may not capture all relevant dimensions; conducted in a single institutional context.
