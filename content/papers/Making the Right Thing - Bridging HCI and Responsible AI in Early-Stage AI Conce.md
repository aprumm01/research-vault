# Making the Right Thing - Bridging HCI and Responsible AI in Early-Stage AI Concept Selection

## Summary
CCS Concepts AI projects often fail due to financial, technical, ethical, or user acceptance challenges—failures frequently rooted in early-stage decisions While HCI and Responsible AI (RAI) research emphasize this, practical approaches for identifying promising concepts early remain limited Drawing on Research through Design, this paper investigates how early-stage AI concept sorting in commercial settings can reflect RAI principles.

## Main Arguments
- Many AI project failures stem from early-stage concept selection decisions
- Both HCI and RAI research emphasize early-stage importance but lack practical tools
- Need to bridge HCI usability focus with RAI ethical considerations
- Commercial settings require practical, implementable concept evaluation methods
- Early concept sorting can reflect RAI principles if properly designed
- "Making the right thing" (concept selection) precedes "making things right" (execution)
- Integrating RAI considerations early prevents downstream ethical issues
- Research through Design offers methodology for developing practical tools
- Commercial constraints require balance between ideals and feasibility

## Limitations & Critiques
- Limited generalizability from commercial settings to other contexts
- Research through Design methodology may lack empirical rigor
- Practical tools may oversimplify complex RAI considerations
- Commercial pressures may compromise thorough ethical evaluation
- Early-stage concept evaluation has inherent uncertainty
- May not capture emergent ethical issues that arise during development
- Limited validation of long-term effectiveness of proposed approaches
- Power dynamics in commercial concept selection not fully addressed
- Cultural and contextual variations in RAI principles underexplored
