# AI Assistance in Enterprise UX Design Workflows: Enhancing Design Brief Creation for Designers

## Summary

This study from Kookmin University explores how ChatGPT affects the process of creating design briefs in corporate UX design settings. Through expert interviews, comparative experiments, and third-party evaluations with 8 senior UX designers (5+ years experience), the researchers investigated what challenges designers face when creating design briefs, how AI tools change their workflow, and what differences exist in efficiency, accuracy, and client satisfaction between AI-assisted and traditional approaches.

The experiment used a within-subjects design where designers first created design briefs for a virtual gaming community platform project without AI tools, then completed the same type of task one week later using ChatGPT throughout the process. Results showed that AI assistance reduced overall operation time by 49% (from 1.2 hours to 0.6 hours) while improving understandability by 4.76% and operability by 4.44%. However, accuracy slightly decreased by 4.17%, as AI-generated content sometimes contained distorted information requiring verification.

The study maps AI's impact across the design brief workflow using user experience maps, identifying specific pain points (accuracy issues, mechanical outputs, poor prioritization) and opportunities (improved retrieval, content personalization, automation) at each stage. The findings suggest AI tools are most valuable for information retrieval, verification, analysis, communication, and decision-making support, but designers must maintain critical oversight of AI outputs.

## Key Concepts

- **Design Brief**: A critical document for project initiation that functions as assessment (reflecting stakeholder needs), agreement (consensus between client and designer), and documentation (iterative development guide)
- **Design Quality Criteria (DQC) Framework**: Evaluation model with three dimensions—strategy (company philosophy, structure, innovation), content (social needs, environmental considerations, feasibility), and performance (budget, timeline, functionality)
- **AI Thinking in Workflows**: Framework proposing three areas where AI integrates into design work—business strategy (information retrieval, cost management, risk management), document content (stakeholder communication, human-computer collaboration), and design decision (decision support, post-cooperation)
- **Human-Centered AI (HCAI)**: Emerging paradigm where AI automation of information tasks prompts a shift in human-computer interaction, with designers becoming "clients" directing AI "contractors"

## Theoretical Framework

The study draws on design brief literature (Jevnaker, 2005; Paton & Dorst, 2011) and frameworks for evaluating design outputs (Petersen & Phillips, 2011; Petersen & Joo, 2015). It positions AI tools within generative AI taxonomy (Strobel et al., 2024), focusing on "Generators" (creating new content) and "Assistants" (domain-specific support). The research situates findings within the broader discourse on AI's transformation of professional design practice.

## Methods

- **Participants**: 8 senior UX design experts from diverse industries (automotive, gaming, e-commerce, social media, augmented reality) with 5-11 years experience
- **Study Design**: Within-subjects comparison across two phases, one week apart
- **Phase 1**: 30-minute interview + design brief creation without AI tools (~72 minutes average); post-task surveys and third-party evaluation
- **Phase 2**: Design brief creation with ChatGPT 4.0 (~37 minutes average); follow-up interviews capturing think-aloud reflections
- **Measures**: Time on task, third-party evaluation on operability/comprehensibility/accuracy, user experience maps tracking emotions and pain points across workflow stages (Prepare → Doing → After)
- **Analysis**: Qualitative interview coding, comparative statistical analysis of task performance metrics

## Main Arguments

- All 8 designers (100%) used AI tools in their daily work, though usage intensity varied; AI helps with information retrieval, verification, analysis, and organizing large amounts of data
- AI significantly improves efficiency (49% time reduction) and subjective satisfaction, but can compromise accuracy when generating lengthy or unfamiliar content
- The role of designers shifts from "executors" to "clients"—AI becomes the contractor while designers direct, verify, and refine outputs
- Key pain points with AI assistance: content distortion when project data is extensive, mechanical/uniform text outputs lacking personalization, insufficient automation for complex multi-step tasks, poor priority judgment
- Opportunities for AI tool development: improved retrieval capabilities, content gap-filling, output format diversity, content personalization, stronger natural language expression, enhanced automation
- AI thinking must be integrated across business strategy, document creation, and design decision-making, not just applied to isolated tasks

## Limitations & Critiques

- Small sample size (n=8) of primarily experienced designers; findings may not generalize to novice designers or other experience levels
- Virtual project scenario may not fully replicate real-world working conditions with actual clients and organizational constraints
- Reliance on qualitative methods limits generalizability; no statistical significance testing reported for comparative metrics
- Study used only ChatGPT 4.0; results may differ with other AI tools (Midjourney, Claude, etc.) or future model versions
- Did not examine long-term effects of AI reliance on skill development or professional confidence
- Third-party evaluators were project experts, but evaluation criteria alignment with real client satisfaction is assumed rather than validated
