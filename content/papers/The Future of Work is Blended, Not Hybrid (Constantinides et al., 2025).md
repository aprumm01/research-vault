# The Future of Work is Blended Not Hybrid

## Summary
1 The way we work is no longer hybrid—it is blended with AI coworkers, automated decisions, and virtual presence reshaping human roles, agency, and expertise We now work through AI, with our outputs shaped by invisible algorithms AI’s infiltration into knowledge, creative, and service work is not just about automation, but concerns redistribution of agency, creativity, and control.

## Key Concepts
- Blended work as successor to hybrid work: human effort and AI output inseparable
- AI as active participant in decision-making, performance evaluation, and creative process
- Redistribution of agency, creativity, and control through AI infiltration
- Erosion of eight-hour workday and work-life boundaries through technology
- AI-mediated work practices across knowledge, creative, and service sectors
- Physical-digital continuum requiring rethinking for equity and accessibility
- AI transparency and accountability as urgent concerns

## Theoretical Framework
[[frameworks/Sociotechnical Systems]] - Integration of AI and human work practices

## Methods
provocation paper, conceptual analysis

## Main Arguments
- Hybrid work concept is obsolete - blended work where AI and human contributions indivisible is reality
- AI is not temporary workplace tool but irreversible shift redefining employment, collaboration, career progression
- Four urgent areas demand attention: (1) social relatedness in AI-mediated work for creativity, (2) work-life boundary reinforcement for wellbeing, (3) digital-physical continuum rethinking for inclusivity, (4) AI transparency and accountability
- Question shifts from "whether we accept AI" to "whether we shape it before it shapes us"
- AI shapes outputs through invisible algorithms - challenges notions of skilled worker, competent professional, accountable leader
- Physical and distributed AI-mediated workspaces demand new design considerations
- Algorithms co-authoring reports and creative work raises authorship and agency questions

## Limitations & Critiques
- Provocation paper lacks empirical evidence or case studies to support claims
- "Blended work" concept needs more precise definition and operationalization
- Limited discussion of sectoral variation - may not apply equally across industries
- Four areas of attention presented without prioritization or implementation guidance
- Does not address power dynamics in who gets to "shape" blended work practices
- Missing consideration of workers who resist or cannot adopt AI-mediated work
- Assumes inevitability of AI integration without exploring alternative futures
