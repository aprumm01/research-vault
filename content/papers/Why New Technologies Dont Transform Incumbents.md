# Why New Technologies Don’t Transform Incumbents

## Summary
AI And Machine Learning Why New Technologies Don’t Transform Incumbents by Sangeet Paul Choudary February 9, 2026 Daniel Day/Getty Images Summary Many incumbent companies are adopting AI aggressively yet seeing only marginal gains, because they are using it to optimize existing work rather than to rethink how work itself is organized The real advantage of AI lies in its ability to break work into.

## Key Concepts
- **Optimization trap**: Incumbent companies using AI to optimize existing work rather than rethinking how work is organized
- **Work decomposition**: AI's real advantage lies in ability to break work into smaller, modular tasks
- **Marginal gains vs transformation**: Difference between incremental improvements and fundamental reorganization
- **Incumbent constraints**: Established companies face organizational inertia, legacy systems, and resistance to disruption
- **Task-level vs process-level adoption**: Distinction between using AI for specific tasks versus redesigning entire workflows
- **Reengineering**: Need to fundamentally reimagine work processes, not just automate existing ones

## Theoretical Framework
Builds on organizational theory regarding incumbent disadvantages, disruptive innovation theory, and business process reengineering concepts. Draws on analysis of AI adoption patterns across enterprises, startups, and established organizations. References work transformation and sociotechnical systems perspectives.

## Methods
- Case analysis of AI adoption in incumbent companies
- Comparison of transformation patterns across different organizational types
- Analysis of work reorganization strategies
- Industry examples (appears to be business/management analysis)

## Main Arguments
- **Incumbents adopt AI aggressively but see marginal gains**: Using AI to optimize existing processes rather than fundamentally transforming them
- **Real advantage in work decomposition**: AI enables breaking work into smaller, modular components that can be reconfigured
- **Optimization ≠ transformation**: Making existing processes faster/cheaper is different from reimagining how work is structured
- **Organizational inertia limits transformation**: Established companies constrained by legacy systems, processes, and mindsets
- **Need for fundamental rethinking**: True AI advantage requires redesigning work organization from the ground up
- **Startups have advantage**: Less constrained by existing processes, can build AI-native work structures

## Limitations & Critiques
- Publication date February 2026 suggests very recent/emerging analysis with limited longitudinal data
- May overgeneralize across different industries and company types
- Limited discussion of specific strategies incumbents could use to overcome constraints
- Potentially oversimplifies complexity of organizational transformation
- May not fully account for successful incumbent transformations
- Appears to be business publication (HBR-style) rather than peer-reviewed academic research
