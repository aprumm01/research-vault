# Nine Ways to Reduce Cognitive Load in Multimedia (Mayer and Moreno)

## Summary
Mayer and Moreno propose a theory of multimedia learning based on dual-channel processing, limited cognitive capacity, and active processing, then use this theory to identify five overload scenarios and nine corresponding strategies for reducing cognitive load in multimedia instruction. The strategies—including pre-training, modality effects, segmentation, coherence, and signaling—provide evidence-based design guidance for instructional multimedia.

## Key Concepts
- Cognitive load in multimedia

## Theoretical Framework
The Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning integrates dual-coding theory (Paivio) and cognitive load theory (Sweller) to identify when and why multimedia instruction produces cognitive overload.

## Methods
Review and synthesis of experimental research; theoretical framework development; identification of overload scenarios with corresponding evidence-based solutions.

## Main Arguments
- Cognitive overload occurs when intended learning processes exceed available cognitive capacity
- Pre-training, segmentation, and weeding out extraneous material reduce extraneous cognitive load
- Matching audio narration with visuals (modality principle) reduces split-attention effects
- Signaling and segmentation support learner-controlled processing of complex material
- All nine principles have empirical support and should guide multimedia instructional design
