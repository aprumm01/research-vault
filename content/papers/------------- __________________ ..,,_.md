# The Attack on ISD (Gordon & Zemke, 2000)

## Summary
Gordon and Zemke's provocative Training magazine article launched a significant debate in the instructional design field by arguing that ISD (Instructional Systems Design) is flawed, out-of-date, and an obstacle to effective performance improvement. The authors contend that the ADDIE model is too slow, too rigid, and too focused on instructional solutions when non-instructional solutions are often more effective. The article catalyzed a years-long professional debate about whether ISD should be reformed or replaced, with responses appearing in subsequent issues and spawning the "attack on ISD" controversy in the field.

## Key Concepts
- ISD (Instructional Systems Design) critique
- ADDIE model limitations
- Rapid instructional design
- Non-instructional performance interventions
- HPT (Human Performance Technology) as ISD alternative

## Theoretical Framework
Practitioner critique of dominant paradigm; draws on practitioner experience and HPT principles to argue that academic ID models have failed to keep pace with organizational reality.

## Methods
Opinion/perspective piece combining practitioner experience, case examples, and selective literature review; published as a provocation intended to generate professional debate.

## Main Arguments
- ISD is too slow for modern organizational needs; the typical development timeline is incompatible with business timelines
- ADDIE's linear structure makes it unsuitable for real-world projects where analysis, design, and development overlap
- Many performance problems called "training needs" are actually organizational, motivational, or environmental in nature
- The field's attachment to ISD prevents adoption of more effective, agile approaches

## Limitations & Critiques
Critics argued Gordon and Zemke overstated the limitations of ISD by attacking a strawman version of the model; defenders noted that ISD, properly applied, is iterative and flexible; the article's provocative framing generated more heat than light in the subsequent debate.
