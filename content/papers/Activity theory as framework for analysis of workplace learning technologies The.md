# Activity theory as framework for analysis of workplace learning technologies The case of generative AI conversational agents

## Summary
Purpose – Technology, such as artificial intelligence (AI), is transforming the way we work; however, it is yet to systemically transform learning at the workplace beyond augmentation of formal education’s learning processes This paper derives functional requirements for technologies that support workplace learning and assesses the suitability and limitations of generative AI conversational agents, as an example of application Design/methodology/approach – Using activity theory (AT) as theoretical framework, we model workplace learning as an activity, intertwined with work and mediated by technology, and expose contradictions that arise when technology developed for formal education is adopted at work.

## Key Concepts
- **Activity Theory (AT)** - Theoretical framework analyzing complex human activities involving subject, object, outcome, tools, community, rules, and division of labor
- **Workplace Learning (WPL)** - Informal, unstructured, self-regulated learning embedded within work activities rather than separate from them
- **Generative AI (GenAI)** - General purpose technology capable of creating context-specific responses and content across numerous applications
- **Informal Learning** - Intentional but uninstruction learneralized learning occurring in workplace, community, and daily life without predetermined outcomes
- **Learning Technology (WPLT)** - Technologies enabling workplace learning through mimetic, personalised, and situated learning opportunities
- **Contradictions in Activity Systems** - Tensions arising when tools designed for formal education are adopted in workplace contexts
- **Knowledge Workers** - Employees who use knowledge as an asset to solve problems in largely distributed work environments

## Theoretical Framework
- **Engeström's Activity Theory** - Second generation AT model visualizing activity systems through subject-object-outcome relationships mediated by tools, community, rules, and division of labor
- **Mwanza's Eight-Step Model** - Framework for identifying elements of activity systems in workplace learning contexts

## Methods
Scoping review using PRISMA guidelines, analyzing 13 papers from Google Scholar (2019-2024) through Activity Theory lens to identify contradictions between traditional and workplace learning

## Main Arguments
- Technology designed for formal education creates systematic contradictions when adopted for workplace learning due to fundamental differences in learning structure, assessment, and outcomes
- Activity Theory provides forward-looking prescriptive framework for deriving functional requirements for workplace learning technologies, moving beyond retrospective use-case analysis
- Twelve primary contradictions identified between traditional and workplace learning across subject, rules, object, outcome, community, division of labor, and tools
- 23 functional requirements derived for WPLT including mimetic learning, continuous learning opportunities, ubiquitous access, peer-based assessment, and knowledge inventory management
- ChatGPT meets some functional requirements (ubiquitous access, situated learning, continuous opportunities) but falls short on others (mimetic learning, peer collaboration, assessment and certification, knowledge authorship tracking)
- GenAI conversational agents more suitable for WPL than traditional education technologies despite need for further development in domain-specific content, peer recommendations, and learning analytics integration
- Integration of GenAI in higher education can facilitate smoother workplace transition by resembling workers' approach to learning and enhancing lifelong learning skills

## Limitations & Critiques
- Theoretical framework approach, while comprehensive, lacks external expert validation
- Data extraction limited to papers meeting specific inclusion criteria in scoping review without methodological quality assessment
- Scoping review restricted to papers with search terms in title, potentially overlooking relevant papers with different framing
- Analysis of ChatGPT represents snapshot of rapidly evolving technology and may quickly become outdated
- No empirical validation of derived functional requirements through workplace implementation studies
- Study acknowledges but does not deeply address ethical concerns including biases, hallucinations, privacy risks, and security threats
- Framework application limited to single case study (ChatGPT) without comparative assessment of other workplace learning technologies
- Does not address implementation challenges, organizational resistance, or resource requirements for adopting GenAI in workplace learning
- Limited discussion of how to transition existing workplace training programs to align with identified functional requirements
- No consideration of different workplace contexts, industries, or cultural factors that might affect applicability of functional requirements
