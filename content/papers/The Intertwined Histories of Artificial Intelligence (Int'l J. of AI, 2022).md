# The Intertwined Histories of Artificial Intelligence (Int'l J. of AI, 2022)

## Summary
Argues that AI and education have been deeply intertwined since the field's founding, with many early AI pioneers being cognitive scientists who simultaneously made foundational contributions to education research. Traces two distinct strands—information-processing psychology and constructivism—that unified researchers across AI and learning sciences in a shared quest to understand both human and machine cognition.

## Key Concepts
- AI and learning sciences co-evolution
- Information-processing psychology
- Constructivism in early AI
- Cognitive science foundations
- Two traditions in AI/education

## Theoretical Framework
Historical narrative tracing intellectual genealogy of AI and education as co-constituted fields; positions early AI as theoretical investigation of cognition, not merely engineering; draws on Goldstein and Papert's articulation of AI's dual purpose.

## Methods
Intellectual history; review of early AI literature (1950s–1970s), primarily from US and UK; focus on leading figures (Newell, Simon, Minsky, Papert); no original empirical data; explicitly not a comprehensive history of AIED.

## Main Arguments
- Early AI pioneers (Newell, Simon, Minsky, Papert) were simultaneously making foundational contributions to education and cognitive science
- Two strands developed: information-processing psychology (Newell/Simon) and constructivism (Papert/Logo)
- The shared quest to understand and improve both human and machine cognition has been lost from contemporary AI and learning sciences
- Reviving this perspective could enrich both fields and reorient AI in education away from narrow application toward deeper theoretical integration

## Limitations & Critiques
Focus on White male US/UK researchers is acknowledged as an artifact of historical exclusion but limits the history; explicitly not comprehensive; does not address non-Western AI or education traditions; analysis stops before the modern deep learning era.
