# AI Hasn't Fixed Teamwork, But It Shifted Collaborative Culture

## Summary

This longitudinal study examines how AI tools affected teamwork in a project-based software development organization over two years (2023-2025). Conducted just after ChatGPT's release in early 2023 and revisited in 2025, the study captures a critical inflection point in AI adoption and its effects on collaborative work.

The key finding is paradoxical: while AI tools did not solve the persistent challenges of team collaboration (communication fragmentation, accountability ambiguity, coordination difficulties), they nonetheless reshaped collaborative culture in important ways. Workers came to normalize AI-assisted work, expect efficiency and transparency, and accept AI as an integral part of professional practice.

## Key Concepts

- **Sociotechnical Imaginaries**: Workers' visions of AI as an "intelligent coordinator" that would solve teamwork problems - expectations that were largely unmet

- **Technology Domestication**: The process by which AI tools became normalized and integrated into everyday work practices

- **Collaborative Culture Shift**: Changes in workplace norms around efficiency, transparency, and professionalism driven by AI integration

- **Individual vs. Collective Impact**: AI improved individual productivity but left collective coordination challenges unresolved

## Methodology

Two-wave longitudinal interview study:
- **Phase 1 (2023)**: 15 interviews shortly after ChatGPT release about expectations and early experiences
- **Phase 2 (2025)**: Follow-up with 10 of the same participants about actual changes

## Key Findings

### Phase 1 (2023): High Expectations
- Participants envisioned AI as intelligent coordinators
- Expected AI to clarify project scopes, track progress, surface relevant information
- Hoped AI would ease interpersonal frictions and communication challenges

### Phase 2 (2025): Shifted Reality
- Core collaboration problems persisted (communication, accountability, visibility)
- AI was used mainly for individual tasks (coding, writing, documentation)
- New expectations emerged around efficiency and output quality
- Workers became comfortable working alongside AI-augmented colleagues

### Three Key Contributions
1. Rare longitudinal account of AI in teamwork at a critical inflection point
2. Evidence that AI reshapes culture while core challenges persist
3. Design implications for tools that scaffold group awareness, not just individual productivity

## Relevance to AI and Design Education

This study offers cautionary insights for educational contexts:
- **Expectation Management**: High hopes for AI transformation may be disappointed
- **Cultural Shifts**: Even without solving problems, AI changes norms and expectations
- **Individual vs. Collaborative Learning**: AI may help individual tasks while undermining collaborative learning
