# Learning from Notes: Organizational Issues in Groupware Implementation (Orlikowski, 2002)

## Summary
Orlikowski's 2002 paper documents an organizational case study of Lotus Notes implementation, arguing that the gap between a technology's designed affordances and its actual organizational use reveals how technology adoption is shaped by social structures, power dynamics, and organizational culture rather than technical features alone. The paper develops the concept of "technologies-in-practice" — the enacted technologies that emerge from actual use rather than designed systems — and demonstrates how different groups in the same organization develop radically different practices with the same tool. It is a foundational text for sociomaterial and practice-based approaches to technology in organizations.

## Key Concepts
- Technologies-in-practice (enacted vs. designed technologies)
- Organizational implementation of groupware
- Sociomaterial analysis of technology use
- Unanticipated technology adoption patterns
- Structural dimensions of technology use

## Theoretical Framework
Practice theory (Giddens' structuration theory, extended to technology); sociomaterial approaches; draws on Orlikowski's earlier work on the duality of technology to develop the technologies-in-practice concept.

## Methods
Longitudinal case study of Lotus Notes implementation in a large professional services firm; qualitative data from interviews, observation, and document analysis over multiple years.

## Main Arguments
- Technology's effects in organizations are not determined by its technical features but by the social structures and practices through which it is enacted
- Different organizational groups develop different "technologies-in-practice" with the same tool, reflecting their different structural contexts
- Management's intended technology use routinely differs from actual practice; bridging this gap requires understanding the social conditions of use
- Implementation failures often stem from misalignment between technology assumptions and organizational structure, not from technical inadequacy

## Limitations & Critiques
Single case study limits generalizability; the professional services firm context has specific power and knowledge dynamics; the sociomaterial framework, while powerful, can underemphasize material constraints; the Lotus Notes case may seem dated but the theoretical insights remain current.
