# Designers' Articulation and Activation of Instrumental Design Judgements in Cross-Cultural User Research

## Summary

This study analyzes how a Scandinavian design team navigated cross-cultural challenges when designing a co-creation workshop with Chinese consumers. Through discourse and thematic analysis of planning, execution, and debrief sessions, researchers examined how designers' instrumental judgements shifted over time as they engaged with cultural complexity. The team moved from totalizing cultural references in planning to more nuanced, translator-mediated interactions during debriefs.

## Key Concepts

- **Instrumental Judgement**: A designer's process of mediation regarding tools, techniques, proportion, and gauge in design activity
- **Design Judgement**: Continuous decisions practitioners make about understanding design situations and determining next steps
- **Cross-Cultural Design**: Design practices foregrounding cultural concerns across geography, ethnicity, nationality, or socio-economic dimensions
- **Co-Creation/Co-Design**: Methods that engage potential users as active participants in the design process
- **Tacit Knowledge**: Implicit knowledge often inaccessible to designers themselves that forms backbone of expertise

## Theoretical Framework

Built on Nelson and Stolterman's (2012) theoretical typology of design judgements, emphasizing instrumental judgement as how designers interact with their tools (including design methods). The framework connects design cognition with professional expertise, relating both to formal methods and lived experience repertoires.

## Methods

- **Data Source**: DTRS11 shared dataset - 13+ hours of video/audio recordings from 21 work sessions spanning 3 months
- **Participants**: Three UX designers (Ewan, Abby, Kenny) plus external consultants and translators
- **Analysis Approach**: 
  - Discourse analysis: Word frequency for third-person plural pronouns to track participant references
  - Thematic analysis: Identifying cultural assumptions and judgement patterns
  - Synthetic analysis: Combining both to reveal instrumental judgement shifts

## Main Arguments

1. **Judgement Evolution**: Design team's instrumental judgements shifted from totalizing cultural stereotypes in planning to nuanced, mediated understanding during debriefs
2. **Translator as Cultural Broker**: Translators served critical role in "nuancing" cultural meanings beyond literal translation, helping team refine their cultural understanding
3. **Making Familiar Strategy**: Team members attempted to engage with unfamiliar cultural concerns by mapping them to analogous concepts within their own cultural experience
4. **Methods as Tools**: Design methods (like co-creation workshops) function as tools requiring instrumental judgement - they are inherently underspecified and require designer interpretation
5. **Cultural Complexity**: Cross-cultural design reveals how cultural assumptions are always present in design work, simply heightened when cultural boundaries are explicitly traversed

## Limitations

- Single case study limits generalizability
- Limited access to actual workshop execution (focused on planning and debrief)
- Scandinavian-Chinese cultural context may not transfer to other cultural combinations
- Team's prior China trip not fully documented in dataset
- No direct participant voice captured (only team's interpretation)
