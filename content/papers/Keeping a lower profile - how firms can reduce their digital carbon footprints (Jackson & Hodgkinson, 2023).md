# Keeping a lower profile: how firms can reduce their digital carbon footprints

## Summary

Jackson and Hodgkinson introduce the concept of **digital decarbonization** and present a new model of knowledge (re)use in organizations. The paper argues that while businesses focus on tangible activities for decarbonization (production processes), much less attention has been paid to the environmental impact of digitalization. The authors propose shifting from "single-use" knowledge toward effective and efficient reuse of data and knowledge to reduce organizational digital carbon footprints.

The paper develops a three-step framework examining how employees seek, validate, and reuse knowledge, identifying pathways that either lead to dark data accumulation (increasing carbon footprint) or knowledge reuse (reducing carbon footprint).

## Key Concepts

### Digital Decarbonization
The reduction of digital carbon footprint as a critical feature of sustainability strategy. Unlike physical decarbonization, digital decarbonization focuses on how knowledge and data are used and reused by organizations and their employees.

### Dark Data
Information assets organizations collect, process, and store during regular business activities but fail to use for other purposes. Currently, 55% of organizational data qualifies as dark data, representing wasted storage and energy.

### Single-use Knowledge
Knowledge acquired for a specific task that is used once and then discarded without being captured, shared, or integrated into organizational memory. This practice leads to:
- Repeated searches for the same information
- Duplicated data storage
- Increased energy consumption
- Growing dark data stockpiles

### Knowledge Creation Framework
Based on Nonaka and Takeuchi (1995), involving four processes:
1. **Socialization** - tacit to tacit knowledge transfer
2. **Externalization** - tacit to explicit conversion
3. **Combination** - explicit knowledge integration
4. **Internalization** - explicit to tacit conversion

## Key Findings

### Three-Step Knowledge Model

**Step 1: The Search**
- Path A: Information broker searching (Google, internal systems) - leads to just-in-time knowledge
- Path B: Hybrid searching - combining human knowledge with information brokers

**Step 2: The Sanity Check**
- Path C: Data consultation with socialization - leads to knowledge reuse potential
- Path D: Single-use knowledge without socialization - leads to dark data

**Step 3: Knowledge Reuse**
- Path E: Single-use after distribution without internalization - creates dark data
- Path F: Single-use after interpretation without organizational memory capture - creates dark data
- Path G: Knowledge reuse - through proper externalization, combination, and internalization into organizational memory

### Quantified Impact
- Information workers in Western Europe lose 50% of their time weekly searching, governing data, and duplicating work
- This equates to 10 hours per week building knowledge that already exists
- 2.5 quintillion bytes of data produced daily by organizations
- IoT data volume predicted to reach 79 zettabytes by 2025

## Relevance to Sustainable Computing

This paper is highly relevant to sustainable computing research for several reasons:

1. **Quantifies digital environmental impact**: Establishes that digitalization accounts for 4% of global GHG emissions, providing baseline data for sustainability metrics.

2. **Organizational behavior focus**: Unlike technical solutions, this paper addresses the human and organizational factors driving digital waste - a critical but understudied dimension.

3. **Knowledge management intersection**: Bridges information systems, sustainability, and organizational learning literature, offering interdisciplinary insights.

4. **Dark data as sustainability concern**: Highlights that data hoarding practices have environmental consequences beyond storage costs.

5. **Practical framework**: The three-step model provides actionable guidance for organizations seeking to reduce digital carbon footprint through behavioral and cultural change.

6. **AI implications**: Notes that AI-enhanced information brokers (search engines, IBM Watson) can accelerate knowledge waste when not coupled with proper knowledge management practices.
