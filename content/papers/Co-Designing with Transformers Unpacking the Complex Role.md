# Co-Designing with Transformers: Unpacking the Complex Role

## Summary
This paper unpacks the complex and often contradictory role that transformer-based AI systems play when introduced as collaborative participants in design processes, examining case studies where teams explicitly co-designed with AI rather than using it as a tool. The authors find that AI as collaborator introduces new dynamics around authorship, creative responsibility, and team communication that existing design collaboration frameworks do not adequately address. The paper calls for new theoretical frameworks that account for AI as a genuine participant in design processes with distinct capabilities, biases, and limitations.

## Key Concepts
- AI as design collaborator (not just tool)
- Transformer models in participatory design
- Authorship and creative responsibility in human-AI design teams
- Design collaboration theory for AI participants
- Bias and limitation awareness in AI co-design

## Theoretical Framework
Participatory design; human-AI teaming theory; CSCW perspectives on computer-mediated collaboration; draws on design collaboration research to examine AI as a non-human participant.

## Methods
Multiple case studies of design projects explicitly framing AI as a collaborator; qualitative analysis of collaboration dynamics, authorship negotiation, and outcome quality; comparative analysis with human-only design teams.

## Main Arguments
- AI as collaborator produces different outcomes than AI as tool: teams develop new responsibility and communication patterns around managing AI's contributions
- The opaqueness of transformer models creates particular challenges for collaborative accountability: it's difficult to understand why AI made particular suggestions
- Existing participatory design frameworks need extension to handle non-human participants with systematic biases and knowledge limitations
- Design teams benefit from explicit protocols for AI contribution evaluation that prevent uncritical acceptance or reflexive rejection

## Limitations & Critiques
Case study design limits generalizability; the concept of AI as "collaborator" remains contested; findings depend on specific transformer capabilities that may rapidly change.
