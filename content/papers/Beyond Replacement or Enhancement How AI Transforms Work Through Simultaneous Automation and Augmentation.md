# Beyond Replacement or Enhancement: How AI Transforms Work Through Simultaneous Automation and Augmentation

## Summary
Clarke and Joffe (~2024–2025, CSCW) reframe the binary debate over AI replacing vs. augmenting workers by examining how creative agency professionals practically reconfigure their division of labor with generative AI tools like ChatGPT. Through 17 ethnomethodologically informed interviews with international creative agency workers, they show that workers don't simply adopt or resist AI — they continuously assign roles to AI, modify and remediate its outputs, and manage those outputs to meet stakeholder expectations. The paper introduces three original concepts: prompting as reflexive delegation, continuous configuration of AI role boundaries, and interpretive templatized trust.

## Key Concepts
- Reflexive delegation — prompting as a form of situated workplace role assignment
- AI role boundary configuration and repair
- Interpretive templatized trust — strategies for managing AI outputs stakeholders will accept
- Division of labor reconfiguration with GenAI
- Remediation of AI outputs

## Theoretical Framework
Ethnomethodology and workplace studies; treats AI tool use as a practical accomplishment embedded in accountability structures, not as adoption of a technology.

## Methods
17 ethnomethodologically informed interviews with workers at international creative agencies; qualitative analysis grounded in workplace studies tradition. Authors: Independent researcher (Canada) and Google Inc. (Canada). Published at CSCW (~2024–2025, 22 pages).

## Main Arguments
- Workers do not simply use AI — they situationally assign it roles, monitor its performance, and repair breakdowns when AI outputs violate workplace norms
- Prompting is not just a technical skill but a form of workplace delegation with accountability implications
- Workers develop templated strategies to make AI outputs legible and acceptable to clients and managers — "interpretive templatized trust"
- The replacement/augmentation binary obscures the complex ongoing labor of configuring AI contributions in practice

## Limitations & Critiques
Creative agency context (advertising, marketing, design services) may not generalize to other knowledge work sectors. Ethnomethodological approach surfaces practices but does not quantify prevalence or organizational-level effects.
