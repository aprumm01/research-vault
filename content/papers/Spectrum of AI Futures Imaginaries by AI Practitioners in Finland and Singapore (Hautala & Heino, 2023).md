# Spectrum of AI Futures Imaginaries by AI Practitioners in Finland and Singapore: The Unimagined Speed of AI Progress (Hautala & Heino, 2023)

## Summary
Johanna Hautala (University of Vaasa) and Hanna Heino (University of Turku) present an empirical study mapping the spectrum of AI futures imaginaries among 35 AI and robotics practitioners based in Finland and Singapore, interviewed between autumn 2018 and summer 2019 -- before the ChatGPT era. The study produces a spectrum of three continuums from best to worst imaginary, along which practitioners position their visions of likely futures. A distinctive retrospective finding is the "unimagined speed" thesis: practitioners in 2018-2019 significantly underestimated the pace of AI development that would follow, revealing a persistent condition of imaginative lag among even technically expert practitioners. Published in Futures 153 (2023) 103247.

## Key Concepts
- Three continuums of futures imaginaries:
  - (1) Human-AI coexistence and meaningful life ↔ AI controls and destroys humans; likely future: toward the best (most optimistic group)
  - (2) Co-creating innovation in democratic society ↔ increasing inequality and technology dependence; likely future: in between
  - (3) Sustainable dispersed-power economy ↔ economy of efficiency and corporate-centered power; likely future: toward the worst (most pessimistic group)
- Decisive questions behind continuums: who controls AI? how are relations structured in practitioner communities and society? what concentration of power is justified?
- "Unimagined speed": practitioners in 2018-2019 did not anticipate the rapid development and public spread of AI that arrived with ChatGPT and generative AI -- retrospectively visible now
- "Outside the core" perspective: Finland and Singapore practitioners represent voices outside dominant US/China AI corporations with their mass social media data advantage
- Individual futures imaginaries: defined as individual interpretations of un/desirable futures in context of collectively held visions -- not reducible to collective or state-level SIs

## Theoretical Framework
Sociotechnical imaginaries (Jasanoff 2015); Mager & Katzenbach (2021) multiple/contested/commodified SIs framing; future-oriented dialectics (Ahlqvist 2022); sociology of expectations (van Lente); utopia-dystopia spectrum (Makridakis 2017; Nam 2019; Angheloiu et al. 2020); care robot imaginaries (Vallès-Peris & Domènech 2020); Futures journal, 2023; builds directly on companion paper Hautala & Ahlqvist (2022/2024).

## Methods
Qualitative interview study: 35 semi-structured interviews with AI and robotics practitioners (26 from industry/applied AI; 9 from robotics academia), Finland and Singapore, autumn 2018 -- summer 2019; interviews ~30-60 min, recorded and transcribed; thematic content analysis using NVivo (initial coding into best/worst/likely categories; iterative construction of three continuums); individual-level analysis rather than aggregate to preserve idiographic tensions; practitioners identified through AI practitioner networks (Turku.AI in Finland; SGInnovate/AI Singapore events in Singapore).

## Main Arguments
- Futures imaginaries exist on a non-linear spectrum rather than a binary utopia/dystopia -- practitioners hold complex, simultaneously optimistic and pessimistic visions within the same imaginary continuum; the binary framing common in AI discourse and national strategies (e.g., Schwab's Fourth Industrial Revolution) is inadequate
- Practitioners who envision the most dystopic worst-case future also envision the most optimistic likely future (continuum 1) -- a coping mechanism of imagining extremes while expecting benign outcomes; practitioners focused on economic power imagine likely futures resembling worst cases (continuum 3)
- The "unimagined speed" finding is retrospectively significant: even AI insiders building the technology in 2018-2019 did not imagine rapid general-public AI adoption -- demonstrating that futures imaginaries among practitioners systematically lag behind actual technological trajectories
- Individual practitioners hold decisive agency in shaping AI futures imaginaries, but their agency is constrained by their position in practitioner communities, socioeconomic structures, and the question of who justifiably concentrates power over AI development

## Limitations & Critiques
Only 35 practitioners in two technologically advanced countries; highly skewed gender (6/35 women, 17%) reflecting the actual gender imbalance in the practitioner community but limiting perspective diversity; conducted in 2018-2019 before the current AI development era -- the "unimagined speed" observation is a retrospective finding not claimed by the authors at the time; Finland and Singapore represent "outside the core" voices but remain in the Global North; no follow-up study to check whether practitioners' imaginaries updated post-ChatGPT.
