# AI in Design Education

## Summary
Schauer, Simbeck, and Pinkwart survey communication and architectural design students at a German and Italian university to assess their AI literacy and attitudes toward generative AI. Students self-reported moderate AI literacy, expressed both optimism about productivity gains and concern about creativity loss, job displacement, and copyright issues, and identified gaps in their knowledge of specific AI tools and ethical considerations. The paper argues for structured integration of AI competencies into design curricula.

## Key Concepts
- AI literacy
- Attitudes toward AI in design education
- Generative AI in creative workflows
- Human-AI co-creation
- Curriculum integration

## Theoretical Framework
The study draws on AI literacy frameworks (Ng et al., 2021) and research on human-AI co-creativity, situating design students within a spectrum from full AI reliance to purely human creativity. It applies a mixed quantitative and qualitative survey methodology informed by design process models.

## Methods
Two-part survey combining quantitative Likert-scale items and qualitative open-ended responses. Participants were communication and architectural design students at universities in Germany and Italy. Analysis examined attitudes, self-assessed AI literacy, and perceived competency gaps.

## Main Arguments
- Design students are broadly aware of AI's potential but hold ambivalent attitudes, recognizing both benefits and risks
- Self-assessed AI literacy is moderate and uneven; students identify specific gaps in tool use and ethical reasoning
- Design curricula largely lack structured AI literacy content, leaving students underprepared
- Integrating AI competencies into design education requires addressing both technical skills and critical ethical reflection

## Limitations & Critiques
The sample is limited to two national contexts and two design disciplines, restricting generalizability. Self-reported AI literacy may not reflect actual competency, and the study does not follow students longitudinally to assess how attitudes or skills change with exposure.
