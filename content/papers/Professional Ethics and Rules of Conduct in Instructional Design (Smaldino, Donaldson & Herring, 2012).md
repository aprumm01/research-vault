# Professional Ethics and Rules of Conduct in Instructional Design (Smaldino, Donaldson & Herring, 2012)


## Summary
Smaldino, Donaldson, and Herring (2012) present a chapter on professional ethics and its application in instructional design and technology practice, appearing in a foundational ID/T textbook (Section IX: Current Issues in Instructional Design and Technology). The chapter centers on the AECT (Association for Educational Communications and Technology) Code of Professional Ethics, examining its three sections -- commitment to the individual, commitment to society, and commitment to the profession -- and demonstrating how ethical practice is positioned as essential to the 2008 AECT definition of educational technology. The authors draw on the ethical competence development model proposed by Clark (1995) and situate professional ethics as a practical guide for daily IT practice.

## Key Concepts
- AECT Code of Professional Ethics (three sections: individual, society, profession)
- Ethical practice as an essential element of the 2008 AECT definition of educational technology
- Commitment to individual: diversity, technology/resource selection, personal rights
- Commitment to society: honest representation, personal gain limits, professional service
- Commitment to profession: professional interactions, research and dissemination, copyright
- Clark's model of ethical competence: identifying pitfalls, open dialogue, self-examination

## Theoretical Framework
Professional ethics in instructional design and technology; draws on the AECT Code of Professional Ethics and the 2008 AECT definition; Clark's (1995) three-stage ethical competence model (pitfall identification, open dialogue, ethical self-examination); Tavani's (2007) perspective on professional obligation; situates ethics within both organizational governance and pedagogical responsibility; chapter in a Smaldino-edited textbook on instructional design and technology.

## Methods
Conceptual/prescriptive chapter reviewing and analyzing the AECT Code of Professional Ethics; applies Clark's ethical competence framework; uses illustrative examples (e.g., L.L. Bean ergonomics, professor posing as student online) to ground abstract principles; no empirical data collection; intended audience is emerging instructional technology professionals and students.

## Main Arguments
- Ethical practice is not peripheral but essential to instructional technology -- the 2008 AECT definition explicitly includes "ethical practice" alongside facilitating learning and improving performance
- The AECT Code of Professional Ethics structures obligations across three domains: the individual (diversity, resource selection, personal rights), society (honest representation, limiting personal gain, professional service), and the profession (collegial behavior, research dissemination, copyright adherence)
- As an IT professional, one is obligated to report the unethical behavior of peers and to help educate others about copyright, privacy, and responsible technology use -- not merely to comply personally
- Developing ethical competence requires more than knowing rules: it requires identifying ethical pitfalls proactively, engaging in open dialogue about ethical dilemmas in professional education settings, and practicing ethical self-examination as an ongoing reflective process
- The rapid growth of digital resources and cyber-technology creates new ethical challenges that require active professional engagement, not passive rule-following

## Limitations & Critiques
As a textbook chapter rather than empirical research, the work is prescriptive rather than descriptive or analytical. The ethical framework is anchored to AECT membership and the US-centric professional context of instructional technology, which may not translate globally. The treatment of ethical dilemmas relies on illustrative examples rather than systematic case analysis. The 2012 publication predates major AI, data privacy, and generative technology debates that now constitute central ethical concerns for ID professionals.
