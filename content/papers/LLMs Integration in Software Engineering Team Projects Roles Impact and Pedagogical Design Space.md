# LLMs Integration in Software Engineering Team Projects: Roles, Impact, and a Pedagogical Design Space for AI Tools in Computing Education

## Summary
This paper investigates GenAI tool use (ChatGPT, GitHub Copilot) in a semester-long second-year undergraduate software engineering team project at Newcastle University. Using surveys (39 students) and interviews (8 students), the study provides qualitative insights into the impact of GenAI on coding experience, individual learning, teamwork dynamics, and self-efficacy. The authors apply learning theory and pedagogy lenses to the data and propose a preliminary design space for educational GenAI programming tools that foregrounds roles, scaffolding, and transparency.

## Key Concepts
- **GenAI roles in learning**: The different functions AI can serve during the learning process (e.g., explainer, code generator, debugger, collaborator) — each with distinct implications for scaffolding and dependency
- **Team dynamics and GenAI**: How AI tool use affects intra-team communication, knowledge sharing, task distribution, and collective learning in software engineering projects
- **Team efficacy vs. self-efficacy**: The distinction between individual confidence in programming and collective team confidence, both of which are impacted differently by GenAI integration
- **Pedagogical design space**: A framework of design dimensions for creating educational GenAI tools, encompassing scaffolding level, transparency mechanisms, and role configuration
- **Transparency in AI tools**: The importance of making AI contributions legible to teammates, students, and educators — addressing accountability in collaborative work
- **Scaffolding in GenAI tools**: The degree to which AI provides context-sensitive help versus direct answers, and how this gradient affects learning and dependency

## Theoretical Framework
The paper explicitly applies learning theory and pedagogy lenses to empirical data, distinguishing it from studies that report only on performance outcomes. It draws on constructivist learning theory (knowledge built through active engagement and reflection), social learning theory (knowledge constructed in collaborative contexts), and the concept of zone of proximal development to analyze how GenAI can either appropriately scaffold learning or short-circuit it.

The design space framework draws on HCI design space methodology, structuring design considerations as axes of variation along which educational GenAI tools can be configured. This allows the paper to move from descriptive findings to prescriptive design guidance — an important bridge for practitioners building or selecting tools.

## Methods
Mixed qualitative study conducted in a 7-week full-time software engineering team project module with second-year undergraduate computer science students at Newcastle University. Students were explicitly permitted to use GenAI tools for coding tasks. Data collection included a post-module survey completed by 39 students with open-ended questions, and semi-structured interviews with 8 students selected for depth. Data were analyzed using thematic analysis, informed by prior learning theory and pedagogy literature. The module structure and GenAI permission policy were documented as context.

## Main Arguments
- **GenAI use in team projects has distinct effects on teamwork not captured by individual-level studies**: AI tools affected how tasks were divided, how knowledge was shared across teams, and whether team members developed shared understanding — creating both opportunities for collaboration and risks of siloed work where only one team member understands AI-generated code.
- **Learning benefits were uneven and contingent on prior ability**: Students with stronger programming backgrounds were better able to use GenAI critically — understanding generated code, modifying it, and learning from it. Weaker students tended toward acceptance without comprehension, raising concerns about widening skill gaps.
- **Over-reliance and skill atrophy concerns were salient**: Students reported awareness of potential skill degradation, particularly in areas like debugging and writing code from scratch — skills they feared would deteriorate with heavy AI assistance.
- **Transparency is a critical underaddressed dimension**: Current GenAI tools provide no visibility into what AI contributed to a team project, creating both accountability problems for educators and equity issues within teams where contributions may be unequal.
- **The proposed design space identifies three key axes**: (1) the role GenAI plays during learning (explainer, generator, collaborator), (2) the scaffolding support tailored to each role (graduated hints vs. full solutions), and (3) mechanisms for transparency to teammates, students, and instructors.
- **Prohibiting GenAI is not a viable long-term strategy**: The growing consensus from students, instructors, and industry perspectives is that learning to work critically with GenAI tools is an essential professional competency that computing education must actively develop.

## Limitations & Critiques
The qualitative sample of 8 interview participants and 39 survey respondents from a single institution and single module limits generalizability. The study design did not include a control group without GenAI access, making it impossible to attribute observed effects directly to AI use. The 7-week intensive project format may not represent more typical semester-long coursework patterns. The proposed design space is preliminary and has not been validated through design or evaluation studies.

Self-report data in the survey may not accurately reflect actual behavior, as students may underreport AI dependence or overstate their critical engagement with generated code. The institution's explicit permission to use GenAI may also create conditions that differ significantly from contexts where AI use is ambiguous or restricted.
