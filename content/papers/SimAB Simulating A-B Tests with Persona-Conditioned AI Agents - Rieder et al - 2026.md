# SimAB: Simulating A/B Tests with Persona-Conditioned AI Agents for Rapid Design Evaluation

**Authors:** Tim Rieder, Marian Schneider, Mario Truss, Vitaly Tsaplin, Alina Rublea, Sinem Dere, Francisco Chicharro Sanz, Tobias Reiss, Mustafa Doga Dogan  
**Affiliation:** ETH Zurich (Rieder, Schneider), Adobe (Truss, Tsaplin, Rublea, Dere, Sanz, Reiss, Dogan)  
**Year:** 2026  
**Type:** System/Tool Paper  
**Field:** Human-Computer Interaction, A/B Testing, Design Evaluation

---

## Overview of the Document

This paper introduces SimAB, a system that reframes A/B testing as fast simulation rather than extended experimentation with real users. The collaboration between ETH Zurich and Adobe Research addresses a fundamental tension in data-driven design: A/B testing is the gold standard for validating design decisions, but its reliance on real user traffic creates significant bottlenecks—especially for low-traffic pages, early-stage prototypes, micro-optimizations, and privacy-sensitive contexts.

The research team grounds their work in extensive practitioner engagement: 14 interviews with experimentation experts across enterprise organizations (including telecommunications, industrial equipment manufacturing, and software distribution) who collectively possess over 81 years of A/B testing experience. These conversations revealed critical pain points: tests are frequently abandoned before reaching statistical significance due to insufficient traffic, small changes don't justify months-long experiments, and practitioners often deploy changes based on intuition rather than data—precisely because rigorous testing is impractical.

SimAB's innovation lies in treating persona-conditioned AI agents as synthetic participants in A/B tests. The system generates diverse user personas from demographic, psychographic, and behavioral attributes; deploys these personas as agents that interact with design variants through screenshots; aggregates their preferences; and synthesizes results into actionable insights—all within minutes rather than months. The validation is substantial: testing against 47 historical A/B tests from real organizations, SimAB achieved 67% overall accuracy, improving to 83% for high-confidence cases and >80% when considering significant lift.

## Research Overview

**Central Research Question:** Can persona-conditioned AI agents provide fast, privacy-preserving evaluation of design variants that accurately predicts real A/B test outcomes, especially in scenarios where traditional testing is infeasible?

The research doesn't position SimAB as a replacement for traditional A/B testing—rather, it targets the scenarios where real testing *doesn't happen* due to practical constraints. The goal is "shift-left in the design process," providing directional guidance during ideation and early iteration when changes are still inexpensive and exploratory.

**The Four-Stage Pipeline:**

**1. Input Processing**
Users provide up to four inputs:
- **Variant Images** (required): Screenshots of Control and Challenger designs
- **Conversion Goal**: The primary success metric (e.g., "increase sign-ups")
- **Target Audience**: Segment information (if provided)
- **Context Documents** (optional): Analytics reports, past experiment results, or domain knowledge

Following the principle of "Shift-Left in the Design Process," SimAB operates on screenshots rather than fully implemented pages, enabling validation during ideation when implementation costs are minimal.

**2. Persona Generation**
The system creates a diverse population of synthetic users represented by 13 attributes across four categories:
- **Demographics**: Name, age range, occupation, income level, education, location
- **Psychographics**: Interests, goals, pain points, technical savviness  
- **Behavioral**: Online behavior patterns, typical browsing context
- **Task-specific**: Specific tasks the persona would perform on the page

Personas are generated by prompting GPT-5-mini-2025-08-07 with variant screenshots, target audience information, and any supplied contextual documents. The generation explicitly instructs diversity to avoid homogeneous populations.

To balance efficiency with diversity, personas are generated in batches of 5-10, with previously generated personas included in context to avoid duplication. The system implements "Audience Restrictions" when users provide known segment distributions—aligning persona generation to match target demographics while maintaining variance.

**3. Persona Simulation**
Each persona becomes an AI agent that evaluates both variants through a multi-turn conversation structured in three phases:

*Phase 1: Contextualization:* The agent receives complete persona specification, conversion goal, criterion for preference, retrieved context, and evaluation guidelines emphasizing clarity, friction points, trust signals, and value communication.

*Phase 2: Variant Presentation with Counterbalancing:* The agent views both variant images in a single prompt, with neutral names ("Image 1"/"Image 2"). To mitigate position bias, the order varies randomly—half see Control first, half see Challenger first. The system validates that both orders produce effective results (tested in Section 5.3).

*Phase 3: Structured Response:* The agent produces a JSON containing:
- **Verdict**: "Image 1", "Image 2", or "None" 
- **Rationale**: Explanation identifying specific elements influencing preference, aligned with design principles to provide "Rationales & Actionable Insights"

The "None" option allows agents to indicate insufficient difference, serving as an important signal for low-baseline-conversion-rate pages.

*Interactive Actions:* For pages extending beyond single screenshots, SimAB supports multi-step flows. Agents can invoke tool functions to manipulate their view—scrolling, zooming, navigating through design steps. This enables evaluation of conversion flows where key elements appear below the fold or across multiple screens.

**4. Summary and Actionable Insight Generation**
Upon experiment completion, SimAB generates an aggregated summary via a final LLM call that:
- Identifies the 3-5 most common preference themes for each variant
- Surfaces actionable improvements from dissenting agents
- Proposes immediate action items to iterate on the Challenger

The summary includes synthetic personas, statistical verdicts, and rationales aggregated into concise stakeholder reports—fulfilling the principle "Rationales & Actionable Insights" rather than just binary winners.

**Key Design Principles (derived from formative study):**

1. **Shift-Left in the Design Process**: Test during ideation using screenshots, not after full implementation
2. **Prioritize Speed Over Perfect Accuracy**: Directional guidance in minutes > perfect answers in months
3. **Audience Restrictions & Additional Context**: Leverage prior knowledge to improve specificity
4. **Rationales & Actionable Insights**: Explain *why* to support iteration, not just reveal *which*

## Theories of Knowledge

The paper builds on several intersecting research areas: A/B testing methodology, synthetic audiences, persona development, and LLM-based evaluation.

**A/B Testing as Gold Standard—and Bottleneck:** A/B testing has been the cornerstone of data-driven design since its web popularization. Research by Kohavi et al. and others established that even small changes can produce measurable impact. However, reaching statistical significance requires large sample sizes—often months of traffic collection, especially for low-traffic pages. Algorithmic approaches like multi-armed bandits and Thompson sampling address *efficiency* rather than the fundamental *dependency on real user traffic*. SimAB addresses this dependency directly by substituting synthetic for real users in scenarios where real testing is impractical.

**LLMs for Design Feedback and Evaluation:** Recent work has explored LLMs as design feedback generators and UI evaluators. Studies show GPT-4 can automate heuristic evaluation of UI mockups and provide useful critiques. However, research also demonstrates performance degradation over iterations as designs improve—suggesting LLMs work better for initial critique than iterative refinement. Prior work on personas has used LLMs to generate neutral user feedback or derive design iterations from multimodal overview. SimAB extends this by structuring LLM outputs as preference judgments aggregated across diverse personas rather than single-expert critiques.

**Synthetic Audiences and User Simulation:** Building on foundational work on generative agents (Park et al.) that simulate believable human behavior through memory and action planning, researchers have explored simulating actual users for design feedback. Work has demonstrated LLM agents navigating real websites, establishing benchmarks for agentic web interaction, and simulating complex multi-turn behaviors for recommendation systems. Research on persona consistency, human-AI workflows for collaborative persona generation, and reinforcement learning approaches for personalization provide foundation for persona development.

The use of AI to simulate human behavior has gained significant traction. Prior work introduced *generative agents* that simulate believable behavior through memory retrieval and action planning, with subsequent research demonstrating generative simulations of real individuals replicating survey responses with high accuracy, including approaches that improve simulation accuracy and large-scale simulations advancing understanding of collective behaviors.

**Concerns About Simulation Validity:** The paper directly engages with substantial critiques of LLM-based simulation validity. Prior work has identified:
- **Inherent biases** from training data (overrepresenting WEIRD populations, underrepresenting minorities)
- **Limited diversity** in behavioral variance even under persona prompts  
- **Value-action gaps** where LLMs replicate aligned survey answers but don't follow through in actions
- **Construct validity failures** documented in GenAI-based personas
- **Robustness issues** where LLMs fail to react consistently when stimuli are reworded

The authors acknowledge these limitations but redefine success as *pragmatically useful simulation* rather than perfect individual-level fidelity. Their validation strategy focuses on aggregate accuracy across diverse personas against real-world ground truth.

**Digital Twins in Experience Research:** The digital twins paradigm has emerged as a powerful extension, with work introducing large-scale digital twins to evaluate designs across multiple studies. SimAB aligns with this trajectory, treating persona-conditioned LLMs as proxies for human participants—not claiming perfect replication but demonstrating pragmatic utility when aggregated.

## Central Arguments

The paper makes several interconnected claims about when and how simulated A/B testing provides practical value despite acknowledged limitations of individual agent accuracy.

**Main Thesis:** Persona-conditioned AI agents, when aggregated at scale, can provide fast directional guidance for design decisions that aligns with real A/B test outcomes—especially valuable in scenarios where traditional testing is infeasible or abandoned due to practical constraints.

**First, speed enables shift-left in the design process.** Traditional A/B testing arrives too late to influence ideation and early iteration. By the time results are available, substantial investment has been made in implementation. SimAB's 1-3 minute runtime (even with early stopping enabled) makes testing accessible during the design phase when changes are still exploratory and inexpensive. As the authors note from practitioner interviews: "Even if live results were instantaneous, they occur at the end of a long design pipeline, after substantial investment in ideation, design, and implementation."

**Second, aggregation overcomes individual imperfection.** The paper doesn't claim individual personas perfectly replicate individual humans. Instead, diverse persona populations collectively surface patterns that match population-level outcomes. The validation demonstrates this: 67% overall accuracy, 83% for high-confidence cases, >80% when considering lift magnitude. The key insight is that imperfect agents, when properly diverse and aggregated, produce useful directional signals.

**Third, bias mitigation through systematic design.** SimAB addresses known LLM biases through architectural choices:
- **Counterbalancing variant presentation order** to mitigate position bias
- **Batched generation with diversity constraints** to prevent homogeneous populations  
- **Audience restriction alignment** when segment distributions are known
- **"None" option** allowing agents to indicate insufficient difference
- **Asymptotic confidence sequences** for statistical aggregation that detects consensus early

**Fourth, actionable insights matter more than binary verdicts.** The emphasis on rationales and improvement suggestions positions SimAB as an explanatory tool rather than a black box oracle. Professional designer feedback (Section 5.4) rated the rationales as particularly valuable—more so than the verdict itself. This aligns with the design principle "Rationales & Actionable Insights": understanding *why* enables iteration beyond simple accept/reject decisions.

## Evidence

The researchers provide extensive validation through historical A/B test replication, robustness analysis, ablation studies, and practitioner feedback.

**Historical A/B Test Corpus**

The validation corpus consists of 47 manually curated real-world A/B tests with documented outcomes, spanning three sources:
1. Large-scale e-commerce web applications (internal)
2. Commercial desktop application by the organization (internal)
3. Publicly available tests (Wikimedia Foundation experiments from 2010-2011)

These experiments targeted varied objectives (sign-ups, engagement, donations, bounce rate reduction) across multiple geographic regions and design change types (call-to-action elements, headlines, layouts, imagery).

**Primary Validation: Predictive Accuracy**

Table 1 presents the confusion matrix showing SimAB's predictions against ground truth:

| Predicted | Ground Truth Challenger | Ground Truth Control | Precision |
|-----------|------------------------|---------------------|-----------|
| Challenger | 22 (TP) | 9 (FP) | 71% |
| Control | 6 (FN) | 8 (TN) | - |
| Recall | 79% | - | Acc: 67% |

The results demonstrate SimAB provides a strong predictive signal, agreeing with the historical experimental outcome in two-thirds of cases (67% agreement rate) and agreeing with 80% of cases where the historical test favored the challenger (recall).

**High-Confidence Subset:** When filtering to tests reaching significance within ≤3 agents, accuracy jumps to 83%, precision 88%, recall 81%. The authors note: "very-high-confidence tests that had significance come at the cost of being at a traditional A/B testing marker—60% of tests reaching significance."

**Significance Threshold Analysis:** The 50% baseline represents random chance. SimAB reached statistical significance (reducing required agent count by over 50%) in about 60% of tests while maintaining accuracy—directly addressing the design requirement "prioritize speed over perfect accuracy."

**Agent Count vs. Accuracy Trade-off** (Figure 7): The analysis reveals diminishing returns: early agents (≤200) provide rapidly increasing accuracy; beyond 400 agents, accuracy plateaus around 0.70. For the subset reaching statistical significance quickly, substantially higher accuracy with fewer agents is achieved. This pattern aligns with intuition: when variants differ distinctly, agents detect the difference early; when differences are subtle, more sampling is needed, and the synthetic signal becomes uncertain.

**Robustness and Consistency Analysis**

**Repeated Experiments (Section 5.2.1):** Running the same experiment (pricing page test) four times produced:
- Consistent winner identification (Challenger) across all runs with significant margin
- Fluctuating exact vote counts (reflecting stochastic persona generation)
- Stable directional signal despite variance

Figure 8 visualizes vote distribution consistency: while exact counts vary (Control: 1000-1500, Challenger: 1500-2000), the margin remains clear across runs. This demonstrates practical reliability—the system won't flip verdicts on repeat execution.

**Counterbalancing Validation (Section 5.3):** Testing whether variant presentation order affects outcomes, the researchers validated that both neutral naming ("Image 1"/"Image 2") and counterbalanced presentation produce effective bias mitigation. This addresses known LLM position biases documented in prior work.

**Ablation Studies**

**Persona Ablation (Section 5.4):** Practitioners completed sessions where they evaluated SimAB outputs with personas removed. Results showed personas provided valuable context and improved trust in the system's recommendations. However, practitioners emphasized that rationales (the *why* behind preferences) were often more valuable than persona details.

**Feedback from Experimentation Practitioners (Section 5.5):**

Multiple practitioners provided direct feedback through online meetings and written responses. Key themes:

*Naming and Terminology:* Practitioners consistently requested avoiding the term "testing" to prevent confusion with traditional A/B testing, suggesting "prediction," "estimation," or "simulation" instead.

*Actionable Items Value:* The improvement suggestions generated from dissenting agent rationales received positive feedback as particularly actionable.

*Early Stopping Benefit:* The ability to detect consensus quickly (often within 31 agents for clear differences, as shown in Figure 5) was praised for enabling rapid iteration.

*Integration Desires:* Practitioners expressed interest in incorporating SimAB into early design workflows, with one noting: "This could let us test things we currently just don't test because the traffic isn't there."

**Real-World Application Evidence**

The Wikipedia donation banner experiment (Figure 2, Figure 5, Figure 6) demonstrates the complete workflow:

**Setup:** Testing whether a prominent group photo banner or appeal text increases Wikimedia Foundation donations.

**SimAB Prediction:** After 31 agents (1-2 minutes), statistical significance reached with Challenger (group photo + appeal) predicted as winner with 25 votes vs. Control's 6 votes, representing 95% confidence.

**Ground Truth Alignment:** This prediction matched the real historical test outcome.

**Rationale Synthesis:** SimAB identified key reasons:
- *Control*: "Less intrusive banner with concise headline and clear CTA"
- *Challenger*: "Large prominent banner with clear visual appeal," "Human element builds trust," "Perceived as friendlier and more authentic"
- *Actionable insights*: Reduce photo visual dominance, simplify header copy, lighten banner, improve perceived intrusiveness

This exemplifies the system functioning as intended: fast directional guidance with explanatory rationales supporting iteration.

**Limitations Acknowledged:**

The paper honestly identifies several constraints:

**Analytics Context Limitation:** Including analytics reports showing 30% "Tech-Savvy Creators" helped, but more elaborate schemes to align persona generation with complex segment distributions remain for future work.

**"None" Option Under-utilization:** The option allowing agents to decline choosing when differences are insufficient was rarely selected, even for very-close tests. This suggests agents may be too willing to commit to a preference.

**Concurrency Requirements:** Running 200 concurrent requests completes experiments in 1-3 minutes, but requires substantial API capacity. The authors note higher concurrency wasn't required for their use case but could be achieved by scaling agent count.

## Conclusion

This research makes a pragmatic case for simulated A/B testing as a complement to—not replacement for—traditional experimentation. The core insight is temporal: testing that provides directional guidance in minutes enables design decisions that months-long experiments cannot inform in time.

**Practical Impact for Design Workflows:**

The formative study revealed that practitioners frequently abandon tests or deploy changes based on intuition specifically because rigorous testing is impractical. SimAB addresses four critical pain point scenarios:

1. **Low-Traffic Pages**: New markets, niche audiences, B2B segments lack traffic volume for timely results
2. **High-Design Variance**: When exploring many alternatives, exhaustive testing becomes infeasible  
3. **Low-Value Changes**: Small improvements don't justify months-long experiments
4. **Instrumentation & Sampling Constraints**: Cookie-opt-in requirements, privacy regulations, and participation biases limit data collection

In these scenarios, SimAB offers "a free lunch"—not perfect information, but directional guidance where the alternative is no data at all.

**The "Shift-Left" Value Proposition:**

By operating on screenshots during ideation, SimAB enables testing before implementation investment. This fundamentally changes the cost structure: exploring 10 design variations takes 10-30 minutes rather than 10 months. Even with imperfect accuracy, rapid iteration converges toward effective designs faster than single perfect measurements taken too late.

**Speed-Accuracy Calibration:**

The trade-off analysis (Figure 7) provides concrete guidance: teams can tune agent count to their speed/confidence requirements. For highly distinct variants (clear winners), 30-50 agents suffice. For subtle differences, 200-400 agents provide maximum accuracy. The early-stopping mechanism automatically detects when consensus emerges, optimizing runtime without sacrificing signal quality for clear-cut cases.

**Integration into Practice:**

The paper positions SimAB explicitly as a screening mechanism—filtering distinct failures before they reach live traffic, not validating final decisions. As one practitioner noted: "This lets us test things we currently just don't test." The system doesn't eliminate traditional A/B testing; it enables exploration and iteration that wouldn't happen otherwise.

**Future Directions Identified:**

1. **LLM Experience Expected to Speed Up:** As models improve, the gap from human performance may narrow, further increasing utility
2. **Domain-Specific Fine-tuning:** Training models on historical test outcomes could improve accuracy for specific product categories
3. **Multi-Page Flows:** Extending beyond single-screenshot evaluation to complete user journeys
4. **Continuous Learning:** Updating persona generation based on accumulating real test outcomes

**Acknowledged Boundaries:**

The authors are explicit about what SimAB doesn't do:
- Doesn't replace final validation with real users
- Doesn't capture long-tail user behaviors or unexpected interactions
- Doesn't account for technical performance differences (load times, bugs)
- Doesn't model novelty effects or habituation over time

These limitations define SimAB's scope: early-stage directional guidance, not end-stage validation.

Six months from now, when you return to this research, remember: the key contribution isn't proving simulated tests are "accurate enough"—it's demonstrating that fast, imperfect directional signals during ideation are more valuable than perfect measurements that arrive too late to influence design decisions. SimAB trades perfection for speed in contexts where speed is the bottleneck, not accuracy.

## Discussion Questions

1. **Validation Scope:** The 47 historical tests provide substantial validation, but they span only a few organizations and domains. How might SimAB's accuracy vary across radically different contexts—e.g., healthcare interfaces, mobile apps, or accessibility-focused designs?

2. **The Persona Diversity Problem:** SimAB generates diverse personas to approximate population variance, but those personas inherit biases from the LLM's training data. How can we validate that persona diversity actually captures human population diversity rather than just generating statistical noise?

3. **Temporal Validity:** Real A/B tests measure actual behavior over time, potentially capturing novelty effects, habituation, and changing user expectations. SimAB captures instant impressions from screenshots. For what kinds of design changes does this gap matter most, and when is instant impression a sufficient proxy?

4. **The "None" Under-utilization:** Agents rarely selected "None" even for very close tests, suggesting they may be too willing to commit to a preference. What does this reveal about LLM decision-making under uncertainty? Should the system prompt differently, or is this over-commitment actually useful for breaking design deadlocks?

5. **Integration Risks:** If teams start relying heavily on SimAB for early-stage screening, might they become less rigorous about eventual validation with real users? How do you prevent a tool designed to *enable* more testing from accidentally *replacing* necessary validation?
