# Catalyst for Creativity or a Hollow Trend?: A Cross-Level Perspective on The Role of Generative AI in Design

## Summary
Naqvi, He, and Kaur (University of Minnesota, ~2023–2024) use the 2022 launch of Midjourney and DALL.E as a natural dividing point to compare how designers who learned design before vs. after AI image tools perceive and use generative AI in their work. Through 28 artifact-based interviews — discussions centered on actual design artifacts participants produced with and without AI — they document a rift in the value system of the design community. Experienced designers are more circumspect about AI's effects on traditional creativity and foundational skills, while designers who encountered AI earlier in their training show different patterns of integration and identity construction.

## Key Concepts
- Pre- vs. post-AI tool introduction design identity
- Value system rift in design communities
- Artifact-based interview methodology
- Midjourney and DALL.E as field-dividing tools
- Marketability vs. creative authenticity tension

## Theoretical Framework
Value-sensitive design and professional identity theory; uses artifact-based interviews to ground abstract values questions in concrete practice.

## Methods
28 artifact-based interviews with designers at varying levels of tool introduction (pre- and post-2022 Midjourney/DALL.E launch); qualitative analysis. Tools tested: Midjourney, DALL.E. University of Minnesota (16 pages, likely CHI or DIS venue).

## Main Arguments
- The 2022 launch of AI image tools created a generational divide in how designers experience and value their craft
- Experienced designers emphasize what AI cannot do (conceptual judgment, foundational craft) as core to design identity
- Newer designers face tension between AI tool marketability (employers want AI skills) and authentic creative development
- Artifact-based interviews are a productive method for studying GenAI's effect on practice — they surface implicit values that abstract questioning misses

## Limitations & Critiques
Recruitment around a specific tool-launch moment may over-represent designers with strong opinions about AI. Cross-sectional design cannot show how individual attitudes change over time. Does not separate effects of Midjourney vs. DALL.E.
