# Catalyst for Creativity or a Hollow Trend?: A Cross-Level Perspective on The Role of Generative AI in Design


## Summary

## Summary
This paper takes a cross-level perspective on generative AI in creative work, examining AI's role at the individual, team, and organizational levels simultaneously to evaluate whether the "AI as creativity catalyst" narrative is substantiated or a hollow trend. The analysis finds that AI genuinely catalyzes creativity at the individual ideation level but introduces coordination challenges and creative friction at the team level, and creates new organizational tensions around authorship, intellectual property, and quality control. The paper argues for level-specific frameworks for evaluating and designing AI creativity support.

## Key Concepts
- Cross-level analysis of AI creativity effects
- Individual vs. team creativity with AI
- Organizational AI creativity management
- Authorship and intellectual property in AI-assisted creative work
- AI as creativity catalyst (critical evaluation)

## Theoretical Framework
Multi-level theory of creativity (Woodman, Sawyer & Griffin); organizational creativity research; human-AI collaboration; draws on creativity research spanning individual, team, and organizational levels.

## Methods
Multi-level qualitative and quantitative synthesis; case studies from creative organizations using AI; analysis of creativity outcomes at individual, team, and organizational levels; literature review across levels.

## Main Arguments
- AI's creativity benefits are real at the individual ideation level: it generates more diverse concepts and reduces blank page anxiety
- At the team level, AI can reduce collaborative negotiation and creative tension that is actually productive for team creativity
- At the organizational level, AI creates new governance challenges around authorship, quality standards, and creative culture
- Evaluating AI as a creativity tool requires level-specific analysis; a tool that catalyzes individual creativity may undermine team or organizational creative culture

## Limitations & Critiques
Cross-level analysis requires different data and methods at each level, creating integration challenges; the findings may be sector-specific; the creative quality outcome measures used across levels are not fully commensurable.
