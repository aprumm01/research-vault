# Generative AI and Creative Work: Narratives, Values, and Impacts (Caramiaux et al.)

## Abstract

Generative AI has gained a significant foothold in the creative and artistic sectors. In this context, the concept of creative work is influenced by discourses originating from technological stakeholders and mainstream media. The framing of narratives surrounding creativity and artistic production not only reflects a particular vision of culture but also actively contributes to shaping it. This article reviews online media outlets and industry discourse to analyze the narratives, values, and impacts associated with GenAI in creative work contexts.

## Summary

Caramiaux et al. argue that the narratives produced by tech companies and mainstream media around GenAI and creative work are not neutral descriptions but active shapers of how creativity, labor, and authorship are understood in AI-integrated practice. The paper uses critical discourse analysis of online media and industry sources to identify the values embedded in dominant narratives — efficiency, productivity, democratization — and examines how these frame both the promise and threat of AI to creative workers. The high-profile authorship (CNRS + Microsoft Research) signals this as a foundational critical text.

## Key Concepts
- Sociotechnical narratives (tech company discourse shaping cultural understanding)
- Creative labor and authorship under AI
- Values embedded in AI discourse (efficiency, democratization)
- Media framing of AI risk and opportunity

## Theoretical Framework
Critical discourse analysis (CDA) applied to technology narratives; draws on media studies, STS, and creative labor scholarship to examine how discourse constructs the meaning of AI in creative work.

## Methods
Review and analysis of online media outlets and industry publications; critical discourse analysis of how GenAI and creative work are framed, with attention to the values and power relations embedded in dominant narratives.

## Main Arguments
- Tech company narratives around GenAI and creativity are not neutral — they encode specific values that favor automation and efficiency
- Mainstream media amplifies these narratives, normalizing particular views of creative labor
- The framing of creative workers as safe from AI displacement is a construction that benefits technology developers
- Critical examination of narratives is necessary before they sediment into policy and professional norms
