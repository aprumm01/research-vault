# Sociotechnical Fictions The Performative Agencies of Fiction in Technological Development

## Summary
This paper coins and develops the notion of ‘sociotechnical fiction’: a type of fiction distinct from literary or cinematic forms, which operates within the technosciences to materialise non-existent, imaginary entities through the production of new technological assemblages Adopting a performative approach to actor-network theory, the research explores how these fictions mediate the continuum between matter and imagination, and between present and future, through a comparative analysis of related concepts such as future visions, promises, expectations, imaginaries, metaphors, and anticipatory practices ‘Sociotechnical fictions’ are thus defined as mediated forms of imagination that address the inherent uncertainty of future-oriented technological projects.

## Key Concepts
- Sociotechnical fiction as mediated forms of imagination in technological emergence
- Performative approach to actor-network theory (ANT)
- Fiction as quasi-object (neither objective nor subjective)
- Epistemic, aesthetic, affective, and normative agencies of fiction
- Fictional expectations in technology-driven capitalism
- Re-enchantment through transforming unknown into known

## Theoretical Framework
Performative actor-network theory (ANT); Fiction-ability as anthropological quality; Performativity (Austin, Butler, Barad); Quasi-objects (Latour)

## Methods
Literature review; Comparative analysis of STS concepts (future visions, promises, expectations, imaginaries, metaphors, anticipatory practices); Case analysis (metaverse, algorithmic counterfactuals, cloud, AI, Theranos, WeWork)

## Main Arguments
- Sociotechnical fictions operate within technosciences to materialise imaginary entities through new technological assemblages
- Mediate continuum between matter/imagination and present/future
- Often unrecognised as fiction, deeply entangled with rational and instrumental practices
- Connect anticipatory dimension of technology with its legitimacy
- Four key agencies: Epistemic (render emerging entities intelligible, resolve ambiguities, create consensus, alleviate uncertainty), Aesthetic (challenge boundaries of possible, stimulate novelty, spark curiosity), Affective (engage with uncertainty and anticipation, intertwine with emotions), Normative (frame expectations, create paths to follow, orient decisions about feasible/necessary/profitable/desirable)
- Play role in re-enchanting expert and non-expert actors
- Can attract attention and resources but also become epistemically toxic

## Limitations & Critiques
- Fiction has been banished from modern truth-producing apparatus despite being part of the real
- Dichotomy between imagination and "objective, hard truths" makes it slippery to validate fiction's presence in rational knowledge production
- Fiction relegated to same place as art: admired yet regarded as reprehensible
- Waning of religious intensity compensated by emergence of alternative mythologies
- Fictionality not immediately apparent in non-literary contexts
- Future is indeterminate and uncertainty cannot be completely dispelled using instrumental reason
- Agencies of sociotechnical fictions unevenly distributed among actors
- Potential to produce certain technological realities over others (politics of fiction)
- Can become epistemically toxic, undermining legitimacy of industrial sectors or research areas
