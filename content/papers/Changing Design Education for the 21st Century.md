# Changing Design Education for the 21st Century

## Summary
Don Norman and Michael Meyer argue that design education has failed to keep pace with the expanding scope and complexity of 21st-century design challenges. Drawing parallels to the professionalization of business and medical education, they propose that design schools must evolve from craft-based studio traditions toward more rigorous academic foundations while maintaining practical relevance. The paper introduces the "Eleven Design Challenges" framework categorizing problems at Performance, Systemic, Contextual, and Global levels, and argues that most design education only addresses the first category.

## Key Concepts
- **Human-Centered Design (HCD)**: Design focused on people, their needs, and societal impact
- **Design Thinking**: Popular but often superficial approach to applying design methods
- **DesignX**: Framework for addressing complex sociotechnical systems
- **Performance Challenges**: Technical design problems requiring traditional craft skills
- **Systemic Challenges**: Problems requiring systems thinking and cross-disciplinary collaboration
- **Contextual Challenges**: Problems embedded in specific social, cultural, and political contexts
- **Global Challenges**: Problems addressing major societal issues like sustainability and inequality
- **Bauhaus**: Historical model for design education that authors both honor and critique

## Theoretical Framework
The authors draw on professionalization theory, comparing design's evolution to the historical development of business education (particularly the 1959 Ford/Carnegie Foundation reports) and medical education (the 1910 Flexner Report). They argue that design must undergo similar transformation from trade-based training to academic discipline while preserving its unique practical orientation.

## Methods
- Comparative analysis of professional education models
- Case studies of design schools (RISD, Parsons, SCAD, Carnegie Mellon)
- Curriculum analysis
- Historical review of Bauhaus influence
- Framework development (Eleven Design Challenges)

## Main Arguments
1. **Education Lags Practice**: Design schools still train for 20th-century problems while practice has moved to complex systems
2. **Craft Orientation Insufficient**: Traditional studio pedagogy develops skills but not broader competencies needed today
3. **Eleven Challenges Framework**: Design problems exist at escalating levels of complexity requiring different skill sets
4. **Business School Model**: Design education should learn from how business schools balance academic rigor and practical relevance
5. **Research Universities Superior**: Schools within research universities provide richer educational experiences than stand-alone design schools
6. **Core + Specialization**: Curriculum should provide common foundation with deep specialization options
7. **Design Thinking Oversold**: Popular design thinking workshops trivialize the discipline's depth

## Limitations & Critiques
- Perspective from elite institutions may not generalize to most design programs
- Business school comparison may import problematic corporate values
- Does not address resource constraints most design programs face
- Dismissive of craft traditions that remain valuable
- Western/American focus with limited global perspective
- Limited engagement with critical design scholarship
- Does not address how to teach systemic and global challenges specifically
- May undervalue tacit knowledge developed through studio practice
