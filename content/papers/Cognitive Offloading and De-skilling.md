# Cognitive Offloading and De-skilling


## Summary
Cognitive Offloading and De-skilling is a thematic node examining the consequences of delegating cognitive tasks to AI tools -- both the efficiency gains of offloading routine work and the risks of eroding the skills and tacit knowledge that come from practicing those tasks. In this research vault, the node is central to understanding practitioner concerns about GenAI in design and UX work: papers in the corpus document how designers and developers fear that over-reliance on AI for ideation, prototyping, and coding may weaken creative judgment, design reasoning, and foundational craft skills -- particularly for junior practitioners who need productive struggle to develop expertise.

## Key Concepts
- Cognitive offloading: using external tools to reduce working memory demands
- De-skilling: erosion of practical skill through reduced practice and automation
- Extended mind thesis: cognition as distributed across brain, body, and environment
- Productive struggle: the role of difficulty in skill and knowledge development
- Junior vs. senior designer vulnerability to AI-driven de-skilling
- Creativity exhaustion as a downstream effect of AI-accelerated workflows

## Theoretical Framework
Cognitive science (Clark and Chalmers' extended mind; Risko and Gilbert on cognitive offloading); labor studies (Braverman's de-skilling thesis updated for AI); design cognition research; connected in this vault to GenAI in UX and Design Practice, Human-AI Creative Collaboration, and vibe coding debates.
