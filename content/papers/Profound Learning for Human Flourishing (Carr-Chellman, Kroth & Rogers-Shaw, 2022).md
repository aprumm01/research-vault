# Profound Learning for Human Flourishing

## Summary
This chapter introduces Profound Learning (PL) as a framework for instructional design that goes beyond skill acquisition to promote human flourishing. The authors argue that traditional instructional design has become overly technical and mechanistic, treating learners like machines to be programmed rather than humans seeking meaning and purpose. Drawing on ancient philosophical traditions from Aristotle and Aquinas as well as modern thinkers like Martha Nussbaum and Alasdair MacIntyre, they propose that learning should be understood within the broader context of what it means to flourish as a human being.

The chapter presents PL as characterized by deep reflection, gaining substantive insights, meaningful learning experiences, perspective changing, relationship to self and others, depth of knowledge, experiencing the vastness beyond assumptions, reevaluation of ideas and values, paradigm changing, and consequential effects on the learner's life. The authors integrate PL with Universal Design for Learning (UDL) principles and the ADDIE model, providing practical questions instructional designers can ask to promote profound learning experiences.

## Key Concepts
- **Profound Learning (PL)**: A formative process emphasizing learner commitment to practices across time that transform, deform, and reform the learner through intentionality
- **Human Flourishing**: The actualization of human purposes through recognizing, pursuing, and fulfilling one's purposes in life; not a static state but an ongoing activity
- **Identity Formation**: The overall process of human learning constituted by transformation, deformation, and reformation
- **Intentionality**: The interface between subjective mental states and the objective world; the origin of learning and foundation of human experience

## Theoretical Framework
The framework draws on Aristotelian virtue ethics, Thomas Aquinas's moral philosophy, and contemporary human flourishing literature (Nussbaum, MacIntyre). It positions learning as intrinsically connected to human development and purpose, rather than as isolated skill acquisition. The authors connect PL to Universal Design for Learning (UDL) guidelines from CAST (2018) and integrate with the ADDIE instructional design model.

## Methods
This is a theoretical/conceptual chapter that synthesizes philosophical traditions with instructional design practice. The authors present tables mapping PL practices to UDL guidelines and ADDIE phases with guiding questions.

## Main Arguments
- Learning design should never abstract instructional events from the ultimate purpose of human flourishing
- Profound Learning operationalizes through five processes: Identity Formation, Transformation, Deformation, Reformation, and Intentionality
- There is infinite knowledge and learning is never-ending; instructional practices must seek to open up and continuously reveal rather than close down
- Lifelong learning is a continuous formation process, not a set of discrete learning events
- Instructional designers should design for three levels of impact: organizational effectiveness, individual skill development, and personal change/flourishing

## Limitations & Critiques
- The framework is highly philosophical and may be challenging to operationalize in corporate training contexts focused on measurable outcomes
- The emphasis on human flourishing may conflict with utilitarian organizational demands for efficiency
- Implementation requires significant paradigm shift from traditional ID practices
