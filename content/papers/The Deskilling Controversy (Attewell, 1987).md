# The Deskilling Controversy (Attewell, 1987)

## Summary
Reviews and critiques Harry Braverman's influential deskilling thesis—that capitalism systematically degrades and deskills work—through theoretical, empirical, and methodological analysis. Uses the computerization of clerical work as a central case to demonstrate that qualitative studies of specific occupations and quantitative industry-wide analyses can yield opposite conclusions, exposing fundamental methodological weaknesses in the deskilling approach.

## Key Concepts
- Deskilling thesis (Braverman)
- Babbage Principle
- Division of labor and management control
- Computerization of clerical work
- Qualitative vs. quantitative methods in labor research

## Theoretical Framework
Engages critically with Braverman's Labor and Monopoly Capital (1974); applies both Marxist and stratification theory lenses; central contribution is methodological: showing how research design shapes conclusions about skill change.

## Methods
Critical literature review; analysis of existing deskilling studies; detailed case analysis of clerical work computerization in the insurance industry; comparison of qualitative case study findings with industry-wide quantitative data.

## Main Arguments
- Braverman overstates the uniformity, pace, and extent of skill degradation across the US economy
- Computerization can simultaneously deskill some roles and upskill or create others; net effects are sector-specific
- Qualitative case studies of specific occupations tend to support deskilling claims while quantitative industry data often shows the reverse—the methodological choice is not neutral
- The Babbage Principle and management control motive are real but insufficient to explain all work reorganization

## Limitations & Critiques
Review paper without original primary data; dated 1987 and predates internet-era automation; focus on US clerical work limits coverage of professional, managerial, or service sector dynamics; does not address the political economy of skill measurement itself.
