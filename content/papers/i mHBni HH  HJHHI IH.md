# Designerly Ways of Knowing (Cross, 1982)

## Summary
Cross's landmark paper establishes "designerly ways of knowing" as a distinct cognitive mode alongside scientific and humanistic ways of knowing, arguing that design is not merely applied science or aesthetics but a fundamental third culture with its own phenomena, epistemology, and values. The paper identifies the "designerly" as characterized by modeling ambiguous problems, using drawings and prototypes as thinking tools, and working with "solution-focused" rather than "problem-focused" strategies. It is foundational for arguments that design deserves its own educational epistemology rather than borrowing from scientific or humanistic traditions.

## Key Concepts
- Designerly ways of knowing (third culture alongside science and humanities)
- Ill-defined problems and solution-focused strategy
- Abductive reasoning and design cognition
- Sketching and prototyping as epistemic tools
- Design as a discipline with its own epistemology

## Theoretical Framework
Philosophy of design cognition; draws on C.P. Snow's two cultures argument, Horst Rittel's wicked problems, and early design methods movement to position design as an autonomous intellectual culture.

## Methods
Conceptual argument based on analysis of design research, design education, and comparisons with scientific problem-solving; no empirical data collection.

## Main Arguments
- Design has its own ways of knowing that cannot be reduced to science or the humanities
- Designers approach problems as ill-defined and use the solution to progressively reframe the problem
- Models, sketches, and prototypes are the primary media through which designers think, not just communicate
- Design education should cultivate designerly cognition, not import scientific method

## Limitations & Critiques
The framework can romanticize design cognition and understate the diversity of practices within design; later empirical design cognition research (Schön, Cross's own work) nuanced the original claims considerably.
