Conference proceedings compilation; papers employ diverse methodologies:
- Participatory design frameworks and case studies

- Tactile interaction studies

## Main Arguments
- Generative AI requires process-centric design education that prioritizes human creativity and experience expertise over tool proficiency
- Participatory design in early ideation stages ensures meaningful stakeholder involvement and improves collaborative outcomes
- Sequential approaches to teaching generative AI (traditional skills first, then AI tools) better preserve design fundamentals and aesthetic sensibilities
- HCI professionals face systemic barriers to adopting ethics practices despite recognizing their importance
- Human-centered design principles must adapt to emerging technologies while maintaining focus on user needs and experiences
- Design education must respond to rapid technological evolution by developing adaptable skillsets rather than technology-specific training
- Sustainable design principles can be effectively integrated from education through professional practice

## Limitations & Critiques
- Slow adoption of ethics practices among HCI professionals despite awareness, indicating implementation gaps
- Generative AI integration challenges in maintaining human creativity primacy
- Need for longitudinal studies on AI-enhanced design education outcomes
- Limited consensus on best practices for integrating emerging technologies in design curricula
- Difficulty ensuring meaningful participation in participatory design frameworks
- Challenges in measuring design capability development beyond technical skills
- Gap between design education principles and real-world professional constraints
- Need for more empirical validation of proposed educational frameworks
- Cultural and contextual variations in design education approaches underexplored
