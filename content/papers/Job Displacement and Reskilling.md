# Job Displacement and Reskilling


## Summary
Job Displacement and Reskilling is a thematic node examining the labor market consequences of AI automation -- both the displacement of existing roles and the demand for new skills and competencies in an AI-augmented economy. In this research vault, the node connects macro-level labor economics arguments to practitioner-level accounts of how workers in design, UX, and knowledge work are experiencing and anticipating career disruption. Papers address historical precedents (technological unemployment debates), current evidence on which roles and skills are most vulnerable, and what reskilling pathways look like for workers whose routines are being automated. The node is also relevant to design education curricula that must prepare graduates for an AI-transformed profession.

## Key Concepts
- Technological unemployment vs. job transformation vs. job creation
- Task-level vs. job-level analysis of AI displacement risk
- Skill complementarity: which human capabilities remain valuable alongside AI
- Reskilling and upskilling: organizational and policy responses to displacement
- Entry-level and junior worker vulnerability under AI augmentation
- Design education as a site for developing AI-proof skills and competencies

## Theoretical Framework
Labor economics (Autor, Frank, Acemoglu); historical technology-and-work scholarship; HPT and training perspectives on reskilling; situated in this vault between AI and Future of Work, Hybrid Intelligence, and design education communities; draws on both quantitative labor research and qualitative practitioner studies.
