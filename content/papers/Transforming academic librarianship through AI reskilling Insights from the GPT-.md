# Transforming academic librarianship through AI reskilling Insights from the GPT-4 exploration program

## Summary
The Journal of Academic Librarianship 50 (2024) 102883 Contents lists available at ScienceDirect The Journal of Academic Librarianship journal homepage: www com/locate/jacalib Transforming academic librarianship through AI reskilling: Insights from the GPT-4 exploration program Leo S Lo University of New Mexico, United States of America A R T I C L E I N F O A B S T R A C T Keywords: Academic librarianship Artificial Intelligence (AI) Reskilling.

## Key Concepts
- AI literacy and reskilling for academic librarians
- Malcolm Knowles' Adult Learning Principles
- Self-directed learning and problem-centered orientation
- GPT-4 vs. ChatGPT (GPT-3.5) capabilities
- Internal motivation and readiness to learn
- Hands-on experimentation and collaborative learning
- Three-phase program structure (Introductory, Exploratory, Evaluation)

## Theoretical Framework
Grounded in Knowles' Adult Learning Principles: (1) self-directed learning, (2) leveraging learners' experiences, (3) readiness to learn, (4) problem-centered orientation, (5) internal motivation, (6) understanding reason for learning. Applied to design AI reskilling initiative responsive to adult learners' needs.

## Methods
Case study of 10-participant, three-month GPT-4 Exploration Program at University of New Mexico College of University Libraries and Learning Sciences (June-September 2023). Mixed-methods evaluation combining pre/post surveys measuring AI familiarity and qualitative participant documentation of projects and experiences.

## Main Arguments
- Less than 7% of academic library employees pay for premium generative AI tools; those who pay have higher AI literacy
- Effective AI reskilling requires cultivating continuous learning culture, adaptability, and collaborative exploration through practical hands-on approach
- Participants' AI familiarity increased from 2.36/5 to 3.63/5, demonstrating significant skill improvement
- Program successfully applied across diverse use cases: publishing workflows, data management plans, patron communication, cataloging/metadata, information studies education, technical documentation, hallucination investigation
- Participants valued hands-on learning and community of practice equally
- Self-directed, problem-centered approach with diverse participants enabled peer learning and relevant skill development
- AI reskilling must address technical aspects plus ethical, legal, and social implications
- Program model is scalable and adaptable for institutions of various sizes and resource levels

## Limitations & Critiques
- Small sample size (10 participants) from single institution limits generalizability
- Short timeframe (3 months) may not capture long-term retention or sustained practice changes
- Self-reported survey data subject to social desirability bias
- Pre-program AI familiarity (2.36) lower than general population (2.71), possibly indicating selection bias
- No control group to compare against alternative training approaches
- Program funded GPT-4 subscriptions, raising sustainability questions for resource-constrained institutions
- Does not examine whether increased AI literacy translates to improved library service outcomes
- Limited discussion of participants who may have struggled or dropped out
