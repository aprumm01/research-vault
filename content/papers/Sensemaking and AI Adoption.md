# Sensemaking and AI Adoption


## Summary
Sensemaking and AI Adoption is a thematic node examining how individuals and organizations make sense of generative AI tools as they integrate them into existing practices -- the interpretive work of understanding what AI can and cannot do, how to use it well, and how to fit it into established workflows and professional identities. In this research vault, the node connects to workplace learning and AI adoption studies, where workers are not passive recipients of AI tools but active sensemakers who adapt, resist, and reshape AI use based on their prior knowledge, professional values, and organizational context. Sensemaking frameworks help explain why AI adoption is uneven, contested, and often surprising.

## Key Concepts
- Sensemaking (Weick): how people retrospectively interpret ambiguous situations and tools
- Enactment: how users' actions shape the technologies they adopt
- Frames and mental models: prior understanding that shapes AI interpretation and use
- Organizational sensemaking of AI: collective interpretation and negotiation
- Resistance and workarounds: when sensemaking leads to non-adoption or modification
- AI adoption as a learning process: building accurate mental models of AI capabilities

## Theoretical Framework
Organizational sensemaking (Weick); enactment theory; organizational learning (Argyris, Schon); technology adoption models (Davis TAM; Orlikowski's structuration); situated in this vault between Workplace Learning and AI and AI and Future of Work communities; draws on qualitative organizational and HCI research.
