## Summary
J. Michael Spector's chapter provides an overview of the theoretical foundations of educational communications and technology, identifying four core areas: psychology of learning, communications theory, systems theory, and curriculum/instruction theory. He argues that the field's identity depends on maintaining engagement with these theoretical foundations rather than becoming purely practice-driven, and examines how theoretical debates shape research directions and practical applications.

## Key Concepts
- Theoretical foundations of educational technology
- Psychology of learning
- Communications theory
- Systems theory
- Curriculum and instruction

## Theoretical Framework
Spector synthesizes learning theory, communications theory, and systems thinking as the interdisciplinary foundations that give educational technology its intellectual coherence.

## Methods
Theoretical synthesis and review; analysis of foundational theories across four domains and their implications for educational technology research and practice.

## Main Arguments
- Educational technology requires grounding in multiple theoretical domains beyond technical applications
- Psychology of learning provides the core framework for understanding what educational technology must support
- Systems theory enables analysis of complex educational environments and technology integration
- The field's coherence and legitimacy depend on maintaining theoretical rigor alongside practical innovation
