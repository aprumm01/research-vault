# Conceptions of Design Practice From Academy to Industry: Implications for HCI and Design Education

## Summary

Through 21 semi-structured interviews with HCI/design students, educators, and practitioners, this study identifies three conceptions of professional design practice that represent increasingly sophisticated understandings: Artifact-Centered, Human-Centered, and Cross-Functional. These conceptions describe how designers understand their role, goals, values, and competency at different stages of professional development.

## Key Concepts

- **Conception of Practice**: How a designer understands what design is, what designers do, and what constitutes competency
- **Artifact-Centered Design**: Design defined by output quality; competency = craft skill
- **Human-Centered Design**: Design defined by user impact; competency = identifying and addressing user needs
- **Cross-Functional Design**: Design within organizational context; competency = facilitating human-centered process amid business constraints
- **Design Complexity**: The multifaceted challenges practitioners face navigating competing demands

## Theoretical Framework

Draws on practice-led research tradition in HCI (Stolterman) and developmental models of designer competency (Gray et al., Sosa-Tzec). Positions conceptions as analytical tools for understanding designer development from education through professional practice.

## Methods

- **Participants**: 21 interviewees (9 students, 8 professionals, 4 educators) from US HCI/design programs
- **Sampling**: Email recruitment + snowball sampling through LinkedIn and professional networks
- **Interviews**: 45-90 minutes via Zoom, recorded and transcribed
- **Analysis**: 
  - Analytic memoing (46 memos across 9 interviews)
  - Open and in-vivo coding (6 interviews)
  - Concept mapping for each participant
  - Codebook with 7 codes applied to all 21 interviews
- **Focus**: Subset of 21 (8 professionals, 9 students, 4 educators) who were former students of design/HCI programs

## Main Arguments

**Three Conceptions of Design Practice:**

| Conception | Role | Goal | Values | Competency |
|------------|------|------|--------|------------|
| **Artifact-Centered** | Craftsperson producing artifacts | High-quality outputs | Aesthetics, technical skill | Skill at craft |
| **Human-Centered** | Creator serving user needs | Usable, impactful artifacts | Usability, empathy, impact | Identifying user needs, designing for them |
| **Cross-Functional** | Facilitator within organization | Feasible human-centered designs | Business impact, collaboration | Navigating constraints, stakeholder buy-in |

**Key Insights:**

1. **Developmental Progression**: Conceptions build cumulatively - professionals don't abandon earlier conceptions but integrate them into more sophisticated understanding

2. **Student Experience Impact**: Students holding only Artifact-Centered conception often feel design "isn't for them" - identifying as craft vs. research-oriented

3. **Transition Challenges**: Moving from Human-Centered to Cross-Functional often involves difficult "keystone moments" where user research gets rejected for business reasons

4. **Educational Implications**: Programs should help students develop realistic expectations about practice complexity and provide experiences across all three conceptions

5. **Workplace Tensions**: Professionals in Artifact-Centered workplaces while holding Human-Centered values experience significant dissatisfaction

## Limitations

- US-centric sample limits cultural generalizability
- Self-selected participants may be more reflective about practice
- Cross-sectional design can't track actual developmental progression
- Interview method relies on self-report and recall
- Limited to design/HCI programs; may not apply to self-taught designers
- Three conceptions may oversimplify complex professional identities
- Educator perspective less developed (only 4 participants)
