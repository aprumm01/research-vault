# Design Judgment


## Summary
Design Judgment is a thematic node capturing research on the expert cognitive and evaluative capacities that distinguish skilled designers -- the ability to make appropriate decisions about design quality, appropriateness, and direction under conditions of uncertainty and incomplete information. In this research vault, the node appears primarily in the context of AI's challenge to design judgment: papers ask whether AI tools support or supplant designers' evaluative reasoning, and whether less experienced designers can develop genuine design judgment when AI accelerates and scaffolds ideation without demanding the productive struggle that builds expertise. Design judgment is also central to discussions of what human designers uniquely contribute as AI handles more execution tasks.

## Key Concepts
- Design judgment as expert evaluative capacity under uncertainty
- Distinction between design judgment and design execution
- Tacit knowledge and experiential learning in developing design judgment
- AI as challenging vs. supporting the cultivation of design judgment
- Reflective practice (Schon) as the process through which judgment develops
- Design criticism and critique as social sites for developing and testing judgment

## Theoretical Framework
Design cognition research (Cross, Lawson, Schon); expertise studies (Dreyfus and Dreyfus stages); tacit knowledge (Polanyi); studio pedagogy as the educational site where design judgment is cultivated; connected in this vault to Design Theory and Cognition, HCI Education and Pedagogy, and AI in Design Education communities.
