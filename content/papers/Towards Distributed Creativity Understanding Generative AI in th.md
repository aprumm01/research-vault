# Towards Distributed Creativity Understanding Generative AI in th

## Summary
This study1 examines the transformative role of generative AI (GenAI) in design through the lens of the material turn, which foregrounds materiality’s agency in shaping human practices In this context, GenAI is not a mere tool but an active participant in co-creation, challenging subject-object distinctions.

## Key Concepts
- **Material Turn in Design Philosophy**: Shift from concepts, language, and discourse to physical objects, materials, and entanglements that shape human experience, knowledge, and action beyond human control
- **GenAI as Active Participant Not Tool**: Generative AI positioned as active participant in co-creation rather than mere tool, challenging subject-object distinctions in design philosophy
- **Distributed Agency**: Move beyond anthropocentric and individualistic interpretations to recognize non-human entities (algorithms, materials, objects) as "participants" in world-building processes
- **Ontological Designing**: "We design our world, while our world acts back on us and designs us" - design constitutes act of redesigning ourselves through creation of external systems and prostheses
- **Relational Ontologies**: Subject and object always constituted in relation to one another, making them inseparable and incomprehensible as isolated entities; any change transforms both in tandem
- **Creativity as Emergent Phenomenon**: Creativity understood as distributed process emerging from dynamic interactions between agents within sociomaterial networks rather than individual human attribute
- **Designer Role Shift from Control to Orchestration**: Designer's role transforms from controlling creator to orchestrator of adaptive interactions within relational systems

## Theoretical Framework
- **Material Turn (Coole & Frost, Bennett & Joyce)**: Emphasizes embedded and embodied nature of experience, highlighting active role of materiality in tangible and concrete form
- **Actor-Network Theory (Latour)**: Explores co-constructive assemblages involving human and non-human actants including technologies and objects
- **Postphenomenology (Ihde)**: Focuses on embodied, experiential, and relational dimension of human-technology interactions
- **4E Cognition**: Frames cognition as embodied, extended, embedded and enacted, emphasizing dynamic interactions between mind, body, and environment
- **New Materialism**: Argues for agency, vitality, and interconnectedness of matter, challenging traditional dualisms

## Main Arguments
- **Material Turn Offers Critical Lens for GenAI**: Material turn provides relevant framework for examining role of GenAI in design by highlighting importance of relational and ontological frameworks
- **Beyond Subject- and Object-Centered Paradigms**: Integration of GenAI into design practice demands move beyond traditional paradigms that place designer, user, or designed object at center
- **Creativity as Sociomaterial Emergence**: Creativity in GenAI context should be understood as distributed process emerging from dynamic interactions between agents within sociomaterial networks
- **Relational Process Replaces Artifact Focus**: Design reveals itself as relational process where creativity unfolds through adaptive interactions rather than production of fixed artifacts
- **Bidirectional Human-Machine Constitution**: Feedback loops through which human and non-human agents mutually constitute each other over time create new, evolving creative ecosystem

## Limitations & Critiques
- **Theoretical Investigation Not Methodological Guide**: Paper explicitly does not present design methodology or technical guide for incorporating GenAI, limiting practical applicability for practitioners
- **Phenomenological Methods Lack Empirical Validation**: Theoretical claims about distributed agency and relational ontologies not supported by empirical studies of actual designer-GenAI interactions
- **Overemphasis on Philosophical Abstraction**: Heavy focus on ontological and philosophical considerations may obscure concrete challenges of integrating GenAI into existing design workflows
- **Limited Treatment of Power and Control**: While discussing decentering, paper underexplores how distributed agency might mask concentration of power in algorithmic systems and platform owners
- **Unclear Boundaries of Agency Attribution**: Framework does not specify criteria for when and how to attribute agency to non-human entities, risking anthropomorphization of computational systems
- **Missing Comparison with Traditional Design**: Lack of comparative analysis between GenAI-mediated design and conventional design processes limits ability to assess claimed transformations
- **Sociomaterial Networks Underspecified**: While positing creativity emerges from sociomaterial networks, paper does not detail mechanisms, scales, or temporal dynamics of these emergent processes
- **Designer as Orchestrator Role Vague**: Shift from control to orchestration described but not operationalized, leaving unclear what specific practices, skills, or responsibilities this entails
