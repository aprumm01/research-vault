# Landscapes of Sociotechnical Imaginaries in Education (Mamlok, 2024)

## Summary
Theoretical examination of how sociotechnical imaginaries operate in educational contexts, drawing on Jasanoff and Hasse to argue that the push to integrate AI in education is itself a sociotechnical imaginary that conditions institutional and pedagogical responses before any specific technology is adopted. Proposes 'Socratic ignorance'—a deliberate epistemic humility about AI's presumed educational benefits—as a conceptual counterweight to uncritical adoption.

## Key Concepts
- **Sociotechnical imaginaries in education**: How shared visions of AI's educational benefits shape policy and practice pre-adoption
- **Hasse's framework**: Cultural-historical approach to technology-in-education that foregrounds material and practice dimensions
- **Socratic ignorance**: Deliberate acknowledgment of what we do not know about AI in learning as an epistemic stance

## Main Arguments
1. The integration of AI in education is driven by sociotechnical imaginaries as much as by evidence of pedagogical benefit
2. Jasanoff and Hasse offer complementary frameworks for analyzing educational AI imaginaries
3. Socratic ignorance is a productive institutional posture for navigating uncertainty about AI in learning
