# Professional Ethics and Rules of Conduct in Instructional Design (Smaldino, Donaldson & Herring, 2012)


## Abstract
*Abstract not yet found — search by title or check source PDF*

## Summary
Smaldino, Donaldson, and Herring's chapter examines the professional ethics obligations of instructional designers, arguing that practitioners often lack awareness of or formal preparation in the ethical standards that govern their field. The authors review AECT and ISPI ethics codes, examine common ethical dilemmas in ID practice, and argue for integrating ethics education into instructional design preparation programs. The chapter positions ethical competence as a core professional skill, not an optional add-on.

## Key Concepts
- AECT Code of Ethics
- ISPI ethical standards
- Informed consent and transparency in instructional design
- Intellectual property and copyright in educational materials
- Conflict of interest in client-practitioner relationships

## Theoretical Framework
Professional ethics applied to instructional design; draws on codes from related professions and educational ethics literature to develop an ID-specific ethical framework.

## Methods
Review and analysis of existing ethics codes (AECT, ISPI); case-based ethical reasoning; normative argument for integrating ethics in ID preparation programs.

## Main Arguments
- Many practicing instructional designers lack formal ethics training, creating gaps in professional conduct
- Ethical dilemmas in ID (client vs. learner interests, intellectual property, representation of effectiveness) are common but rarely discussed in professional literature
- Professional codes provide a starting point, but practitioners need judgment skills to navigate ambiguous situations
- Ethics education should be woven throughout ID preparation, not confined to a single course

## Limitations & Critiques
The chapter's focus on professional codes may underemphasize critical perspectives on whose interests ID serves; the treatment of cultural and equity dimensions of ethics is limited.
