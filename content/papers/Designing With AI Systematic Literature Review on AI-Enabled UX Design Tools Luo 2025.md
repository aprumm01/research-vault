# Designing With AI: A Systematic Literature Review on the Use, Development, and Perception of AI-Enabled UX Design Tools

## Summary
This systematic literature review analyses 83 empirical studies (from 11,638 initial candidates, 2000–2024) across ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, and Scopus to map how AI is used, developed, and perceived in UX design practice. The review finds that AI is most commonly applied to evaluative activities (usability testing, user research analysis), that generative AI is increasingly used for ideation and prototyping, and that the field suffers from a technology-first development orientation that neglects designer needs. The paper identifies over-reliance risks and calls for more human-centered AI tool design.

## Key Concepts
- **Designing With AI vs. Designing for AI**: The paper focuses on AI as a support tool in the design process (designing with AI), not on designing AI products — a distinction clarified to address ambiguity in prior literature
- **Predictive AI in UX Design**: AI using historical data patterns (regression, classification, decision trees) to predict user satisfaction, analyse usability testing, and assess new product ideas — the dominant form in the literature
- **Generative AI in UX Design**: AI generating novel content (text, images, prototypes) to support brainstorming, visual communication, and UX writing — rapidly growing in relevance since 2022
- **Design Thinking Model**: The paper uses the Hasso-Plattner five-stage model (Empathize/Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test) to map AI usage across the UX design process, finding AI concentrated in evaluation and research activities
- **Overreliance Risk**: A recurring concern in the literature — designers fear that AI may disempower creativity and critical thinking, particularly for junior designers who might develop prompting skills at the expense of design reasoning
- **Technology-First Development Orientation**: The dominant approach in AI design tool development focuses on technical performance (model accuracy, dataset size) rather than understanding designer needs, workflows, and contextual appropriateness
- **Human-Centered AI Tool Design**: The paper's normative recommendation — future AI design tools should be developed with designer involvement, focusing on creativity support, contextual appropriateness, and preservation of designer agency

## Theoretical Framework
The paper adopts the UX design process as its structural framework, operationalised through the Hasso-Plattner Design Thinking model and IDEO's grouped version. This allows systematic mapping of where in the design process different AI applications appear, enabling identification of gaps and imbalances. The review uses a two-category classification of AI technologies (predictive and generative) to trace the historical evolution of AI's role in design and highlight how generative AI has disrupted prior patterns.

The theoretical backdrop also draws on Shneiderman's Human-Centered AI principles, which warn against overreliance on automation and the risk of user behaviour manipulation. The paper situates its findings within debates about AI's dual role: as efficiency enhancer (automating repetitive tasks, processing large data sets) and as potential creativity displacer (replacing the divergent, exploratory thinking central to design practice).

## Methods
Systematic literature review following a grounded theory literature review approach. Initial search across ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, and Scopus (2000–2024) yielded 11,638 papers; after iterative screening and relevance assessment, 83 empirical studies were retained for full analysis. Papers were coded along three dimensions: use (which design activities AI supports), development (how AI tools are built and evaluated), and perception (how designers experience and evaluate AI tools). The grounded theory approach allowed themes to emerge inductively from the coded data rather than being imposed a priori.

## Main Arguments
- AI in UX design is predominantly used for evaluative and research activities (user behaviour analysis, usability testing, A/B testing automation), with ideation and prototyping support growing but still secondary — revealing an asymmetric distribution of AI's role across the design process
- Generative AI tools (ChatGPT, Midjourney) have been adopted by UX practitioners primarily for brainstorming, visual communication, and UX writing, but their integration into formal design research or user testing workflows is still limited
- The reviewed literature reveals a significant technology-first orientation: most AI design tools are developed without involving designers in the design process, with evaluation metrics focused on technical performance rather than designer experience or contextual fit
- Overreliance risk is identified as a cross-cutting concern: designers express worry that AI may replace divergent creative thinking, particularly for novices who may develop AI-prompting fluency rather than foundational design skills
- There is a mismatch between what AI design tools currently deliver (technical automation of narrow tasks) and what designers and researchers envision (AI that supports creativity, co-creation, and contextual reasoning)
- The paper calls for a future research agenda that re-centres human values in AI design tool development: involving designers as co-creators, focusing on AI tools that augment rather than replace judgment, and attending to the social and organisational contexts of design work

## Limitations & Critiques
As a systematic review, the paper is limited to what is published in the selected databases, which may under-represent practitioner knowledge and recent industrial developments. The 2000–2024 search window means rapidly evolving generative AI applications (particularly post-2022) are captured only in the most recent portion of the sample. The use of grounded theory for a systematic review introduces methodological tension; the paper acknowledges this but may be susceptible to reviewer subjectivity in coding. The UX design field is broad and the review's focus on HCI literature may not capture usage patterns in adjacent design disciplines (graphic design, industrial design, service design).
