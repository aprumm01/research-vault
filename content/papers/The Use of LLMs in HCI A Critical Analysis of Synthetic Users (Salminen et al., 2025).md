# The Use of Large Language Models in HCI: A Critical Analysis of Synthetic Users (Salminen et al., 2025)

## Summary
Salminen, Amin, Jung, and Jansen present a conceptual critique of synthetic users -- AI entities generated by LLMs that aim to emulate human behavior and interactions -- whose adoption is growing in industry despite widespread researcher skepticism. The paper investigates the root causes driving synthetic user adoption, maps potential use cases, and identifies key methodological and epistemological risks. The authors find that synthetic users emerge from legitimate pressures in user research (cost, privacy, recruitment constraints), but present fundamental problems: normative biases, inability to generate novel insights, and epistemological incompatibility with the foundational premise of user-centered design (UCD). Rather than dismissing synthetic users, the paper frames their proliferation as a symptom of underlying structural problems in HCI research that demand attention.

## Key Concepts
- Synthetic users: LLM-generated entities used as replacements or supplements for human research participants
- Epistemological challenge: synthetic user data is normative (average) rather than phenomenological (particular)
- Industry adoption pressure: 69% of market researchers have used synthetic data; 70% predict it will exceed 50% of all data in 3 years
- Value-action gap: LLMs can state user values but cannot enact genuine user behavior
- Structural HCI problems: cost, privacy, recruitment difficulty as underlying drivers of synthetic user adoption
- "User research without the user": the paradoxical framing used by commercial synthetic user platforms

## Theoretical Framework
Conceptual/critical analysis; user-centered design principles and epistemology; HCI research methodology literature; positions against the growing synthetic user industry (e.g., Synthetic Users platform); builds on prior critical HCI work questioning LLM simulation validity; presented at Augmented Humans International Conference 2025 (AHs 2025, Abu Dhabi).

## Methods
Conceptual inquiry: literature review and critical analysis rather than empirical study; maps use cases for synthetic users across HCI research stages; identifies key risks through theoretical analysis; draws on industry reports, prior empirical findings, and epistemological frameworks in research methodology; short conference paper (5 pages).

## Main Arguments
- Synthetic users are not merely a technical solution but a symptom: their adoption reveals real structural problems in HCI research -- funding constraints, ethical approval burdens, recruitment costs, and privacy regulations -- that would persist even if synthetic users were perfect
- The fundamental epistemological problem is that synthetic users produce normative biases: they output averages, consensus, and socially acceptable responses rather than the particular, surprising, idiosyncratic, and novel insights that real users provide -- the very insights that make user research valuable
- For usability testing and user interviews specifically, synthetic users are most problematic: these methods depend on observing authentic behavior under realistic conditions, not LLM-generated plausible behavior
- Rather than dismissing synthetic users as simply bad, HCI should treat their emergence as a diagnostic signal and address the underlying structural problems that make them attractive

## Limitations & Critiques
Conceptual paper with no primary empirical evidence; the critique risks conflating all synthetic user use cases (some of which, like early ideation, may have legitimate low-stakes applications) with high-stakes research substitution; the alternative proposed (addressing structural HCI problems) is programmatic rather than immediately actionable; short conference paper format limits depth of analysis.
