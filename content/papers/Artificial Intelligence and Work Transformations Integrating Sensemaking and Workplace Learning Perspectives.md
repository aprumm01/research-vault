# Artificial Intelligence and Work Transformations Integrating Sensemaking and Workplace Learning Perspectives

# Study Partner Note: AI and Work Transformations

## Summary
This paper explores how organizational members make sense of AI adoption and how perceived AI features trigger sensemaking processes that shape workplace learning modes. The study matters because it addresses a gap in understanding how organizations navigate the uncertainties and organizational changes posed by AI implementation, moving beyond technology-centric dichotomies to examine socio-technical dynamics.

## Key Concepts
- **Sensemaking**: The cognitive process by which organizational members interpret and respond to AI adoption
- **Exploitative vs. Explorative Learning Paths**: Two distinct workplace learning trajectories triggered by different AI feature perceptions
- **Abstract vs. Concrete Features of AI**: Technology characteristics that differentially activate sensemaking processes
- **Socio-technical Processes**: The integration of technical systems with social and organizational factors in technology adoption
- **Workplace Learning Trajectories**: Pathways of organizational learning shaped by initial interpretations of AI technology

## Theoretical Framework
The authors combine sensemaking theory (Weick, 1995) with organizational learning perspectives to analyze how initial interpretations of AI trigger different workplace learning modes. This socio-technical lens moves beyond purely technical or human-centric framings to examine the interplay between technology features and organizational interpretation.

## Methods
The study employed an exploratory qualitative approach using 23 focus groups across four large Swedish manufacturing companies, capturing participants' imaginative ideas and reflections on AI adoption. Data were analyzed using the Gioia method, a systematic qualitative technique for developing conceptual categories from raw data.

## Main Arguments
- Abstract AI features (e.g., general automation concepts) trigger sensemaking that leads to exploitative learning paths, potentially narrowing organizational responses
- Concrete AI features (e.g., specific use cases, tangible applications) activate sensemaking processes that enable explorative learning paths
- Socio-technical processes—not technology alone—are crucial drivers of successful AI adoption trajectories
- Initial sensemaking processes shape downstream organizational learning and change outcomes

## Limitations & Critiques
The study is limited to manufacturing contexts in Sweden and relies on focus group data, which may reflect aspirational rather than actual workplace experiences with AI. The paper also does not deeply examine how power dynamics, organizational hierarchies, or individual differences influence sensemaking processes across organizational members.
