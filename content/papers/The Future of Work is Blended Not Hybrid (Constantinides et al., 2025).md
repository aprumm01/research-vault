# The Future of Work is Blended, Not Hybrid

## Summary
This provocation paper argues that the concept of "hybrid work" is obsolete and must be replaced with "blended work" to capture the fundamental transformation occurring in knowledge and creative work. While hybrid work describes the spatial flexibility of working between physical and virtual spaces, blended work captures how human effort and AI output have become inseparable, with algorithms co-authoring reports, making automated decisions, and reshaping human roles, agency, and expertise.

The authors distinguish between three work modes: Traditional (fixed location, clear boundaries), Hybrid (flexible location using digital tools while maintaining human control), and Blended (AI-assisted work where human and AI contributions become indistinguishable). They present comparison tables for both knowledge workers and creative workers across these modes, highlighting advantages and disadvantages of each.

The paper presents four key considerations for designing blended work: (1) Designing for social relatedness in AI-mediated work environments to counter reduced serendipitous encounters; (2) Designing for job meaningfulness and wellbeing as AI-driven tools erode work-life boundaries; (3) Rethinking workspaces across the digital-physical continuum through spatial integration, AI-adaptive environments, and XR; and (4) Ensuring AI transparency and accountability as AI becomes active in decision-making and content generation.

## Key Concepts
- **Blended Work**: Work mode where human effort and AI output become inseparable, with AI actively participating in decision-making, content generation, and workflow orchestration
- **Hybrid Work**: Traditional conception of flexible work focusing on spatial dimensions (office vs. remote) while maintaining human-centric control
- **Instrumental vs. Constitutive AI**: AI as means-to-an-end (instrumental) versus AI that fundamentally reshapes working conditions as a team member itself (constitutive)
- **Critical Acceptance**: Stance demanding that any AI delegation be accompanied by clear system disclosure, human oversight, and situated reflection

## Theoretical Framework
The paper draws on HCI, CSCW, and AI collaboration literature to distinguish blended work from related concepts like Human-AI Collaboration. While Human-AI Collaboration focuses on designing effective AI collaborators, blended work examines broader personal and societal implications for autonomy, ownership, and accountability.

## Methods
Conceptual provocation paper drawing from existing literature in HCI, CSCW, and AI/work studies. The authors present comparison tables synthesizing advantages and disadvantages across work modes, supported by extensive references to empirical and theoretical work.

## Main Arguments
- The shift to blended work demands urgent attention to AI-mediated work practices, work-life boundaries, physical-digital interactions, and AI transparency
- Blendedness is not just about where work happens but about who (or what) performs it and how human roles evolve alongside AI
- Workers in lower organizational tiers are more likely to encounter AI as surveillance and control instruments rather than as collaborative partners
- AI can reshape the meaningfulness of work by fragmenting labor into micro-tasks that lack autonomy, skill variety, or sense of purpose
- The future of work must balance productivity with humanity, ensuring AI enhances work-life balance rather than eroding it
- HCI researchers and designers are well-equipped to shape future best practices ensuring integrity, agency, and traceability

## Limitations & Critiques
- The paper acknowledges it addresses knowledge and creative work only; broader work practices would require different technological and policy considerations
- Authors situate themselves in Western European contexts (Cyprus, Netherlands, Germany) during the 21st century
- The paper used ChatGPT 4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet to refine clarity and enhance readability, raising questions about authorship the paper itself discusses
- The rapidly evolving nature of AI development means recommendations may need continuous updating
- Participatory AI methods require ongoing sessions that may be difficult to sustain as AI develops rapidly
