## Summary
Muller and Fiesler's CHI 2020 workshop summary documents a collaborative inquiry into the past, present, and future of design fictions — speculative design artifacts that explore possible and preferable futures to stimulate reflection, debate, and ethical inquiry. The workshop surfaces tensions in design fiction practice: whether it is primarily artistic, analytical, or activist; how it relates to speculative design and critical design; and how it can be made more inclusive and diverse. The summary synthesizes emerging consensus and open questions for the design fiction research community.

## Key Concepts
- Design fiction (artifacts from speculative futures)
- Speculative design and critical design
- Diegetic prototypes
- Futures literacy and ethical foresight
- Participatory and inclusive futures

## Theoretical Framework
Speculative design tradition (Dunne & Raby); science fiction prototyping; futures studies; draws on Sterling's design fiction concept and critical design to situate the workshop's conceptual scope.

## Methods
Workshop summary; collaborative sense-making across ~30 design fiction researchers and practitioners; synthesis of position papers and discussion sessions.

## Main Arguments
- Design fiction is a diverse practice with multiple legitimate goals (exploration, critique, participation, provocation)
- The field lacks consensus on methodological standards and evaluation criteria, which both enables creativity and limits scholarly rigor
- More attention is needed to whose futures are centered in design fiction and who is excluded from speculative exercises
- Design fiction has growing relevance for AI ethics, policy, and HCI research as a tool for anticipating sociotechnical consequences

## Limitations & Critiques
As a workshop summary, the document reflects the perspectives of a relatively homogeneous research community; the lack of methodological standards noted in the workshop has not yet been fully resolved in subsequent literature.
