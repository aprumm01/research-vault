# Collaboration with Conversational AI for UX Evaluation (Kuang et al.)

## Summary
Kuang et al. investigate how UX evaluators can interact with conversational AI assistants to support usability test analysis, examining what types of questions evaluators naturally ask and whether voice or text interfaces affect their analysis behavior. Using a Wizard-of-Oz design probe study with 20 participants, they identify five categories of questions evaluators ask (user actions, user mental model, AI help requests, product/task information, user demographics) and find that text-based AI assistants elicited more questions but that both modalities rated equally in satisfaction and trust.

## Key Concepts
- Conversational AI assistants for UX evaluation
- Wizard-of-Oz methodology
- Voice vs. text AI interaction modalities
- Usability test analysis support
- Evaluator autonomy and analysis efficiency

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on HCI literature on AI-assisted analysis, collaborative inquiry, and mixed-initiative systems; frames conversational AI as a Q&A partner that can augment evaluator autonomy during usability analysis rather than replacing evaluator judgment.

## Methods
Wizard-of-Oz design probe study with 20 participants who interacted with simulated AI assistants (via text or voice) while analyzing usability test recordings; coding of question types; questionnaire measures of efficiency, satisfaction, and trust.

## Main Arguments
- UX evaluators have a rich and diverse set of questions during analysis that current non-interactive AI tools (visualizations, dashboards) cannot support
- Five distinct question categories emerge, suggesting AI assistants must handle varied epistemic needs: behavioral facts, interpretive inferences, self-help, contextual grounding, and participant information
- Text interfaces elicit more questions than voice, but voice is not less trusted — both have roles in evaluation support contexts
- Future conversational AI assistants for UX evaluation should support multi-turn dialogue, handle uncertainty gracefully, and integrate with analysis workflows

## Limitations
- Wizard-of-Oz simulation limits ecological validity; real AI limitations (errors, latency, misunderstandings) would significantly affect evaluator behavior
- 20-participant sample is small; question taxonomy may not be exhaustive across UX evaluation contexts
