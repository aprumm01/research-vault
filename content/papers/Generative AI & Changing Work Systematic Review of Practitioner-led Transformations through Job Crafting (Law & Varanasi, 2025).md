# Generative AI & Changing Work: Systematic Review of Practitioner-led Transformations through Job Crafting (Law & Varanasi, 2025)


## Summary
Law (Denison University) and Varanasi (New York University) conduct a systematic literature review of 23 ACM Digital Library papers from 2022-2024 to understand how practitioners across 18 professions have transformed their work in response to GenAI integration. Applying the theoretical lens of job crafting (Zhang et al.'s approach/avoidance crafting framework), the review reveals that workers have primarily engaged in three types of transformation: task crafting (delegating peripheral tasks, engaging GenAI in discovery, analysis, and artifact creation), relational crafting (restructuring collaborations and bypassing peers in favor of GenAI), and cognitive crafting (repositioning professional identity). The paper introduces the concept of "AI managerial labor" to describe the new burden of monitoring and refining GenAI outputs that workers have taken on alongside task delegation.

## Key Concepts
- Job crafting lens: approach crafting (creating opportunities) vs. avoidance crafting (reducing negatives)
- AI managerial labor: new work of overseeing, correcting, and refining GenAI outputs
- Task crafting across seven stages: discovery, analysis, artifact creation, quality assurance, delivery, logistics, overall management
- Relational crafting: bypassing peers and subordinates via GenAI
- Task fragmentation into piecework: erosion of workflow boundaries and professional identity
- Tensions: workload reduction vs. identity shift; AI efficiency vs. role boundary erosion

## Theoretical Framework
Systematic literature review using thematic synthesis (Thomas and Harden approach); job crafting theory (Wrzesniewski and Dutton; Zhang et al.'s approach/avoidance refinement) as primary analytical lens; draws on HCI work practice literature, labor studies, and organizational theory; ACM Digital Library corpus (2022-2024); arXiv preprint 2502.08854 (submitted CHI 2025).

## Methods
Systematic literature review of ACM Digital Library papers (2022-2024); initial corpus of 665 papers screened via title, abstract, and full-text review; 23 papers included covering 18 occupations across 11 industries; thematic synthesis approach with two-reviewer process using peer debriefing and prolonged deliberation; 24 descriptive codes synthesized into 13 analytical themes; codebook available on OSF.

## Main Arguments
- Workers across 18 professions have primarily used GenAI for task crafting: delegating peripheral discovery and analysis tasks, using AI for artifact creation, and leveraging AI for quality assurance and logistics management
- A new category of labor -- "AI managerial labor" -- has emerged, comprising additional work to monitor, refine, and correct GenAI outputs, creating a paradox where workload reduction in one area creates new burdens in another
- Practitioners have restructured collaborations by increasing GenAI reliance where they previously relied on peers and subordinates, sometimes bypassing human collaborators entirely to avoid negative interactions or achieve efficiency gains
- These transformations have fragmented traditionally cohesive tasks into piecework, eroding established workflow boundaries, blurring role responsibilities, and creating tensions around professional identity
- Existing job crafting frameworks require evolution to accommodate GenAI-specific dynamics, particularly the recursive nature of managing AI that is itself managing tasks

## Limitations & Critiques
The review is limited to ACM Digital Library papers, excluding important work in other venues (e.g., CHI, CSCW full conference papers not in ACM DL, Springer, Elsevier). The 2022-2024 timeframe captures only early GenAI adoption and may not reflect more mature or evolved practices. Purposive sampling prioritized conceptual richness over exhaustiveness. Most included papers focus on technology and design roles, limiting coverage of non-technical professions. The paper is an arXiv preprint and had not undergone formal peer review at time of writing.
