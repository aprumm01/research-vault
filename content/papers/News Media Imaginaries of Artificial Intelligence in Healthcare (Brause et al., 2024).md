# News Media Imaginaries of Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare: A Qualitative Analysis Across China, Germany, and the United States (Brause et al., 2024)

## Summary
Saba Rebecca Brause, Heng Yang, Mike S. Schäfer (University of Zurich), and Jing Zeng analyze how news media construct sociotechnical imaginaries of AI in healthcare across China, Germany, and the United States -- three countries with contrasting healthcare systems, technological outlooks, and media structures. Using LDA topic modelling on 15 newspapers (2012-2021) followed by directed thematic analysis, the study identifies two powerful cross-national imaginaries: (1) enhancing healthcare with AI (present in all three countries), and (2) using AI to manage pandemics/epidemics (fully developed in China and the US, present as outlier in Germany). Lower-level national differences within each imaginary reflect systemic differences in healthcare organization, state structure, and media environment. Published in Emerging Media 2(4):581-610, 2024.

## Key Concepts
- Cross-national SIs: powerful AI healthcare imaginaries emerge across very different sociopolitical and cultural contexts, showing that SIs can be "collectively held" across nations, not only within them
- Two core imaginaries: (1) enhancing healthcare at micro (US/Germany: individual patient level) and macro (China: standardizing care across a vast population) scales; (2) AI for pandemic/epidemic management at individual patient/hospital level (US) vs. crowd/top-down infection control (China)
- Systemic determination of imaginary content: US private system → individual autonomy and efficiency framing; China state-controlled system → macro-level capacity building; Germany's cautious technological culture → reduced imaginary diversity
- Speaker contribution: technology sector speakers construct all five imaginaries; civil society only present in US and German coverage; government speakers only in Chinese coverage
- "Discursive opportunity structures" (Ferree et al. 2002): different national media systems shape which imaginaries can emerge and which speakers gain visibility

## Theoretical Framework
Sociotechnical imaginaries (Jasanoff 2015); public communication about technology (PCT) scholarship (Brossard 2013; Ferree et al. 2002; Gerhards & Schäfer 2006); scientific public spheres framework; most-different-systems comparative design (Mills et al. 2010); healthcare AI literature (Bohr & Memarzadeh; Topol 2019); published in Emerging Media 2(4), 2024.

## Methods
Most-different-systems comparative design; LDA topic modelling (Blei et al. 2003) on three national newspaper datasets to identify healthcare-related AI topics; one healthcare topic emerged per country dataset; thematic analysis of articles within each topic using Jasanoff's (2015) SI dimensions as analytical framework (future role of technology, specific AI technology, desirability evaluation); speaker coding (technology sector, civil society, government, healthcare providers, etc.); 5 newspapers per country (leading newspapers), coverage 2012-2021.

## Main Arguments
- Cross-national SIs are empirically real: two powerful imaginaries of AI in healthcare are shared across China, Germany, and the US despite major differences in healthcare and media systems -- demonstrating that "collectively held" imaginaries can cross national boundaries when the technology sector drives global discourse
- National context shapes imaginary content at lower levels: the first imaginary (enhancing healthcare) emphasizes micro-level individual patient benefits in the US/Germany but macro-level system capacity in China, reflecting each country's healthcare system logic and state structure
- Technology sector speakers dominate the construction of all five imaginaries across all three countries -- consistent with evidence that Big Tech companies have "digitally colonized" healthcare (Ozalp et al. 2022); civil society only features in Western coverage; government only in Chinese coverage
- Germany's traditionally cautious approach to emerging technologies, combined with a strongly state-critical media environment, produces fewer and less diverse AI healthcare imaginaries than the US or China -- demonstrating that techno-cultural hesitancy shapes imaginative capacity, not just regulatory outcomes

## Limitations & Critiques
Only newspaper media analyzed -- does not capture social media, policy documents, or practitioner discourse; LDA topic modelling selects one topic per country dataset as the healthcare topic, potentially missing overlapping or peripheral healthcare AI coverage; thematic analysis is directed by the SI framework rather than fully inductive; the 2012-2021 period predates the current generative AI wave; Chinese state-controlled media environment limits interpretation of apparent imaginary consensus as genuine public vision vs. state-orchestrated narrative.
