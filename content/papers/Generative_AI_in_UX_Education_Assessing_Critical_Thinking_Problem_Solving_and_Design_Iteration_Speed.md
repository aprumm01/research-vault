# Generative AI in UX Education: Assessing Critical Thinking, Problem Solving, and Design Iteration Speed

## Summary
This study investigates how the adoption of generative AI tools (ChatGPT, Figma AI, UX Pilot) in UX education affects students' critical thinking, problem-solving ability, and design iteration speed. Using a mixed-methods approach combining pre-experiment surveys, controlled experiments, and post-experiment surveys, the researchers examined UI/UX undergraduate students at Mae Fah Luang University in Thailand. The study addresses two research questions: whether there are significant differences in usability between AI-assisted and non-AI-assisted designs, and to what extent AI support tools affect problem solving, critical thinking, creativity, and efficiency.

The controlled experiment divided 12 participants into two groups (AI-assisted and non-AI) who completed three Figma UI design tasks of increasing complexity (login page, checkout flow, and transportation booking app). Design outputs were evaluated by three independent evaluators using rubrics based on Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics. Results revealed that while AI primarily enhanced iteration speed and the design process, it did not necessarily improve the usability of final results or students' problem-solving and critical thinking abilities. Notably, AI-assisted students spent less time on ideation (1-10 minutes vs. 1-18 minutes) but completed only one iteration per task, while non-AI participants generated two iterations in the same timeframe.

The study concludes that while AI tools improve efficiency in design production and ideation, they present challenges for nurturing independent thought and creativity. The authors argue that critical thinking and ethical considerations should be taught alongside AI tools, emphasizing that students must learn to think independently rather than relying solely on AI-generated results.

## Key Concepts
- **Design Iteration Speed**: The time required to complete ideation, prototyping, and refinement cycles in the UX design process; AI tools significantly reduced ideation time but limited the number of iterations
- **Critical Thinking in Design**: The ability to independently evaluate, justify reasoning, and make novel design decisions; findings suggest AI may hinder development of this skill
- **Human-AI Collaboration**: The interaction between human designers and AI tools where AI serves as a "second brain" or "brainstorming partner" rather than a solution generator
- **AI-Generated Bias**: The tendency of AI to produce designs similar to existing applications, potentially reinforcing stereotypes and lacking human-centered perceptiveness

## Theoretical Framework
The study draws on human-centered AI perspectives, particularly Shneiderman's work on human-centered AI, and literature examining the cognitive impacts of AI tools on design students. The framework positions AI as both an enabler of efficiency and a potential barrier to developing foundational design thinking skills. The research integrates concepts from design thinking methodology with concerns about cognitive automation and the balance between human creativity and AI assistance.

## Methods
- **Design**: Mixed-methods experimental design with three phases: (1) pre-experiment survey, (2) controlled experiment, and (3) post-experiment survey
- **Participants**: 119 students for pre-experiment survey; 12 students selected via purposive sampling for the controlled experiment (6 AI-assisted, 6 non-AI), all from UX/UI undergraduate programs
- **Tasks**: Three Figma UI design tasks of increasing complexity: low (login page), medium (3-page checkout flow), high (3-page transportation booking app)
- **Evaluation**: Independent ratings by three evaluators using rubrics based on Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics; four dimensions assessed: Usability (25 points), Problem Solving (15 points), Critical Thinking (10 points), and Creativity (15 points)
- **Analysis**: Inter-rater reliability via ICC, independent t-tests comparing AI vs. non-AI groups, Cohen's d effect sizes, and thematic analysis of qualitative responses

## Main Arguments
- AI tools significantly improve design iteration speed and efficiency but do not improve final design quality in terms of usability, problem-solving, or critical thinking
- Non-AI participants completed more iterations and refinements, emphasizing the importance of iterative practice for developing design knowledge
- AI creates a potential enhancement in creativity only for simpler, low-complexity tasks (Cohen's d = 1.20 approaching significance)
- Students demonstrate high awareness of ethical concerns (72.3% worried about unintentional plagiarism; 83.2% felt need to double-check AI content)
- AI tools should be positioned as assistants in design ideation rather than standard tools for design principles education
- Critical judgment and human-centered principles must remain central to UX/UI curriculum despite AI integration

## Limitations & Critiques
- **Small sample size**: Only 12 participants in the controlled experiment from a single university in Thailand, limiting generalizability
- **Excluded standardized measures**: Did not include System Usability Scale (SUS) analysis or usability testing with potential end users
- **Limited AI tools**: Only examined ChatGPT and UX Pilot; other AI tools may produce different results
- **Single institution context**: Results may not transfer to students from different cultural or educational backgrounds
- **Short-term assessment**: Did not examine longitudinal effects of AI use on skill development
- **No collaborative work examination**: Study focused on individual tasks; collaborative AI-human workflows in real-life projects were not investigated
