# Instructional Design Theory


## Summary
Instructional Design Theory is the thematic community in this research vault encompassing the theoretical and conceptual foundations of instructional systems design -- the principles, models, and frameworks that guide how instruction is planned, developed, and evaluated to produce learning and performance outcomes. Papers and frameworks in this community range from foundational learning theories (behaviorism, cognitivism, constructivism) and their implications for design, to specific design models (ADDIE, Dick and Carey), evaluation frameworks (Kirkpatrick), and professional ethics standards. The community also intersects with HPT through the shared commitment to evidence-based, systematic design for performance improvement.

## Key Concepts
- Systematic design: analysis, design, development, implementation, evaluation
- Learning objectives as the bridge between analysis and instruction design
- Alignment: objectives, instruction, and assessment as an integrated system
- Foundational learning theories as the psychological basis for design decisions
- Gagne's nine events of instruction as a cognitivist design template
- Constructivist and situative alternatives to behavioral and cognitive ID models

## Theoretical Framework
Instructional systems design (ISD) tradition; behavioral, cognitive, and constructivist learning theory; Gagne's conditions of learning; Merrill's first principles of instruction; situated in this vault as the central framework linking HPT, ADDIE, Kirkpatrick, and learning theory nodes; connects to AI in Design Education through questions about AI-assisted instructional design.
