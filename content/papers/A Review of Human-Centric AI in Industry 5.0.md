# A Review of Human-Centric AI in Industry 5.0

# Study Partner Note: Human-Centric AI in Industry 5.0

## Summary
This review examines how human-centric AI and data science are transforming mechanical automation in Industry 5.0, moving beyond Industry 4.0's efficiency focus to prioritize human well-being, collaboration, and sustainability. The paper matters because it maps the intersection of AI/ML, data science, and mechanical engineering while identifying critical gaps in scalable explainability, ergonomic integration, and real-time human feedback systems.

## Key Concepts
- **Human-Centric AI**: AI systems designed to enhance (not displace) human capabilities while promoting transparency, trust, and co-adaptability
- **Human-Robot Collaboration (HRC)**: Harmonious interaction between humans and machines in shared industrial environments
- **Explainable AI (XAI)**: AI models that provide transparent, interpretable decision-making to build trust
- **Enabling Technologies**: Cobots, digital twins, cyber-physical systems, and edge AI
- **Human-in-the-Loop Systems**: Adaptive decision-making frameworks that integrate real-time human input

## Theoretical Framework
The paper employs a systems integration lens rooted in the European Commission's Industry 5.0 vision, which repositions manufacturing around human well-being, societal value, and sustainability rather than pure technological optimization. It bridges mechanical engineering, data science, and human factors through a human-centric design philosophy.

## Methods
Systematic literature review of 150+ high-impact peer-reviewed articles published between 2015–2025 from major scientific databases, examining technologies, frameworks, and integration approaches across AI, data science, and mechanical automation domains.

## Main Arguments
- Industry 5.0 represents a paradigm shift from automation-only (4.0) to human-centered innovation that integrates AI, data science, and mechanical systems
- XAI, predictive analytics, and human-in-the-loop frameworks are redefining mechanical automation as cognitive and user-oriented
- Key enabling technologies (cobots, digital twins, cyber-physical systems, edge AI) facilitate ergonomic, transparent, and secure human-machine interaction
- Critical research gaps exist: lack of scalable industrial AI explainability, poor ergonomic-robotics integration, and inadequate real-time human feedback mechanisms

## Limitations & Critiques
The review identifies fragmentation in existing literature but doesn't deeply address implementation barriers or economic feasibility of human-centric retrofitting in existing industries. The paper would benefit from empirical case studies demonstrating scalability and ROI of these integrated systems in real manufacturing contexts.
