## Summary
This systematic review examines the emerging literature on human-centric AI in Industry 5.0, analyzing how the Industry 5.0 framework's emphasis on human wellbeing, sustainability, and resilience differs from Industry 4.0's productivity-centered focus. The review synthesizes research on collaborative robots, AI workplace integration, and human-machine collaboration to assess how Industry 5.0 principles are being operationalized. The paper argues that human-centric AI requires not just technical design choices but organizational and policy commitments to worker agency and wellbeing.

## Key Concepts
- Industry 5.0 (human-centric, sustainable, resilient)
- Industry 4.0 vs. 5.0 comparison
- Human-robot collaboration in manufacturing
- Worker wellbeing and AI design
- Collaborative robots (cobots)

## Theoretical Framework
Industry 5.0 framework (European Commission); human-centered design; sociotechnical systems theory; draws on manufacturing and workplace AI research.

## Methods
Systematic literature review of Industry 5.0 and human-centric AI research; thematic coding across 60+ studies; analysis of human-centric AI implementation examples and outcomes.

## Main Arguments
- Industry 5.0's human-centric framing represents a genuine paradigm shift from the efficiency-optimization focus of Industry 4.0
- Human-centric AI in manufacturing requires co-design with workers, not just ergonomic design of human-robot interfaces
- Sustainable and resilient AI systems require diversity of approaches and human adaptability, not just technical redundancy
- The transition to Industry 5.0 requires policy interventions and organizational commitments beyond technical capability development

## Limitations & Critiques
Industry 5.0 as a framework is relatively new and some cited implementations may be primarily rhetorical; the review spans heterogeneous manufacturing contexts; the human-centric claims of many Industry 5.0 implementations have not been independently validated.
