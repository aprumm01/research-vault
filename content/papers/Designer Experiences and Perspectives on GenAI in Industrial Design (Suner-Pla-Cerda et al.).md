# Designer Experiences and Perspectives on GenAI in Industrial Design (Süner-Pla-Cerdà et al.)

## Abstract

The introduction of generative AI (GenAI) tools has created significant disruption in the design professions as they become increasingly integrated into creative processes. While there is an abundance of case-specific, applied studies testing how to integrate AI into design workflows, this study focuses on the lived experiences and perspectives of industrial designers who are actively navigating this disruption, examining how GenAI tools affect their professional identity, design judgment, and sense of creative authorship.

## Summary

Süner-Pla-Cerdà et al. investigate how practicing industrial designers are experiencing and making sense of GenAI integration in their work, finding that the tools challenge established professional identities and design judgment while also opening new creative possibilities. The study foregrounds the perspective of practitioners rather than evaluating tool outputs, contributing an experience-centered account of professional disruption. Key tensions involve authorship, skill relevance, and the nature of design expertise in an AI-assisted context.

## Key Concepts
- Professional identity in industrial design
- Design judgment under AI assistance
- Creative authorship and AI co-creation
- Practitioner experience and sense-making

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on professional identity theory and design cognition research; uses qualitative phenomenological approach to understand how designers interpret their own experiences with GenAI tools in professional practice.

## Methods
Qualitative empirical study; likely interviews or focus groups with practicing industrial designers; thematic analysis of experience accounts; published in AI & Society 2025.

## Main Arguments
- Industrial designers are actively renegotiating their professional identities in response to GenAI tools
- Design judgment remains a distinctly human competence that GenAI tools cannot fully replicate
- Authorship concerns are central to how designers relate to AI-generated outputs
- The disruption is experienced unevenly depending on specialization and career stage
