# The Attack on ISD

## Summary
This Training Magazine article chronicles the growing critique of Instructional Systems Design (ISD) and the ADDIE model in corporate training contexts at the turn of the millennium. Gordon and Zemke examine why traditional ISD approaches were facing criticism for being too slow, too rigid, and out of touch with rapid business changes. The piece presents both sides of the debate - critics who argue ISD is obsolete and defenders who contend the methodology remains sound but is often poorly implemented.

## Key Concepts
- **ISD (Instructional Systems Design)**: Systematic approach to designing instruction based on analysis, objectives, and evaluation
- **ADDIE Model**: Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation - the classic ISD framework
- **Rapid Prototyping**: Alternative approach emphasizing quick iteration over extensive front-end analysis
- **Performance Support**: Just-in-time learning solutions as alternatives to formal training
- **HPT (Human Performance Technology)**: Broader approach considering non-training solutions to performance problems

## Theoretical Framework
The article is primarily journalistic rather than theoretical, but it implicitly engages with debates about systematic vs. agile approaches to instructional design. It reflects tensions between behaviorist foundations of ISD and emerging constructivist and situated learning perspectives that emphasize context-dependent, learner-centered approaches.

## Methods
- Journalistic investigation
- Interviews with ISD practitioners, critics, and thought leaders
- Review of contemporary debates in training literature
- Case examples from corporate training contexts

## Main Arguments
**Critics' Position:**
1. ISD is too slow for rapidly changing business environments
2. Extensive front-end analysis is often unnecessary and wasteful
3. The linear ADDIE model doesn't match how learning actually happens
4. ISD produces sterile, boring training that fails to engage learners
5. The approach is better suited to military/industrial contexts than modern knowledge work

**Defenders' Position:**
1. Poor implementations shouldn't condemn the methodology itself
2. Skipping analysis leads to training that doesn't address real performance gaps
3. ISD is flexible and can be adapted to various contexts and timelines
4. Critics often propose alternatives that reinvent ISD principles under new names
5. Rapid approaches may sacrifice quality and effectiveness for speed

## Limitations & Critiques
- Article presents the debate without resolving it - readers left to draw own conclusions
- Limited empirical evidence presented for either position
- Corporate training focus may not generalize to other educational contexts
- Dated (2000) - doesn't address subsequent developments in e-learning, mobile learning, AI
- Binary framing (ISD vs. alternatives) may oversimplify the range of design approaches
- Voices of learners largely absent from the debate
