# AI Rivalry as a Craft

## Summary
Varanasi et al. explore how writing professionals reshape their roles through GAI resisting and embracing strategies via 25 semi-structured interviews (avg. 17 years experience, 12+ months GAI exposure). Using job crafting theory, the study identifies four resisting strategies—maximizing human potential, reinforcing professional identity, carving occupational niches, preserving network credibility—and three embracing strategies—enhancing desirable workflows, minimizing mundane tasks, engaging AI-managerial labor. Human-driven (resisting) strategies craft both identity and practices by positioning human labor as visible differentiator and engaging in AI rivalry (new phenomenon mirroring professional competition). GAI-driven (embracing) strategies craft only practices, amplifying AI collaboration while reducing human interdependencies but requiring invisible labor for prompt engineering, output verification, and workflow reconfiguration. The study argues productivity/efficiency metrics insufficiently assess value of divergent strategies and advocates holistic evaluation fostering equitable dialogue between resisters and embracers.

## Key Concepts
- Job crafting: Bottom-up worker-driven modification of tasks, relationships, and role boundaries to enhance work outcomes and derive meaning
- Approach vs avoidance crafting: Proactive role expansion aligning with preferences/strengths vs reducing negative work aspects
- AI rivalry as craft: Writers competing with GAI to demonstrate human superiority, mirroring interprofessional rivalry patterns previously observed only between humans
- AI-managerial labor: Invisible work of prompt engineering, verifying GAI outputs, troubleshooting errors, reconfiguring workflows for effective GAI utilization
- Human-driven expansion strategies: Role crafting emphasizing human-centric tasks, skillsets, and visible labor differentiation from GAI users
- GAI-driven expansion strategies: Delegating tedious/emotionally taxing tasks to GAI while carving dedicated time for effective tool integration
- Identity crafting: Reshaping professional self-concept and visibility to networks/end-users through human labor emphasis
- Practice crafting: Modifying workflow activities, task configurations, and collaboration patterns without identity transformation
- Platform-mediated creative labor: Gig economy dynamics prioritizing scale/output over autonomy, blurring individual-work boundaries

## Theoretical Framework
Employs job crafting theory (Wrzesniewski & Dutton) positioning agency in workers rather than management for work design, focusing on role crafting (tasks, perceptions) and relational crafting (colleague interactions). Differentiates approach crafting (expanding opportunities aligned with goals) from avoidance crafting (reducing negative aspects). Builds on HCI creative work scholarship tracing digital transformation's shift from tangible to intangible outcomes, conventional to unconventional practices, and individual to platform-mediated labor. Engages creativity-support tools (CST) research on supporting/extending practices while transforming professional identities. References social constructivist understanding of GAI integration balancing techno-deterministic narratives with worker-led micro-level lived experiences.

## Methods
IRB-approved qualitative interview study (Mar-Jun 2024) with 25 U.S.-based writing professionals via quota sampling ensuring diversity in roles, industries, company sizes, task natures, seniority, gender. Recruited through university publishing/marketing team, LinkedIn/Twitter/Facebook, Writers Guild mailing lists, Upwork, writing subreddits. Screening survey captured demographics, responsibilities, GAI attitudes, participation interest. 32.5 hours semi-structured remote video interviews (65-90 min, avg. 77 min) focusing on daily practices, GAI attitudes, task/relationship changes, skill development, industry perspectives; screen-sharing for work examples. Demographics: 10 men/12 women/3 non-binary, ages 24-65 (avg 42.6), 4-35 years experience (avg 17.8), 12-27 months GAI exposure (avg 15.84), roles spanning journalist, paralegal, ghostwriter, poet, screenwriter, technical writer, content strategist across law, entertainment, business, tech, fiction domains. Inductive thematic analysis via open coding (82 initial codes), collaborative refinement (61 final codes), abductive theoretical coding mapping to job crafting elements (role/relational, avoidance/approach). Compensation: $50 Amazon vouchers or Upwork contracts.

## Main Arguments
- Human-driven (resisting) strategies enable dual crafting of identity and practices by emphasizing visible human labor, carving exclusive human-driven niches, and appealing to communities valuing authentic authorship, while GAI-driven (embracing) strategies craft only practices without identity transformation
- AI rivalry emerges as novel phenomenon where writers proactively compete against GAI capabilities to demonstrate human superiority, mirroring patterns historically observed only in human professional competition and representing new form of technological rivalry
- GAI adoption introduces significant invisible labor (AI-managerial work) including prompt engineering, output verification, error troubleshooting, and workflow reconfiguration that productivity metrics fail to capture, creating hidden costs offsetting efficiency gains
- Resisters emphasize identity visibility to networks/end-users and carve niches through human authenticity claims, while embracers reduce human interdependencies and emotional labor by delegating tedious tasks but extend delegation to challenging essential tasks
- Productivity and efficiency metrics insufficient for holistic assessment as they privilege embracing strategies' quantifiable outputs over resisting strategies' qualitative contributions to professional standards, credibility preservation, and human skill development
- Common goals across divergent strategies: Both groups pursue competitive edge, skill development, and work satisfaction, but resisters achieve through human capability enhancement while embracers achieve through AI-augmented throughput
- Platform-mediated creative labor dynamics (scale prioritization, autonomy erosion, identity visibility constraints) amplified by GAI as algorithmic oversight makes worker identities increasingly precarious and requires new forms of articulation work
- Equitable GAI integration requires fostering dialogue between resisters and embracers, creating collective learning spaces, and developing metrics valuing both human-centric contributions and AI-augmented productivity rather than defaulting to efficiency benchmarks

## Limitations & Critiques
- U.S.-only sample limits cross-cultural generalizability; GAI adoption, resistance patterns, and job crafting strategies may differ substantially in Global South, European, or Asian contexts with different labor protections and professional norms
- 12-month minimum GAI exposure captures early-stage practices; may miss longer-term habituation patterns, strategy evolution, or delayed consequences emerging after 2-3 years of sustained use
- Quota sampling emphasizes diversity over representativeness; cannot establish prevalence of resisting vs embracing strategies or demographic predictors of strategy selection across writing profession
- Self-selection bias: Volunteers with strong GAI opinions (positive or negative) may over-represent polarized positions compared to ambivalent professionals with mixed practices
- Retrospective accounts subject to rationalization: Participants may post-hoc justify strategies as intentional crafting when decisions were reactive or situational
- No longitudinal tracking: Single-timepoint interviews cannot capture strategy shifts, trajectory changes, or reversal from embracing to resisting (or vice versa) as GAI capabilities or labor market conditions evolve
- Interview-only data lacks observational validation: Cannot verify claimed practices, assess actual output quality differences, or measure productivity impacts beyond self-reports
- Missing organizational context: Cannot isolate individual agency (job crafting) from managerial pressures, workplace policies, or client demands shaping GAI adoption/resistance
- Compensation ($50) may influence participation toward lower-income freelancers or gig workers rather than highly-paid staff writers with stable employment
- Focus on writing profession limits transferability to other creative fields (graphic design, music production, video editing) with different GAI modalities, workflow integration challenges, and identity stakes
