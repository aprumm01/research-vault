# Evolving Roles and Workflows of Creative Practitioners in the Age of Generative AI

## Summary
Creative practitioners (like designers, software developers, and architects) have started to employ Generative AI models (GenAI) to produce text, images, and assets comparable to those made by people While HCI research explores specific GenAI models and creativity support tools, little is known about practitioners’ evolving roles and workflows with GenAI models across a project’s stages This knowledge is key to guide the development of the new generation of Creativity Support Tools.

## Key Concepts
- Practitioners perceive their role as **project managers with creative vision** orchestrating information across multiple GenAI models, rather than traditional workers executing individual tasks
- Relationship with GenAI CSTs differs fundamentally from non-GenAI creativity support tools
- Creative practitioners want **creative agency** across all stages but desire **social relationships** with GenAI that can empathize and modulate responses based on emotional state
- Practitioners do not want complete automation but consider trade-offs: articulation challenges, model stochasticity, and alignment issues vs benefits like goal development, streamlined processes, cold start help, and serendipitous discoveries
- Creative process is changing to focus on **project-level and artifact-level orchestrations**
- GenAI is being added as one of many tools in practitioners' toolboxes for real-world multi-stage projects

## Theoretical Framework
- **Double Diamond process** (British Design Council): Four iterative phases - Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver - used to map creative workflows
- **Mixed-Initiative Co-Creativity (MI-CC)**: Framework for understanding degree to which human and computational agents take initiative in creative process
- **Creativity Support Tools (CST)** principles: Path of Least Resistance, Predictability, Low Thresholds/High Ceilings/Wide Walls
- Wallace's creativity model: Knowledge acquisition, unconscious processing, idea generation, and evaluation

## Methods
survey, interview

## Main Arguments
- Creative practitioners conceptualize their roles differently with GenAI: as orchestrators with larger vision rather than task executors
- Real-world projects involve using multiple GenAI tools across multiple creative domains (e.g., ChatGPT for ideation, Midjourney for visuals, specific tools for implementation)
- Practitioners desire both creative agency and empathetic social relationships with GenAI models that can perceive and adapt to their emotional state
- GenAI is not used for complete automation but involves careful trade-offs between articulation challenges and benefits like workflow streamlining and serendipitous discoveries
- Creative workflows are evolving toward project-level orchestration across multiple models, sessions, and creative stages
- Design opportunities exist for building more human-centered GenAI CSTs that support practitioners' actual needs and workflows

## Limitations & Critiques
- Study focuses on early adopters of GenAI in creative practice (professional experience 3-27 years)
- Data collection timeframe (April-May 2023) captures specific moment in rapidly evolving GenAI landscape
- Sample includes 10 interviews + 17 YouTube videos, then verified with 31 survey respondents
- Participants predominantly from specific creative fields (graphic design, software development, UI/UX, architecture, writing)
- Need to verify if existing CST design principles and evaluation methods still hold true for GenAI-enabled tools
