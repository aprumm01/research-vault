# Activity Theory


## Summary
Activity Theory (specifically Cultural-Historical Activity Theory, CHAT) frames human action as mediated by tools, rules, community, and division of labor within a structured activity system. Developed from Vygotsky's zone of proximal development through Leont'ev's activity-action-operation hierarchy to Engestrom's third-generation CHAT, the framework analyzes how contradictions within activity systems drive expansive learning and organizational transformation. In this research vault, Activity Theory grounds the analysis of how workplace learning technologies and AI tools mediate professional practice -- particularly how introducing AI into design and performance work restructures the activity system's components and surfaces new contradictions.

## Key Concepts
- Activity system: subject, object, mediating tools, rules, community, division of labor
- Zone of proximal development (Vygotsky) extended to collective activity (Engestrom)
- Expansive learning: transformation of entire activity systems through contradiction resolution
- Tool mediation: how artifacts (including AI) reshape human action
- Contradictions as drivers of organizational and professional learning

## Theoretical Framework
Cultural-Historical Activity Theory (CHAT); Vygotsky's sociocultural psychology; Leont'ev's activity-action-operation levels; Engestrom's expansive learning model; applied in workplace learning, technology studies, and organizational change research; relevant in this vault to analysis of AI as a mediating tool in design and performance work.
