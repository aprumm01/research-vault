# Generative AI Integrated Educational Model for User-Centered Design

## Summary
This study develops and evaluates an AI-integrated educational model for teaching user-centered design in higher education. Based on design thinking theory, the model integrates ChatGPT and Midjourney into the divergent and convergent phases of the Double Diamond design process. The empirical research, conducted with 48 third-year Visual Communication students at Chengyi College, Jimei University (China), demonstrates that this educational model can foster students' creativity and problem-solving skills.

The model operationalizes AI integration across four design phases: Discover (market research with ChatGPT), Define (persona development and problem definition), Develop (brainstorming and mood boards with Midjourney), and Deliver (detailed design with CAD tools). Students worked in 12 teams on a travel app project, following the model to learn both user-centered design skills and AI literacy. The research employed mixed methods including observation, design journal analysis, interviews, questionnaires, and comparison of final project scores with previous cohorts.

Results show statistically significant improvement in student performance (mean score 86.15 in 2024 vs. 82.12 in 2023, p < .001) and moderate usability scores for both ChatGPT (68.4 SUS) and Midjourney (78.975 SUS). Students valued AI for market research, persona development, and visual inspiration but expressed mixed feelings about brainstorming and noted AI limitations in detailed design phases.

## Key Concepts
- **User-Centered Design (UCD)**: A multidisciplinary design approach focused on understanding users, their needs, and involving them throughout the design process
- **Double Diamond Model**: British Design Council's framework with four phases (Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver) combining divergent and convergent thinking
- **AI Literacy**: The skills needed to effectively utilize AI tools in design practice, including prompt engineering and critical evaluation of AI outputs
- **Cognitive Co-Evolution**: The interplay between human creative thinking and AI-generated content in the design process

## Theoretical Framework
The study is grounded in design thinking theory, specifically the Double Diamond model which emphasizes alternating phases of divergent thinking (exploring possibilities) and convergent thinking (focusing on solutions). This framework is extended by mapping specific AI tools (ChatGPT for text/analysis, Midjourney for imagery) to appropriate phases where their capabilities can augment human design thinking.

## Methods
- **Design**: Mixed methods quasi-experimental study
- **Participants**: 48 third-year Visual Communication students divided into 12 teams
- **Intervention**: AI-integrated UI design curriculum using the developed educational model (June-July 2024)
- **Data Collection**: Observation, Fabrie design journals, interviews, System Usability Scale (SUS) questionnaire, final project scores
- **Analysis**: Descriptive statistics, independent samples t-test comparing 2023 (n=43) and 2024 (n=48) cohorts

## Main Arguments
- AI tools can enhance each phase of user-centered design when integrated purposefully, with ChatGPT supporting research and analysis and Midjourney supporting ideation and visual exploration
- Students benefit from AI in areas they might not have considered (e.g., user research content), though over-reliance on AI must be prevented by emphasizing traditional methods like focus groups and observation
- Human judgment remains essential as AI engages in rational, convergent processing that may produce predictable results, requiring designers to maintain dominant position in the creative process
- The ambiguity of AI-generated imagery can inspire creativity but also exposes AI's deficiency in interaction logic and detailed design
- Educators should combine traditional design approaches with AI technologies, converting students into "design strategists" who reflect on and evaluate AI outputs

## Limitations & Critiques
- Relatively small sample size (48 students) limits statistical power and generalizability
- Single institution and discipline (Visual Communication) may not represent other design education contexts
- Comparison with 2023 cohort introduces potential confounds beyond AI integration (different students, instructors, project specifics)
- SUS scores indicate moderate usability with room for improvement, particularly in system complexity and confidence
- Students expressed concerns about technology dependency, plagiarism, and erosion of critical thinking skills
- AI tools' capabilities change rapidly, potentially dating the specific integration strategies
