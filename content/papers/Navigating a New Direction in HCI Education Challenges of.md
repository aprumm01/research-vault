# Navigating a New Direction in HCI Education: Challenges of

## Summary
This paper examines the challenges HCI educators face as the field evolves in response to AI, examining how HCI education must adapt its curricula, methods, and pedagogical frameworks to prepare students for practice in AI-mediated technology contexts. Through surveys and interviews with HCI faculty, the authors document the challenges of teaching HCI when the fundamental nature of human-computer interaction is being transformed by AI capabilities. The paper identifies specific pedagogical adaptations and curriculum changes HCI programs are making and recommends a forward-looking HCI education framework.

## Key Concepts
- HCI education in the age of AI
- Curriculum adaptation for AI-transformed practice
- AI-mediated interaction and HCI theory
- Pedagogical challenges in evolving fields
- New HCI competencies for AI contexts

## Theoretical Framework
Design education research; HCI pedagogical frameworks; technology education theory; draws on HCI discipline history and education reform literature.

## Methods
Survey and interview study with HCI faculty across programs; qualitative analysis of curriculum adaptation strategies; thematic analysis of challenges and responses.

## Main Arguments
- AI is transforming the object of HCI study itself (what is interaction when AI mediates it?), requiring conceptual as well as practical curriculum updates
- HCI education faces a dual challenge: teaching current AI tools and practices while maintaining the discipline's foundational human-centered principles
- The rapid pace of AI change makes curriculum stability increasingly difficult; HCI programs need more modular, updateable curricula
- Students need both tool fluency and critical AI evaluation skills to navigate practice in AI-transformed technology contexts

## Limitations & Critiques
Survey-based study of faculty views may not capture student experience or actual learning outcomes; HCI education varies considerably across institutions; the rapid pace of change means recommendations may quickly become outdated.
