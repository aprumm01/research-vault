# Design Thinking


## Summary
Design Thinking is a human-centered innovation process and mindset structured around five phases: Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, and Test. Popularized by IDEO and Stanford's d.school, it imports design sensibility -- including tolerance for ambiguity, abductive reasoning, iterative refinement, and radical collaboration -- into non-design domains. In this research vault, Design Thinking functions as a primary lens for understanding how AI tools are integrated into UX and design workflows; multiple papers in the corpus examine AI adoption across DT phases, with highest adoption in Ideate and lowest in Empathize and Test. The corpus also critically examines whether DT's human-centered commitments are preserved or undermined when AI accelerates or replaces design reasoning.

## Key Concepts
- Five-phase DT process: Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test
- Abductive reasoning as the logic of design (Faste, Martin)
- Human-centered design as a core commitment of DT
- Radical collaboration across disciplines
- AI adoption patterns across DT phases: Ideate highest, Empathize/Test lowest
- DT as exportable methodology vs. DT as mindset

## Theoretical Framework
Human-centered design tradition; Rolf Faste's design thinking pedagogy at Stanford; IDEO's applied design methodology; Roger Martin's integrative thinking model; situates DT within broader design research traditions (Cross, Schon, Simon); relevant in this vault to AI in design education, UX practice, and instructional design debates.
