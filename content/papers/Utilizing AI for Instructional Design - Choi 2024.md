# Utilizing AI for Instructional Design - Choi 2024

## Summary
Recently, generative AI has been at the center of disruptive innovation in various settings, including educational sectors This article investigates ChatGPT, which is one of the most prominent generative AI in the market, to explore its usefulness and potential for instructional design Four researchers used a set of prompts to generate a course map for an online course that is aimed to teach the topic of makerspace and conducted SWOT analysis to identify strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats of using generative AI for instructional design.

## Key Concepts
- ChatGPT as performance empowering partner for rapid prototyping of instructional design course maps
- SWOT analysis framework identifying strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats of GenAI for instructional design
- Alignment among learning objectives, instructional activities, and assessments as critical course design component
- Large language models' capability in generating measurable learning outcomes using suitable action verbs
- Domain knowledge and instructional design expertise still required to warrant quality and reliability despite AI efficiency
- Time-limited instructional design challenge potentially addressed through expedited information search and curation tasks
- Hallucination problem evidenced by fabricated non-existent reading materials and references undermining reliability
- Student-centered learning promotion through instructors devoting more time to scaffolding and engagement design

## Theoretical Framework
- SWOT (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats) analysis framework for evaluating AI tools
- Alignment principle connecting learning objectives, instructional activities, and assessment mechanisms
- Constructionist learning principles applied to makerspace education context
- Action verb taxonomy for creating measurable learning objectives avoiding ambiguous terms

## Methods
Four researchers collaboratively developed seven sequential prompts to generate complete four-week online makerspace graduate course map via ChatGPT. Each researcher independently executed all prompts in single chat session ensuring continuity. Generated documents contained module outlines, detailed plans, course materials, hands-on activity guidelines, discussion prompts, quiz questions, assignment instructions, and rubrics. Team members reviewed all documents providing effectiveness comments, which were then categorized using SWOT framework through collaborative analysis.

## Main Arguments
- ChatGPT successfully generates suitable learning objectives with clear measurable action verbs and aligns activities with those objectives
- Tool provides diversity of instructional activities preventing repetition and promoting varied engagement approaches across modules
- Discussion prompts encourage critical thinking by connecting learning to personal experiences and broadening instructor knowledge spaces
- Cross-referencing capability automatically links previous materials and assignments enhancing course coherence without explicit instruction
- Rapid prototyping decreases instructor workload enabling focus shift toward student-centered scaffolding and engagement design
- Contextual understanding limitations cause ChatGPT to design infeasible activities (in-person for online courses, unrealistic timeframes)
- Hallucination of non-existent references and materials creates critical reliability issues potentially making courses obsolete
- Generic and shallow assessments focusing on factual recall rather than higher-order thinking skills limit pedagogical utility

## Limitations & Critiques
- ChatGPT fabricates non-existent reading materials and references undermining entire course reliability and credibility
- Limited contextual understanding causes disregard for online delivery format designing in-person activities instead
- Unrealistic time expectations for assignments (summarizing entire books, designing and facilitating activities within one week)
- Vague instructions lacking detailed guidelines requiring substantial instructor scaffolding before student use
- Repetitive discussion activity structures across modules risking boredom and decreased student engagement
- Quiz and assessment questions focus excessively on factual recall missing higher-order thinking skill measurement
- Generic assignment questions lack relevance to students' personal experiences and could be answered by AI itself
- Unreliable output production with system failures stopping mid-generation requiring careful command division
- Analysis limited to four-week makerspace graduate course constraining generalizability to other contexts and levels
- Single AI tool focus (ChatGPT) without comparing alternative generative AI platforms for instructional design
- Four-researcher sample providing limited diversity of instructional design perspectives and expertise levels
- Domain knowledge and expert judgment still essential suggesting AI cannot autonomously produce quality course designs
