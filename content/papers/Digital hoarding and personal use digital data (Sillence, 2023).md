## Summary

This study examines digital hoarding in the context of personal use digital data, an understudied area compared to workplace digital hoarding research. The authors developed the Digital Behaviors for Personal Use Questionnaire (DBPUQ) to measure hoarding behaviors associated with personal technology and validated its relationship with the established Digital Hoarding Questionnaire (DHQ).

The research involved 167 participants who completed both questionnaires and reported on their main personal storage device. The study found significant correlations between DHQ scores and personal digital behaviors, with higher DHQ scores correlating with difficulty finding items, attachment to items, and distress at potential loss. High scorers on the DHQ stored significantly more items (mean 35,622) than low scorers (mean 8,024) and were less likely to delete information items.

Notably, device choice (smartphone vs. other devices) did not significantly affect information item management behaviors, deletion activity, or affective responses to data, contrary to the researchers' expectations.

## Key Concepts

- **Digital Hoarding Questionnaire (DHQ)**: A validated 10-item questionnaire measuring digital hoarding tendencies through two factors: "accumulation" and "difficulty deleting"
- **Digital Behaviors for Personal Use Questionnaire (DBPUQ)**: A newly developed instrument measuring hoarding behaviors on personal devices, including information item types, deletion activity, organization behaviors, and affective responses
- **Affective responses to digital data**: Emotional attachment and distress related to information items, particularly around potential loss or deletion
- **Personal Information Management (PIM)**: The practice of organizing, storing, and retrieving digital information for personal use

## Key Findings

1. **DHQ-Behavior Correlation**: Total DHQ scores significantly correlated with deletion activity (r = .384, p < .001) and total items stored (r = .381, p < .001)

2. **High vs. Low Hoarders**: High scorers on DHQ stored 4.4 times more information items on average than low scorers

3. **Behavioral Differences**: High digital hoarders were:
   - Less likely to delete information items
   - More attached to their stored items
   - More distressed at the thought of losing items
   - Found it more difficult to locate stored items

4. **Device Neutrality**: No significant differences found between smartphone users and other device users in terms of:
   - Total information items stored
   - Deletion activity
   - Information item management behaviors
   - Affective responses to data

5. **Information Item Distribution**: Photos and emails (read, unread, and archived) comprised the largest volume of stored items across all participants

## Relevance to Sustainable Computing

This paper directly addresses sustainability concerns related to digital data storage:

1. **Environmental Impact**: The authors cite research showing unwanted digital photographs alone could generate 10.6 kg of CO2 emissions annually per adult in the UK due to storage requirements

2. **Data Minimization**: Understanding digital hoarding tendencies is foundational for developing interventions to reduce unnecessary data accumulation, thereby reducing the environmental footprint of data storage

3. **Behavior Change Design**: The findings suggest that interventions must address affective responses to data (attachment, distress) not just practical organization tools, to effectively reduce digital clutter

4. **Device-Specific Solutions**: While device type did not significantly impact behaviors, the authors note that understanding the role of device type remains important for designing appropriate, device-specific behavior change interventions

5. **Sustainability Awareness**: The paper explicitly connects digital hoarding to sustainability issues, noting the need for "increased awareness around digital data hoarding in relation to sustainability issues more broadly"
