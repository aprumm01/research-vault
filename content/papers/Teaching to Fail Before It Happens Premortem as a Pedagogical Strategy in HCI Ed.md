# Teaching to Fail Before It Happens Premortem as a Pedagogical Strategy in HCI Education

## Summary
1 This “teachable moment” paper introduces the premortem technique as a reflective and forward-looking pedagogical strategy for HCI education A premortem invites students to imagine that their project has already failed and then work backward to identify the reasons for that failure—flipping the common “will this work ” framing into “what went wrong.

## Key Concepts
- Premortem technique: imagining project failure and working backward to identify causes
- Prospective hindsight as cognitive strategy to counteract planning fallacy and overconfidence
- Legitimizing failure as learning tool by formalizing dissent in team contexts
- Metacognitive awareness development through anticipatory reflection
- Shifting from "will this work?" to "what went wrong?" framing
- Counteracting groupthink and over-optimism in design teams
- Low-overhead, high-impact pedagogical intervention

## Main Arguments
- Premortem technique helps students surface hidden risks and recognize blind spots in design projects
- Formalizing dissent through premortem exercises breaks groupthink without damaging team morale
- Students become more aware of effective teamwork practices and more committed to avoiding identified pitfalls
- Initial satisfaction dip followed by highest-level satisfaction at semester end demonstrates productive discomfort
- Premortem complements existing studio practices like critique, speculative design, and risk analysis
- Technique cultivates anticipatory judgment and design foresight without requiring extensive resources
- Broader distribution of problem categories identified compared to standard risk assessment methods

## Limitations & Critiques
- Primarily conceptual and anecdotal - lacks rigorous empirical evaluation in HCI education context
- Potential to demoralize teams or create friction if not facilitated skillfully
- May cause decision paralysis in already-anxious teams - alternative scaffolds needed
- Boundary conditions unclear - situations where premortems less helpful or counterproductive not well-defined
- Gap between recognizing problems and executing preventive actions (e.g., teams identifying scope issues but not scaling back)
- Best practices for facilitation underspecified - how to ensure all voices heard, moderate tone, prioritize issues
- Limited guidance on adapting technique for different educational backgrounds, personalities, project types
