## Summary
Uusitalo et al. study how UX and industrial designers at Aalto University use generative AI tools, finding that practitioners experience GenAI as "clay to play with" — a malleable, exploratory material that affords rapid creative iteration rather than a finished product generator. The metaphor captures how professional designers are integrating GenAI into their creative process as a generative substrate rather than an answer machine. The paper contributes a material-centric account of how GenAI is being appropriated in professional design practice.

## Key Concepts
- GenAI as material (clay metaphor)
- Exploratory and iterative GenAI use in design
- UX and industrial design practice with AI
- Material-centric design theory applied to AI tools

## Theoretical Framework
Material-centric design theory and HCI practice research; draws on design materials theory (Ingold, Schön's reflective practice) to frame how designers experience and work with GenAI as a creative material.

## Methods
Qualitative empirical study at Aalto University with UX and industrial designers using GenAI tools; likely interviews, observations, or design sessions; published at CHI 2025 or similar venue.

## Main Arguments
- Professional designers experience GenAI as a malleable material for exploration, not a product generator
- The clay metaphor captures the non-deterministic, iterative nature of productive GenAI use
- This material framing differs from efficiency-focused accounts of AI adoption in design
- Design education should cultivate "playing with AI" skills, not just prompt engineering techniques
