# Clay to Play With: Generative AI Tools in UX and Industrial Design Practice (Uusitalo et al., DIS 2024)

## Summary
Uusitalo et al. report an interview study with 10 UX and industrial designers at Nokia/Aalto exploring GenAI adoption and appropriation in professional design practice, finding wide variation in attitudes from threat-oriented negativity to empowerment — differences explained primarily by sense of agency and perceived control. Using the Coping Model of User Adaptation as their analytical lens, they show that metacognitive skills (self-awareness about one's own design process) are the key differentiator between designers who find GenAI empowering versus threatening. The title metaphor — "clay to play with" — captures GenAI's ambiguous, malleable potential.

## Key Concepts
- Coping Model of User Adaptation
- Agency and perceived control in technology adoption
- Metacognition in design practice
- Generative AI appropriation vs. adoption
- Ownership and creative credit in AI-assisted work

## Theoretical Framework
Coping Model of User Adaptation (Beaudry & Pinsonneault); situates technology adoption as an appraisal process where users assess threats and opportunities, then select coping strategies accordingly.

## Methods
Semi-structured interview study with 10 UX and industrial design specialists varying in tenure and GenAI experience; reflexive thematic analysis following Braun and Clarke.

## Main Arguments
- Designer attitudes toward GenAI range widely from threat to empowerment, primarily determined by sense of agency
- Metacognitive awareness of one's own design process mediates whether GenAI feels like a tool or a threat
- Sensemaking about GAI's role in the design process is ongoing and unresolved for most practitioners
- Ownership and agency concerns (who "made" the design?) are central sources of uncertainty

## Limitations & Critiques
Small sample (n=10) from a specific organizational context (Nokia/Aalto collaboration); findings may not transfer to freelancers or other design disciplines beyond UX and industrial design.
