# The Electronic Sweatshop: How Computers Are Transforming the Office (Review, 1992)

**Year:** 1992
**Venue:** Chicago Press on behalf of The History of Science Society St
**Institution:** 0 Published by: The University of Chicago Press on behalf of

## Summary
Reviews Barbara Garson's investigative book examining how computers are reorganizing office work along factory-like lines of surveillance, deskilling, and productivity monitoring—extending the factory assembly line model to white-collar work. The review highlights Garson's argument that the "electronic sweatshop" intensifies managerial control while reducing worker autonomy and skill.

## Key Concepts
- Electronic sweatshop
- Office work computerization
- Surveillance and monitoring of knowledge workers
- Deskilling of clerical and professional work
- Factory-style organization of white-collar labor

## Theoretical Framework
Labor process theory; Braverman-influenced analysis of computerization as a mechanism of managerial control; investigative journalism combined with sociological framing.

## Methods
Book review of Garson's investigative journalism work; draws on Garson's ethnographic and interview research in multiple corporate settings; no original primary research by the reviewer.

## Main Arguments
- Computers reorganize office work according to factory efficiency logic: standardized tasks, electronic monitoring, reduced worker discretion
- Workers across industries—from airline reservations to McDonald's management—experience computerization as intensified control, not liberation
- The "electronic sweatshop" creates new forms of alienation in ostensibly "knowledge" jobs
- Technology serves management's interest in controlling and cheapening labor, not workers' interests in meaningful or autonomous work

## Limitations & Critiques
Based on investigative journalism rather than systematic social science; may overstate the uniformity of deskilling across diverse office contexts; dated to the early 1990s PC era; does not anticipate the rise of knowledge work, remote work, or gig economy dynamics.
