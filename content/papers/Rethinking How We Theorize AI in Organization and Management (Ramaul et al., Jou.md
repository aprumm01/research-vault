## Summary
Uses Alvesson and Sandberg's problematizing review method to examine how organization and management theory (OMT) has conceptualized AI, uncovering two foundational assumptions—rationality and anthropomorphism—borrowed from computer science and economics. Argues these assumptions constrain theorizing and risk turning AI into a "hembig" (hegemonic, ambiguous, big) concept that obscures the socio-material entanglements shaping AI in organizations.

## Key Concepts
- Problematizing review
- Rationality assumption in OMT
- Anthropomorphism in AI theorizing
- "Hembig" concept (hegemonic, ambiguous, big)
- Socio-material entanglements

## Theoretical Framework
Alvesson and Sandberg's (2011, 2020) problematizing review methodology; root metaphor analysis; critiques the "relational turn" in OMT as necessary but insufficient without explicit attention to foundational assumptions.

## Methods
Selective critical reading of AI-related articles in the most influential OMT journals; document and conceptual analysis; problematizing review as methodology rather than systematic literature review.

## Main Arguments
- OMT has absorbed rationality assumptions (AI as optimizing agent) and anthropomorphism (AI as quasi-human) from adjacent fields without critical reflection
- These assumptions lead to oversimplification of AI's actual role in organizational processes
- Risk of AI becoming a "hembig" concept: overexploited, ambiguously defined, with fuzzy conceptual boundaries
- Scholars should build distinctive OMT theory scaffolding grounded in social science rather than relying on CS/economics framings

## Limitations & Critiques
Highly conceptual; selective review methodology may miss important contributions; no primary empirical data; the critique of rationality and anthropomorphism may not apply equally across all OMT research programs.
