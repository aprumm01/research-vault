# Constructivism


## Summary
Constructivism holds that learners actively construct knowledge through experience and social interaction rather than passively receiving transmitted content. Piaget's cognitive constructivism emphasizes individual schema development through assimilation (fitting new experience to existing schemas) and accommodation (revising schemas to fit new experience); Vygotsky's social constructivism foregrounds the zone of proximal development and the mediating role of language, culture, and more knowledgeable others. Dewey's pragmatist tradition emphasizes learning through experience and reflection. In this research vault, constructivism grounds both the instructional design approaches discussed (PBL, studio pedagogy, co-creation) and the critique of AI tools that may short-circuit constructive learning processes by providing answers before struggle.

## Key Concepts
- Schema construction, assimilation, and accommodation (Piaget)
- Zone of proximal development and scaffolding (Vygotsky)
- Learning through experience and reflection (Dewey)
- Social construction of knowledge through dialogue and collaboration
- Constructivism vs. instructivism: implications for AI-assisted learning
