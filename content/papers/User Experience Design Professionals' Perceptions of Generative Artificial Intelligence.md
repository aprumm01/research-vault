## Summary
Li et al.'s CHI 2025 paper presents a comprehensive study of UX design professionals' perceptions of generative AI, examining how practitioners across roles (UX researchers, interaction designers, content designers, managers) understand and integrate generative AI. The study finds significant role-specific variation in adoption driven by different relationships to knowledge, creativity, and craft. UX researchers show more skepticism than interaction designers due to the centrality of contextual human understanding in research work.

## Key Concepts
- UX professional perceptions of generative AI
- Role-differentiated AI adoption (UXR vs. IxD vs. content)
- Creative craft and AI mediation in UX
- UX professional identity in AI context
- CHI 2025 empirical study

## Theoretical Framework
Professional cognition; human-AI collaboration; technology acceptance; draws on UX practice research.

## Methods
Semi-structured interviews with UX professionals across roles; thematic analysis of perceptions and adoption strategies.

## Main Arguments
- UX professionals' AI perceptions are shaped by role-specific relationships to knowledge and craft
- UX researchers more skeptical than IxD practitioners regarding AI
- Creative craft concerns drive mixed attitudes among experienced practitioners
- Field needs role-aware AI integration frameworks

## Limitations & Critiques
CHI venue sample may skew toward sophisticated practitioners; perceptions may shift with AI tool evolution; does not track actual behavior.
