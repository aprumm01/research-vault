# Mapping Value Sensitive Design onto AI for Social Good Principles

## Summary
Extends Value Sensitive Design (VSD) to address two challenges unique to machine learning AI: the opacity of learning processes (making it difficult to understand how values are embedded) and value disembodiment (AI systems adapting in ways that drift from intended values over time). Proposes a threefold modified VSD approach integrating AI4SG principles, distinguishing promoted from respected values, and extending assessment across the full technology lifecycle.

## Key Concepts
- Value Sensitive Design (VSD)
- AI for Social Good (AI4SG) principles
- Value disembodiment
- Transparency and accountability
- Full lifecycle assessment

## Theoretical Framework
VSD framework (Friedman et al.); AI4SG principles as design norms; SARS-CoV-2 contact tracing app as illustrative case for applying the modified approach.

## Methods
Conceptual/theoretical; no original empirical data; framework development illustrated through a concrete contact tracing application use case.

## Main Arguments
- ML-based AI poses two challenges to standard VSD: opacity of learning and value drift through adaptation
- AI4SG principles (fairness, transparency, accountability, etc.) can serve as design norms from which specific requirements are derived
- VSD must distinguish values the design promotes (actively advances) from those it merely respects (does not violate)
- Lifecycle monitoring is essential to catch unintended value consequences as AI systems learn and adapt post-deployment

## Limitations & Critiques
Theoretical framework not yet empirically validated; AI4SG principles remain somewhat fuzzy and contested across different stakeholder groups; the contact tracing example is illustrative rather than evaluative; operationalizing the framework in practice requires significant additional work.
