# Negotiating AI(s) futures competing imaginaries of AI by stakeholders in the US, China, and Germany

## Summary
This paper examines how artificial intelligence (AI) imaginaries are negotiated by key stakeholders in the United States, China, and Germany, focusing on how public perceptions and discourses shape AI as a sociotechnical phenomenon Drawing on the concept of sociotechnical imaginaries in public communication, the study explores how stakeholders from industry, government, academia, media and civil society actively co-construct and contest visions of the future of AI The comparative analysis challenges the notion that national perceptions are monolithic, highlighting the complex and heterogeneous discursive processes surrounding AI.

## Key Concepts
- **Sociotechnical imaginaries** of AI negotiated by stakeholders
- Comparative analysis across US, China, and Germany
- **Competing visions** of AI futures by industry, government, academia, media, civil society
- Public perceptions and discourses shaping AI as sociotechnical phenomenon
- Heterogeneous and complex discursive processes (not monolithic national perceptions)
- Co-construction and contestation of AI futures
- Cross-national differences and similarities in AI imaginaries

## Theoretical Framework
- **Sociotechnical imaginaries** (Jasanoff & Kim): Collectively held visions of desirable futures
- **Public communication** of science and technology
- **Discourse analysis**: Examining how AI futures are rhetorically constructed
- Comparative policy and innovation studies

## Methods
case study, interview

## Main Arguments
- AI imaginaries are actively negotiated by diverse stakeholders, not predetermined
- US, China, and Germany show both similarities and differences in AI visions
- National AI perceptions are not monolithic but heterogeneous and contested
- Multiple stakeholder groups (industry, government, academia, media, civil society) co-construct AI futures
- Public communication processes shape AI as sociotechnical phenomenon
- Comparative analysis reveals complex discursive negotiations across contexts
- Understanding competing imaginaries essential for responsible AI governance

## Limitations & Critiques
- Three-country comparison may not capture full global diversity of AI imaginaries
- Stakeholder categories (industry, government, etc.) may be too broad
- Temporal snapshot may miss evolution of imaginaries over time
- Balance between national-level and stakeholder-level analysis
- Case study and interview methodology captures explicit discourses but may miss implicit assumptions
- Need for ongoing comparative research as AI imaginaries continue evolving
