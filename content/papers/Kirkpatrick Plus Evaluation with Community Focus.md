# Kirkpatrick Plus: Evaluation and Continuous Improvement with a Community Focus

## Summary
This article extends Donald Kirkpatrick's classic four-level evaluation framework (Reaction, Learning, Behavior, Results) by adding a fifth level focused on societal value-added. The authors argue that in the changing organizational environment of the late 1990s, training evaluation must go beyond organizational results to consider contributions to external clients and communities. They integrate Kirkpatrick's model with Kaufman's Organizational Elements Model (OEM) to create a proactive evaluation framework oriented toward continuous improvement rather than summative judgment.

## Key Concepts
- **Kirkpatrick's Four Levels**: Reaction, Learning, Behavior, Results - the dominant training evaluation framework
- **Level 5 (Societal Contributions)**: Proposed extension examining value-added to external stakeholders and society
- **Organizational Elements Model (OEM)**: Kaufman's framework connecting inputs, processes, products, outputs, and outcomes
- **Mega Results**: Societal-level outcomes beyond organizational boundaries
- **Proactive Evaluation**: Evaluation beginning with societal outcomes and working backward
- **Reactive Evaluation**: Traditional approach starting with training inputs and working forward
- **Costs-Consequences Analysis**: Method for comparing intervention costs to results at multiple levels

## Theoretical Framework
The paper integrates two major frameworks:
1. **Kirkpatrick's Evaluation Model** (1959): The standard four-level hierarchy for training evaluation
2. **Kaufman's Organizational Elements Model**: A systems planning framework connecting organizational elements to societal outcomes

The authors argue these frameworks are compatible and complementary, with OEM extending Kirkpatrick by adding societal considerations and reframing evaluation as continuous improvement rather than post-hoc judgment.

## Main Arguments
1. **Societal Value Missing**: Kirkpatrick's model ends at organizational results, ignoring broader social impact
2. **Proactive Approach**: Evaluation should begin with desired societal outcomes and work backward to design training
3. **Continuous Improvement**: Evaluation should be formative and ongoing, not just summative
4. **Level 1 Revision**: Separating process evaluation (acceptability) from resource evaluation (quality/availability)
5. **Costs-Consequences Integration**: ROI analysis can be conducted at any Kirkpatrick level using OEM structure
6. **External Accountability**: Organizations increasingly must demonstrate value to communities, not just shareholders
7. **Under-Utilization of Upper Levels**: Only 2% of organizations evaluate at Level 4, indicating need for better frameworks

## Limitations & Critiques
- Published in 1998, predates major developments in learning analytics and digital evaluation
- Societal outcomes are difficult to measure and attribute to specific training interventions
- May add complexity that discourages evaluation altogether
- Assumes organizational willingness to consider societal impact
- Limited empirical validation of the proposed framework
- Does not address power dynamics in defining "societal value"
- Corporate training focus may not transfer to other educational contexts
