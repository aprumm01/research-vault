## Summary
Lim, Stolterman, and Tenenberg's foundational paper offers a theoretical analysis of prototyping, arguing that prototypes function as both "filters" (foregrounding certain design dimensions while suppressing others) and "manifestations" (making design ideas concrete and communicable). The paper rejects the notion that prototyping is merely about testing finished ideas, instead positioning it as the primary medium through which design thinking develops. By understanding prototypes through these two functions simultaneously, designers and researchers gain a more nuanced account of why prototyping is central to design practice.

## Key Concepts
- Prototype as filter (selective representation of design dimensions)
- Prototype as manifestation (externalization of design intent)
- Dimensions of prototyping (material, visual, functional, spatial, temporal)
- Fidelity vs. focus in prototype design
- Prototyping as epistemic practice

## Theoretical Framework
Philosophy of design practice; draws on Stolterman's design theory, Schön's reflective practice, and design cognition research to build a conceptual framework for understanding prototype function.

## Methods
Conceptual analysis and framework development; draws on design theory literature and examples from professional and student design practice.

## Main Arguments
- A prototype is not a simplified version of a final product; it is a purposively constructed artifact for exploring specific design dimensions
- The "filter" function explains why prototypes with different fidelity serve different investigative purposes
- The "manifestation" function explains how prototypes make tacit design thinking visible and shareable
- Understanding these two functions together helps designers make principled prototyping decisions rather than defaulting to habit

## Limitations & Critiques
The framework is conceptual and does not include systematic empirical validation; some argue the distinction between filter and manifestation collapses in practice; the framework focuses on individual prototyping and undertheorizes collaborative prototyping dynamics.
