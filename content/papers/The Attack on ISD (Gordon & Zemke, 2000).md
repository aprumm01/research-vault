# The Attack on ISD (Gordon & Zemke, 2000)

# Study Partner Note: "The Attack on ISD"

## Summary
Gordon and Zemke argue that Instructional Systems Design (ISD), the dominant ADDIE-based model in corporate training for decades, is increasingly problematic and outdated. The paper matters because it challenges the foundational methodology of the training profession, presenting critiques from respected industry insiders about ISD's speed, coherence, effectiveness, and underlying assumptions.

## Key Concepts
- **ADDIE Model**: The five-step framework (Assess, Design, Develop, Instruct, Evaluate) that forms the core of ISD
- **ISD (Instructional Systems Design)**: The dominant "systems approach" methodology for corporate training design
- **Performance Gap**: The discrepancy between desired job performance and actual performance that triggers training needs
- **Needs Analysis**: The investigative process to determine whether a performance problem is actually a training problem
- **Task/Job Analysis**: Identifying what expert performers do and what trainees must know to replicate that performance

## Theoretical Framework
The paper employs a critical industry review lens, examining ISD through systems thinking while questioning whether that systems approach remains adequate for modern training challenges. The authors don't propose a single alternative theory but synthesize critiques from rival approaches (action learning, experiential learning, self-directed learning).

## Methods
This is a journalistic/industry analysis paper based on interviews with six training experts and synthesis of informal professional discussions at conferences and online forums. The authors organize criticisms thematically rather than conducting empirical research.

## Main Arguments
- ISD is too slow and cumbersome for contemporary training needs
- The model lacks coherent theoretical grounding ("no there there")
- ISD, when applied as designed, produces ineffective training solutions
- ISD is built on outdated philosophical assumptions about how learning and performance work

## Limitations & Critiques
The paper relies on anecdotal evidence from industry insiders rather than empirical data, and doesn't provide detailed alternative frameworks—it diagnoses problems without prescribing solutions. The critique may underestimate ISD's flexibility or fail to distinguish between flawed *applications* of the model versus inherent limitations of the methodology itself.
