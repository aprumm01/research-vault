# Design Thinking and Agile Design

## Summary
This paper examines the relationship between Design Thinking — a human-centered, divergent-convergent problem-solving methodology — and Agile software development — an iterative, sprint-based development approach. The authors argue that while the two frameworks are complementary in principle (both emphasize iteration, user feedback, and embracing change), they can conflict in practice due to different timelines, team structures, and validation standards. The paper offers a synthesis model for integrating Design Thinking's front-end exploration with Agile's development cycles.

## Key Concepts
- Design Thinking (empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test)
- Agile software development (sprints, backlog, user stories)
- Double diamond process model
- Human-centered design in software contexts
- Sprint 0 and discovery phases

## Theoretical Framework
Process methodology synthesis; draws on IDEO's design thinking framework, the British Design Council's double diamond, and Agile manifesto principles to identify complementarities and tensions.

## Methods
Comparative framework analysis; case study of integrated Design Thinking-Agile teams; practitioner interviews and literature review.

## Main Arguments
- Design Thinking excels at problem framing and user research; Agile excels at efficient, iterative delivery — neither alone is sufficient
- The most common friction occurs when Agile sprint timelines are too short for meaningful design exploration
- A hybrid approach requires organizational commitment to "Sprint 0" or "Discovery" phases before development sprints begin
- Shared vocabulary between designers and developers is essential for effective integration

## Limitations & Critiques
Integration success depends heavily on organizational context and team maturity; the synthesis model may work differently in product vs. service design contexts; "Design Thinking" itself is a contested and varied practice across organizations.
