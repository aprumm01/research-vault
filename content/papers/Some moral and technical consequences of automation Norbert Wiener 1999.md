# Some moral and technical consequences of automation Norbert Wiener 1999

## Summary
Reflections Introduction to Wiener's Article The following article by Norbert Wiener makes two points - the first explains the possibility of making computer programs 'human like" by building in learning and adaptivity in them; the second cautions us on the danger that may arise due to the large difference in time constant between human thinking and action and machine's computation and action Wiener makes the point that a good chess playing program must take into account not only the moves as they occur in individual games but the record of games previously played by its opponent This point is quite prophetic.

## Key Concepts
- Machines transcending designer limitations through speed and precision
- Learning machines that adapt policy based on game records
- Multiple levels of consideration in decision-making (tactics, strategy, meta-strategy)
- Logical types (Russell) applied to game-playing and machine learning
- Time scale differences between human and machine operation
- Machines as literal-minded agencies (sorcerer's apprentice problem)

## Theoretical Framework
Cybernetics; Game theory critique (von Neumann-Morgenstern); Bertrand Russell's logical types; Feedback systems and control theory

## Methods
Conceptual analysis; Game-playing machine examples (checkers, chess); Historical military strategy examples (Nelson, Napoleon); Thought experiments and analogies (slavery, sorcerer's apprentice)

## Main Arguments
- Assumption that "nothing can come out of machine which has not been put into it" should be rejected
- Machines can and do transcend limitations of designers in speed, precision, and performance
- Even when machines don't transcend intelligence, they transcend humans in task performance
- Understanding of machine performance may be delayed until long after task completion - criticism ineffective until after relevance
- By slowness of human actions, effective control of machines may be nullified
- Game-playing machines should consider not just individual game moves but records of previous games (like great generals Nelson, Napoleon studying opponents)
- Learning machines show originality in tactics and strategy weighting - can defeat their programmers
- Checker machines after 10-20 hours show marked superiority to their programmers
- Complete subservience and complete intelligence do not go together (slavery paradox)
- Human brain superior as self-organizing system that modifies itself rather than rigid accuracy/speed
- Development of science is control/communication process on 50-year time scale vs. individual's limited sector

## Limitations & Critiques
- Removal of effective understanding from designer/operator of many stages by which machine reaches conclusions
- Ataxia (lack of information) can be as much deprivation as paralysis - may not know when to turn machine off until too late
- When machine operates at pace we cannot keep, we may not know until too late when to intervene
- Disastrous results expected when two agencies foreign to each other coupled to achieve common purpose with incomplete communication
- If mechanical agency action so fast/irrevocable we cannot intervene, must ensure purpose in machine is purpose we really desire, not merely colorful imitation
- Machine and human operate on distinct time scales - do not gear together without serious difficulties
- Individual scientist can only contemplate very limited sector of long-term science process
- Communication between individual and process difficult and limited
- Liaison between man and process requires imaginative forward glance at history that is difficult, exacting, only limitedly achievable
- Naive assumption that faster we rush to employ new powers the better must be questioned
- Must exert full strength of imagination to examine where full use of new modalities may lead
