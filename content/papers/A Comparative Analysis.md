## Summary
This chapter from Fitzpatrick's evaluation textbook synthesizes and compares the major schools of thought in program evaluation, organizing approaches into four categories: expertise-oriented, consumer-oriented, decision-oriented, and participant-oriented. The chapter argues that no single approach is universally superior and that evaluators should select frameworks based on the purpose, context, and stakeholders of the evaluation. It remains a foundational reference for understanding the epistemological diversity within evaluation practice.

## Key Concepts
- Expertise-oriented evaluation (expert judgment)
- Consumer-oriented evaluation (product quality standards)
- Decision-oriented evaluation (management information)
- Participant-oriented evaluation (stakeholder empowerment)

## Theoretical Framework
Meta-evaluative framework comparing evaluation epistemologies; pragmatic pluralism that values multiple approaches rather than advocating for a single model.

## Methods
Synthetic comparative analysis of existing evaluation frameworks and schools of thought; textbook chapter from Fitzpatrick, Sanders, and Worthen's Program Evaluation: Alternative Approaches and Practical Guidelines.

## Main Arguments
- Each evaluation tradition reflects different assumptions about knowledge, value, and the purpose of evaluation
- No single approach is sufficient for all evaluation contexts
- Evaluators must understand the strengths and limitations of multiple paradigms
- The choice of evaluation approach should match the program's goals and stakeholder needs
