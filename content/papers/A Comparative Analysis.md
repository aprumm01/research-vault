# A Comparative Analysis

## Summary
Chapter 10 of Fitzpatrick, Sanders, and Worthen's *Program Evaluation: Alternative Approaches and Practical Guidelines* synthesizes the major evaluation traditions into a comparative framework organized into four categories: (1) expertise- and consumer-oriented approaches (judging overall quality), (2) approaches oriented to program characteristics, (3) decision-oriented approaches, and (4) participant-oriented approaches. The authors identify the conceptual contributions, methodological strengths, and contextual limitations of each tradition, arguing that no single approach is universally superior.

## Key Concepts
- **Expertise-oriented evaluation**: Expert judgment as the primary evaluation mechanism (e.g., accreditation models)
- **Consumer-oriented evaluation**: Evaluation to serve end-users and clients (Scriven's Consumer Reports model)
- **Decision-oriented evaluation**: Evaluation designed to inform specific decisions (CIPP model)
- **Participant-oriented evaluation**: Responsive and naturalistic approaches centering stakeholder voices
- **Methodological pluralism**: No single evaluation model is appropriate for all contexts

## Theoretical Framework
Comparative meta-evaluation. Synthesizes the conceptual foundations of major evaluation schools and assesses their practical utility across different evaluation purposes and contexts.

## Methods
Literature synthesis and expert analysis. Organizes existing evaluation frameworks into a typology and compares them across criteria: theoretical assumptions, methods, stakeholder orientation, and practical applicability.

## Main Arguments
1. Different evaluation purposes require different approaches; using only one model risks systematic blind spots
2. The four categories represent fundamentally different epistemological commitments and stakeholder orientations
3. Evaluators must be methodologically pluralistic, selecting approaches based on context
4. Understanding the conceptual roots of each approach is prerequisite to competent evaluation practice

## Limitations
Comparative framework inevitably simplifies complex traditions that differ internally; categorization schemes are analytical tools, not rigid taxonomies. The field has continued evolving since publication.
