# User Experience artificial intelligence Assessing the qualities of AI-infused systems

## Summary
SpringerBriefs in Applied Sciences and Technology PoliMI SpringerBriefs Davide Spallazzo · Martina Sciannamè · Mauro Ceconello User Experience + Artificial Intelligence Assessing the Qualities of AI-infused Systems SpringerBriefs in Applied Sciences and Technology PoliMI SpringerBriefs Series Editors Barbara Pernici, DEIB, Politecnico di Milano, Milano, Italy Stefano Della Torre, DABC, Politecnico di Milano, Milano, Italy Bianca M Colosimo, DMEC, Politecnico di Milano, Milano, Italy Tiziano Faravelli, DCHEM, Politecnico di Milano, Milano, Italy Roberto Paolucci, DICA, Politecnico di Milano, Milano, Italy Silvia Piardi, Design, Politecnico di Milano, Milano, Italy.

## Key Concepts
- AIXE (AI user eXperience Evaluation) scale as statistically validated quantitative assessment tool for AI-infused products
- Six core UX dimensions framework: pragmatic, hedonic, intelligence, trustworthiness, meaningfulness, and conversational
- AI-infused products' probabilistic and evolving nature presenting unique challenges due to opacity and behavioral modification over time
- Gap between technological advancement and user experience evolution requiring human-centered design intervention
- Four novel UX dimensions specific to AI systems: intelligence, trustworthiness, meaningfulness, and conversational quality
- Traditional UX evaluation methods insufficient for dynamic and complex AI systems requiring new assessment approaches
- Smart speakers as emblematic materialization of domestic AI systems revealing interaction and usability difficulties
- Meet-AI project as first systematic effort addressing peculiar UX challenges posed by embedded AI systems

## Theoretical Framework
- Multi-method research strategy combining umbrella, scoping, and systematic literature reviews
- User-centered validation approach involving expert users in UX dimension identification
- 33-question Likert-scale questionnaire covering 12 descriptors across 6 UX dimensions
- Statistical validation methodology including iterative refinement through multiple testing phases

## Methods
Comprehensive multi-method approach including three-stage literature review (umbrella, scoping, systematic) to assess existing UX evaluation methods' applicability to AI products. Expert user engagement for UX dimension validation. Iterative questionnaire development with statistical validation. Real-world application testing smart speakers with 1600 respondents from UK and USA in 2021 and 2023, enabling longitudinal and cross-cultural comparison.

## Main Arguments
- Traditional UX evaluation methods built for static systems inadequate for targeting dynamic AI complexity and evolution
- Four AI-specific dimensions (intelligence, trustworthiness, conversational, meaningfulness) resonate most strongly with users compared to traditional dimensions
- UX shortcomings in AI products stem from technologist-led development lacking human-centered focus on user needs
- Technologically sophisticated AI systems consistently deliver opaque and confusing interfaces betraying usability standards
- Users generally lack proper understanding of probabilistic AI system implications affecting their ability to effectively interact
- AIXE scale enables holistic evaluation capturing nuances of AI interactions while maintaining benchmarking rigor
- Smart speaker UX perceived as slightly above average (54%) with particular strengths in pragmatic and intelligence dimensions
- Declining trust and meaningfulness scores over time reveal growing skepticism about data privacy and perceived value

## Limitations & Critiques
- Sample restricted to UK and USA respondents limiting generalizability to other cultural and technological contexts
- Data collected after minimum 14-day usage period rather than immediately post-task introducing overall perception bias
- Quantitative nature permits comparability but only allows researcher speculation about rating motivations
- Focus on smart speakers as primary application case may not fully represent broader AI product categories
- Lack of qualitative investigations to enrich understanding of quantitative findings
- Generational satisfaction gaps suggesting tool may not equally capture experiences across all demographics
- Longitudinal data limited to two time points (2021, 2023) constraining trend analysis depth
- Ethical and societal impact dimensions acknowledged but not fully developed in assessment framework
- AIXE scale application primarily tested on mature products rather than prototypes in development stages
- Regional regulatory differences (European vs. US privacy environments) affecting trustworthiness perceptions not deeply explored
