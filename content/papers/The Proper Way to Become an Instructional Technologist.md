# The Proper Way to Become an Instructional Technologist

## Summary
The Proper Way to Become an Instructional Technologist Rieber, L Instructional Design Technology Instructional Technology Learning Design students I’m an instructional technologist, which means I: ● Help people learn new things ● Solve problems in education and training or find people who can.

## Key Concepts
- Instructional technologist as problem solver in education and training
- Diversity of pathways into the instructional technology field
- Relationship between theory, research, and practice in instructional design
- Studio-based approaches to graduate education
- Contextual and situated nature of instructional design work

## Theoretical Framework
Constructivist learning theory (Piagetian), Flow theory (Csikszentmihalyi), situated action theory, play theory

## Methods
Reflective essay and personal narrative examining professional identity formation

## Main Arguments
- There is no single "proper way" to become an instructional technologist; the field benefits from diverse backgrounds
- Instructional design in practice is messy, contextual, and idiosyncratic, not the linear process depicted in textbooks
- Graduate programs should focus on education (preparing for lifelong learning) rather than training (technical skills)
- Theory, research, and practice must be interdependent; practitioners should engage with theory while theorists ground work in practice
- The Wright Brothers' approach exemplifies ideal integration of theory, research, and hands-on practice
- Universities should prepare reflective practitioners who can adapt to changing contexts, not technicians performing specific jobs

## Limitations & Critiques
- Highly personal and reflective rather than empirical
- Limited exploration of specific graduate program structures or assessment approaches
- Acknowledges difficulty in teaching philosophical concepts effectively
