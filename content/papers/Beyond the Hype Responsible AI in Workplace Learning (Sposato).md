## Summary
Sposato argues for evidence-based rather than hype-driven approaches to responsible AI integration in workplace learning, calling for systematic evaluation of AI tools against established learning science principles before adoption. The paper reviews current AI integration practices in workplace learning and finds them largely driven by novelty and commercial interest rather than learning effectiveness evidence. It proposes principles for responsible AI adoption that center learner outcomes and ethical considerations.

## Key Concepts
- Evidence-based AI integration in workplace learning
- Responsible AI adoption frameworks
- Workplace learning effectiveness under AI
- Hype cycle critique of EdTech AI

## Theoretical Framework
Evidence-based practice and responsible AI frameworks applied to workplace learning; draws on learning science and organizational learning literature to establish evaluation criteria for AI tool adoption.

## Methods
Analytical/review paper examining current AI integration practices in workplace learning contexts; argues from learning science evidence rather than technology capability; published in workplace learning journal.

## Main Arguments
- Current AI integration in workplace learning is too often hype-driven rather than evidence-based
- Responsible AI adoption requires systematic evaluation against learning effectiveness criteria
- AI tools must be assessed against established principles of how adults learn in work contexts
- Organizations need responsible AI adoption frameworks that center learning outcomes over efficiency metrics
