# Beyond the Hype: Responsible AI in Workplace Learning (Sposato)

## Summary
Sposato provides an evidence-based framework for responsible AI integration in workplace learning by synthesizing findings from 180 papers through an integrative literature review. The paper reveals critical misalignments between how organizations are implementing AI in training and established learning principles: AI is effective for personalized content delivery and routine skill development but struggles to support transformative learning and social learning dynamics. The paper bridges social cognitive theory (Bandura) and organizational learning theory to offer practical guidance for evidence-based AI implementation decisions.

## Key Concepts
- Evidence-based AI integration in workplace learning
- Social cognitive theory (Bandura) applied to AI-mediated learning
- Organizational learning theory
- Personalized vs. transformative learning with AI
- Social learning dynamics and AI limitations

## Theoretical Framework
Integrative literature review following Torraco's framework; synthesizes social cognitive theory (individual learning via observation, modeling, self-efficacy) with organizational learning theory (collective knowledge creation, double-loop learning); identifies where AI tools support vs. undermine each framework's prescriptions.

## Methods
Integrative literature review; 180 papers from multiple databases; Boell and Cecez-Kecmanovic hermeneutic framework for iterative searching and reading; conceptual synthesis generating an integrated theoretical framework.

## Main Arguments
- Organizations are deploying AI in workplace learning driven by technological enthusiasm rather than evidence of pedagogical effectiveness — creating misalignment with established learning principles
- AI excels in delivering personalized content at scale but lacks the social and relational elements required for transformative and social learning — types of learning organizations most need for adaptation
- Cultural variation and ethical considerations (bias, privacy, transparency) substantially influence whether AI learning tools succeed or fail in specific organizational contexts
- Evidence-based implementation requires practitioners to match AI tool capabilities to specific learning objectives rather than applying AI as a universal solution

## Limitations
- Conceptual/review paper; specific implementation recommendations require empirical validation in target organizational contexts
- Rapidly evolving AI capabilities mean some of the literature reviewed may already be outdated
