# Integrating AI into Instructional Design for Higher Education

## Summary

This qualitative case study examines AI integration in a digital photography education course in Turkey, using the TPACK (Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge) framework and constructivist learning theory. The research explores how AI tools can enhance personalized learning while also identifying risks of student passivity and over-reliance on AI-generated content. The study offers practical insights for instructional designers seeking to balance AI capabilities with pedagogical goals.

## Key Concepts

- **TPACK framework**: Integration of technological, pedagogical, and content knowledge in teaching
- **Constructivist learning with AI**: Using AI as a tool for knowledge construction rather than passive consumption
- **Personalized learning**: AI-enabled adaptation to individual learner needs and pace
- **Over-reliance risk**: Danger of students becoming dependent on AI rather than developing independent skills
- **AI-enhanced feedback**: Real-time, personalized feedback on student work

## Theoretical Framework

Dual framework combining:
1. **TPACK (Mishra & Koehler, 2006)**: Framework for understanding technology integration in teaching
2. **Constructivist Learning Theory**: Students actively construct knowledge through experience and reflection

## Methods

- Qualitative case study design
- Setting: Digital photography course in Turkish university
- Data collection: Interviews with students and instructors, course observations, artifact analysis
- Thematic analysis of data

## Main Arguments

1. **Enhanced personalization**: AI tools effectively adapted to individual student skill levels and learning pace
2. **Risk of passivity**: Students sometimes accepted AI suggestions uncritically, reducing active learning
3. **TPACK application**: Successful AI integration requires deep understanding of all three knowledge domains
4. **Scaffolded introduction**: AI tools should be introduced gradually with explicit metacognitive instruction
5. **Balance imperative**: Instructors must balance AI assistance with activities requiring independent thinking

## Limitations & Critiques

- Single course case study limits generalizability
- Turkish higher education context may not transfer to other settings
- Focus on creative/visual domain may not apply to other disciplines
- Short-term study; long-term effects not examined
- Student self-report data may not reflect actual learning processes
