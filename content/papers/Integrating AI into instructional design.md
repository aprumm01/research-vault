# Integrating AI into instructional design

## Summary
Received: 7 Feb 2025 This study investigates the impact of artificial intelligence (AI)-supported education in higher education, specifically examining its integration into a digital photography course and its effects on both students and instructors A qualitative research methodology was employed, and participants were selected through purposive sampling The study involved one instructor and 38 students, with data collected through semi-structured interviews and analyzed using content analysis within a qualitative case study design.

## Key Concepts
- **TPACK Framework Application**: Integration requires balancing technological (AI tools), pedagogical (constructivist methods), and content (photography skills) knowledge for effective instruction
- **MRK Instructional Design Model**: Task-centered, flexible oval structure allowing initiation at any stage with continuous feedback and iterative revisions particularly suited for AI integration
- **Individualized Learning Facilitation**: AI enables personalized content adaptation, real-time feedback, and adaptive learning pathways tailored to individual learner needs and pacing
- **Immediate Feedback Mechanisms**: AI-powered real-time critique of composition, exposure, lighting helps students understand fundamental photography principles through iterative practice
- **Technical Skill Development**: AI tools assist with adjusting technical parameters while encouraging experimentation and reflective creative practice
- **Critical Evaluation Enhancement**: Students develop ability to assess AI-generated feedback rather than accepting outputs uncritically
- **Academic Language Proficiency**: AI supports development of professional terminology and communication skills in technical creative disciplines
- **Learner Passivity Risk**: Overreliance on AI may diminish student agency, reducing active participation and independent analysis
- **Information Reliability Challenges**: AI outputs susceptible to hallucinations, biases, and misinformation requiring robust verification practices

## Theoretical Framework
- **TPACK Framework**: Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge emphasizing intersection of technology, pedagogy, and content (Mishra & Koehler, 2006)
- **Constructivist Learning Theory**: Learners as active knowledge constructors through interaction and reflection (Vygotsky, 1978)
- **MRK Instructional Design Model**: Holistic framework with flexible entry points and continuous feedback loops

## Methods
Qualitative case study design with purposive criterion sampling. Semi-structured interviews with 1 instructor and 38 students from Digital Photography in Nature course at Turkish university. Data collected through Zoom (40-min student interviews) and in-person (80-min instructor interview). Content analysis using both manual coding and MAXQDA 2020. Trustworthiness ensured through credibility, transferability, dependability, and confirmability criteria. Peer debriefing with 25% blind coding by independent PhD researchers achieving consensus on 5 themes and 17 codes.

## Main Arguments
- **Pedagogical Framework Necessity**: AI integration requires sound pedagogical framework rather than unstructured implementation for effective outcomes
- **Applied Disciplines Underexplored**: Comprehensive AI integration across all course phases remains underexplored in hands-on creative fields despite transformative potential
- **Dual Impact Recognition**: AI enhances learning efficiency while introducing risks of passivity and uncritical acceptance
- **Socioeconomic Access Mediator**: AI tools mitigate equipment access barriers by providing virtual alternatives to expensive specialized equipment
- **Human Complement Not Replacement**: AI should augment rather than replace human instruction in pedagogical decision-making
- **Educator Competency Requirement**: Successful implementation requires enhanced teacher competencies supported by professional development programs

## Limitations & Critiques
- **Single-Course Context**: One digital photography course at one Turkish university limits transferability to other disciplines and contexts
- **Limited Baseline Comparison**: Lacked control group receiving traditional instruction, making causal attribution difficult
- **Self-Report Bias**: Interview data may reflect post-hoc rationalization rather than actual learning processes
- **Novice AI User Sample**: 92% had minimal prior AI experience, potentially amplifying both enthusiasm and concerns
- **Short-Term Assessment**: Single semester without longitudinal tracking of skill retention or continued AI adoption
- **Missing Quantitative Outcomes**: Qualitative design lacks measurable learning outcome comparisons between AI-supported and traditional approaches
