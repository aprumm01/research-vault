# AI Hasn’t Fixed Teamwork, But It Shifted Collaborative


## Abstract
When AI entered the workplace, many believed it could re- shape teamwork as profoundly as it boosted individual pro- ductivity. Wo

## Summary
This paper examines how AI tools have changed collaborative dynamics in professional teams, arguing that while AI has not solved fundamental teamwork challenges (coordination, communication, trust), it has shifted where and how those challenges manifest. Through case studies and interviews with teams using AI tools, the authors document how AI changes the distribution of work within teams, the nature of handoffs, and the skills required for effective collaboration. The paper identifies new collaboration competencies required in AI-mediated team contexts.

## Key Concepts
- AI and team collaboration dynamics
- Work redistribution in AI-augmented teams
- Handoff challenges in human-AI-human workflows
- New collaboration competencies for AI-mediated work
- Trust and communication in AI-assisted teamwork

## Theoretical Framework
CSCW (Computer-Supported Cooperative Work); team cognition; human-AI teaming; draws on distributed cognition and coordination theory to analyze AI effects on team dynamics.

## Methods
Case studies of professional teams using AI tools; qualitative interviews with team members; observation of AI-mediated collaboration sessions; analysis of coordination patterns.

## Main Arguments
- AI tools shift where collaboration challenges occur rather than eliminating them: new friction points emerge around AI output evaluation and integration
- Teams develop new handoff practices for AI-generated outputs that require new competencies in quality assessment and contextual appropriateness evaluation
- Trust in AI varies within teams, creating asymmetric human-AI relationships that affect team coordination
- Effective AI-augmented teams develop shared norms around AI use that individual AI adoption cannot achieve

## Limitations & Critiques
Case study design limits generalizability; team AI tool use is rapidly evolving; the specific tools studied may not represent the landscape broadly; long-term effects on team collaboration culture are not tracked.
