# Reconciling ADDIE and Agile Instructional Design Models: A Case Study

## Summary
This case study examines the tension between ADDIE (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation) — the dominant sequential instructional design model — and Agile development methodologies that emphasize iteration, speed, and user involvement. The author documents an institutional effort to reconcile these approaches, arguing that neither pure ADDIE nor pure Agile adequately meets the demands of modern instructional development projects. The proposed reconciliation retains ADDIE's analytical rigor while incorporating Agile's iterative development cycles and rapid prototyping.

## Key Concepts
- ADDIE model (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation)
- Agile instructional design
- Iterative development and rapid prototyping
- Scrum-based ID workflow
- Design-development cycle compression

## Theoretical Framework
Process methodology for instructional design; draws on software engineering's Agile manifesto and traditional systems-based instructional design to propose a hybrid methodology.

## Methods
Case study of an instructional design team's adoption of a hybrid ADDIE-Agile approach; qualitative documentation of process changes and outcomes.

## Main Arguments
- ADDIE's linear structure creates bottlenecks and misaligns with client expectations for iterative feedback
- Agile methodologies without ADDIE's analytical front-end risk building efficient solutions to the wrong problems
- A hybrid approach requires front-end analysis (ADDIE) followed by sprint-based development cycles (Agile)
- Institutional adoption requires training and a culture shift beyond adopting new tools or terminology

## Limitations & Critiques
Single case study limits generalizability; the hybrid model may work differently in corporate vs. academic contexts; the case predates more recent developments in Agile instructional design literature.
