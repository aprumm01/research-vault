# Exploring Challenges to Support Designers Learning to Co-create with AI Manufacturing Design Tools (Gmeiner et al., CMU)

## Abstract

AI-based design tools are proliferating in manufacturing and industrial design contexts. This paper investigates the challenges and opportunities that arise when designers learn to co-create with these AI-based tools, particularly in the context of manufacturing design. The CMU HCI Institute team examines how learning to work effectively with AI tools in high-stakes manufacturing environments differs from general creative AI tool use, and what support structures designers need.

## Summary

Gmeiner et al. investigate the learning challenges designers face when incorporating AI tools into manufacturing design workflows, finding that co-creation with AI in industrial/manufacturing contexts involves distinct challenges around trust, interpretability, and domain knowledge that differ from creative AI tool use. The paper bridges HCI education and professional learning, offering implications for how design tools should be designed to support onboarding and skill development. The CMU context grounds the work in rigorous HCI methodology.

## Key Concepts
- Human-AI co-creation in manufacturing design
- Learning challenges in AI tool adoption
- Designer trust and AI interpretability
- Support structures for AI-assisted professional learning

## Theoretical Framework
Draws on human-AI interaction and learning science frameworks; likely uses participatory design or contextual inquiry methods to understand designers' learning experiences with manufacturing AI tools.

## Methods
Qualitative empirical study (likely interviews or design probes) with industrial/manufacturing designers learning to use AI-based design tools; analysis of challenges and opportunities organized around learning dimensions.

## Main Arguments
- Learning to co-create with AI in manufacturing design is a distinct challenge from using creative AI tools
- Designers need scaffolded support structures, not just tool tutorials, to develop effective AI collaboration skills
- Trust and interpretability are central barriers to effective AI co-creation in high-stakes design contexts
- Tool design should incorporate explicit support for the learning process, not just the design task
