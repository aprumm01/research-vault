# Competences for the Modern Designer: Systematic Literature Review (2026)


## Abstract
Higher education institutions offering education in design need to review their teaching content in relation to a changing society and digitalization. In emerging post-digital era higher education is in a state of change. Metacognitive skills and a focus on digitalized world’s competencies are becoming integral components of curricula in modern days. During the 21 st century emphasis in design education has been on design thinking, user-centered design, and the utilization of service designers. On the other hand, contemporary designers are expected to have the capacity to navigate complexity and address wicked problems. Designers are now being called upon to contribute to complex problems, in a highly decentralized and heterogeneous contexts within development processes that are increasingly connected. The change in the field of design and in the professional definition is so rapid that there is no curriculum that can predict the future. This raises two research questions: What are future competences for designers? How these changes should be considered in the design education? The scope of this systematic literature review encompasses scholarly works published in international peer-reviewed academic journals during the period spanning from 1.1.2018 to 31.3.2024. The primary objective is to discern evolving perceptions of the design profession in the future and to identify the key competencies that emerging designers should adeptly master as they navigate the landscape of a digitalized working environment.

**DOI:** [10.1177/14740222251342646](https://doi.org/10.1177/14740222251342646)

## Summary
This 2026 systematic literature review examines the competences required for modern designers, synthesizing recent research to update understanding of design expertise in contexts where AI tools have become pervasive. The review identifies core stable competencies (empathy, systems thinking, conceptual development, iteration) alongside new AI-mediated competencies (AI direction, quality evaluation of AI outputs, ethical AI use). The paper argues that the core of design expertise is shifting from production skills toward meta-cognitive and judgment competencies that complement AI capabilities.

## Key Concepts
- Modern designer competencies framework
- Systematic literature review methodology
- Stable vs. AI-emergent competences in design
- Metacognitive design competences
- Design judgment in AI-mediated practice

## Theoretical Framework
Competency-based education; design expertise research; systematic review methodology; draws on design education and professional competency literature from 2010-2026 to identify shifts in the competency landscape.

## Methods
Systematic literature review following PRISMA protocol; analysis of design competency frameworks from design education, professional organization, and empirical research sources; coding and synthesis of competency categories and their AI-era evolution.

## Main Arguments
- Core design competencies centered on empathy, systems thinking, and conceptual development remain stable and central in the AI era
- New AI-specific competencies (directing AI, evaluating AI quality, ethical AI deployment) are now necessary additions to the design competency framework
- The center of gravity in design expertise is shifting from production skills toward judgment, curation, and meta-cognitive awareness
- Design education programs need to explicitly revise curricula to develop the judgment competencies that complement rather than replicate AI capabilities

## Limitations & Critiques
Systematic review captures published literature which may lag practice; competency frameworks risk reifying skills that are fluid and context-dependent; the 2026 publication date means it captures AI-era competency discussion but may not yet have empirical validation of the new competency claims.
