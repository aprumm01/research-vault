# Co-Designing with Transformers: GenAI in Interactive System Design Education

## Summary

This DIS 2025 paper presents a mixed-methods study examining how graduate HCI students used GenAI tools in an interactive device design course at Cornell Tech. Through 12 post-class group interviews with 17 students, the researchers identified nine main use cases of GenAI in HCI design, mapped them onto the Double Diamond design process, and developed a taxonomy of usage patterns (Benchmark, Booster, Executor, Amplifier). The study reveals both benefits (enhanced creativity, faster iteration) and risks (shallow learning, reduced reflection) of GenAI in design education.

## Key Concepts

- **Nine GenAI use cases**: Brainstorming, use case development, storyboard generation, asset generation, prototyping coding assistance, hardware integration, documentation, design reflection, learning support
- **Usage taxonomy**: Benchmark (comparison), Booster (assistance), Executor (delegation), Amplifier (beyond capability)
- **Double Diamond mapping**: GenAI use across Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver phases
- **Signal crafting in academia**: Students' strategic disclosure of GenAI use (connects to professional context)
- **Sentiment categories**: High efficacy, assisted success, uncertain performance, under-performing, negative utility, ethical concerns

## Theoretical Framework

- **Double Diamond Design Process**: Framework for mapping GenAI use to design phases
- **User-centered design (UCD) principles**: Lens for evaluating AI's impact on design practice
- **HCI education literature**: Context for understanding technology integration in design curricula

## Methods

- Mixed methods study at Cornell Tech (Fall 2023)
- Graduate-level Interactive Device Design course
- 48 students in two-week sprints, final project
- 12 group interviews with 17 students (10 groups participated)
- Multi-stage coding: inductive coding, deductive thematic analysis, manual sentiment analysis
- Journey mapping to UX process phases

## Main Arguments

1. **Widespread adoption**: All students used GenAI despite no instructor encouragement; most for prototyping
2. **Execution phase dominance**: GenAI most beneficial during Develop phase; less useful for Discovery and Reflection
3. **Sentiment varies by use case**: Documentation and learning support mostly positive; design reflection and learning support more negative/uncertain
4. **Shallow learning risk**: Amplifier and Executor patterns may bypass learning and critical reflection
5. **Attribution challenges**: Students struggled to accurately recall and attribute their GenAI use
6. **Ethical concerns**: Students worried about skill development, academic integrity, and AI's impact on learning
7. **Financial accessibility**: Subscription costs create equity concerns among students

## Limitations & Critiques

- Single course at elite institution (Cornell)
- Master's students may differ from undergraduates
- Self-reported data subject to recall and social desirability bias
- Rapidly evolving GenAI tools may change dynamics
- HCI/design context may not generalize to other disciplines
- Interview timing (post-semester) may affect memory accuracy
