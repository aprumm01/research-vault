# The Anatomy of Prototypes: Prototypes as Filters, Prototypes as Manifestations of Design Ideas (Lim et al., 2008)

## Summary
Lim, Stolterman, and Tenenberg propose a conceptual framework for understanding prototypes in HCI and design: the anatomy of prototypes. They argue that prototypes serve two fundamental functions beyond evaluation. First, prototypes act as filters, selectively revealing certain design qualities while suppressing others, allowing designers to isolate and explore specific aspects of a design space. Second, prototypes are manifestations, physical or digital embodiments of design ideas that enable reflection, communication, and refinement. The framework challenges the dominant view of prototypes as evaluation tools and repositions them as generative instruments for design thinking.

## Key Concepts
- Prototypes as filters: selectively revealing design qualities
- Prototypes as manifestations: embodying design ideas
- Fundamental prototyping principle: simplest form that filters qualities of interest
- Design space exploration and traversal
- Distinction from requirements-oriented prototyping in software engineering

## Theoretical Framework
Human-computer interaction and design theory; draws on Schon's reflective practice, design space theory (Goel and Pirolli; Moran and Carroll), and Buxton's sketching framework; grounded in two case studies of design practice; positions prototyping within a broader philosophy of design as reflection-in-action rather than requirement satisfaction.

## Methods
Theoretical framework development grounded in two case studies of actual design practice; qualitative analysis of how designers use prototypes during early and mid-stage design exploration; builds on prior HCI prototyping literature (experience prototyping, paper prototyping, low vs. high fidelity).

## Main Arguments
- Prototypes are not primarily evaluation tools; they are design-thinking enablers that designers use organically throughout the entire design process
- The filter dimension of prototypes allows designers to isolate specific design qualities for examination without committing to other qualities
- The manifestation dimension enables designers to communicate rationales and stimulate reflection about design decisions
- The anatomy framework provides a systematic language for prototype planning that goes beyond the low/high fidelity distinction, which is too coarse for design practice

## Limitations & Critiques
The framework is derived from a limited number of case studies, which constrains the generalizability of the findings. The theoretical account of prototypes as filters and manifestations is compelling but abstract; the authors acknowledge that operationalizing the framework for design education or practice guidance requires further work. The framework was developed specifically in HCI and design contexts; its applicability to other engineering and product development domains is not directly addressed.
