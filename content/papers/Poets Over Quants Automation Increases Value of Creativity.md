## Summary
This Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin study examines how automation and AI threats change people's lay beliefs about which skills are most valuable in the workforce. Across multiple experiments, the authors find that perceived threats from automation and AI increase the value people place on creative skills relative to quantitative skills—a psychological response suggesting that when machines can "out-quant" humans, creativity becomes a marker of distinctive human value.

## Key Concepts
- Automation and skill valuation
- Creativity vs. quantitative skills
- Lay beliefs about workforce
- AI threat perception
- Labor market psychology

## Theoretical Framework
Social psychology of lay theories and motivated cognition frame the investigation of how automation threats shift people's implicit beliefs about human value in the labor market.

## Methods
Experimental psychology; multiple studies examining how manipulated automation threat affects participants' ratings of creative vs. quantitative skill importance; published in Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin.

## Main Arguments
- Automation threats cause people to value creative skills more highly relative to analytical/quantitative skills
- The psychological response to AI threat is to emphasize distinctly human creative capacities
- These lay beliefs may influence educational and career choices in response to AI adoption
- Creativity is culturally constructed as the last domain of irreducibly human value in an automated world
