# s10639-024-13043-w

## Summary
There is a growing consensus that AI literacy requires a holistic lens, including not only technical knowledge and skills but also social and ethical considerations Yet, providing holistic AI education for upper-primary students remains challenging due to the abstract and complex nature of AI and a lack of pedagogical experiences in schools Against this backdrop, this study employs a design-based research (DBR) methodology to develop pedagogical solutions and theoretical knowledge.

## Key Concepts
- Holistic AI literacy encompassing technical knowledge, skills, and social/ethical considerations
- Embodied constructionist pedagogical approach integrating embodied analogies with constructionist making
- Unplugged activities (physical embodied analogies) combined with plugged activities (digital tools) for AI education
- Cyclical understand-make-reflect process scaffolding conceptual, technical, and ethical AI dimensions
- Upper-primary students (sixth graders) as crucial yet under-examined target for AI literacy development
- Design-based research methodology enabling iterative pedagogical solution development in authentic classrooms
- Abstract and complex nature of AI creating challenges for age-appropriate pedagogy
- Inclusive holistic AI education addressing knowledge gaps in primary school contexts

## Theoretical Framework
The embodied constructionist approach synthesizes embodied cognition theory with constructionist learning principles for developmentally appropriate AI education. Embodied cognition posits that abstract concepts become meaningful through bodily experiences and physical analogies, making complex AI mechanisms accessible to young learners. Constructionism (Papert) emphasizes learning through making tangible artifacts enabling externalization and refinement of understanding. The cyclical understand-make-reflect process creates scaffolded progression: (1) embodied analogies ground abstract AI concepts in physical experience, (2) constructionist making with digital tools enables hands-on exploration of technical implementation, (3) reflection activities surface ethical implications and critical perspectives. This integration addresses primary education challenges where AI's invisibility and complexity typically preclude meaningful engagement beyond superficial tool use.

## Methods
interview, design-based research

## Main Arguments
- Holistic AI literacy requires integrating technical competencies with social and ethical understanding rather than treating these as separate
- Upper-primary students capable of grasping complex AI concepts when supported by age-appropriate embodied constructionist pedagogy
- Two-year implementation with 107 sixth graders demonstrates feasibility of holistic AI education in authentic classroom contexts
- Unplugged embodied activities make abstract AI mechanisms concrete before plugged digital tool engagement
- Cyclical understand-make-reflect process enables gradual comprehension across AI literacy dimensions
- Case studies reveal both overall class progress and individual student struggles informing further pedagogical refinement
- Design-based research methodology produces both practical pedagogical solutions and theoretical knowledge about AI education
- Age-appropriate pedagogy essential for inclusive AI literacy development during crucial developmental period

## Limitations & Critiques
- Implementation required two-year iterative development suggesting high resource demands for schools lacking DBR expertise
- Single school context (107 students) limits generalizability across diverse educational settings and cultures
- Teacher debriefings suggest significant professional development needed for effective facilitation
- Qualitative case study approach provides rich description but limited quantitative evidence of learning gains
- Embodied analogies risk oversimplifying AI mechanisms potentially requiring correction in later learning
- Unclear how approach scales beyond research-supported implementation to widespread adoption
- Balance between unplugged and plugged activities may require adjustment for different student populations
- Limited assessment of whether holistic literacy translates to ethical AI use behaviors beyond classroom reflections
