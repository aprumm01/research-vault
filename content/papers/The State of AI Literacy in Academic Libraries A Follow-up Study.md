# The State of AI Literacy in Academic Libraries A Follow-up Study

## Summary
This follow-up study examines how AI literacy among U academic library employees evolved from 2023 to 2024 Building on previous baseline research, the study applies a TPACK-informed framework covering technical knowledge, practical skills, critical thinking, ethical awareness, and societal impact.

## Key Concepts
- AI literacy defined through TPACK framework (Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge)
- Longitudinal measurement of AI literacy development (2023-2024)
- Impact of premium AI tool access versus training participation
- Barriers to AI literacy development in academic libraries
- ChatGPT as dominant generative AI tool in library contexts

## Theoretical Framework
TPACK framework (Mishra & Koehler, 2006) adapted for academic library context, integrating technological knowledge, pedagogical knowledge, and content knowledge

## Methods
Longitudinal survey study comparing 2023 (N=658) and 2024 (N=701) cohorts of U.S. academic library employees. Mixed methods approach combining Likert-scale measures analyzed via Mann-Whitney U tests and qualitative open-ended responses about barriers.

## Main Arguments
- AI literacy among academic library employees improved significantly from 2023 to 2024, with mean understanding rising from 2.96 to 3.34
- Support staff and early-career professionals showed largest gains, narrowing literacy gaps across roles and experience levels
- ChatGPT emerged as dominant generative AI tool with significant increases in both professional (p<10^-20) and personal use (p=0.0284)
- Access to premium AI tools strongly correlated with improved literacy across all dimensions (all p<0.0001), while training participation showed no significant effects
- Persistent barriers include cost, limited training opportunities, time constraints, institutional resistance, and ethical concerns
- Hands-on engagement with advanced tools appears more influential than formal training sessions alone

## Limitations & Critiques
- Self-reported measures may not accurately reflect actual competencies
- Causality between premium tool access and literacy cannot be definitively established (correlation vs. causation)
- Training effectiveness analysis did not account for quality, duration, or delivery format variations
- Sample limited to U.S. academic libraries, limiting generalizability
- One-year timeframe may not capture longer-term literacy development patterns
