## Summary
This work examines the motivational and volitional dimensions of human performance, addressing how goals, intentions, and self-regulatory processes translate motivation into sustained action and performance outcomes. The paper engages with performance improvement frameworks that must account not just for ability but for the motivational and volitional factors that determine whether capable individuals actually perform effectively in organizational settings.

## Key Concepts
- Motivation and performance
- Volition and self-regulation
- Goal setting
- Performance improvement
- Human performance technology

## Theoretical Framework
Motivation theory (expectancy-value, goal-setting) and volitional theory (action control) are integrated to explain how individuals translate intentions into sustained performance behavior.

## Methods
Conceptual and theoretical analysis; synthesis of motivational psychology and performance improvement literature.

## Main Arguments
- Motivation alone is insufficient for performance; volition—the ability to protect intentions and maintain effort—is equally critical
- Performance interventions must address both motivational antecedents and volitional maintenance of goal-directed behavior
- Goal setting is effective only when paired with self-regulatory strategies for sustaining effort
- Organizations can design environments that support volitional engagement, not just initial motivation
