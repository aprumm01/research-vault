# Prioritizing Human Creativity A Sequential apprach to teaching generative ai in graphic design education

## Summary
into graphic design education through a sequential pedagogical approach Focusing on two case studies—a foundational typography course and an advanced studio course—it investigates how students develop design skills before engaging with AI tools The typography course, designed without GenAI tools, prioritizes technical skills, iterative processes, and conceptualization, while the advanced course introduces AI as a tool for exploration and ideation.

## Key Concepts
- Sequential pedagogical approach teaching design fundamentals before introducing generative AI tools
- Typography as ideal entry point for graphic design education without AI dependency
- Conceptualization and iteration as core design processes requiring hands-on development before AI augmentation
- Technical skills foundation including type anatomy, spacing principles, and layout fundamentals
- AI positioned as exploration and ideation tool rather than replacement for creative decision-making
- Distinction between exploratory creativity (AI strength) and complex creativity requiring critical evaluation (human strength)
- Ethical considerations of AI training data including ownership, attribution, and cultural bias
- Partnership on AI Field Guide providing checkpoints for responsible AI use in creative practice

## Theoretical Framework
The sequential approach integrates foundational skills pedagogy with responsible AI integration by establishing core competencies before tool adoption. The framework positions typography as bridge between technical craft and conceptual art, enabling students to develop letterform manipulation, spatial relationships, and hierarchy understanding independent of AI assistance. Drawing on Boden's distinction between exploratory and transformational creativity, the pedagogy preserves human agency in critical evaluation and emotional complexity while leveraging AI for ideation support. The approach requires: (1) mastery of design software, terminology, and iterative processes through analog and digital exploration, (2) development of research and conceptualization capabilities, (3) introduction of AI as augmentation tool with explicit ethical frameworks, and (4) continuous distinction between human and machine contributions to creative process.

## Methods
case study

## Main Arguments
- Strong foundational typography skills prepare students to use GenAI tools effectively while retaining creative control
- Sequential curriculum mitigates risk of compromising creative process development when AI introduced prematurely
- Students with established design fundamentals report enhanced conceptualization using AI primarily for ideation rather than substituting core competencies
- Typography education without AI builds essential understanding of hierarchy, form, and spatial relationships applicable across design domains
- Ethical AI use requires confronting training data concerns, ownership questions, and cultural bias rather than treating ethics as afterthought
- AI excels at exploratory creativity within parameters but lacks human capacity for critical evaluation and transformation of ideas
- Transparent documentation of AI use including tools, prompts, and collaborative nature supports accountability in design portfolios

## Limitations & Critiques
- Study limited to two courses at single institution limiting generalizability across diverse educational contexts
- No longitudinal tracking of students to assess whether foundational approach produces lasting advantages in professional practice
- Unclear whether sequential timing optimal or if alternative integration models could achieve similar outcomes
- Limited comparison with programs using different AI introduction sequences to validate approach superiority
- Student comfort levels with AI tools varied significantly (only 38.9% comfortable or very comfortable) suggesting approach may not address all barriers
- Technical difficulties reported by 35.3% of students indicating infrastructure and tool access challenges
- Does not address how approach scales to online or resource-constrained educational environments
- Ethical frameworks introduced may not adequately prepare students for rapidly evolving AI capabilities and governance landscapes
