# methodology-legend-and-rhetoric-the-constructions-of-ai-by-academia-industry-and-policy-groups-for-lifelong-learning - eynon-young-2020

## Summary
Artificial intelligence (AI) is again attracting significant attention across all areas of social life One important sphere of focus is education; many policy makers across the globe view lifelong learning as an essential means to prepare society for an “AI future” and look to AI as a way to “deliver” learning opportunities to meet these needs AI is a complex social, cultural, and material artifact that is understood and constructed by different stakeholders in varied ways, and these differences have significant social and educational implications that need to be explored.

## Key Concepts
- Social construction of artificial intelligence
- Stakeholder perspectives on AI (academia, industry, policy)
- AI for lifelong learning contexts
- Methodology, legend, and rhetoric in AI discourse
- Complex social, cultural, and material artifact understanding
- Varied constructions of AI by different groups
- Educational implications of AI framing
- AI future preparedness narratives
- Policy maker perspectives on AI and education
- Discursive framing of AI capabilities

## Methods
- Interview methodology
- Comparative discourse analysis across stakeholder groups
- Qualitative thematic analysis
- Policy document analysis
- Cross-sector stakeholder comparison

## Main Arguments
- AI is constructed differently by academia, industry, and policy groups
- These varied constructions have significant social and educational implications
- "Methodology" refers to academic systematic approaches to AI
- "Legend" captures industry narratives and hype around AI capabilities
- "Rhetoric" reflects policy discourse framing AI as solution to educational challenges
- Stakeholder constructions shape how AI is developed and deployed in education
- Lifelong learning framed as necessary response to "AI future"
- AI simultaneously positioned as problem driver and solution provider
- Need to critically examine whose construction of AI dominates policy and practice
- Differences in AI understanding create tensions in educational implementation

## Limitations & Critiques
- Limited to three stakeholder categories, may miss other important perspectives
- Interview-based methodology captures stated views not necessarily practices
- Temporal specificity - 2020 publication may not reflect current discourse
- Geographic scope of stakeholder interviews not specified
- May not capture evolving nature of AI constructions over time
- Power differentials between stakeholder groups underexplored
- Limited discussion of learner/student constructions of AI
- Does not fully address how competing constructions are negotiated
- May overemphasize discursive dimensions at expense of material practices
