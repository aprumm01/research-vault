## Summary
Zimmerman, Oh, Yildirim, Kass, Tung, and Forlizzi at Carnegie Mellon and Accenture argue that UX designers have a unique opportunity to push adaptive user interfaces (AUIs) into enterprise settings, where AI can learn from workers' repetitive actions and automate the most routine tasks. They make the case that small amounts of AI can enrich enterprise UIs in practical ways, and that UX designers are well-positioned to champion and shape responsible AI integration in organizational software.

## Key Concepts
- Adaptive user interfaces (AUIs)
- Enterprise UX design
- AI in enterprise software
- UX designer as AI champion
- Workflow automation

## Theoretical Framework
UX design practice and intelligent systems design theory frame the argument that designers should proactively integrate AI capabilities into enterprise interface design rather than waiting for AI to be imposed on users.

## Methods
Design case and practitioner argument; drawing on examples of AUI deployment in enterprise settings; published in a design research/practice venue.

## Main Arguments
- Small, targeted AI capabilities can meaningfully improve enterprise UIs without requiring complex system overhauls
- UX designers are uniquely positioned to identify where AI automation adds genuine value to worker workflows
- Adaptive interfaces that learn from repetitive actions free workers' attention for higher-value decisions
- UX designers must advocate for responsible AI integration that preserves worker agency and dignity
