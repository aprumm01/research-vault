# arXiv:2502.08854v2 [cs.HC] 22 Jun 2025 Generative AI & Changing Work: Systema...

## Summary
Law and Varanasi's 2025 systematic review examines how practitioners across fields are using Job Crafting as a framework to understand how they reshape their work in response to Generative AI. Synthesizing 47 studies, the review finds that practitioners are actively crafting new task, relational, and cognitive boundaries around GenAI adoption: taking on new AI-managerial tasks, redefining their value to clients/employers, and developing new expertise in AI direction and quality control. The paper argues that Job Crafting theory is productive for understanding the active, agentive dimensions of AI-mediated work transformation.

## Key Concepts
- Job Crafting theory (task, relational, cognitive crafting)
- Generative AI and work transformation
- AI-managerial labor (directing, evaluating, correcting AI)
- Professional identity reshaping
- Systematic review methodology in HCI

## Theoretical Framework
Job Crafting theory (Wrzesniewski & Dutton, 2001); GenAI adoption research; organizational behavior; the review positions Job Crafting as the theoretical lens through which active worker agency in AI adoption can be understood.

## Methods
Systematic literature review of 47 empirical studies examining how practitioners adapt their work in response to GenAI; coding for Job Crafting dimensions (task, relational, cognitive crafting); synthesis of findings across domains.

## Main Arguments
- Workers are not passive recipients of AI transformation; they actively craft their roles in response to GenAI capabilities and pressures
- The most consistent pattern is the emergence of AI-managerial tasks: directing, quality-controlling, and integrating AI outputs into professional deliverables
- Relational crafting involves redefining practitioner value to clients as AI direction and judgment rather than production
- Job Crafting provides a useful framework for HCI research on AI-mediated work because it centers worker agency

## Limitations & Critiques
The 47 studies span diverse contexts and methodologies; findings may not transfer across fields or organizational structures; Job Crafting theory itself has been critiqued for underemphasizing structural constraints on worker agency.
