# Simulating the Human in HCD with ChatGPT - Schmidt et al - 2024

## Key Concepts
- LLMs as world models encoding human experiences at scale
- Generative AI enhancing human-centered design toolkit
- Simulating user interactions without direct human involvement
- Interactive personas that can be "actively interrogated"
- Human-AI collaboration and cocreation in intelligent systems
- Transparency in AI use within HCD processes

## Theoretical Framework
ISO 9241-210:2019 HCD process framework; User modeling and task modeling traditions in HCI; Model-based development

## Methods
Conceptual analysis applying LLMs/ChatGPT to HCD phases: (1) Understanding and specifying user context, (2) Specifying user requirements, (3) Producing design solutions, (4) Evaluating against requirements; Prompt engineering and few-shot prompting; Role descriptions and knowledge base integration

## Main Arguments
- LLMs can support HCD by replacing humans, adding AI agents to iterative processes, and extending HCD methods
- LLMs encode human experiences from internet data (forums, reviews, tutorials, papers, support sites)
- Models can speed up design process without requiring direct user involvement (similar to task/user modeling)
- LLMs particularly valuable for hard-to-reach users who share experiences online but unavailable for focus groups
- Can revolutionize stakeholder involvement through scalable representation of diverse groups
- Enable auto-generation of personas with deeper backgrounds (including marginalized groups, accessibility needs)
- Support semi-repetitive tasks: generating survey/interview questions, creating storyboards, translating content
- Allow researchers to explore broader scope than traditional methods
- Future systems require designing for AI as stakeholder and user, not just obedient tool

## Limitations & Critiques
- LLMs reproduce human biases and stereotypes from training data (gender, racial stereotypes)
- Vague prompts yield speculative responses and hallucinations
- Need for clear specifications, few-shot prompting, or fine-tuning for underrepresented scenarios
- Prompt engineering challenges: Can users identify biased outputs? Can prompts be sufficiently neutral?
- Fundamental concern: As LLMs generate more content, data may reflect LLM "perspective" more than human experiences
- "Simulated surveys" validity hard to prove and often questioned
- Unethical and fraudulent to "sell" simulated surveys as real user surveys
- Reports of LLM-based bots emulating participants in online surveys
- Must be transparent about where/how AI tools used and whether they augmented or fell short of HCD
- AI will not replace user studies but rather enhance toolkit
- Important issue: transparency about where AI used and how insights were gathered (paramount)
