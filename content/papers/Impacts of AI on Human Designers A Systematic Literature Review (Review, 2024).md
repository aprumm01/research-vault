# Impacts of AI on Human Designers A Systematic Literature Review

## Summary
professional communication researchers Although challenges in designing with and for AI have been generally investigated, current systematic literature reviews have neglected to consider the tensions between AI and human designers during design work and how that work is impacted Our study extends previous work by offering a more nuanced understanding of how AI affects designers’ work, thought processes, and solutions.

## Key Concepts
- **Reframing Design Roles**: AI tools influence both convergent (strategic selection) and divergent (creative exploration) thinking, fundamentally reshaping designers' cognitive processes and professional identity
- **Human-AI Collaboration Dynamics**: Multidirectional communication between designers, AI systems, and end-users creates novel challenges requiring clear articulation of AI behavior and receptivity to feedback
- **Cognitive Expansion**: AI serves as third agent in design process, broadening perspectives through generative outputs that provide inspirational materials and starting points beyond traditional methodologies
- **Critical Reflection Facilitation**: AI tools promote deeper consideration of design problems, ethical implications, and in-situ experiences of biases, compelling designers to confront ramifications of decisions
- **Expectation Alignment**: Misalignment between designer expectations and AI capabilities leads to frustration, with novices fixating on intended results while professionals demonstrate broader adaptability
- **Design Fixation Risk**: AI-generated outputs can paradoxically both overcome fixation through novel perspectives and create new fixation through pattern-based similarities
- **Authorship Transformation**: AI integration fundamentally questions attribution of creative work and ownership, prompting "if machine does work, who is artist?"
- **More-than-Human Design**: AI enables expansion beyond human-centered paradigms to consider nonhuman actors including animals, plants, and technologies
- **Workflow Integration Tensions**: AI tools require abandoning established workflows, risking decreased efficiency while offering potential for enhanced creative focus
- **Ethical Consciousness**: AI provides in-situ experiences of biases and prompts broader community impact consideration

## Theoretical Framework
- **PRISMA Framework**: Systematic review methodology providing structured approach to identification, screening, and inclusion of studies
- **Hybrid Inductive/Deductive Thematic Analysis**: Flexible analytical approach allowing codes to emerge from data while applying existing frameworks, prioritizing reflexive theme generation over traditional interrater reliability
- **Human-AI Collaboration Spectrum**: Conceptual framework positioning AI roles from utilitarian tools to collaborative entities to expert partners, with varying expectations for satisfaction versus surprise
- **Sociotechnical Tensions Framework**: Understanding AI integration through lens of tensions—positive, negative, and ambiguous—that defy binary interpretations

## Methods
Systematic literature review using PRISMA framework across 7 databases (ACM Digital Library, IEEE Xplore, Sage, ScienceDirect, Springer, Taylor & Francis, Wiley). Initial search of 1,973 articles narrowed to final corpus of 44 articles (2017-2024) through exclusion criteria. Hybrid inductive/deductive thematic analysis conducted using Miro as remote qualitative workspace, with both authors independently coding and collaboratively reconciling 64 initial codes into 8 subthemes, then 4 final themes. Cohen's Kappa of 0.87 for screening and 0.92 for eligibility assessment.

## Main Arguments
- **AI as Design Process Transformer**: AI fundamentally reshapes not just outputs but designers' thinking, workflows, and creative processes across entire design spectrum from ideation to prototyping
- **Triadic Relationship Emergence**: Introduction of AI as third agent creates dynamic shift in designer-tool interactions, potentially yielding more nuanced outcomes through symbiotic human-AI relationship
- **Communication Paradigm Shift**: Effective multidirectional communication requires designers to clearly articulate AI behavior to end-users while remaining receptive to feedback, introducing novel challenges in computational co-creativity tool design
- **Expertise-Dependent Engagement**: Designer experience significantly impacts AI interaction, with novices relying on automation while professionals integrate AI as auxiliary instrument within nuanced methodology
- **Role Evolution Necessity**: Designers increasingly function as curators and directors of AI-generated content, requiring new competencies in prompt engineering and AI model tuning
- **Value-Sensitive Integration Imperative**: Most effective AI use positions systems in supportive role augmenting human creativity rather than replacing it, requiring frameworks aligned with human values
- **Reprofessionalization of Practice**: AI integration drives accentuation of embodied design qualities, intellectualization of processes, and necessity for flexible technical skill development
- **Critical Engagement Requirement**: Value of AI extends beyond outputs to capacity for prompting designers to critically engage with own ideas and design process, leading to more holistic reflective approaches

## Limitations & Critiques
- **Selective Data Extraction**: Reliance exclusively on results/discussion sections may have missed valuable insights from methods or introduction sections regarding innovative approaches or data collection techniques
- **Preprint Exclusion Gap**: Omission of arXiv and similar preprints may overlook emerging trends and novel ideas given rapid AI advancement pace, affecting timeliness and scope
- **Limited TPC Integration**: Focused on empirical design studies neglecting broader technical/professional communication literature exploring AI in information design beyond designer-focused empirical work
- **Corpus Concentration**: 68% of articles from ACM database and 77% published since 2023 suggests recency bias and potential database-specific publication patterns
- **Design Field Boundaries**: Focus on interaction, UX, communication, and product design may not capture AI impacts in other creative domains
- **Contextual Understanding Deficit**: AI systems lack contextual awareness and cultural nuance, producing technically proficient but emotionally hollow designs lacking human experience meaning
- **Explainability Gap**: AI inability to explain outputs creates significant communication gap, leaving designers confused by unexpected outcomes without rationale understanding
- **Bias Perpetuation Risk**: AI systems trained on existing datasets risk perpetuating or amplifying existing design biases, particularly with nondiverse training data
- **Homogenization Threat**: Machines trained on similar datasets produce increasingly uniform outputs, potentially stifling creativity and diversity in design outcomes
- **Deskilling Concern**: Overreliance on AI tools may atrophy fundamental design skills and critical thinking abilities through reduced independent practice
