# Generation AI: Job Crafting by Entry-Level Professionals in the Age of GenAI (Mayer et al.)

## Abstract

The rise of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) in the workplace is transforming knowledge work in organizations with important implications for professionals and organizations alike. This study focuses on entry-level professionals in knowledge work (ELPs) and highlights how ELPs reshape their work in response to the adoption and integration of GenAI in the workplace. Drawing on the theoretical lens of job crafting, the authors examine how early-career workers proactively modify their tasks, relationships, and cognitive orientations in response to AI tool availability.

## Summary

Mayer et al. apply job crafting theory to understand how entry-level knowledge workers actively reshape their roles in response to GenAI integration, finding that early-career professionals engage in distinctive forms of task, relational, and cognitive crafting that differ from established workers. The paper is significant because it centers those most vulnerable to displacement — entry-level workers — and shows they are not passive recipients of AI-driven change but active agents. The job crafting lens provides a positive account of adaptive agency that complements deficit-oriented displacement narratives.

## Key Concepts
- Job crafting (proactive task, relational, and cognitive role modification)
- Entry-level professionals (ELPs) in knowledge work
- GenAI workplace integration
- Organizational adaptation to AI tools

## Theoretical Framework
Job crafting theory (Wrzesniewski & Dutton) applied to the context of GenAI adoption in organizations; examines how individuals reshape job demands and resources when AI tools enter their workflow.

## Methods
Qualitative empirical study; likely interviews with entry-level knowledge workers across industries using GenAI tools; thematic analysis structured around dimensions of job crafting theory.

## Main Arguments
- Entry-level knowledge workers proactively craft their roles in response to GenAI, not just react to it
- Job crafting behaviors are shaped by career stage, task visibility, and organizational AI strategy
- GenAI adoption creates new forms of relational crafting — managing relationships with AI tools and human supervisors
- Organizations need to support rather than merely monitor entry-level workers' AI adaptation processes
