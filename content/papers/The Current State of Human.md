# The Current State of Human

## Summary
Cho, Jo, Park, Kang, and Chen conduct a citation network analysis of Performance Improvement Quarterly (PIQ) from 1988-2010 to empirically characterize the intellectual structure and evolution of the Human Performance Technology (HPT) field. By identifying the most-cited works, core author networks, and thematic clusters in HPT's most prominent journal, the analysis reveals HPT's intellectual foundations, internal communities of inquiry, and the degree to which the field has developed a coherent, self-referential scholarly identity versus remaining practice-driven with limited theory-building.

## Key Concepts
- Human Performance Technology (HPT)
- Citation network analysis
- Intellectual structure of applied fields
- Performance Improvement Quarterly (PIQ)
- Theory development vs. practice orientation in HPT

## Theoretical Framework
Bibliometric methodology; citation network analysis following de Solla Price's tradition; uses co-citation analysis and visualization to map intellectual communities; theoretical framework draws on sociology of science and field development theory.

## Methods
Citation network analysis of all articles published in PIQ from 1988-2010; co-citation analysis; identification of core works, most-cited authors, and thematic clusters; network visualization and temporal analysis of field evolution.

## Main Arguments
- HPT has a practice-driven identity with limited indigenous theory development — most foundational citations reference behavioral, instructional design, and organizational development literature rather than HPT-specific theoretical work
- The field shows stable intellectual clusters around performance analysis, instructional design, and organizational systems — with relatively weak connections between clusters
- HPT's most foundational works are methodological and prescriptive rather than theoretical — a pattern consistent with a field that defines itself through systematic practice rather than explanatory theory
- Stronger theory development would enhance HPT's credibility as a discipline and its capacity to address novel performance contexts (including AI-mediated work)

## Limitations
- Citation analysis captures formal scholarly communication; practitioner knowledge and conference proceedings are underrepresented
- PIQ is one major HPT journal; analysis of multiple journals would provide a fuller picture of the field's intellectual structure
