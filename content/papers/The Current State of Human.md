## Summary
Cho et al. conduct a citation network analysis of Performance Improvement Quarterly from 1988 to 2010 to map the intellectual structure and current state of Human Performance Technology (HPT) as a field. They find that HPT has developed a coherent citation network with identifiable clusters around performance analysis, intervention design, and evaluation, but that theory development remains limited relative to practice-oriented contributions. The analysis reveals HPT's intellectual dependencies and areas for scholarly growth.

## Key Concepts
- Human Performance Technology (HPT)
- Citation network analysis
- Performance Improvement Quarterly
- Field development
- Theory vs. practice

## Theoretical Framework
Bibliometrics and citation analysis provide the methodological framework, drawing on de Solla Price's network analysis concepts to map intellectual influence and clustering in HPT scholarship.

## Methods
Citation network analysis of Performance Improvement Quarterly (1988–2010); mapping of citation clusters and influential works; quantitative analysis of field structure.

## Main Arguments
- HPT has developed a coherent intellectual network over 22 years, with identifiable clusters around core topics
- Theory development in HPT is limited relative to practice-oriented contributions
- The field shows strong internal citation practices but limited engagement with adjacent disciplines
- Future HPT scholarship should invest in theory-building to strengthen the field's scientific foundation
