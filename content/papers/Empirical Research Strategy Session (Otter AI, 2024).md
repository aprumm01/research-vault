## Key Concepts
- **Novelty as Academic Currency**: Research contributions rewarded for perspective shifts, new knowledge about causal relationships, or ecosystem understanding rather than efficiency solutions
- **Genre Discovery in Research**: Finding one's academic style and identifying compatible scholarly communities through writing approach and citation patterns
- **Academia-Practice Gap**: HCI academics characterized as "armchair scientists" removed from day-to-day design practice, contrasting with practitioners' ground expertise
- **Expectation Management in Peer Review**: Strategically selecting subcommittee members and suggesting reviewers who understand research style to minimize mismatch
- **Classes of Problems vs Approaches**: Distinguishing enduring human behavior problems (designer-developer tensions) from evolving tooling/process solutions
- **Empirical Building Blocks**: Junior scholars establish credibility through multiple empirical studies before making theoretical contributions
- **Historical Paradigm Shifts**: AI as disruptive technology compared to steam engine transformation—creating new jobs while displacing existing ones
- **Student-to-Industry Transition**: Gap between university curriculum and industry job requirements, complicated by outdated course bulletins and incomplete data
- **Reverse Engineering Research Design**: Starting with dissertation-worthy question then unpacking into 3-4 smaller empirical studies
- **Network-Based Scholarship**: Academic papers as conversation initiators with broader community, requiring right reviewer matches for productive feedback

## Methods
Conversational transcript from research strategy session between experienced HCI academic and UX practitioner. Discussion method involves collaborative problem scoping, literature positioning advice, and career mentorship on navigating academic publishing and research design.

## Main Arguments
- **Practitioners possess unique ground expertise**: Day-to-day UX practice provides insights academics lack, requiring translation from solutions to core problems for research contribution
- **Problem scoping determines research success**: Effective research requires separating problems from solutions, identifying where contribution sits in "funnel" of theoretical to applied
- **Academic publishing is social network navigation**: Success depends on finding right community, citing relevant scholars, and connecting with reviewers who understand research style
- **Multiple pathways to contribution exist**: Can propose tools, make theoretical arguments, identify gaps, or reflect on empirical data—no single correct approach
- **Generative AI creates pedagogical urgency**: Need to revamp educational curriculum for UX students to transition to AI-integrated industry practice
- **Data quality limits AI solutions**: Job posting analysis hindered by outdated course bulletins, generic syllabi, incomplete information—"model only as good as data provided"
- **Systematic career progression essential**: Junior scholars build credibility through empirical work before attempting high-level theoretical arguments at dissertation conclusion
- **Educational-industry alignment is timely**: Multiple states addressing degree-to-wage connections, making curriculum-job requirement research particularly relevant

## Limitations & Critiques
- **Informal conversational format**: Lacks structured research methodology, systematic literature review, or empirical evidence beyond anecdotal experiences
- **Insider perspective without critical distance**: Discussion assumes academic norms without questioning underlying power structures in peer review
- **Individual navigation emphasis**: Focuses on strategic positioning within existing systems rather than critiquing or reforming those systems
- **Limited attention to equity issues**: Briefly mentions feminist HCI but doesn't deeply engage with diversity, access, or representation in academia
- **Pragmatic focus may reinforce status quo**: Advice centers on "playing safe" and fitting into existing scholarly conversations rather than challenging them
- **Data quality concerns underexplored**: Identifies problems with educational data but doesn't propose solutions for improving information completeness
- **Assumed academic career trajectory**: Guidance presumes traditional PhD-to-faculty path without considering alternative research careers
- **Reviewer gaming strategies**: Emphasis on selecting "right" reviewers may perpetuate echo chambers and limit critical feedback
- **Historical comparisons oversimplified**: Steam engine analogy treats technological displacement as inevitable without examining political choices
- **Student perspective absent**: Discussion between faculty and practitioner lacks actual student voices on educational needs and industry transitions
