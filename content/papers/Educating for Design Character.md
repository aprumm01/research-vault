# Educating for Design Character in Higher Education: Challenges in Studio Pedagogy

## Summary
This DRS2020 conference paper uses co/autoethnographic methods to explore the challenges of cultivating "design character" in higher education studio settings. The authors, all design educators at Indiana University, reflect on their experiences trying to develop students as whole persons - not merely teaching skills and knowledge but nurturing judgment, ethics, and professional identity. They identify four challenge contexts where tensions arise: with students, with colleagues, with curriculum structures, and with themselves as educators.

## Key Concepts
- **Design Character**: The holistic development of a designer beyond skills and knowledge - including judgment, values, ethics, and professional identity
- **Studio Pedagogy**: Teaching approach centered on project-based learning, critique, and apprenticeship-like mentoring
- **Co/autoethnography**: Research method combining collaborative reflection with individual autobiographical inquiry
- **Signature Pedagogy**: The distinctive teaching approaches that characterize professional education in a field
- **Whole Student Development**: Educational philosophy attending to students' complete growth as persons and professionals

## Theoretical Framework
The paper draws on studio pedagogy traditions from architecture and design education, combined with broader scholarship on professional formation and character development. The authors position design character as complementary to but distinct from design skills (technical capabilities) and design knowledge (conceptual understanding). They implicitly draw on Aristotelian virtue ethics in emphasizing character formation.

## Methods
- Co/autoethnographic research approach
- Collaborative reflective writing among three design educators
- Narrative analysis of teaching experiences
- Thematic organization around four challenge contexts

## Main Arguments
**Challenge Context 1 - Students:**
- Students often resist ambiguity and want clear procedures
- Developing judgment requires experiences students find uncomfortable
- Students' prior educational experiences may not prepare them for studio culture

**Challenge Context 2 - Colleagues:**
- Not all faculty share commitment to character development
- Institutional pressures prioritize measurable outcomes over character
- Disagreements about what constitutes good design character

**Challenge Context 3 - Curriculum:**
- Formal curriculum structures constrain studio pedagogy
- Assessment requirements conflict with developmental goals
- Time pressures limit deep mentoring relationships

**Challenge Context 4 - Ourselves:**
- Emotional labor of caring for whole students
- Uncertainty about whether our approaches are effective
- Tensions between personal values and institutional demands

## Limitations & Critiques
- Limited to three educators at one institution
- Autoethnographic method may lack generalizability
- "Design character" concept remains somewhat underspecified
- Does not provide clear solutions to the challenges identified
- Primarily focused on challenges rather than successful strategies
- Western/American context may not transfer to other cultural settings
- Limited attention to student perspectives on character development
