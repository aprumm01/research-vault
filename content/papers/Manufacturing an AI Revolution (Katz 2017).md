## Summary
Yarden Katz argues that the contemporary "AI revolution" is a manufactured narrative shaped by institutional power, venture capital interests, and military funding rather than a purely technical breakthrough. Drawing on the history of AI from the 1950s onward, Katz shows how AI's periodic revivals are driven as much by economic and political forces as by scientific progress—and that the current wave of AI enthusiasm serves specific ideological and financial interests that deserve critical scrutiny.

## Key Concepts
- AI history and politics
- AI hype cycles
- Institutional power and technology

## Theoretical Framework
Political economy and critical theory of technology frame the analysis of how AI narratives are constructed to serve institutional interests, drawing on Latour's concept of how truth is "clothed in technical capacity and institutional power."

## Methods
Historical and critical analysis; archival research on AI funding, promotion, and institutional development from the 1950s to 2017.

## Main Arguments
- The current AI boom is shaped by military funding, venture capital, and media amplification as much as genuine technical advance
- AI periodically "revives" when institutional interests align around promoting it as a solution
- The narrative of AI as inevitable or revolutionary serves to naturalize specific economic and political arrangements
- Critical scrutiny of who benefits from AI hype is essential for democratic oversight of the technology
