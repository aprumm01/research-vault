# Educating for Design Character in Higher Education: Challenges in Teaching Design Judgment (MurdochKitt et al.)

## Summary
MurdochKitt, Culén, and Mainsah's Design Research Society paper examines the challenge of educating for design character — the dispositional qualities (curiosity, care, integrity, courage) that distinguish excellent designers beyond technical skill. Drawing on virtue ethics and phronesis, the paper argues that design character cannot be taught through method instruction alone but requires pedagogical approaches that cultivate practical wisdom through reflection, mentorship, and exposure to complex design challenges. It positions character education as a legitimate and necessary dimension of higher design education.

## Key Concepts
- Design character (dispositional qualities of excellent designers)
- Virtue ethics and phronesis in design education
- Mentorship and modeling in studio pedagogy
- Practical wisdom (phronesis) in design
- Beyond skill: dispositional competencies

## Theoretical Framework
Virtue ethics (Aristotle); MacIntyre's account of practices and virtues; Stolterman's design theory; applied to higher design education pedagogy.

## Methods
Conceptual framework development; qualitative case analysis from design education contexts; philosophical argumentation drawing on virtue ethics literature.

## Main Arguments
- Technical skill without design character produces competent but not excellent designers
- Design character includes epistemic virtues (curiosity, rigor) and ethical virtues (care for users, integrity)
- Studio pedagogy can cultivate character through reflective practice, modeling by instructors, and engagement with complex, value-laden design challenges
- Assessment of design character is possible through portfolio evidence and reflective documentation, though it requires different tools than skill assessment

## Limitations & Critiques
"Design character" resists precise operationalization; the virtue ethics framework may carry cultural assumptions that don't transfer across design education contexts globally; empirical evidence for the pedagogical claims is limited.
