## Key Concepts
- Prototypes as filters: Selectively reveal specific aspects of design ideas
- Prototypes as manifestations: Material expressions of design concepts
- Design space exploration: Prototypes enable reflection and navigation of possibilities
- Generative role: Beyond evaluation, prototypes generate new design insights
- Fidelity dimensions: Low vs high fidelity as filtering mechanism
- Reflection-in-action: Prototypes support designer thinking through making

## Theoretical Framework
- Schön's reflective practice: Reflection-in-action through prototyping
- Design exploration framework: Prototypes as tools for navigating design space
- Material thinking: Physical manifestations enabling conceptual development
- Filter model: Prototypes selectively emphasize certain design qualities

## Methods
Two case studies examining prototype use in design processes, analyzing how designers employ prototypes for exploration and reflection rather than just evaluation

## Main Arguments
- Lack of fundamental knowledge about prototypes despite their established role in HCI and design
- Existing typologies (low/high fidelity) center on evaluation, not design exploration
- Prototypes serve generative role enabling designers to reflect on design activities
- Two key dimensions: filters (what aspects are revealed) and manifestations (how ideas materialize)
- Prototypes not just for testing but for thinking - "prototyping as conversation with materials"
- Filter dimension determines what design questions can be explored
- Manifestation dimension affects how design ideas develop through interaction with materials
- Framework provides discourse for understanding fundamental characteristics of prototypes
- Shifts focus from evaluation-centric to exploration-centric view of prototyping

## Limitations & Critiques
- Framework not absolute - acknowledges it may need adaptation for new prototyping approaches
- Not prescriptive - represents way of thinking rather than method guaranteeing "good" prototypes
- Based on only two case studies which limits generalizability of framework
- Case studies focused on specific domains (house design, mobile phone interface) may not transfer to all HCI contexts
- Case studies used existing/reinterpreted data from previous research rather than new empirical work
- Framework requires further research to validate applicability across different design contexts
- Limited examination of how framework performs with unconventional prototyping methods
- Does not provide empirical comparison of framework effectiveness vs. other prototyping approaches
- Conceptual framework needs testing in diverse real-world design practices
- May be difficult for practitioners to operationalize filtering and manifestation dimensions in practice
- Focus on HCI/interaction design may limit applicability to other design disciplines
