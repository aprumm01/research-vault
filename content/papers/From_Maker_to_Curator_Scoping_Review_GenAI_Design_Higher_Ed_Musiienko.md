# From Maker to Curator: A Scoping Review of Generative Artificial Intelligence in Design Higher Education

## Summary
This comprehensive scoping review synthesizes 156 studies on generative AI in design higher education published between 2017-2026, drawn from 1,800 initial records in Web of Science. The review maps the extent, nature, and characteristics of research across design disciplines, finding that publication volume increased 6.4-fold after ChatGPT's release. The paper identifies major gaps—theoretical underdevelopment, methodological weakness, and geographic concentration—and proposes a research agenda centered on the emerging paradigm shift from designer as "maker" to designer as "curator" of AI-generated content.

## Key Concepts
- **Maker-to-Curator Paradigm Shift**: The central conceptual contribution—designers are transitioning from hands-on creators of artifacts to directors and curators who define vision, orchestrate experience, and judge AI output alignment with values
- **Competency Gap**: The finding that 85-86% of educators and students use AI, yet lack of formal training has created a gap where adoption velocity outpaces critical literacy and ethical framework development
- **TPACK (Technological Pedagogical Content Knowledge)**: The most prominent theoretical framework found in the reviewed studies—originally developed for general educational technology, applied here to understand how AI tools transform all three knowledge domains simultaneously
- **Prompt Engineering as Design Competency**: Research evidence that semantic richness in prompts correlates with more expressive AI-generated images, suggesting prompt literacy represents a genuinely new design competency rather than merely a technical skill
- **Metadesign**: An emerging concept requiring designers to design the process itself—the prompts, constraints, and evaluation criteria—rather than just the artifacts
- **Signature Pedagogy**: Shulman's term for the studio model as design education's distinctive pedagogical space, now disrupted by AI at the levels of skill development, assessment, and professional identity
- **Research Agenda Gaps**: Three major deficits identified—outcome reporting (66.7% of studies unspecified), challenge documentation (74.4% unspecified), and theoretical frameworks (only 26.3% employed any)

## Theoretical Framework
The review employs scoping review methodology following Arksey and O'Malley's framework, refined by Levac et al. and the Joanna Briggs Institute, with reporting adherent to PRISMA-ScR guidelines. The author's analytical lens integrates the TPACK framework with design education's distinctive pedagogical traditions—studio learning, the critique, designerly ways of knowing (Cross)—to situate AI disruption within the specific context of design education rather than treating it as a general higher education issue.

The "maker-to-curator" framing provides a coherent narrative for understanding the paradigm shift: if AI can now perform the making tasks that previously defined design expertise, then design education's core mission must shift toward cultivating the judgment, vision, and contextual reasoning that AI cannot replicate.

## Methods
Web of Science Core Collection searched using 18 query combinations of design education and AI-related terms. From 1,800 initial records, 156 studies met eligibility criteria: addressing AI/GenAI technologies within design disciplines (architecture, fashion, graphic, product, UX/UI, interaction, or general design education) in higher education contexts, published in English 2017-2026. Data were extracted using a standardized form capturing 21 variables across bibliometric, methodological, technological, and pedagogical dimensions.

## Main Arguments
- Research volume exploded 6.4-fold post-ChatGPT (86.5% of studies published after 2022), indicating a field in rapid formative development rather than a mature evidence base—making synthesis and agenda-setting particularly urgent
- Geographic concentration (China 31.4%, USA 13.5%) combined with linguistic filtering (English only) creates serious blind spots in the global picture of design AI education, particularly for non-Western pedagogical traditions
- The field concentrates on creativity development (35.9%), assessment (27.6%), and curriculum design (22.4%), while under-researching graphic design specifically, professional identity development, and long-term skill trajectories
- Only 26.3% of studies employed theoretical frameworks—predominantly TPACK, design thinking, and experiential learning—indicating theoretical underdevelopment relative to the complexity of the phenomenon being studied
- Methodological quality is weak: 53.8% employed lower-quality research designs (typically descriptive surveys or case studies without comparison conditions), limiting the field's capacity to make causal claims
- The proposed research agenda prioritizes longitudinal studies (to distinguish transient novelty effects from sustained trajectories), theoretical integration, under-researched disciplines, and ethical frameworks for responsible AI integration

## Limitations & Critiques
Web of Science coverage may exclude important conference proceedings and regional journals, particularly from Asia where much relevant work is published. The English-language restriction systematically excludes non-Anglophone scholarship. The 2017-2026 timeframe captures the beginning of the GenAI era but may be too short to observe mature pedagogical responses. As a scoping review rather than a systematic review, it maps the landscape without formally evaluating evidence quality or synthesizing effect sizes.

The maker-to-curator framing, while evocative, risks overstating the degree to which AI can substitute for craft knowledge—design judgment may remain deeply entangled with making practice in ways the metaphor elides.
