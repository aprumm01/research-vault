# Navigating a New Direction in HCI Education: Challenges of Teaching More-Than-Human Perspectives

## Summary
This paper examines the challenges educators face when teaching more-than-human perspectives in human-computer interaction (HCI) and technology design education. The authors developed a collection of teaching activities and conducted pilots with nearly 800 students across four European countries, exploring how these perspectives can be taught in various educational settings. More-than-human design challenges the long-standing user-centered and human-centered paradigms by considering the needs and agency of non-human entities such as animals, plants, ecosystems, and technologies like IoT and AI.

The paper builds on challenges previously identified by Bekker et al. (2023), including issues of inclusion, representation, human and non-human designers, outcome and effects, the role of technology, and bias. Through developing and piloting teaching activities, the authors identified several new emerging challenges: bridging between education and industry, feelings of hopelessness among students, teaching HCI research-based more-than-human perspectives, the role of AI in more-than-human design teaching, and lack of common terminology. The work contributes to an open educational resource (OER) that offers teaching activities addressing more-than-human perspectives.

The authors argue that addressing these challenges requires collaborative effort from the HCI research community to rethink and reshape the HCI curriculum, integrating more-than-human perspectives into the core of HCI education while encouraging critical thinking and fostering an inclusive approach that considers diverse human, more-than-human fellow beings, and things involved in design processes.

## Key Concepts
- **More-Than-Human Design**: An approach that moves beyond human-centrism to consider the needs, agency, and perspectives of non-human entities (animals, plants, ecosystems, technologies) in design processes
- **Inclusion**: Identifying and including more-than-human actors affected by or affecting a design, ensuring diverse perspectives are considered
- **Representation**: Finding ways to give voice to non-human entities who cannot speak for themselves in design processes
- **More-Than-Human Personas**: Visual representations of non-human stakeholders (e.g., seaweed, granite) to ensure their needs are considered in design decisions
- **Ecological Probes**: Non-human collectors used by students to explore natural environments and develop sensitivity toward more-than-human worlds
- **Post-Anthropocentric Design**: Design approaches that decenter humans and consider broader ecological and technological entanglements

## Theoretical Framework
The paper draws on post-anthropocentric and more-than-human design perspectives that challenge traditional human-centered design paradigms. It references related concepts including posthumanism, feminism, environmental humanism, multispecies design, and design for the post-human era. The theoretical foundation emphasizes recognizing the value and agency of all entities—human and more-than-human—and understanding the intimate entanglement between humans and non-human beings and things. The work is situated within the broader context of responding to the ecological crisis and moving toward more sustainable and just design practices.

## Methods
The research involved developing and piloting an open educational resource (OER) with teaching activities across four European countries with nearly 800 students. The teaching activities were designed to address previously identified challenges and were iteratively refined through reflective conversations between the team of educators. The development process used a learning-by-doing approach, with new challenges emerging through piloting activities and discussions with students. The OER targets educators in technology design programmes in higher education, serving as an inspirational repository rather than a full curriculum.

## Main Arguments
- More-than-human perspectives remain a niche area in both HCI education and industry practice, requiring integration into mainstream curriculum
- A significant gap exists between academic teaching of more-than-human perspectives (exploratory, speculative, activism-influenced) and industry practices (capitalist, profit-driven)
- Students may experience feelings of hopelessness when confronting the notion that technology may not fix all problems, particularly regarding climate and ecological crises
- Technology, being designed by humans, inherently over-represents human perspectives and requires critical examination of embedded biases
- The lack of common terminology (more-than-human, non-human, posthuman, multispecies design) creates confusion and hinders field development
- AI's role in more-than-human design requires repositioning from tool to "designing-with" partner that engages with agency and co-constitutive relations
- Educators must balance introducing non-Western perspectives aligned with more-than-human ecological worldviews while avoiding cultural appropriation

## Limitations & Critiques
- The OER does not currently offer teaching activities addressing the challenge of technology as co-creator in design processes
- The research and teaching activities are situated in the West/Global North, potentially introducing bias when attempting to incorporate non-Western perspectives
- More-than-human design research remains largely within academic circles and may not directly address issues faced by designers in industry
- There are few academic examples of how to assess whether students have become responsible designers practicing more-than-human design
- The emerging field lacks concrete HCI research-based methods on which to base design-oriented teaching approaches
- Students and educators from Western backgrounds may risk cultural appropriation when introducing non-Western ecological worldviews
