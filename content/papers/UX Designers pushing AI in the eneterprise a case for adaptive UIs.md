# UX Designers pushing AI in the eneterprise a case for adaptive UIs

## Summary
John Zimmerman, Carnegie Mellon University Changhoon Oh, Carnegie Mellon University Nur Yildirim, Carnegie Mellon University Alex Kass, Accenture Teresa Tung, Accenture Jodi Forlizzi, Carnegie Mellon University I M A G E B Y A N N I K K A A R T / S H U T T E R S T O C K C O M UX Designers Pushing AI in the Enterprise: A Case for Adaptive UIs Insights → Adaptive user interfaces (AUIs) provide an untapped opportunity for UX designers to innovate with AI → AUIs seem well suited for.

## Key Concepts
- Adaptive user interfaces as low-risk AI integration recognizing and automating frequent repetitive tasks
- UX designers positioned as AI innovators through AUI identification and design
- Enterprise applications offering measurable ROI through worker time-savings versus consumer applications
- Collective intelligence combining machine automation with human contextual understanding
- Three common AUI design patterns: ranked choices, highlighted actionable entities, and form autofill
- Six-step UX process for AUI innovation from opportunity recognition to development communication
- Design ethics attending to worker agency, identity, self-efficacy, and unintended consequences
- Telemetry data and labeled datasets as essential resources for AUI training
- Return on investment calculation based on task frequency, worker population, and time-cost savings

## Theoretical Framework
Practice-oriented design guidance drawing on human-AI interaction principles, emphasizing user-in-the-loop automation that maintains human control while leveraging machine learning capabilities.

## Methods
Practitioner perspective article based on collaborative research between Accenture and Carnegie Mellon, presenting design process framework and pattern identification rather than empirical study.

## Main Arguments
- UX designers uniquely positioned to recognize AUI opportunities through user-centered perspective on repetitive tasks
- AUIs provide untapped low-hanging fruit for enterprise AI innovation with easier cost-benefit calculation than complex AI initiatives
- Current design processes and tools do not scaffold AUI design, requiring new systematic approaches
- Enterprise applications offer better AUI adoption potential than consumer apps due to measurable worker time value
- User-in-the-loop approach of AUIs reduces risk compared to fully automated AI systems
- Design teams should lead AUI innovation but currently lack training, methods, and prototyping tools
- Ethical design must prioritize worker felt experience, avoiding unhealthy dependencies and Taylorist productivity focus
- AUIs work best for tasks workers consider boring, repetitive, and tedious freeing attention for human intelligence work

## Limitations & Critiques
- Prescriptive process assumes universal applicability across enterprise contexts without empirical validation
- Focus on productivity and time-savings risks reinforcing work intensification rather than humanistic goals
- Limited discussion of worker resistance or concerns about surveillance through telemetry data collection
- Does not address power dynamics in who decides which tasks are automated or worth workers' time
- Minimal engagement with labor studies literature on automation impacts beyond individual efficiency
- Assumes workers will use freed time for higher-value work without examining systemic constraints
- Pattern identification based on existing consumer applications may limit imagination for enterprise-specific approaches
- Ethics sidebar acknowledges but does not resolve fundamental tensions between efficiency and worker wellbeing
