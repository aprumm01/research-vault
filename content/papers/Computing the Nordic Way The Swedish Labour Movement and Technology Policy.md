# Computing the Nordic Way: The Swedish Labour Movement and Technology Policy

## Summary
Traces how the Swedish reformist labour movement framed digital technology as a problem or solution from the post-war period through 2000, revealing how non-formal adult education (folkbildning) served as a governance tool oscillating between techno-utopian adaptation of people to technology and techno-dystopian democratic control of technology. Argues that current assumptions about digital citizenship replay historical patterns.

## Key Concepts
- Educational imaginaries
- Sociotechnical imaginary
- Folkbildning (non-formal popular education)
- Digital citizenship
- Techno-utopianism vs. techno-dystopianism

## Theoretical Framework
Sociotechnical imaginaries (Jasanoff 2015); popular education history; digital citizenship as a politically constructed category; positions education as a governance technology in digital policy.

## Methods
Empirical analysis of materials from Swedish reformist labour movement associations; historical document analysis spanning the post-war period to 2000; discursive analysis of educational imaginaries.

## Main Arguments
- Digital technology discourses oscillate between utopian (adapt people to machines) and dystopian (control machines for people) framings across decades
- Non-formal adult education is consistently mobilized as a governance instrument in both framings
- The ideal of "digital citizenship" is politically constructed to meet anticipated technological futures rather than responding to actual citizen needs
- Contemporary AI literacy discourses replay the same oscillation between adaptation and democratic control

## Limitations & Critiques
Specific to the Swedish Nordic context and the reformist labour movement tradition; limited to organizations' official documents rather than workers' or learners' perspectives; period ends at 2000, predating the most significant recent AI developments.
