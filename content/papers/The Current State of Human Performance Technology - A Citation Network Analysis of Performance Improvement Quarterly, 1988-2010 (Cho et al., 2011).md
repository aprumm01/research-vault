# The Current State of Human Performance Technology: A Citation Network Analysis of Performance Improvement Quarterly, 1988-2010

## Summary
This study conducted a citation network analysis (CNA) of human performance technology (HPT) to examine its current state as a field. The authors analyzed articles published in Performance Improvement Quarterly (PIQ) from June 1988 to April 2010, coding 647 articles and identifying 575 citation links to understand the structural attributes, influential articles, and cohesive subgroups within the HPT scholarly community.

The findings revealed that the PIQ network showed widely but loosely connected structural attributes with less centralization than comparable fields like human resource development (HRD). The network contained 367 connected nodes with 19 distinctive components, indicating that HPT articles are more broadly linked but less densely clustered than HRD research. The 10 most influential articles were identified, with Wedman and Tessmer (1993) ranking highest in both citation frequency and centrality.

Through faction analysis, five cohesive subgroups emerged representing the key research themes in HPT: (1) Performance, (2) Instructional Design, (3) Performance Support, (4) Organization/Workplace, and (5) Transfer of Training. These themes overlap with HRD research areas, suggesting opportunities for interdisciplinary collaboration while also indicating that instructional design serves as a distinguishing topic for HPT compared to related fields.

## Key Concepts
- **Human Performance Technology (HPT)**: A field defined as engineering systems that allow people and organizations to perform in ways that satisfy all stakeholders, heavily practice-oriented with roots in the 1970s training profession
- **Citation Network Analysis (CNA)**: A social network analysis approach to examining relational and network patterns in citation data, providing a systematic way to study a field's structure and influential works
- **Closeness Centrality**: A measure reflecting how close a node is to others in a network, computed as the inverse of the sum of geodesic distances between nodes
- **Factions**: Cohesive subgroups of nodes that have strong interconnections internally and weaker connections with external nodes
- **Small World Network**: A network characterized by high fractionalization and small number of steps between nodes; the PIQ network did not fit this pattern

## Theoretical Framework
The study draws on social network analysis (SNA) theory from sociology, particularly the work of Jo, Jeung, Park, and Yoon (2009) for examining structures and patterns of relationships among scholarly works. The authors also reference Pasteur's Quadrant (Stokes, 1997) to frame the importance of balancing fundamental understanding with practical problem-solving in applied fields like HPT. The CNA methodology builds on previous citation analysis work by Sleezer and Sleezer (1998) in HRD, enabling comparative analysis between related fields.

## Methods
The study employed citation network analysis using the following procedures:
1. **Data Collection**: Collected all articles from PIQ (June 1988 - April 2010), manually coding 647 peer-reviewed articles with title, authors, year, volume, issue, and type
2. **Citation Coding**: Identified and coded 575 citation links between PIQ articles, with cross-checking for reliability
3. **Network Analysis**: Used UCINET software for comprehensive network analysis and Pajek/NetDraw for visualization
4. **Analysis Techniques**: Computed network attributes (density, centralization, geodesic distances), identified influential articles through citation frequency and closeness centrality, conducted co-citation analysis, and performed faction analysis to identify cohesive subgroups

## Main Arguments
- HPT needs to examine its current state from a research perspective to envision its future, as theory and practice are intertwined and cannot advance independently
- Traditional research methods (content analysis, surveys, Delphi) have limitations in revealing network patterns and relational structures among scholars
- The PIQ citation network is widely but loosely connected with evenly distributed influence, unlike star-shaped or small world networks dominated by few influential nodes
- Five distinct research themes (factions) characterize HPT scholarship: performance, instructional design, performance support, organization/workplace, and transfer of training
- Instructional design distinguishes HPT from related fields like HRD, and HPT has a strong base in this area that can be an asset for the field's strategic positioning
- Future HPT research should move toward more theory development and interdisciplinary collaboration, particularly with HRD

## Limitations & Critiques
- The study analyzed only PIQ articles, potentially missing influential works published in other venues
- SNA tools measure network patterns quantitatively but interpretation depends entirely on researcher judgment and qualitative discretion
- Few statistical techniques exist for generalizing network findings to broader populations
- Citation data are binary (cited or not), failing to capture qualitative aspects like the strength or nature of intellectual relationships
- The study found that most influential instructional design articles were published in the 1990s, with recent articles not appearing on main paths, suggesting possible stagnation in that subfield
- HPT has a tendency to cite its own articles most frequently, which may replicate outdated theories rather than advancing the field through interdisciplinary engagement
