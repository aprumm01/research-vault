# Intellectual Assembly Lines The Rationalization of Managerial, Professional, and Technical Work Perrolle, Judith A. 1984

## Summary
computer technology in the context of the theo- includes the evaluation and control of the work of others To argue these mental activities can be organized in assembly line fashion presumes that computers can perform as technical experts, can acquire a kind of judgment based upon general principles and experience, and can make managerial decisions These are precisely the claims of the research area known as artificial intelligence, which is part of the emerging field of.

- Proletarianization of professional work

## Theoretical Framework
- Labor process theory - rationalization and deskilling of work
- Taylorism/Scientific management applied to knowledge work
- Marxist analysis of technology and labor
- Braverman's deskilling thesis extended to professional work
- Political economy of technology

## Methods
- Historical analysis
- Theoretical examination
- Critical analysis of AI and computer technology
- Conceptual framework development

## Main Arguments
- Computer technology enables rationalization of managerial and professional work previously considered immune
- AI and expert systems can perform technical judgment traditionally requiring human expertise
- Intellectual work is being organized along assembly line principles through computerization
- Managerial evaluation and control functions can be automated through AI systems
- Professional work is subject to same deskilling processes as manual labor
- Claims of AI performing expert judgment represent fundamental shift in work organization
- Division of mental labor parallels earlier division of manual labor
- Technology-driven rationalization threatens professional autonomy and discretion
- Historical trajectory suggests continued automation of cognitive work

## Limitations & Critiques
- Written in 1984, predates actual widespread AI implementation
- May overestimate capability of early AI and expert systems
- Does not fully account for new forms of skilled work created by technology
- Deterministic view of technology may underplay human agency and resistance
- Limited empirical evidence for predicted deskilling of professionals
- May not account for ways professionals adapt and maintain expertise
- Could underestimate complexity of tacit knowledge and judgment
- Historical analysis primarily focused on industrial contexts, limited service sector analysis
