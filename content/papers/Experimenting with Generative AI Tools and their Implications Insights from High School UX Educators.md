# Experimenting with Generative AI Tools and their Implications: Insights from High School UX Educators

## Summary
Weinberg and Chan (Pratt Institute, ~2024–2025) document high school UX educators' experiences experimenting with generative AI tools in interdisciplinary UX courses through educator interviews. Their three core findings concern how GAI reshapes the learning environment: it creates new forms of student-to-student and student-to-AI collaborative learning; it modifies brainstorming and visualization workflows; and it introduces concerns and barriers around curriculum integration that educators are still navigating. The paper focuses on a distinctive and underrepresented context — pre-college design education — where AI integration decisions happen with less institutional support than in higher education.

## Key Concepts
- GAI in high school UX and design education
- Student-to-AI collaborative learning
- Modified brainstorming and visualization workflows
- Educator concerns about curriculum integration
- Pre-college design pedagogy

## Theoretical Framework
Design pedagogy and technology integration; situates GAI adoption in the specific constraints and opportunities of high school teaching contexts.

## Methods
Interviews with high school UX educators at interdisciplinary UX/design courses; qualitative thematic analysis. Pratt Institute (6 pages, likely CHI or design education conference, ~2024–2025).

## Main Arguments
- High school UX educators are experimenting with GAI tools independently, without institutional guidance or curriculum frameworks
- Student-to-GAI collaborative learning creates new peer dynamics — students teach each other how to use AI, not just the instructor
- GAI modifies rather than replaces brainstorming and visualization — it accelerates early phases but requires more critical evaluation than traditional methods
- Educators face concerns about equity (access to paid AI tools), academic integrity, and whether AI use develops or atrophies foundational design skills

## Limitations & Critiques
Small sample of educator interviews; Pratt Institute context (NYC arts school) may not represent diverse high school contexts. Short paper (6 pages) limits depth. Tools used are not specified.
