# Utilizing Generative AI for Instructional Design: Exploring Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats

## Summary
This study investigates the potential of ChatGPT as an instructional design tool by having four researchers collaboratively use it to generate a course map for a 4-week online master's course on makerspaces. Using a SWOT (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats) analysis framework, the research team developed a series of prompts based on backward design principles and evaluated ChatGPT's outputs for various instructional components including learning objectives, course materials, activities, discussion prompts, quiz questions, assignments, and rubrics.

The findings reveal that ChatGPT demonstrates promise as a "performance empowering partner" that can support rapid prototyping of course maps while simultaneously highlighting critical limitations. Strengths include generating suitable learning objectives, aligning activities with objectives, suggesting appropriate toolkits, providing detailed rubrics, and offering diverse instructional activities. However, significant weaknesses emerged including limited contextual understanding, low reliability of resources (fabricating non-existent references), vague instructions, repetitive discussion activities, lack of depth in assessments, and unreliable output completion.

The study concludes that while ChatGPT holds potential for expediting time-consuming instructional design tasks, it requires instructional designers' nuanced understanding of domain knowledge and careful adaptation and contextualization of outputs.

## Key Concepts
- **Automated Instructional Design**: Using computational methods to streamline course planning processes, building on early work in programmed instruction and courseware authoring
- **Backward Design**: Wiggins and McTighe's (1998) instructional design approach that begins with identifying desired results (learning objectives), then determines acceptable evidence (assessments), before planning learning experiences
- **Course Map**: A planning document showing module objectives, assessments, hands-on activities, and assignments that provides structure for course development
- **Performance Empowering Partner**: The conceptualization of AI as a tool that augments rather than replaces instructional designers' capabilities

## Theoretical Framework
The study draws on the history of automated instructional design from Skinnerian programmed instruction through cognitive science approaches like ACT-R (Adaptive Control of Thought-Rationale). It positions ChatGPT within this lineage while recognizing its distinctive capability for conversational, task-agnostic interaction. The SWOT framework provides the analytical structure, borrowed from strategic management to identify internal factors (strengths/weaknesses) and external factors (opportunities/threats).

## Methods
- **Design**: Collaborative design-based research with SWOT analysis
- **Participants**: Four researchers (one professor of information science with makerspace teaching experience, two professors of instructional technology, one instructional designer)
- **Process**: Developed 7 sequential ChatGPT prompts using backward design framework; each researcher generated outputs independently; team reviewed and categorized findings using SWOT
- **Tool**: ChatGPT 3.5 (free version)
- **Context**: 4-week online master's course titled "Makerspaces as Learning Environments"

## Main Arguments
- **Strengths**: ChatGPT can generate clear, measurable learning objectives; align activities with objectives; suggest appropriate resources and toolkits; provide detailed rubrics; offer diverse activities across modules; and cross-reference materials
- **Weaknesses**: Limited contextual understanding leads to infeasible activities (e.g., in-person activities for online course); fabricates references that don't exist; provides vague, open-ended instructions lacking scaffolding; generates repetitive discussion prompts; creates shallow quiz questions focused on factual recall; produces inconsistent outputs
- **Opportunities**: Can decrease instructors' workload through rapid prototyping; may promote more student-centered learning by freeing designers for deeper pedagogical work
- **Threats**: Quality control is essential as outputs vary across users; instructional designers must verify all suggested resources and adapt content to specific contexts

## Limitations & Critiques
- Limited to one course topic (makerspaces); different disciplines may yield different results
- Used ChatGPT 3.5; newer versions or variations may perform differently
- Conducted during early period of ChatGPT availability; capabilities continue to evolve rapidly
- Four researchers may have different prompting styles affecting output variability
- Does not include student learning outcomes data to validate instructional effectiveness
- SWOT analysis is researcher-interpreted rather than empirically validated with broader user testing
- Concern raised that expediting early phases with ChatGPT may reduce instructors' independent research skills and engagement with source materials
