# Perceptions and Integration of Generative Artificial Intelligence in Creative Practices and Industries: A Scoping Review and Conceptual Model

## Summary
This scoping review examines how creative professionals across four domains—visual art and design, writing and literature, performing arts, and environmental/spatial design—perceive and integrate Generative AI (GenAI) technologies into their workflows. Analyzing 57 empirical papers from 2022-2025 following PRISMA-ScR guidelines, the review identifies universal trends while mapping domain-specific differences. A key finding is that career stage emerges as a critical factor: entry-level professionals demonstrate enthusiasm and view GenAI as a natural digital tool extension, while senior practitioners express skepticism about expertise devaluation.

The review reveals that all fields consistently position GenAI in early-stage conceptualization rather than final production, developing hybrid methodologies that preserve human judgment in convergent creative phases. Integration levels follow an inverse relationship with traditional notions of "pure" creativity—fields prioritizing embodied practice and cultural authenticity (fine arts, literary fiction, classical music) show the most significant resistance, while commercially oriented domains embrace higher adoption for efficiency gains. The authors propose a conceptual framework positioning creative professionals' attitudes along two dimensions: knowledge codifiability (tacit/embodied to explicit/codifiable) and output materiality (physical/permanent to digital/ephemeral).

The paper contributes a comprehensive mapping of GenAI applications across creative disciplines, identifies patterns of adoption and resistance, and offers a framework for understanding field-appropriate integration strategies for education and practice.

## Key Concepts
- **Knowledge Codifiability**: The spectrum from tacit/embodied knowledge that resists formalization to explicit/codifiable knowledge that can be systematized for AI processing
- **Output Materiality**: The range from physical/permanent artifacts embedded in social and cultural relations to digital/ephemeral outputs
- **Hybrid Methodologies**: Approaches combining traditional and AI-augmented practices that preserve human control over final creative decisions
- **Peripheral-to-Core Adoption**: The pattern where AI tools enter creative practice through non-creative support tasks before gradually approaching core creative work
- **Meta-Creation**: The shift from direct creation to curation and refinement of AI-generated possibilities

## Theoretical Framework
The conceptual framework draws on Polanyi's tacit-explicit knowledge spectrum and theories of material culture to position creative fields in a two-dimensional space. Fields with high tacit knowledge and physical outputs (embodied material practice) show low GenAI integration, while fields with explicit processes and digital outputs (systematic digital practice) show high integration. This framework explains both resistance and adoption patterns across creative domains.

## Methods
- PRISMA-ScR scoping review methodology
- Systematic search across 18+ academic databases
- 2,393 initial results screened to 57 included papers
- Data extraction covering author information, methods, creative domain, sample characteristics, and key findings
- Qualitative synthesis organized around five analytical categories: professional perceptions, integration patterns, integration levels, domain-specific applications, and impact on creative practice

## Main Arguments
- GenAI integration follows predictable patterns based on field characteristics, not just individual choice
- Career stage significantly shapes attitudes, with generational differences reflecting distinct conceptions of creative practice and professional identity
- All creative fields share common concerns about authenticity, ownership, and cultural preservation despite different integration levels
- The transition from creator to curator represents a fundamental shift in what constitutes creative expertise
- Educational approaches must balance tacit/embodied knowledge preservation with explicit/codifiable skill development

## Limitations & Critiques
The review acknowledges methodological limitations including definitional fluidity in how creative professionals discuss GenAI, the predominance of small-scale qualitative studies, reliance on self-reported data, and cross-sectional approaches that cannot reveal longitudinal attitude evolution. The categorical separation of creative fields may miss hybrid practices and cross-disciplinary pollination. Participant representation skews toward Western/Northern, digitally-connected practitioners, potentially missing Global South perspectives and diverse adoption patterns. The rapid pace of GenAI advancement may also date findings quickly.
