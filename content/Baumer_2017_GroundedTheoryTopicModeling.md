# Comparing Grounded Theory and Topic Modeling: Extreme Divergence or Unlikely Convergence?

## Metadata
- **Authors:** Eric P.S. Baumer, David Mimno, Shion Guha, Emily Quan, Geri K. Gay
- **Year:** 2017
- **DOI/URL:** https://epublications.marquette.edu/mscs_fac/560
- **Source PDF:** [[Comparing Grounded Theory and Topic Modeling_ Extreme Divergence.pdf]]

---

## Overview
This paper applies two fundamentally different analytical methods — [[grounded theory]] (interpretive social science) and [[topic modeling]] (statistical machine learning) — to the same survey dataset about social media nonuse. Rather than arguing for one over the other, the authors demonstrate surprising process similarities and complementary results, and propose ways the two approaches might be productively combined. It is a methods-comparison paper with real implications for mixed-methods research design.

## Research Overview
- **Research question:** What are the similarities and differences in process and outcomes when grounded theory and topic modeling are applied to the same textual dataset? Can they be combined productively?
- **Methodology:** Comparative methods analysis; same dataset (survey open-text responses about social media nonuse) analyzed via (1) grounded theory coding and (2) LDA topic modeling; results compared systematically
- **Context:** Information science; *Journal of the Association for Information Science and Technology* (JASIST), June 2017; Cornell University + Marquette University

## Theories of Knowledge
- **Epistemological stance:** Pluralist — the paper bridges interpretivist (grounded theory) and positivist/computational (topic modeling) traditions explicitly
- **Key frameworks:**
  - [[Grounded Theory]] — Glaser & Strauss (1967); Charmaz (2006); inductive, iterative coding from data
  - [[Topic Modeling]] / [[Latent Dirichlet Allocation]] (LDA) — Blei, Ng & Jordan (2003); statistical regularities in word co-occurrence
  - [[Thick Description]] — Geertz (1973); the value of interpretive depth
  - [[Mixed Methods Research]] — the paper is a landmark for methodological pluralism in text analysis
- **Knowledge construction:** Both methods construct knowledge differently — GT through researcher interpretation and iteration; TM through statistical inference — but both are data-driven and can converge on similar themes

## Central Arguments
1. Grounded theory and topic modeling appear radically different epistemologically, but their processes share surprising structural similarities: both are iterative, both involve researcher judgment in labeling emergent categories.
2. Applied to the same data, the two methods produce some overlapping and some complementary insights — neither is strictly superior.
3. Each method has unique advantages: GT produces richer, more interpretively nuanced accounts; TM scales to massive datasets and is less susceptible to researcher bias.
4. Combining both in a sequential or iterative mixed-methods design can overcome the limitations of each used alone.

## Evidence
- Dataset: survey responses about social media nonuse from prior studies
- GT analysis: open coding, axial coding, memo-writing; produced themes around privacy, distraction, authenticity, and social pressure
- TM analysis: LDA with multiple topic numbers tested; topics aligned substantially with GT themes, plus revealed additional patterns the human coders missed
- Key finding: "The two analyses produce some similar and some complementary insights" — convergence where expected; divergence where the methods' different sensitivities to context vs. frequency matter

> "These methods involve surprisingly similar processes and produce surprisingly similar results." (Baumer et al., 2017, p. 1)

> "Algorithmically defined topics can arguably provide an analysis driven more by the documents than by human preconceptions." (Baumer et al., 2017)

## Conclusion
The paper rejects the framing of GT vs. TM as a binary epistemological choice. Researchers should select methods based on research questions, data scale, and the kind of knowledge sought — and should consider combining them. The paper is foundational for anyone doing qualitative-quantitative mixed analysis of text.

## Bias Check
- Single dataset limits generalizability — nonuse of social media is a specific, self-selected population
- Authors are from computing/information science backgrounds; the grounded theory analysis may not reflect how a trained qualitative sociologist would apply GT
- The paper's optimism about convergence may understate the real epistemological tensions between paradigms

## Discussion Questions
1. How does this paper's argument about [[mixed methods]] apply to qualitative research in design and HCI — where are the productive intersections?
2. What is lost when [[grounded theory]]'s interpretive richness is compared to a computational method on its own terms?
3. Could this approach be applied to interview transcripts or design research data, not just survey responses?
4. How does the choice of number of topics in [[LDA]] introduce researcher subjectivity that undermines TM's "objective" appeal?
5. What ethical considerations arise when topic modeling is applied to sensitive qualitative data?
