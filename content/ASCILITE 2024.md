# ASCILITE 2024

## Summary
Zhang, Atiq, and Chow (University of Melbourne) present findings from a study exploring AI's role in UX research, focusing on current use patterns, challenges, and AI literacy gaps among HCI students and UX practitioners. Through in-depth interviews, they find that AI is primarily used for routine tasks (transcription, data summarization) while its potential as a creative partner in insight generation remains largely untapped. The paper argues that HCI education must deliberately develop AI literacy competencies to prepare students for AI-augmented UX practice.

## Key Concepts
- **AI in UX research**: Current applications limited to text processing, transcription, and large-data handling
- **AI literacy**: Capacity to critically understand, evaluate, and effectively use AI tools in research workflows
- **Human-AI partnership in research**: Underexplored potential of AI as a collaborative thought partner in qualitative research
- **HCI curriculum gaps**: Insufficient integration of AI literacy into HCI/UX education programs

## Theoretical Framework
Human-Computer Interaction and design education research. Frames AI adoption in professional practice through an AI literacy lens, drawing on technology acceptance and competency development frameworks.

## Methods
Qualitative study using in-depth interviews with UX researchers and HCI students at the University of Melbourne. Thematic analysis of current AI use, barriers, and readiness for AI adoption.

## Main Arguments
1. UX researchers use AI instrumentally for data handling but have not leveraged AI for deeper analytical or creative tasks
2. Limited AI education in HCI programs produces graduates unprepared for the AI-augmented workplace
3. Integrating AI literacy into HCI curricula is a professional readiness requirement, not optional
4. Ethical use of AI in UX research (transparency with participants, accuracy of AI-assisted insights) is undertheorized

## Limitations
Single-institution sample; Melbourne-based HCI students may not represent UX education globally. Short conference paper format limits depth. Rapid evolution of AI tools means use patterns may change quickly.
