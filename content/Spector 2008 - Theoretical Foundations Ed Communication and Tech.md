# Spector 2008 - Theoretical Foundations Ed Communication and Tech

## Summary
Spector's 2008 handbook chapter surveys the theoretical foundations of educational communications and technology, organizing the field's intellectual roots into four foundational areas: psychology of learning, communications theory, systems theory, and computer science. Spector argues that educational technology is inherently interdisciplinary, drawing on these foundational areas without being reducible to any of them. The chapter examines how each foundational area contributes distinct concepts, methods, and commitments that shape how educational technologists design, research, and evaluate instructional interventions.

## Key Concepts
- **Psychology of learning**: Behavioral, cognitive, and constructivist traditions providing theories of how people learn
- **Communications theory**: Shannon-Weaver model, message design, and encoding/decoding frameworks informing instructional media design
- **Systems theory**: General systems thinking applied to instructional design; ADDIE and related frameworks
- **Computer science**: Computational thinking, human-computer interaction, and algorithmic approaches to instruction
- **Interdisciplinarity**: The field's identity as drawing on but not reducible to its foundational disciplines

## Theoretical Framework
Meta-theoretical survey; Spector does not advocate a single theoretical framework but maps the landscape of theoretical resources available to educational technologists, tracing their intellectual origins and practical implications.

## Methods
Analytical literature review synthesizing foundational disciplines and their contributions to educational technology research and practice.

## Main Arguments
1. Educational technology is constitutively interdisciplinary; its theoretical resources span psychology, communications, systems theory, and computer science
2. Understanding the foundational areas helps practitioners make principled choices among competing theoretical frameworks for specific problems
3. No single theoretical tradition is sufficient; complex learning challenges require synthesis across foundational areas
4. The field's evolution reflects shifts in which foundational area is dominant—from behavioral psychology to cognitive science to constructivism to sociotechnical systems

## Limitations
The four-foundation framework is one organizational scheme among possible alternatives; it may not fully account for philosophical, sociological, or cultural foundations increasingly important in the field. Published in 2008; does not address AI, learning analytics, or more recent theoretical developments.
