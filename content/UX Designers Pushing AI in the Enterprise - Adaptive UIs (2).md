# UX Designers Pushing AI in the Enterprise - Adaptive UIs (2)

## Summary
Zimmerman, Oh, Yildirim, Kass, Tung, and Forlizzi (Carnegie Mellon and Accenture) argue for adaptive user interfaces (AUIs) as a key mechanism through which UX designers can introduce AI into enterprise contexts. AUIs learn from user interaction patterns to automate repetitive tasks, surface relevant information, and personalize workflows—providing AI value without requiring users to explicitly prompt or interact with AI systems. The paper positions UX designers as the organizational actors best positioned to identify, design, and advocate for beneficial AI integration in enterprise environments.

## Key Concepts
- **Adaptive User Interfaces (AUIs)**: Interfaces that use AI/ML to learn from user behavior and adapt their functionality to individual patterns and preferences
- **Task automation within UI**: AUIs identify repetitive workflows and automate them, freeing cognitive resources for higher-value decisions
- **UX designers as AI advocates**: UX practitioners as the bridge between AI capabilities and human-centered organizational needs
- **Enterprise AI integration**: The challenge of introducing AI into large organizational contexts with complex legacy systems and diverse user populations
- **AI that "knows your job"**: AUIs that develop models of individual work patterns and proactively support recurring tasks

## Theoretical Framework
Human-computer interaction and UX design research, grounded in user-centered design principles. Positions AI not as a replacement for human judgment but as a UI layer that reduces friction for routine tasks while preserving human control over consequential decisions.

## Methods
Conceptual/position paper drawing on Accenture's enterprise AI deployment experience and CMU HCI research on adaptive interfaces. Illustrative examples from procurement, clinical information retrieval, and legal contract management.

## Main Arguments
1. UX designers are uniquely positioned to identify which repetitive enterprise tasks are candidates for AI-driven automation through AUIs
2. AUIs that learn from behavior patterns provide AI value to users who would never intentionally interact with an "AI feature"
3. UX designers must advocate for AI integration that enhances rather than surveils or controls workers
4. The UX-AI design challenge is not "what can AI do?" but "what repetitive work should AI handle so humans can focus on judgment-intensive tasks?"

## Limitations
Short practitioner-oriented paper; theoretical depth is limited. Enterprise AI deployment experience from Accenture may reflect high-resource organizational contexts not representative of smaller organizations. AUI surveillance risks (monitoring work patterns) are acknowledged but not deeply analyzed.
