# Moore

## Summary
Moore's foundational work on transactional distance theory argues that distance education is not primarily a geographical phenomenon but a pedagogical one: "transactional distance" refers to the psychological and communication space between learner and instructor. Moore identifies three types of interaction critical to distance learning—learner-content, learner-instructor, and learner-learner—and argues that the interplay of dialogue (communication between learner and teacher) and structure (program design rigidity) determines transactional distance. Higher transactional distance requires greater learner autonomy.

## Key Concepts
- **Transactional distance**: The psychological and communication gap between learner and instructor in any learning environment
- **Dialogue**: Communication between learner and teacher; reduces transactional distance
- **Structure**: The degree to which a program can accommodate learner's individual needs; rigid structure increases transactional distance
- **Learner autonomy**: The extent to which learners can self-direct their learning; greater autonomy is required when transactional distance is high
- **Three types of interaction**: Learner-content, learner-instructor, learner-learner

## Theoretical Framework
Transactional distance theory, drawing on Dewey's concept of transaction (interaction that transforms both parties) and applied to distance education. Moore later developed the "Equivalency Theorem" suggesting any interaction type can substitute for another.

## Methods
Theoretical and conceptual; Moore developed the framework through analysis of correspondence education and early distance learning programs, refined over decades of practice and scholarship.

## Main Arguments
1. Distance education must be understood as a distinct pedagogical form, not merely conventional education delivered at a distance
2. Transactional distance—not physical distance—is the key variable affecting learning quality in distance education
3. Effective distance education design must balance structure and dialogue to achieve appropriate transactional distance for the learner population
4. Learner autonomy is a critical and underappreciated variable in distance education design

## Limitations
Transactional distance theory was developed before the internet era; its application to synchronous online learning, social media, and AI-mediated learning is contested. The framework is primarily descriptive and does not provide specific design prescriptions.
