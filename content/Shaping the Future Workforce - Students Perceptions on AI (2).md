# Shaping the Future Workforce - Students Perceptions on AI (2)

## Summary
Ferhataj, Biçoku, and Memaj (European University of Tirana and University of Elbasan, Albania) analyze university students' perceptions of AI and human-centric technologies in shaping the future workforce within the Industry 5.0 framework. The study finds that students hold generally optimistic but nuanced views about AI: they see AI as augmenting rather than replacing human work, emphasize the importance of human-centric values in Industry 5.0, and identify critical thinking, creativity, and ethical reasoning as the human capabilities most important to develop alongside AI literacy.

## Key Concepts
- **Industry 5.0**: The vision of a human-centric, sustainable, and resilient industrial transformation that complements rather than replaces Industry 4.0's efficiency emphasis
- **Human-centric technologies**: Technologies designed to enhance human capabilities and well-being rather than simply automate tasks
- **AI literacy**: Students' capacity to understand, critically evaluate, and effectively use AI tools
- **Human-AI collaboration framing**: Students' tendency to view AI as a collaborative tool rather than a replacement threat
- **Future workforce readiness**: The gap between current education and the competencies students believe future AI-integrated workplaces will require

## Theoretical Framework
Industry 5.0 framework (European Commission's vision) combined with educational research on student perceptions of emerging technologies. Survey-based quantitative design with descriptive and inferential statistics.

## Methods
Survey study with university students in Albania across multiple programs. Quantitative analysis of perceptions, concerns, and readiness assessments related to AI and human-centric technologies.

## Main Arguments
1. Students in Industry 5.0 contexts see AI as a partner in human-centric work, not primarily a displacement threat
2. Students identify a gap between current educational preparation and the AI literacy and human-centric competencies they believe future workplaces will require
3. Creativity, critical thinking, and ethical reasoning are consistently identified as the distinctively human capabilities worth cultivating
4. Educational institutions must explicitly prepare students for human-AI collaboration, not just AI tool use

## Limitations
Albanian student sample in a specific Eastern European educational context; findings may not generalize broadly. Self-report perceptions may not reflect actual AI literacy or workplace readiness. Cross-sectional design cannot track how perceptions change with AI experience.
