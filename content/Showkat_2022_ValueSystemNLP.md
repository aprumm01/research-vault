# "It's Like the Value System in the Loop": Domain Experts' Values Expectations for NLP Automation

## Metadata
- **Authors:** Dilruba Showkat, Eric P. S. Baumer
- **Year:** 2022
- **DOI/URL:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3532106.3533483
- **Source PDF:** [[It's Like the Value System in the Loop.pdf]]

---

## Overview
This study examines how domain experts — specifically investigative journalists and legal experts — conceptualize and articulate their values expectations for automated NLP (Natural Language Processing) systems. Using [[participatory design fiction]] workshops, the authors surface tensions between how NLP tools are built (driven by technical benchmarks) and how domain experts imagine they should behave (driven by professional ethics and values). The title quote captures the core finding: experts want their value systems embedded in the automation loop, not added as afterthoughts.

## Research Overview
- **Research question:** What values do domain experts expect to be embedded in automated NLP systems, and how can design fiction surface those expectations?
- **Methodology:** Qualitative; two co-speculation [[design fiction]] workshops with participants from two non-profit organizations (investigative journalists + legal experts); thematic analysis of workshop outputs
- **Context:** HCI/CSCW; DIS 2022; focus on journalism and legal research — fields where text processing is core and professional values are explicit

## Theories of Knowledge
- **Epistemological stance:** Interpretive; constructivist — values are socially situated and domain-specific, not universal or technically derivable
- **Key frameworks:**
  - [[Value Sensitive Design]] — designing technology informed by human values
  - [[Design Fiction]] — using speculative artifacts to elicit not-to-design implications
  - [[Participatory Design]] — domain experts as co-designers, not passive users
  - [[AutoML]] / [[AutoNLP]] critique — questions the assumption that reducing technical barriers is inherently beneficial
- **Knowledge construction:** Values are constructed through professional practice and community norms; they must be elicited through co-speculation rather than extracted from user requirements alone

## Central Arguments
1. NLP systems are often designed in isolation from their primary users (domain experts), leading to value misalignment at deployment.
2. Neither ML technologies nor the field of ML in general is value-neutral — embedded assumptions need to be made explicit.
3. [[Design fiction]] is uniquely productive for eliciting "not-to-design" implications — what experts do *not* want systems to do — which conventional requirements methods miss.
4. Domain expertise is systematically undervalued in model building; this creates downstream harms and professional friction.

## Evidence
- Two separate workshops with journalists (investigative reporting, fact-checking) and legal experts (legal research, document review)
- Speculative NLP tool concepts were specifically crafted to probe tensions: automation of editorial judgment, algorithmic source suggestion, automated legal summarization
- Key finding: participants consistently foregrounded professional accountability, source credibility, contextual judgment, and editorial discretion as non-negotiable values
- Tensions emerged around: transparency vs. efficiency, automation vs. professional identity, optimization for scale vs. preservation of nuance

> "It's like the value system in the loop" — participant describing their expectation that NLP tools would internalize and enact professional ethical standards, not just produce outputs. (Showkat & Baumer, 2022)

## Conclusion
Domain experts' values expectations are rich, domain-specific, and often in tension with how NLP systems are built and evaluated. Design fiction workshops are a powerful method for surfacing these expectations before systems are deployed. The authors call for greater inclusion of domain expertise at the design stage, not just as evaluation criteria post-hoc.

## Bias Check
- Participants from only two non-profit organizations — limited generalizability; both organizations likely share progressive professional ethics cultures
- Design fiction method may prime participants toward critical reflection and skepticism; may not surface values of experts comfortable with automation
- Authors' framing is sympathetic to domain expertise; less attention to cases where automation might genuinely improve value alignment

## Discussion Questions
1. How do [[professional values]] in journalism and law compare to values in UX or design practice — what would design professionals say about NLP automation of their work?
2. Can [[participatory design fiction]] be scaled without losing the contextual richness that makes it productive?
3. What does it mean to "put the value system in the loop" technically — is this [[value alignment]] by another name?
4. How should systems handle conflicts between different domain experts' value expectations within the same organization?
5. What is the relationship between this work and [[algorithmic accountability]] frameworks?
