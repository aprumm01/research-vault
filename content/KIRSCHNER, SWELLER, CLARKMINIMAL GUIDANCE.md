# KIRSCHNER, SWELLER, CLARKMINIMAL GUIDANCE

## Summary
Kirschner, Sweller, and Clark (2006) argue against minimally guided instructional approaches—including constructivist, discovery, problem-based, experiential, and inquiry-based methods—on the grounds that they ignore human cognitive architecture and contradict decades of empirical research. Drawing on cognitive load theory and the distinction between novice and expert learners, they contend that worked examples and explicit, direct instruction are more effective and efficient for novices than problem-based or discovery approaches, because novices lack the schema required to learn productively from unguided inquiry.

## Key Concepts
- **Minimally guided instruction**: Instructional approaches (discovery learning, PBL, inquiry learning) where learners construct knowledge with little direct instructional guidance
- **Cognitive Load Theory**: Framework holding that working memory has limited capacity; instruction must minimize extraneous load while maximizing germane load
- **Worked examples**: Showing learners how to solve problems rather than having them solve novel problems; highly effective for novices
- **Expert-novice distinction**: What works for experts (open-ended exploration) does not work for novices (who lack schemas to guide exploration)
- **Long-term memory schema**: The cognitive structures that enable experts to recognize patterns; novices need instruction to build these schemas

## Theoretical Framework
Cognitive architecture (Sweller's CLT), evolutionary psychology applied to learning, and 50+ years of empirical research on instructional effectiveness. Argues that instructional design must align with the structure and limitations of human working memory.

## Methods
Theoretical synthesis and meta-analytic review of experimental research on instructional effectiveness; selective review of CLT evidence and evidence against unguided approaches.

## Main Arguments
1. Minimally guided instruction ignores how the human cognitive system works; working memory is limited and needs guidance to build long-term memory schemas
2. Constructivist and discovery methods are intuitively appealing but empirically inferior to guided instruction for novice learners
3. The distinction between minimally guided and guided instruction is often blurred by proponents of constructivist approaches; the key variable is the amount of direct guidance provided
4. Evidence from cognitive load theory, worked examples research, and expertise studies consistently favors explicit, direct instruction for novices

## Limitations
The paper has been extensively contested; Hmelo-Silver et al. (2007) demonstrate that PBL and inquiry learning are not minimally guided. Selective literature review may underrepresent evidence favorable to guided inquiry. The argument may apply primarily to narrow declarative and procedural knowledge, not complex professional competencies.
