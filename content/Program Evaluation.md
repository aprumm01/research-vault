# Program Evaluation

## Summary
Chapter 1 of Fitzpatrick, Sanders, and Worthen's *Program Evaluation: Alternative Approaches and Practical Guidelines* (Third Edition) introduces the purpose, uses, and foundational concepts of program evaluation. The chapter establishes evaluation as distinct from research and informal judgment, defines formative and summative evaluation, distinguishes needs assessment from process and outcome evaluation, and addresses internal vs. external evaluator roles. The authors argue that evaluation is fundamentally about judgment—making value-laden determinations about program worth, merit, and significance.

## Key Concepts
- **Formal vs. informal evaluation**: Formal evaluation uses systematic data collection and explicit criteria; informal evaluation is intuitive and ad hoc
- **Formative evaluation**: Evaluation during program development to improve the program
- **Summative evaluation**: Evaluation after program completion to judge worth for continuation or adoption
- **Merit vs. worth**: Merit = intrinsic value; worth = value relative to a specific context or need
- **Needs assessment**: Determining gaps between current and desired performance states

## Theoretical Framework
Evaluation theory drawing on Scriven's logic of evaluation, Stufflebeam's CIPP model, and the broader literature on evaluation use. Frames evaluation as applied social science with explicit value commitments.

## Methods
Introductory conceptual chapter; establishes definitional groundwork for subsequent chapters on specific evaluation approaches.

## Main Arguments
1. Evaluation is inherently a judgment activity, not merely data collection; evaluators must grapple with whose values and criteria count
2. The formative/summative distinction has profound implications for evaluator role and methods
3. Informal evaluation—ubiquitous in professional practice—lacks rigor and systematic attention to evidence
4. External evaluators offer independence; internal evaluators offer contextual knowledge; the appropriate choice depends on purpose

## Limitations
Introductory textbook chapter providing conceptual grounding rather than theoretical advances. The formative/summative distinction, while useful, may be too binary for complex programs with iterative improvement cycles.
