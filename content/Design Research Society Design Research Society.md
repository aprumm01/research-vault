# Design Research Society Design Research Society

## Summary
Schneider and Csűrös (Moholy-Nagy University of Art and Design Budapest / Future Potentials Observatory) theorize generative AI's role in design through the lens of design philosophy and the "material turn" in design theory. They argue that GenAI constitutes a new kind of distributed creative agency that challenges established notions of authorship, intentionality, and material resistance in design. Drawing on posthumanist and new materialist frameworks, the paper positions GenAI not as a tool but as an active participant in distributed creativity—co-shaping design outcomes through its own "generative logic."

## Key Concepts
- **Distributed creativity**: Creativity understood as distributed across human designers, materials, tools, and context rather than residing in individual human genius
- **Material turn in design**: Theoretical move emphasizing the active role of materials and technologies in shaping design processes and outcomes
- **Generative logic**: GenAI's internal tendencies (learned distributions, latent spaces) that actively shape outputs beyond designer intent
- **Posthumanist design**: Design theory that decenters the human designer as sole creative agent
- **Authorship dissolution**: The challenge GenAI poses to traditional conceptions of creative ownership and intentional design

## Theoretical Framework
Posthumanist and new materialist philosophy (Barad, Latour, Ingold) applied to design theory. The "material turn" frames design materials—including AI—as having affordances and resistances that actively participate in shaping design outcomes.

## Methods
Conceptual/theoretical analysis presented at EKSIG 2025 (Design Research Society Experiential Knowledge conference). No empirical data; philosophical argument and design theory synthesis.

## Main Arguments
1. GenAI is not a neutral tool but an active participant in design with its own generative logic shaped by training data and model architecture
2. Distributed creativity frameworks better account for human-GenAI design practice than tool-use or authorship models
3. The material turn's insights—that materials resist and redirect designer intent—apply to GenAI: its latent spaces and learned biases push back against designer intentions
4. Design philosophy must evolve to account for AI as a creative agent without collapsing into either AI determinism or naive tool instrumentalism

## Limitations
Highly theoretical paper that may be difficult to translate into practical design education or practice guidance. Posthumanist frameworks are contested and not universally accepted in design research. Conference paper format limits depth of argument.
