# EDUCATIONAL PSYCHOLOGIST,42(2), 99–107

## Summary
Hmelo-Silver, Duncan, and Chinn (2007) respond to Kirschner, Sweller, and Clark's (2006) argument that minimally guided instruction is ineffective. The authors demonstrate that Kirschner et al. mischaracterize problem-based learning (PBL) and inquiry learning (IL) as "minimally guided" when in fact both approaches are highly scaffolded. Drawing on empirical evidence, Hmelo-Silver et al. argue that PBL and IL produce deep learning, transfer, and professional competencies that direct instruction cannot match, precisely because they structure guided inquiry rather than abandoning guidance.

## Key Concepts
- **Scaffolding in PBL/IL**: Structural supports including problem design, facilitator guidance, structured debriefing, and collaborative protocols that distinguish PBL/IL from unguided discovery
- **Mischaracterization of PBL**: Kirschner et al. conflate PBL and IL with pure discovery learning; Hmelo-Silver et al. show these are empirically distinct approaches
- **Transfer evidence**: PBL graduates demonstrate superior transfer of learning to novel clinical and professional contexts compared to conventionally instructed peers
- **Cognitive load in PBL**: Well-designed PBL manages cognitive load through scaffolding, not by eliminating it through direct instruction
- **Knowledge in use**: PBL develops integrated, applicable knowledge rather than inert knowledge acquired through direct instruction

## Theoretical Framework
Constructivist and situated learning theories; draws on empirical research from medical, science, and professional education. Argues that scaffolded inquiry is consistent with—not contrary to—cognitive learning principles.

## Methods
Systematic review and synthesis of empirical research on PBL and inquiry learning outcomes; meta-analyses and longitudinal studies from medical education and science education.

## Main Arguments
1. PBL and IL are not minimally guided—they employ extensive scaffolding that addresses cognitive load concerns
2. Kirschner et al.'s critique applies to pure discovery learning, not to the scaffolded inquiry of PBL and IL
3. Empirical evidence consistently shows PBL and IL produce strong transfer and professional competency outcomes
4. The debate is not "guidance vs. no guidance" but "what kinds of guidance best support complex learning"

## Limitations
Most PBL evidence comes from medical education; generalizability to other disciplines is sometimes contested. Long-term outcomes are more favorable than short-term knowledge acquisition—which may disadvantage PBL in contexts emphasizing exam performance.
